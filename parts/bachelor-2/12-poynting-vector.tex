\chapter{Electromagnetic Energy and the Poynting Vector}\label{ch:b2:poynting-vector}

A battery lights a bulb through two copper wires. Where does the
energy travel? Not inside the copper, it turns out: the electrons
there drift at a millimetre per second and carry next to nothing. The
energy flows through the \emph{space around the wires}, in the
electromagnetic field, and enters the bulb's filament through its
sides. The Sun delivers a kilowatt to every square metre of a sunny
roof the same way, across a hundred and fifty million kilometres of
vacuum. This chapter derives from \omterm{thm:b2:maxwell-equations:maxwell}{Maxwell's equations} the balance
sheet of electromagnetic energy --- how much is stored in a field, and
the vector, Poynting's, that says where it goes --- and applies it to a
wire, a capacitor, a coil and a beam of light.

\section{The energy balance of the field}

\begin{theorem}[Poynting's theorem]\label{thm:b2:poynting-vector:poynting}
\index{Poynting vector}\index{Poynting's theorem}\index{electromagnetic energy density}
Define the \emph{\omterm{thm:b2:poynting-vector:poynting}{electromagnetic energy density}} and the \emph{\omterm{thm:b2:poynting-vector:poynting}{Poynting
vector}}
\[
u = \frac{\varepsilon_0E^2}2 + \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} \quad (\unit{J/m^3}) , \qquad
\vect\Pi = \frac{\vect E\wedge\vect B}{\mu_0} \quad (\unit{W/m^2}) .
\]
\omterm{thm:b2:maxwell-equations:maxwell}{Maxwell's equations} imply, at every point,
\[
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \operatorname{div}\vect\Pi = -\vect j\cdot\vect E ,
\]
and, for any fixed volume $V$ bounded by $\Sigma$,
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\iiint_Vu\,\dd\tau = -\iint_\Sigma\vect\Pi\cdot\vect n\,\dd S - \iiint_V\vect j\cdot\vect E\,\dd\tau :
\]
the field energy inside $V$ decreases by what flows out through the
boundary --- the flux of $\vect\Pi$ --- and by what the field gives to
the charges ($\vect j\cdot\vect E$, the Joule power in a conductor). $\vect\Pi$
is the energy flux density: the power crossing a unit surface normal
to it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From Maxwell--Ampère, $\vect j = \operatorname{\vect{curl}}\vect B/\mu_0 - \varepsilon_0\partial_t
\vect E$, so $\vect j\cdot\vect E = \vect E\cdot\operatorname{\vect{curl}}\vect B/\mu_0 -
\partial_t(\varepsilon_0E^2/2)$. The identity $\operatorname{div}(\vect E\wedge\vect B) = \vect B
\cdot\operatorname{\vect{curl}}\vect E - \vect E\cdot\operatorname{\vect{curl}}\vect B$ (check it
on the components) and Maxwell--Faraday give $\vect E\cdot\operatorname{\vect{curl}}
\vect B = -\operatorname{div}(\vect E\wedge\vect B) - \vect B\cdot\partial_t\vect B = -\mu_0
\operatorname{div}\vect\Pi - \partial_t(B^2/2)$. Collect: $\vect j\cdot\vect E = -\operatorname{div}
\vect\Pi - \partial_tu$. Integrate over $V$ and apply the \omterm{def:b2:maxwell-equations:operators}{divergence} theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not fixed]\label{rem:b2:poynting-vector:interpretation}
The theorem fixes only the flux of $\vect\Pi$ through closed surfaces
and the total $u$; adding to $\vect\Pi$ any divergence-free field would
change nothing observable. The choice above is the simplest, it is the
one that agrees with the momentum carried by light (below), and it is
used universally. The densities $\varepsilon_0E^2/2$ and $B^2/2\mu_0$ are the
energies of the capacitor and of the inductor of the Year 1 volume,
now assigned to every point of space: a field of \qty{1}{MV/m} (the
breakdown limit of air) stores \qty{4.4}{J/m^3}, a field of \qty{1}{T}
stores \qty{0.4}{MJ/m^3} --- a hundred thousand times more, which is why
energy storage in magnets, not in capacitors, reaches the megajoule.
\end{remark}

\section{Where the energy flows: three circuits}

\begin{example}[The resistive wire]\label{ex:b2:poynting-vector:wire}
A cylindrical wire of radius $a$ carries the steady current $I$ under the
field $E = j/\gamma$ along its axis; just outside it, $B = \mu_0I/2\pi a$
(azimuthal). At the surface, $\vect\Pi = \vect E\wedge\vect B/\mu_0$ points
\emph{radially inward}, with magnitude $EI/2\pi a$; its flux through the
lateral surface of a length $L$ is $EI/2\pi a\times2\pi aL = (EL)I = UI =
RI^2$: the Joule heat enters the wire through its sides, delivered by
the surrounding field, not carried down the wire by the electrons.
Inside, $\Pi(r) = (E/\mu_0)(\mu_0Ir/2\pi a^2) = EIr/2\pi a^2$: the inward flow
decreases toward the axis as the energy is deposited layer by layer.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The capacitor and the solenoid]\label{ex:b2:poynting-vector:capsol}
A capacitor with circular plates being charged: $\vect E$ axial, $\vect B =
\mu_0\varepsilon_0(r/2)\dot E\,\vect e_\theta$ (\cref{ch:b2:maxwell-equations}), so
$\vect\Pi = -\varepsilon_0(r/2)E\dot E\,\vect e_r$, inward; through the cylinder of
radius $R$ and height $d$ its flux is $\varepsilon_0\pi R^2d\,E\dot E = \dd(\tfrac12
\varepsilon_0E^2\,\pi R^2d)/\dd t$: the electric energy enters from the edge. A
solenoid whose current grows: $\vect B$ axial, the induced $\vect E = -(r/2)
\dot B\,\vect e_\theta$, $\vect\Pi = -(rB\dot B/2\mu_0)\vect e_r$, again inward, with
flux $\dd(B^2/2\mu_0\times\text{volume})/\dd t$. In every case the energy
reaches the device through the field outside it; the wires only guide
the field.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Energy in a static crossed field]\label{rem:b2:poynting-vector:static}
A charged capacitor placed in the field of a permanent magnet has
$\vect\Pi = \vect E\wedge\vect B/\mu_0 \ne \vect 0$ everywhere between its plates,
although nothing changes and nothing heats. There is no contradiction:
$\vect\Pi$ there has zero \omterm{def:b2:maxwell-equations:operators}{divergence}, its lines close on themselves, and
its flux through any closed surface vanishes --- the theorem says
nothing about a circulating flow that delivers nothing. Only fluxes
through closed surfaces are physical.
\end{remark}

\section{Light: intensity and radiation pressure}

\begin{proposition}[Energy of a plane wave]\label{prop:b2:poynting-vector:wave}
\index{intensity (electromagnetic)}\index{radiation pressure}\index{irradiance}
For a plane wave in vacuum --- the solution of \cref{ch:b2:plane-waves-polarization},
with $\vect B = \vect k\wedge\vect E/\omega$, $B = E/c$ and $\vect E \perp \vect B$ ---
the electric and magnetic energy densities are equal and
\[
u = \varepsilon_0E^2 , \qquad \vect\Pi = \varepsilon_0cE^2\,\vect u = cu\,\vect u ,
\]
$\vect u$ being the direction of propagation: the energy travels at $c$.
For a sinusoidal wave of amplitude $E_0$ the \emph{intensity}
(irradiance) is the mean flux,
\[
I = \langle\Pi\rangle = \frac{\varepsilon_0cE_0^2}2 = \frac{E_0^2}{2\mu_0c} ,
\]
and the wave carries a momentum density $\vect\Pi/c^2$ (admitted), so
that it exerts on a surface it strikes at normal incidence the
\emph{\omterm{prop:b2:poynting-vector:wave}{radiation pressure}} $P = I/c$ if absorbed and $2I/c$ if reflected.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$B^2/2\mu_0 = E^2/2\mu_0c^2 = \varepsilon_0E^2/2$; $\vect E\wedge\vect B/\mu_0 = E^2/\mu_0c =
\varepsilon_0cE^2$ along $\vect k$; $\langle\cos^2\rangle = \tfrac12$. The momentum
density $\vect\Pi/c^2$ follows from the Lorentz force on the charges of
the absorbing surface (or from relativity: $p = E/c$ for light): the
momentum $\Pi S\,\dd t/c^2\times c$ arriving per unit time on a surface $S$ is
a force $\Pi S/c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Sunlight, laser, microwave]\label{ex:b2:poynting-vector:orders}
Sunlight at the top of the atmosphere, $I = \qty{1.36}{kW/m^2}$: $E_0 =
\sqrt{2I/\varepsilon_0c} = \qty{1.0}{kV/m}$, $B_0 = E_0/c = \qty{3.4}{\micro T}$, energy
density \qty{4.5}{\micro J/m^3}, pressure \qty{4.5}{\micro Pa} --- \qty{0.7}{kg}
of force on a square kilometre, yet enough to steer a solar sail and to
blow the dust of a comet into its tail. A \qty{1}{mW} laser pointer on a
\qty{1}{mm^2} spot: \qty{1}{kW/m^2}, like the Sun; focused to \qty{1}{\micro m^2}:
\qty{1}{GW/m^2}, $E_0 = \qty{0.9}{MV/m}$, near the breakdown of air. A
\qty{1}{kW} microwave oven with a \omterm{prop:b2:waves-on-strings:modes}{standing wave} filling $\qty{30}{L}$: energy
density $\sim 10^{-7}$ J/m$^3$ and $E_0$ of a few kilovolts per metre.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Energy bookkeeping]\label{met:b2:poynting-vector:method}
(1) Find $\vect E$ and $\vect B$ (statics, quasi-statics or waves). (2)
Form $\vect\Pi = \vect E\wedge\vect B/\mu_0$ and $u$. (3) Choose a closed
surface around the device and compute the flux of $\vect\Pi$: it must
equal the Joule power plus the rate of change of the stored energy
inside --- a check on the fields. (4) For a wave, the mean of $\Pi$ is
the intensity; divide by $c$ for the pressure, by the photon energy
$hf$ for the photon flux.
\end{method}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:poynting-vector:9}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:poynting-vector:10}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:poynting-vector:11}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:poynting-vector:12}

\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: The energy of a line, a panel and an oven}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- following the electromagnetic
energy from a power line into a house, from the Sun into a solar panel,
and from a magnetron into a cup of water}]
\label{pb:b2:poynting-vector:1}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The line.}
A coaxial power cable (core radius $a = \qty{1.0}{cm}$, braid radius $b =
\qty{3.0}{cm}$, dielectric $\varepsilon_r = 2.3$) carries a direct current $I =
\qty{500}{A}$ at $U = \qty{20}{kV}$; the core has conductivity $\gamma =
\qty{6e7}{S/m}$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fields $\vect E(r)$ and $\vect B(r)$ in the dielectric (perfect
        conductors first); values at $r = a$.
  \item \omterm{thm:b2:poynting-vector:poynting}{Poynting vector} in the dielectric; its flux through the
        annular section: check it equals $UI$.
  \item Energy densities $\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_rE^2/2$ and $B^2/2\mu_0$ at $r = a$
        (in a dielectric the electric energy density carries $\varepsilon_r$);
        which dominates, and by how much?
  \item At what fraction of the speed of light does the energy "move"
        (take $\Pi/u$ at $r = a$)? Compare with $c/\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}$.
  \item Now the core is resistive: field $E_z$ inside it; the
        \omterm{thm:b2:poynting-vector:poynting}{Poynting vector} just outside its surface acquires a radial
        component: magnitude, and the power per metre it delivers to
        the core; check against $RI^2$ per metre.
  \item Ratio of the radial to the axial component of $\vect\Pi$ at $r =
        a$: what does it say about where the energy goes?
  \item Magnetic energy per metre of cable, $\int B^2/2\mu_0\,\dd\tau$, and
        electric energy per metre, $\int\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_rE^2/2\,\dd\tau$;
        recover them as $\tfrac12\Lambda I^2$ and $\tfrac12\Gamma U^2$ with the
        inductance and capacitance per metre of \cref{ch:b2:dispersion-wave-packets}.
  \item A \qty{10}{km} line: power delivered, power lost, fraction.
        Repeat with $U = \qty{200}{kV}$ and $I = \qty{50}{A}$ (same power).
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The panel.}
Sunlight at noon on a clear day: $I = \qty{1.0}{kW/m^2}$; a photovoltaic
panel of \qty{1.6}{m^2} and $20\%$ efficiency; photon energy \qty{2.0}{eV}
on average.
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item $E_0$, $B_0$, energy density and photon flux in the beam.
  \item Electric power delivered; heat to be evacuated; temperature
        rise if the panel loses heat by both faces at $h = \qty{10}{W/(m^2.K)}$.
  \item \omterm{prop:b2:poynting-vector:wave}{Radiation pressure} on the panel (absorbing); force; compare
        with its weight (\qty{18}{kg}).
  \item Energy received per day (\qty{6}{h} of equivalent full sun) and
        per year; the area needed to supply a household's
        \qty{3500}{kWh/year}.
  \item The panel produces \qty{30}{V} at \qty{10.7}{A}: check the power;
        Poynting flux along the \qty{5}{m} of two-wire cable to the
        inverter --- where, in the field picture, does that power
        travel?
  \item At the Earth's orbit the Sun radiates \qty{1.36}{kW/m^2}: total
        power of the Sun; the mass it converts per second ($E = mc^2$,
        $c = \qty{3e8}{m/s}$).
  \item Number of photons striking the panel per second.
  \item Photon \omterm{thm:b2:flow-balances:momentum}{momentum flux} at the Earth: total radiation force of the
        Sun on the Earth (disk radius \qty{6400}{km}, absorbing);
        compare with gravity (\qty{3.5e22}{N}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The oven.}
A microwave oven delivers \qty{800}{W} at \qty{2.45}{GHz} into a cavity of
\qty{30}{L}; a cup of \qty{250}{g} of water absorbs $80\%$ of it.
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Wavelength; is the cavity large compared with it? Why are the
        walls metal?
  \item Time to heat the water from \qty{20}{\degreeCelsius} to \qty{80}{\degreeCelsius}
        ($c = \qty{4.18}{kJ/(kg.K)}$); power absorbed per unit volume.
  \item If the power flowed as a plane wave through the cup's
        \qty{50}{cm^2} section, intensity and $E_0$; actual fields are
        \omterm{prop:b2:waves-on-strings:modes}{standing waves} of comparable amplitude --- how far apart are
        the hot spots, and why does the plate turn?
  \item Energy stored in the cavity field if its quality factor is
        $Q = 2\pi\times\text{(stored energy)}/\text{(energy lost per period)}
        \approx 10^3$ when empty: stored energy, mean energy density, $E_0$
        --- compare with the breakdown of air (\qty{3}{MV/m}).
  \item Water absorbs because its molecules are dipoles driven at
        \qty{2.45}{GHz}: the local Joule-like power is $\tfrac12\omega\varepsilon_0
        \varepsilon''E_0^2$ with $\varepsilon'' \approx 10$ for water: $E_0$ in the water
        needed for the power density of question 16; is it consistent?
  \item Photon energy at \qty{2.45}{GHz} in eV, and the number of photons
        absorbed per second by the cup; why microwaves can heat but
        not ionize (ionization needs some \qty{10}{eV}).
  \item \omterm{prop:b2:poynting-vector:wave}{Radiation pressure} of the cavity field on the walls (of the
        order of the energy density $u$): is it worth worrying about?
  \item Why does an empty oven (no load) risk damage, in Poynting
        terms?
  \item Sum up in a table, for the line, the panel and the oven: the
        carrier of energy, $\vect\Pi$'s direction, the power, and what
        converts it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
