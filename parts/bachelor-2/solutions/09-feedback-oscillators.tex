\begin{solution}{exo:b2:feedback-oscillators:6}
(a) $\underline\beta = Z_p/(Z_s + Z_p)$ with $Z_s = R + 1/\jmath C\omega$, $Z_p = R/(1 +
\jmath RC\omega)$: $\underline\beta = 1/[3 + \jmath(RC\omega - 1/RC\omega)]$. (b) Modulus
$1/\sqrt{9 + (RC\omega - 1/RC\omega)^2}$, maximal $1/3$ at $\omega_0$; phase $-\arctan
[(RC\omega - 1/RC\omega)/3]$, zero only at $\omega_0$. (c) $R^2C^2\ddot v + (3 - K)RC
\dot v + v = 0$: damped, sustained, growing. (d) Growth time constant
$2RC/0.2 = 10RC = \qty{1.6}{ms}$; $\ln10^4 = 9.2$ constants: \qty{15}{ms}. The
final amplitude is set by the non-linearity (saturation), not by the
seed, which only sets the delay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:feedback-oscillators:11}
(a) $f_0 = 1/2\pi\sqrt{LC} = \qty{32.5}{kHz}$; $Q = L\omega_0/r = \qty{5.4e4}{}$. (b) Near
resonance the \omterm{def:b2:feedback-oscillators:loop}{feedback} phase turns by $2Q/\omega_0$ per unit $\omega$: a
parasitic phase shift $\delta\varphi$ moves the frequency by $\delta\omega/\omega_0 =
\delta\varphi/2Q$ --- $10^{-6}$ for $Q = 10^5$, percent for $Q = 1/3$. (c) \qty{1}{Hz};
$-0.04 \times 400 = \qty{-16}{ppm}$: $16 \times 10^{-6} \times 2.6 \times 10^6 = \qty{41}{s}$ slow per
month. (d) Low frequency means low power in the dividing logic, $2^{15}$
divides exactly to \qty{1}{Hz}, and the tiny tuning-fork crystal fits a
watch.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:feedback-oscillators:12}
(a) $v_- = v_+ = v$, $v_{\text{out}} = v(1 + R_2/R_1)$; the current entering the
dipole, $i = (v - v_{\text{out}})/R = -vR_2/RR_1$: $v/i = -RR_1/R_2$. (b) $C\dot v +
v(1/R_p - R_2/RR_1) + \frac1L\int v\,\dd t = 0$: sustained when $R = R_pR_2/R_1$,
at $\omega = 1/\sqrt{LC}$ (growing for smaller $R$). (c) $f = \qty{5.0}{kHz}$, $R \lesssim
\qty{50}{k\ohm}$ (say \qty{48}{k\ohm}). (d) Saturation; the tank's $Q = R_p
\sqrt{C/L} = 160$ gives a far steadier frequency than the \omterm{prop:b2:feedback-oscillators:wien}{Wien network}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:feedback-oscillators:1}
\textbf{1.} $R = 1/2\pi f_0C = \qty{15.9}{k\ohm}$.

\textbf{2.} $\underline\beta = 1/[3 + \jmath(RC\omega - 1/RC\omega)]$: real, $= 1/3$, at
$\omega_0 = 1/RC$.

\textbf{3.} $R^2C^2\ddot v + (3 - K)RC\dot v + v = 0$; time constant $2RC/0.15 =
\qty{2.1}{ms}$; $\ln1000 = 6.9$: \qty{15}{ms}.

\textbf{4.} A sine with flattened tops: odd harmonics. A reference rich
in harmonics also detects the signal's harmonics (and noise there):
the result no longer measures the fundamental alone.

\textbf{5.} The lamp: as the amplitude rises it warms, $R_1$ rises and
$K = 1 + R_2/R_1$ falls to $3$. A thermistor in place of $R_1$ would do
the opposite (it belongs in place of $R_2$).

\textbf{6.} $f \propto 1/C$: \qty{990}{Hz}. The lock-in reference is taken from
the chopper itself (a detector on the wheel), so it follows.

\textbf{7.} $Q \sim 1/3$: a small phase shift anywhere in the loop moves the
frequency by percent; a quartz's $10^5$ holds it to $10^{-6}$.

\textbf{8.} $50\,\text{nV} \times \sqrt{10^5} = \qty{16}{\micro V}$ rms: SNR $0.6$.

\textbf{9.} $B = \qty{100}{Hz}$: \qty{0.5}{\micro V}, SNR $20$ --- but the filter's
centre drifts with its components, and the \qty{50}{Hz} pickup is only
attenuated by $\sim 1/[Q(f_0/f - f/f_0)] = 0.005$: \qty{5}{\micro V}, half the
signal.

\textbf{10.} Below a few hundred hertz the amplifier's $1/f$ noise and
drifts dominate; at \qty{1}{kHz} the noise floor is white and low, and
offsets do not count.

\textbf{11.} A square modulation between $0$ and $A_0$ is $A_0/2 + (2A_0/\pi)
\cos\omega_0t + \dots$: the lock-in keeps the fundamental, $0.64A_0$.

\textbf{12.} \qty{100}{Hz} is mixed to $900$ and \qty{1100}{Hz} and removed by
the low-pass.

\textbf{13.} $\tfrac12A\cos\varphi + \tfrac12A\cos(2\omega_0t + \varphi)$; DC term $\tfrac12A\cos\varphi$.

\textbf{14.} $1/2\pi f\tau = 1/2\pi \times 2000 = 8 \times 10^{-5}$: \qty{-82}{dB}.

\textbf{15.} $B = 1/4\tau = \qty{0.25}{Hz}$: $e_n\sqrt B = \qty{25}{nV}$ against $A =
\qty{10}{\micro V}$: SNR $400$; $\tau = \qty{10}{s}$: \qty{7.9}{nV}, SNR $1300$.

\textbf{16.} To $950$ and \qty{1050}{Hz}: attenuated by $1/2\pi \times 950 = 1.7 \times
10^{-4}$ (\qty{-75}{dB}): \qty{0.17}{\micro V} of ripple, a tenth of the signal ---
still worth a notch filter upstream.

\textbf{17.} $\cos60^\circ = 0.5$: half the signal. A second multiplier with
the reference shifted by \qty{90}{\degree} gives $Y = \tfrac12A\sin\varphi$; with $X =
\tfrac12A\cos\varphi$, $A = 2\sqrt{X^2 + Y^2}$ and $\varphi = \arctan(Y/X)$, whatever $\varphi$.

\textbf{18.} $\tau\ln100 = \qty{4.6}{s}$; the noise falls as $1/\sqrt\tau$, the
measurement time grows as $\tau$.

\textbf{19.} The difference term is $\tfrac12A\cos(2\pi \times 1\,\text{Hz}\,t + \varphi)$,
passed by the filter with $1/\sqrt{1 + (2\pi)^2} = 0.16$: a slow
oscillation instead of a level. The reference must be \emph{coherent}
with the signal: take it from the chopper.

\textbf{20.} Bandwidth $1/2\pi\tau = \qty{0.16}{Hz}$: above \qty{0.32}{S/s};
\qty{10}{S/s} is comfortable.

\textbf{21.} At least \qty{2}{kS/s}; an \omterm{thm:b2:feedback-oscillators:shannon}{anti-aliasing filter} is mandatory;
fourth order at \qty{5}{kHz}: $80\log_{10}(100/5) = \qty{104}{dB}$ at \qty{100}{kHz}
(and \qty{46}{dB} at \qty{19}{kHz}, the noise that would alias onto \qty{1}{kHz}).

\textbf{22.} Step \qty{30.5}{\micro V}; \qty{10}{mV} is $330$ steps; the amplified
noise (\qty{16}{mV} rms) spans many steps and, averaged, lets the mean be
resolved far below one step: it helps.

\textbf{23.} $N = 20000$; the white noise falls by $\sqrt N = 140$; a
\qty{1}{s} boxcar has a noise bandwidth $1/2T = \qty{0.5}{Hz}$ --- twice the
analog $RC$'s, comparable.

\textbf{24.} The samples walk slowly through the sine: a \qty{1}{Hz} alias.
The lock-in does the same thing on purpose: sampling at the reference
phase is multiplying by the reference.

\textbf{25.} Chopper: signal moved to \qty{1}{kHz}, noise unchanged (but
the $1/f$ part avoided). Amplifier: both $\times$ gain. Multiplier: signal
to DC, noise spread. Low-pass: signal kept, noise cut to $1/4\tau$.
Sampler/ADC: signal digitized, aliases and quantization controlled by
the filter and the bit count.
\end{solution}
