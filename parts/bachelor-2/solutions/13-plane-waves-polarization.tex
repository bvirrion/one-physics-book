\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:4}
$\operatorname{div}\vect E = \partial_xE_x = 0$, $\operatorname{div}\vect B = 0$;
$\operatorname{\vect{curl}}\vect E = E_0k\sin(\omega t - kz)\,\vect e_y = -\partial_t\vect B$ iff $\omega =
kc$ --- Faraday fixes both the direction ($\vect e_y$) and the magnitude
($E_0/c$) of $\vect B$; $\operatorname{\vect{curl}}\vect B = -(E_0k/c)\sin(\omega t - kz)\,\vect e_x
= \mu_0\varepsilon_0\partial_t\vect E$. $\vect E$ along $\vect e_z$ would have $\operatorname{div}
\vect E \ne 0$ with no charge: impossible --- the wave is transverse.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:5}
(a) $\vect E = E_0(\cos(\omega t - kz), \pm\sin(\omega t - kz), 0)$: modulus $E_0$,
angle $\pm(\omega t - kz)$. (b) $(\cos, \sin) + (\cos, -\sin) = (2\cos, 0)$: linear
along $x$; a relative phase $\varphi$ turns the direction by $\varphi/2$. (c)
$I_0/2$ at every angle. (d) A \omterm{prop:b2:plane-waves-polarization:plates}{quarter-wave plate} turns circular light
into linear light, which a polarizer then extinguishes; \omterm{def:b2:plane-waves-polarization:states}{natural light}
stays unpolarized.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:6}
(a) $e = \lambda/4(n_s - n_f) = \qty{16}{\micro m}$; half-wave \qty{32}{\micro m}. (b) $\delta =
(\pi/2)(589/450) = \qty{2.1}{rad}$: elliptical light. (c) $\delta \propto e/\lambda$: a
plate of $m + \tfrac14$ waves has $\dd\delta/\dd\lambda$ and $\dd\delta/\dd T$ larger by
$4m + 1$. (d) $\delta \propto e$: a micrometre on \qty{16}{\micro m} is $6\%$ of $\pi/2$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:7}
(a) The slow component changes sign: $(\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha) \to (\cos\alpha,
-\sin\alpha)$. (b) A polarization at angle $\alpha$ from a plate at $\beta$ comes
out at $2\beta - \alpha$: rotation $2\beta$; \qty{45}{\degree}. (c) Reverses the
handedness. (d) $I = I_0\sin^22\beta$: four maxima and four extinctions
per turn.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:8}
$\cos^{2N}(\pi/2N)$: $0$, $0.25$, $0.61$, $0.78$, $0.976$, $\to 1$. A
continuously twisting medium (optical activity, a liquid-crystal cell)
rotates the polarization without loss.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:9}
(a) $I_{\max} = I_n/2 + I_p$, $I_{\min} = I_n/2$; $p = I_p/(I_n + I_p) = (I_{\max} -
I_{\min})/(I_{\max} + I_{\min})$. (b) $p = 4/6 = 0.67$. (c) The crossed polarizer
passes $I_n/2 = 0.05I$: $95\%$ removed. (d) No --- both give a constant
intensity; a \omterm{prop:b2:plane-waves-polarization:plates}{quarter-wave plate} is needed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:10}
(a) Phases $\omega t - k(z\cos\theta \pm x\sin\theta)$: sum $2E_0\cos(kx\sin\theta)\cos(\omega t
- kz\cos\theta)\,\vect e_y$. (b) $\Delta x = \lambda/2\sin\theta$: \qty{3.6}{\micro m} at \qty{5}{\degree},
$\lambda/2 = \qty{316}{nm}$ at \qty{90}{\degree}. (c) $v_\varphi = c/\cos\theta > c$: a pattern's
phase; the energy goes along $z$ at $c\cos\theta$ (and each wave at $c$).
(d) $B_z = (\sin\theta/c)(E_1 - E_2)$, $E_y = E_1 + E_2$: $\langle\Pi_x\rangle \propto
\langle E_1^2 - E_2^2\rangle = 0$; $\Pi_z \propto \cos\theta(E_1 + E_2)^2$, maximal in the
bright planes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:11}
(a) No voltage: rotated by \qty{90}{\degree}, passes the crossed polarizer:
bright; with voltage: dark. (b) Leak $\sin^25^\circ = 7.6 \times 10^{-3}$: contrast
$130$. (c) The twist works as a stack of retarders whose $\delta \propto 1/\lambda$:
exact at \qty{550}{nm}, the rotation is off at the ends of the spectrum,
which leak --- the dark state looks purplish. (d) When the external
polarizer is crossed with the screen's output polarizer; the angle is
the screen's polarization axis.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plane-waves-polarization:12}
(a) $\vect e_x = \tfrac12[(\vect e_x + \iu\vect e_y) + (\vect e_x - \iu\vect e_y)]$; after $\ell$
the two components have phases $k_L\ell$ and $k_R\ell$, and recombine into
linear light at the angle $(k_L - k_R)\ell/2 = \pi(n_L - n_R)\ell/\lambda$. (b) $n_L -
n_R = \theta\lambda/\pi\ell = 0.116 \times 589 \times 10^{-9}/0.314 = \qty{2.2e-7}{}$. (c)
$0.01/6.65 \times 0.1 = \qty{1.5e-4}{g/mL}$. (d) The Faraday rotation is fixed by
$\vect B$, not by the direction of travel: on the return trip it adds
(\qty{45}{\degree} $+$ \qty{45}{\degree} $=$ \qty{90}{\degree}), and the input polarizer blocks
the returning light --- an isolator.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:plane-waves-polarization:1}
\textbf{1.} $f = \qty{4.74e14}{Hz}$, $k = \qty{9.9e6}{rad/m}$, $hf = \qty{3.1e-19}{J} =
\qty{1.96}{eV}$; $3.2 \times 10^{16}$ photons per second.

\textbf{2.} $I = 0.01/\pi(0.5 \times 10^{-3})^2 = \qty{1.3e4}{W/m^2}$; $E_0 = \qty{3.1}{kV/m}$,
$B_0 = \qty{10}{\micro T}$; $\vect E = E_0\cos(\omega t - kz)\vect e_x$, $\vect B = (E_0/c)\cos(\omega t
- kz)\vect e_y$.

\textbf{3.} $u = I/c = \qty{4.2e-5}{J/m^3}$; $P/c = \qty{3.3e-11}{J}$ per metre of
beam; \qty{30}{cm}, $4.7 \times 10^5$ wavelengths.

\textbf{4.} $\langle\Pi\rangle = I$ along $z$, oscillating as $\cos^2$ at $2f$;
$P/c = \qty{3.3e-11}{N}$ on black, \qty{6.7e-11}{N} on a mirror.

\textbf{5.} $E_0/5 \times 10^{11} = 6 \times 10^{-9}$; $B_0$ is a fifth of the Earth's
field.

\textbf{6.} $I/c = \qty{4.2e-5}{Pa}$.

\textbf{7.} $v_0 = eE_0/m\omega = \qty{0.18}{m/s}$; $F_m/F_e = v_0B_0/E_0 = v_0/c = 6 \times
10^{-10}$: the magnetic force on slow charges is negligible.

\textbf{8.} $\vect E = E_0[\cos(\omega t - kz)\vect e_x \pm \sin(\omega t - kz)\vect e_y]$ with
$E_0 = \qty{2.2}{kV/m}$ (same power), $\vect B = \vect e_z\wedge\vect E/c$: the
intensity is unchanged but $\Pi = \varepsilon_0cE_0^2$ is now \emph{constant} in
time --- the energy flow no longer pulses.

\textbf{9.} $10\cos^230^\circ = \qty{7.5}{mW}$; $0$; $7.5 \times \cos^445^\circ = \qty{1.9}{mW}$.

\textbf{10.} Bright: $\tfrac12$; dark: $0$.

\textbf{11.} After the first polarizer, equal components on the axes;
after the retarder, phase $\delta$ between them; projection on the crossed
direction $\propto(1 - \eu^{\iu\delta})/2$: $I \propto \sin^2(\delta/2)$.

\textbf{12.} Black; half intensity.

\textbf{13.} Circular output looks equally bright through polarizing
sunglasses at any head angle.

\textbf{14.} $\tfrac12 \times 0.85 \times 0.85 \times \tfrac13 = 12\%$; the first polarizer
(half absorbed) and the colour filters (two thirds).

\textbf{15.} Bright $0.36$, dark $\tfrac12 \times 10^{-3}$: contrast $\approx 700$.

\textbf{16.} Circular light, whatever the filter's rotation: the
polarization-sensitive splitter receives the same power however the
filter is turned.

\textbf{17.} \omterm{def:b2:plane-waves-polarization:states}{Circular polarization} keeps its handedness when the head
tilts; linear glasses tilted by \qty{20}{\degree} leak $\sin^220^\circ = 12\%$ into
the wrong eye.

\textbf{18.} $F = 2IS/c = \qty{9.1}{mN}$; $a = \qty{1.8e-4}{m/s^2}$; $3\%$ of solar
gravity.

\textbf{19.} Intercepted power $\propto\cos\theta$, reflected momentum change
along the normal $2p\cos\theta$: $F = (2IS/c)\cos^2\theta$ along the normal.

\textbf{20.} $F = 9.1 \times \cos^235^\circ = \qty{6.1}{mN}$, tangential part $F\sin35^\circ
= \qty{3.5}{mN}$: $a_t = \qty{7e-5}{m/s^2}$, \qty{2.2}{km/s} per year --- $7\%$ of
the orbital speed.

\textbf{21.} $(1/0.39)^2 = 6.6$; gravity scales the same way.

\textbf{22.} $0.1 \times 1360 = 2\sigma T^4$: $T = \qty{190}{K}$ --- the mirror stays
cold.

\textbf{23.} $1000/1.8 \times 10^{-4} = \qty{5.6e6}{s}$, two months; a black sail
gets half the force.

\textbf{24.} $1.36 \times 10^6/3.2 \times 10^{-19} = \qty{4.3e21}{}$ photons per second,
each with $hf/c = \qty{1.1e-27}{kg.m/s}$; reflected: $2Np = \qty{9.1}{mN}$.

\textbf{25.} Energy ($u$, $I$): the beam stop and the photodiode.
Momentum ($I/c$): the sail. Polarization (Malus, the plates): the
screen and its polarizers.
\end{solution}
