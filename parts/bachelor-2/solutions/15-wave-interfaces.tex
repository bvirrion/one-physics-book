\begin{solution}{exo:b2:wave-interfaces:1}
$r = (Z_2 - Z_1)/(Z_2 + Z_1)$, $R = r^2$: air--water $0.9995$, $99.9\%$;
water--steel $0.93$, $86\%$; tissue--bone $0.63$, $40\%$; tissue--air

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:wave-interfaces:7}
(a) $\omega_p/\omega = 3$: $\underline n = -\iu\sqrt8 = -2.83\iu$; $r = (1 + 2.83\iu)/(1 - 2.83
\iu)$, $|r| = 1$, phase $2\arctan2.83 = \qty{141}{\degree}$. (b) $c/\sqrt{\omega_p^2 - \omega^2}
= \qty{5.6}{m}$. (c) $E \propto \cos(kx + \varphi/2)$: first node at $kx = -(\pi + \varphi)/2$,
$x \approx \qty{-45}{m}$ below the layer. (d) The index falls with height, so
Descartes bends the ray away from the vertical; it turns round where
$n(z) = \sin\theta_0$, i.e.\ $\omega_p^2 = \omega^2\cos^2\theta_0$, below the level $\omega = \omega_p$;
a frequency whose $\omega\cos\theta_0$ exceeds the layer's maximum $\omega_p$
escapes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:wave-interfaces:10}
(a) $\vect E = E\vect e_z$; $\vect k\wedge\vect e_z = (k_y, -k_x, 0)$, so the tangential
$B_y = -(n/c)\cos\theta\,E$ for the incident and transmitted waves and
$+(n_1/c)\cos\theta_1E_r$ for the reflected one. (b) $1 + r = t$ and $n_1\cos\theta_1
(1 - r) = n_2\cos\theta_2t$: $r_s$ as stated. (c) $\theta = 0$: $(n_1 - n_2)/(n_1 +
n_2)$; $\theta_1 \to 90^\circ$: $r_s \to -1$. (d) $\cos\theta_2 = 0.882$: $r_s = -0.30$, $R_s =
9.2\%$; $R_p = 0.85\%$; against $4.3\%$ at normal incidence.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:wave-interfaces:11}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:wave-interfaces:12}
(a) Nodes of $\vect E$ at both mirrors: $\sin kL = 0$, $L = m\lambda/2$, $f_m = mc/2L$.
(b) $1$, $2$, \qty{3}{GHz}: none at \qty{2.45}{GHz} --- a real oven's

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:wave-interfaces:1}
\textbf{1.} $R = 4.3\%$; $0.957^{16} = 0.50$; half the light strays.

\textbf{2.} $n = 1.23$, $e = \qty{112}{nm}$; no durable solid has so low an
index (MgF$_2$, $1.38$, is the lowest): residual $1.3\%$.

\textbf{3.} $0.987^{16} = 0.81$; $0.997^{16} = 0.95$. Stray reflections fog the
image: veiling glare, low contrast.

\textbf{4.} $\varphi = \pi \times 550/440 = 1.25\pi$: $R \approx 0.0256 + 0.0025 + 2 \times 0.008 \times
\cos1.25\pi = 1.7\%$ --- more than at \qty{550}{nm}, as at the red end: a
magenta reflection.

\textbf{5.} Each interface reflects $|r| = 0.97/3.73 = 0.26$; the quarter-wave
spacing puts successive reflections in phase, so the amplitudes add
instead of cancelling; the stack's reflectance is $1 - 4(n_L/n_H)^{2N}
\approx 1 - 10^{-9}$ for $N = 20$.

\textbf{6.} The in-phase condition holds only near the design
wavelength (a band of some $\pm15\%$); the rest passes: a green mirror
looks magenta in transmission.

\textbf{7.} Into the transmitted wave: interference redistributes,
$R + T = 1$.

\textbf{8.} $19\%$; with $Z_m = \sqrt{Z_1Z_2} = \qty{6.9e6}{kg.m^{-2}.s^{-1}}$: $100\%$ at
\qty{5}{MHz}.

\textbf{9.} $r_1 = 0.63$, $r_2 = 0.62$; at \qty{3}{MHz} the round-trip phase is
$0.6\pi$, at \qty{7}{MHz} $1.4\pi$: $R \approx 0.39 + 0.39 - 0.24 = 0.54$, $T \approx 0.46$
at both: the matching holds only over a band around \qty{5}{MHz}, while a
short pulse needs a wide band --- a compromise.

\textbf{10.} $t_1 = 2 \times 410/30 \times 10^6 = 2.7 \times 10^{-5}$, $t_2 = 2.0$: $t = 5.5 \times 10^{-5}$,
$T = t^2Z_1/Z_3 = 5.6 \times 10^{-8}$: \qty{-72}{dB}.

\textbf{11.} $t = 0.095 \times 1.03 = 0.098$, $T = 0.098^2 \times 30/1.6 = 0.18$: the gel
merely removes the air.

\textbf{12.} $0.9^N = 0.01$: $N = 44$ round trips --- a long ring-down; the
backing absorbs the backward wave and kills it.

\textbf{13.} $R = 0.40$ at the bone; $2 \times 5 \times 0.5 \times 5 = \qty{25}{dB}$ of
\omterm{def:b2:dispersion-wave-packets:complex}{attenuation}: $0.40 \times 3.2 \times 10^{-3} = 1.3 \times 10^{-3}$ (\qty{-29}{dB}); the bone
absorbs what it does not reflect: nothing returns from behind it.

\textbf{14.} $R = 1$, $r = -1$.

\textbf{15.} Short at $x = 0$: $v = -2\iu v_i\sin kx$, $i = (2v_i/Z_0)\cos kx$;
at $x = -d$: $v/i = \iu Z_0\tan kd$; infinite at $d = \lambda/4$.

\textbf{16.} $R_s \parallel \infty = R_s$: $r = (R_s - Z_0)/(R_s + Z_0) = 0$ for
\qty{377}{\ohm}; $d = \qty{7.5}{mm}$.

\textbf{17.} The sheet sees the full incident field: $|v|^2/2R_s =
E_0^2/2Z_0$, the incident intensity --- all absorbed.

\textbf{18.} \qty{8}{GHz}: $kd = 72^\circ$, $Z_{\text{in}} = \iu\qty{1160}{\ohm}$, $R_s \parallel
Z_{\text{in}} = (340 + 111\iu)\,\Omega$, $R = |r|^2 = 2.6\%$ (\qty{-16}{dB}); \qty{12}{GHz}:
$kd = 108^\circ$, the same modulus: \qty{-16}{dB}. Useful over about $\pm20\%$.

\textbf{19.} Oblique incidence shortens the effective quarter wave
($d\cos\theta$) and changes the wave impedance with the angle: the
match is lost.

\textbf{20.} One screen works at one frequency and one angle; graded
lossy layers and shaped surfaces absorb and deflect over a broad band.

\textbf{21.} $\underline n = -\iu\sqrt{(\omega_p/\omega)^2 - 1} = -3.6\iu$; $|r| = 1$, phase
$2\arctan3.6 = \qty{149}{\degree}$; depth $c/3.6\omega = \qty{22}{nm}$. The wave never
enters the metal to be split into two comparable reflections: there is
nothing for a \omterm{prop:b2:wave-interfaces:optics}{quarter-wave layer} to cancel a modulus-one reflection
against.

\textbf{22.} \qty{30}{W}; $30/(0.01 \times 235) = \qty{13}{K/s}$.

\textbf{23.} Nodes every $\lambda/2 = \qty{250}{nm}$; $E \propto \cos(kx + \varphi/2)$ with $\varphi =
149^\circ$: first node at $x = -(90^\circ + 74.5^\circ)\lambda/360^\circ = \qty{-230}{nm}$; a
grain there sits in the dark.

\textbf{24.} $I = \qty{1e7}{W/m^2}$, $E_0 = \sqrt{2I/\varepsilon_0c} = \qty{87}{kV/m}$, $j_s =
2E_0/\mu_0c = \qty{460}{A/m}$.

\textbf{25.} Metal: \omterm{prop:b2:maxwell-equations:boundary}{surface currents} in a \omterm{def:b2:dispersion-wave-packets:complex}{skin depth}, $r \approx -1$, limited
by the Joule loss of those currents. \omterm{def:b2:waves-in-media:plasma}{Plasma}: evanescent penetration,
$|r| = 1$ with a frequency-dependent phase, limited by collisions.
Dielectric stack: many weak reflections added in phase, $r \to 1$ in a
band, limited by the number of layers and the bandwidth.
\end{solution}
