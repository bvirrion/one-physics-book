\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:2}
$1.25I_0 \pm I_0$: $V = 0.8$. $1.01I_0 \pm 0.2I_0$: $V = 0.2$ --- the interference term
goes as the \emph{amplitude} ratio, $1/10$, not the intensity ratio. Filter:
$V = 2\sqrt{0.5}/1.5 = 0.94$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:3}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:4}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:5}
(a) $i = 0.8$ and \qty{1.4}{mm}; second red \qty{2.8}{mm}, third violet \qty{2.4}{mm}.
(b) Order $p$ red at $1.4p$ mm meets order $p + 1$ violet at $0.8(p + 1)$: from
$p = 2$ the orders overlap. (c) $\delta = ax/D = \qty{2.5}{\micro m}$: missing $\lambda = \delta/(p +
\tfrac12)$: $714$, $556$, \qty{455}{nm}. (d) White centre; then fringes with a
blue inner edge and a red outer edge; by the third order the colours
mix into white.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:7}
(a) $a = 2h = \qty{0.4}{mm}$: $i = \lambda D/a = \qty{1.25}{mm}$. (b) At $x = 0$ the paths
are equal yet the fringe is dark: the reflection adds $\pi$ (sign
reversal at grazing incidence on the denser medium). (c) $\delta = ax/D <
\qty{50}{\micro m}$: $100$ fringes. (d) The sea is the mirror, the cliff-top

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:8}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:9}
(a) Only the first (air $\to$ oil, denser); oil $\to$ water goes to a lower
index: $\delta = 2ne + \lambda/2$, bright at $2ne = (p + \tfrac12)\lambda$. (b) $2ne =
\qty{1160}{nm}$: reinforced $773$ (edge of the visible), \qty{464}{nm} (blue);
cancelled $1160/p$: \qty{580}{nm} (yellow): blue. (c) $2ne = \lambda/2$: $e = \qty{72}{nm}$.
(d) $\delta = 2ne\cos r$ falls with the angle: the reinforced wavelengths
move toward the blue. (e) Both reflections then reverse the sign, no
extra $\lambda/2$: bright at $2ne = p\lambda$, dark at $(p + \tfrac12)\lambda$ --- the
quarter-wave anti-reflection layer.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:10}
(a) $(n - 1)e = \qty{10}{\micro m}$, $20$ fringes, toward the covered slit (where
the geometric path must shorten by \qty{10}{\micro m} to restore $\delta = 0$):
$x = (n - 1)eD/a = \qty{2}{cm}$. (b) The white fringe sits there; the glass's
dispersion ($\Delta n \approx 0.01$ across the visible, i.e.\ \qty{0.2}{\micro m} of path)
is below $\ell_c$: still visible, slightly coloured. (c) Measure the
shift of the white fringe: $e = (\text{shift})\,a/(n - 1)D$. (d) $e(n - 1)\theta^2
(1 + 1/n)/2 = \qty{63}{nm}$ at \qty{5}{\degree}: an eighth of a fringe.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:11}
(a) $\theta = \qty{2.3e-7}{rad}$: $a = 1.22\lambda/\theta = \qty{3.1}{m}$. (b) Its limit $1.22\lambda/d =
\qty{0.057}{''}$ is above the star's diameter, and the atmosphere blurs
to $1''$. (c) $1.22\lambda/100 = \qty{7e-9}{rad} = \qty{1.4}{mas}$. (d) The fringes need
$\delta < \ell_c = \lambda^2/\Delta\lambda = \qty{32}{\micro m}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:12}
(a) $\delta = 2ne\cos r + \lambda/2$; $\cos r = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2i/n^2} \approx 1 - i^2/2n^2$.
(b) $2ne/\lambda = 10186.8$: the centre, with $p + \tfrac12$ between $10186.5$ and
$10187.5$, is neither bright nor dark. (c) $i_p^2 = 2n^2[1 - (p + \tfrac12)\lambda/
2ne]$ for $p = 10186$, $10185$, $10184$: $i = 0.011$, $0.024$, \qty{0.032}{rad}, $r = fi
= 5.4$, $11.8$, \qty{15.8}{mm}. (d) The angle $i$ alone fixes the order: every
source point feeds the same ring at the same angle, so the rings, seen
at infinity, survive a broad source.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:two-wave-interference:1}
\textbf{1.} Each mirror images $S$ by symmetry at the distance $d$ from
the contact line; the two images lie on the circle of radius $d$, at
the angle $2\alpha$ apart: $a = 2\alpha d = \qty{2}{mm}$.

\textbf{2.} $S_1$ and $S_2$ are two images of the same vibration: their
phase jumps are the same.

\textbf{3.} $i = \lambda(d + D)/a = 589 \times 10^{-9} \times 1.7/2 \times 10^{-3} = \qty{0.50}{mm}$: ten
fringes across \qty{5}{mm}.

\textbf{4.} $\delta = ax/(d + D) = \qty{2.9}{\micro m}$ at the edge, $p = 5$: far below
$\ell_c/\lambda = 34\,000$.

\textbf{5.} Shift $bD'/d = 20 \times 10^{-6} \times 1.7/0.2 = \qty{0.17}{mm}$, a third of
$i$: contrast $|\operatorname{sinc}(\pi ab/\lambda d)| = \operatorname{sinc}(1.07) = 0.82$.

\textbf{6.} $\pi ab/\lambda d = 5.3$: $|\sin5.3|/5.3 = 0.16$.

\textbf{7.} Amplitudes $\sqrt{0.9}$, $\sqrt{0.8}$: $V = 2\sqrt{0.72}/1.7 = 0.998$.

\textbf{8.} $(n - 1)e = \qty{5}{\micro m}$: $8.5$ fringes; trivially followed with
sodium light; in white light the white fringe moves by $8.5i = \qty{4.2}{mm}$
and is still there (the glass's dispersion costs a fraction of a
micrometre).

\textbf{9.} A white central fringe, two or three coloured fringes on
each side, then a uniform white.

\textbf{10.} Air $\to$ oil reverses, oil $\to$ water (lower index) does not:
$\delta = 2ne + \lambda/2$; bright for $2ne = (p + \tfrac12)\lambda$.

\textbf{11.} $e = \lambda/4n$: red \qty{112}{nm}, green \qty{95}{nm}, violet \qty{72}{nm}.

\textbf{12.} $2ne = \qty{1740}{nm}$: reinforced $1740/(p + \tfrac12)$: $696$ (red), $497$
(blue-green), \qty{387}{nm}; cancelled $1740/p$: $580$ (yellow), \qty{435}{nm}:
a purplish mixture of red and blue-green.

\textbf{13.} The thickness varies along the road; each colour is bright
where $2ne$ has the right value, so the bands are contours of equal
thickness.

\textbf{14.} The orders fall: the colours run through the sequence
toward the thin-film end; at $e \to 0$ the single sign reversal leaves
$\delta = \lambda/2$ for every colour: dark --- as for a soap film in air.

\textbf{15.} A few nanometres: $2ne \ll \lambda$ for all colours, the two
reflections are in antiphase and cancel: black.

\textbf{16.} $\sin r = \sin60^\circ/1.45$, $r = \qty{37}{\degree}$, $\cos r = 0.80$: $2ne\cos r
= \qty{1390}{nm}$, bright at $928$, $557$, \qty{398}{nm}: the red of question 12 has
turned green-yellow --- toward the blue.

\textbf{17.} When $2ne$ exceeds $\ell_c \approx \qty{1}{\micro m}$, i.e.\ beyond some
\qty{0.4}{\micro m} of oil, the orders overlap and the colours fade to grey.

\textbf{18.} The two rays reflected from the film for any source
direction meet back on the film: the fringes are localized there and
a broad, multidirectional source merely adds identical patterns.

\textbf{19.} $\ell_c/\lambda$: $34\,000$ with sodium, $1.7 \times 10^7$ with the laser.

\textbf{20.} $b < \lambda d/4a$: \qty{15}{\micro m} for the mirrors, \qty{59}{\micro m} for
the slits.

\textbf{21.} Draw two source points: for each, the two reflected rays
recombine at the same point of the film, with the same $\delta = 2ne\cos r$
at near-normal incidence; the film's pattern does not move, Young's
does.

\textbf{22.} $\lambda/\Delta\lambda = 589/0.02 \approx 30\,000$.

\textbf{23.} $550/30 \approx 18$ fringes.

\textbf{24.} Coherent light scattered by the screen's grains interferes
with random phases at the eye: speckle.

\textbf{25.} Mirrors: \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}{division of wavefront}, fringes everywhere
(non-localized), number limited by $\ell_c/\lambda$, a wide source washes
them out. Film: \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:film}{division of amplitude}, fringes on the film, colours
limited by $\ell_c \sim \qty{1}{\micro m}$, a wide source is harmless.
\end{solution}
