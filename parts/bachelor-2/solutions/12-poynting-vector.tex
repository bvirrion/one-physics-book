\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:5}
(a) $j = \qty{3.2e6}{A/m^2}$, $E = j/\gamma = \qty{0.053}{V/m}$; $B = \mu_0I/2\pi a = \qty{2}{mT}$;
$\Pi = EB/\mu_0 = \qty{84}{W/m^2}$, radially inward. (b) $84 \times 2\pi a = \qty{0.53}{W}$
per metre; $R = 1/\gamma\pi a^2 = \qty{5.3}{m\ohm/m}$, $RI^2 = \qty{0.53}{W}$. (c) $\Pi(r)
= EIr/2\pi a^2$, so $2\pi r\Pi = EIr^2/a^2$ and the shell receives $\dd(2\pi r\Pi)
= 2EIr\,\dd r/a^2 = (j^2/\gamma)2\pi r\,\dd r$. (d) The battery's surface charges
set up an electric field along the whole circuit, outside the wires;
$\vect E\wedge\vect B$ there carries the energy from battery to load.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:6}
(a) $E = Q/\varepsilon_0\pi R^2$ axial, $B = \mu_0Ir/2\pi R^2$ azimuthal, $\vect\Pi = -\varepsilon_0
(r/2)E\dot E\,\vect e_r$. (b) $\varepsilon_0(R/2)E\dot E\cdot2\pi Rd = \dd(\tfrac12\varepsilon_0E^2\pi R^2d)/
\dd t$. (c) $\dot E = I/\varepsilon_0\pi R^2 = \qty{1.4e13}{V.m^{-1}.s^{-1}}$; $\Pi(R) = \qty{3.2e6}{W/m^2}$;
times $2\pi Rd = \qty{3.1e-4}{m^2}$: \qty{1.0}{kW} $= UI$ with $U = Ed = \qty{1}{kV}$. (d)
Yes: smaller by $(\omega R/c)^2$-type factors (\cref{ch:b2:maxwell-equations}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:7}
(a) $\vect E = -(r/2)\dot B\,\vect e_\theta$, $\vect\Pi = -(rB\dot B/2\mu_0)\vect e_r$: inward
while $B$ grows. (b) $(RB\dot B/2\mu_0)2\pi R = \dd(\pi R^2B^2/2\mu_0)/\dd t$. (c)
Outside $\vect B = \vect 0$, so $\vect\Pi = \vect 0$: the energy is injected at the
winding itself, where the generator drives the current against the
induced field ($\vect j\cdot\vect E < 0$ there); just inside the sheet
$\vect\Pi$ is already the inward flow of (a).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:8}
(a) $E = U/r\ln(b/a)$ radial, $B = \mu_0I/2\pi r$ azimuthal. (b) $\vect\Pi = (UI/
2\pi r^2\ln(b/a))\vect e_z$, along the core current. (c) $\int_a^b\Pi\,2\pi r\,\dd r =
UI$. (d) $E$ gains an axial component $j/\gamma$; $\vect\Pi$ tilts toward the
core, and its radial flux into a metre of core is $RI^2$ per metre.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:9}
(a) $F_{\text{rad}} = I_0(D_0/D)^2\pi a^2/c$, $F_g = GM\cdot\tfrac43\pi a^3\rho/D^2$: ratio
$3I_0D_0^2/4cGM\rho a$, independent of $D$. (b) Ratio $> 1$ for $a < 3I_0D_0^2/
4cGM\rho = \qty{0.3}{\micro m}$. (c) Fine dust is pushed away from the Sun while
keeping the comet's orbital speed, so the dust tail points away and
curves; the ion tail, driven by the solar wind, is straight.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:10}
(a) $\dot E < 0$: $\vect\Pi$ points outward at the edge. (b) $-\dd W_e/\dd t$
leaves the capacitor and $RI^2$ enters the wire; in the quasi-static
regime they are equal --- nothing accumulates in between. (c) $W_e =
Q^2/2C$, $\dd W_e/\dd t = (Q/C)\dd Q/\dd t = -UI = -RI^2$. (d) $\tfrac12LI^2$
vanishes at both ends ($I = 0$) and is negligible in between for a small
loop.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:11}
(a) $\vect\Pi = (EB/\mu_0)\vect e_y$, uniform: $\operatorname{div}\vect\Pi = 0$. (b) Zero.
(c) Along $y$ through the gap, closing through the fringing fields at
the edges of the plates and of the magnet. (d) A body at rest in static
fields carries no current: $\vect j\cdot\vect E = 0$, no heating. The
circulating $\vect\Pi$ is bookkeeping, not a flow that deposits energy.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:poynting-vector:12}
Faraday: $\partial_xE_y = -2E_0k\sin kx\cos\omega t$ equals $-\partial_tB_z$ since
$\omega = kc$. (a)
$\vect\Pi = (E_yB_z/\mu_0)\vect e_x = -(E_0^2/\mu_0c)\sin2kx\sin2\omega t\,\vect e_x$: mean
zero. (b) $u = 2\varepsilon_0E_0^2(\cos^2kx\cos^2\omega t + \sin^2kx\sin^2\omega t)$: electric
at $x = 0, \lambda/2, \dots$, magnetic at $\lambda/4, \dots$, sloshing between them
twice per period. (c) Momentum: each wave has $I = \varepsilon_0cE_0^2/2$, the
reflected one reverses its momentum: $2I/c = \varepsilon_0E_0^2$. Laplace: at $x =
0$, $B = (2E_0/c)\sin\omega t$ just outside and $0$ inside, so $j_s = B/\mu_0$; the
force per unit area on the sheet is $\tfrac12j_sB = B^2/2\mu_0 = 2\varepsilon_0E_0^2\sin^2
\omega t$. (d) Its mean is $\varepsilon_0E_0^2 = 2I/c$.
\end{solution}
