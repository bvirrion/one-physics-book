\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:4}
Exact $\sqrt{1 + 10^{-4}} - 1 = \qty{49.9988}{\micro m}$, paraxial \qty{50}{\micro m}: a

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:5}
(a) A sphere of radius $f$ centred at $F'$ has, on the lens plane, the
phase $-\pi r^2/\lambda f$ relative to the axis (paraxially): exactly the
lens's. (b) Total phase $\pi r^2/\lambda\,(1/d - 1/f) = -\pi r^2/\lambda d'$: a wave
converging at $d'$ with $1/d' = 1/f - 1/d$. (c) The glass adds $(n - 1)e(r)
\times2\pi/\lambda$: $e(r) = e_0 - r^2/2(n - 1)f$; sag $10^{-4}/0.1 = \qty{1}{mm}$. (d) A
phase defined modulo $2\pi$ is the same phase --- at the design
wavelength; at others the steps are no longer whole wavelengths.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:6}
(a) From a plane \omterm{def:b2:scalar-light-model:path}{wavefront} at $x = -D$ to $(x, y)$ then to $F(f, 0)$: $D + x
+ \sqrt{(x - f)^2 + y^2} = D + f$, so $\sqrt{(x - f)^2 + y^2} = f - x$, $y^2 = 4fx$,
with $F$ at the focus. (b) The circle $x = 2f - \sqrt{4f^2 - y^2} \approx y^2/4f +
y^4/64f^3$ agrees to second order only. (c) $y^4/64f^3 = \qty{1.6}{\micro m}$, doubled
by reflection: \qty{3}{\micro m}, six wavelengths --- hence parabolic
mirrors. (d) At $\lambda \sim \qty{1}{cm}$ the same error is a thousandth of a
wavelength.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:7}
(a) $L(x) = n_1\sqrt{a^2 + x^2} + n_2\sqrt{b^2 + (d - x)^2}$: $L' = n_1\sin\theta_1 -
n_2\sin\theta_2 = 0$. (b) Near the stationary path neighbouring paths have
equal phases and their waves add; elsewhere they cancel. (c) $n_1 = n_2$:
$\sin\theta_1 = \sin\theta_r$. (d) Stigmatism means all rays from $A$ to $A'$ have
equal paths: a whole family of stationary paths, not one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:8}
(a) $I = 2I_0(1 + \cos\Delta\varphi)$: $4I_0$ and $0$. (b) $N = 10^7$: mean $2I_0$, residual
$\sim 2I_0/\sqrt N = 6 \times 10^{-4}$ of it. (c) During \qty{10}{ps} the phase is
frozen: it would see fringes, jumping every \qty{0.1}{ns}. (d) Lasers with
$\tau_c \sim \qty{1}{\micro s}$ and a detector faster than that: the fringes exist
for a microsecond and were photographed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:9}
(a) Small angles: $\tan i \approx n\tan r$, the rays appear to come from $h/n$.
(b) \qty{0.90}{m}; the \omterm{def:b2:scalar-light-model:path}{optical path} is $n \times 1.2 = \qty{1.6}{m}$, \qty{0.4}{m} more.
(c) $n \times 1 = \qty{1.33}{m}$. (d) At large angles the refraction is stronger
and the apparent depth smaller.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:10}
(a) Two equal waves with phase difference $2\pi\nu\delta/c$. (b) Average
over the band:
\[
\frac1{\Delta\nu}\int\cos\frac{2\pi\nu\delta}c\,\dd\nu = \cos\frac{2\pi\nu_0\delta}c\,\operatorname{sinc}\frac{\pi\Delta\nu\,\delta}c .
\]
(c)
$I_{\max, \min} = 2I_0(1 \pm |\operatorname{sinc}|)$: $V = |\operatorname{sinc}(\pi\Delta\nu\delta/c)|$,
zero at $\delta = c/\Delta\nu$. (d) $\Delta\nu = c\Delta\lambda/\lambda^2 = \qty{1.9e13}{Hz}$: $\delta =
\qty{16}{\micro m}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:11}
(a) $100$ photons per second on \qty{38}{mm^2}: \qty{2.6e6}{m^{-2}.s^{-1}}, $\times4 \times
10^{-19}$ J: \qty{1e-12}{W/m^2}. (b) $10^{-13.1} \times 1000 = \qty{8e-11}{W/m^2}$, $2 \times 10^8$
photons per square metre and second, $8000$ per second into the pupil
--- well above (a): the eye's real limit is set by background and by
the spread over many rods. (c) $(200/7)^2 = 800$, $+7.3$ magnitudes: $m \approx
13$. (d) $N$ photons fluctuate by $\sqrt N$: the signal-to-noise grows as
the square root of the exposure.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:scalar-light-model:12}
(a) $d(1 + r^2/2d^2 - r^4/8d^4 + \dots) - d$. (b) $r^4/8d^3 < \lambda/4$. (c) $r <
(2\lambda d^3)^{1/4}$: \qty{3.2}{cm} for \qty{1}{m}; \qty{1}{mm} for \qty{1}{cm}, an angle of
\qty{0.1}{rad}. (d) Spherical aberration is this quartic term; the other
aberrations are its off-axis cousins.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:scalar-light-model:1}
\textbf{1.} $\nu_1 = \qty{5.0934e14}{Hz}$, $\nu_2 = \qty{5.0882e14}{Hz}$: $\Delta\nu = \qty{5.2e11}{Hz}$.

\textbf{2.} $c/\Delta\nu_{\text{nat}} = \qty{30}{m}$.

\textbf{3.} $\sqrt{k_BT/m} = \qty{425}{m/s}$: $\Delta\nu_D \approx 2\nu v/c \approx \qty{1.4}{GHz}$, a
hundred times the natural width.

\textbf{4.} $c/\Delta\nu_D \approx \qty{20}{cm}$; with collisions, about \qty{2}{cm}.

\textbf{5.} $\lambda^2/\Delta\lambda = 589^2/0.6$ nm $= \qty{0.58}{mm}$: coincidence to
opposition every \qty{0.29}{mm}; about $1000$ fringes per beat.

\textbf{6.} The contrast oscillates with a period of \qty{0.58}{mm} in path
difference (zero at $0.29$, $0.87$, \qty{1.45}{mm}, \dots) inside an envelope
that fades over a few centimetres: some fifty beats, then nothing.

\textbf{7.} \qty{3e18}{} photons per second; \qty{2.1}{eV}, the yellow of the
spectrum.

\textbf{8.} No: independent atoms, random phases. The two lines of one
lamp come from different atoms too and do not interfere with each
other: the beats are two fringe systems adding in \emph{intensity}.

\textbf{9.} $\lambda^2/\Delta\lambda = \qty{0.85}{mm}$; $c/\Delta\nu = \qty{300}{m}$.

\textbf{10.} $\qty{3.3e15}{}$ per second, spaced $c/N = \qty{90}{nm}$ along the beam:
ten thousand photons within a \omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length}. Interference is not
photons meeting: each photon interferes with itself.

\textbf{11.} $\Delta\nu = c\Delta\lambda/\lambda^2 = \qty{360}{GHz}$; mode spacing $c/2nL = \qty{43}{GHz}$:
about eight modes.

\textbf{12.} $e \times 3.3 \times 10^{15} = \qty{0.5}{mA}$.

\textbf{13.} \qty{2}{mm} $>$ \qty{0.85}{mm}: no fringes; \qty{10}{m} $<$ \qty{300}{m}: fringes.

\textbf{14.} A nanosecond pulse is \qty{30}{cm} long, far longer than the
\qty{0.85}{mm} \omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length}: the line width, not the pulse, sets the
coherence; successive pulses interfere only if the laser's phase
survives the off period --- it does not.

\textbf{15.} Coherent light scattered by the rough wall interferes at
the retina with random path differences: speckle. The lamp's
micrometre-scale coherence averages all that out.

\textbf{16.} $\lambda^2/\Delta\lambda = \qty{17}{\micro m}$: far too short for speckle on a
rough wall --- no speckle, a smoother image.

\textbf{17.} $(n - 1)e = \qty{1}{mm}$: $1540$ fringes.

\textbf{18.} $r = \qty{6.65}{\degree}$; extra path $\approx e(n/\cos r - \cos(i - r)/\cos r)
= 3.021 - 2.010 = \qty{1.011}{mm}$, \qty{11}{\micro m} more than untilted: some
twenty fringes more.

\textbf{19.} Five pixels per fringe; at one pixel per fringe the pixel
integrates a whole period and the contrast collapses.

\textbf{20.} \qty{1e-12}{W} on the pixel $\times$ \qty{10}{ms} $= \qty{1e-14}{J}$: $3 \times 10^4$
photons, noise $0.6\%$.

\textbf{21.} $I = 2I_0[1 + \cos(2\pi\delta/\lambda)]$: one fringe per wavelength of
path difference.

\textbf{22.} The eye averages the kilohertz motion into a uniform
field; the photodiode follows it.

\textbf{23.} $(n - 1) \times 10 = \qty{2.9}{mm}$: $4500$ fringes; a $1\%$ change of
pressure moves them by $45$ fringes --- the interferometer is a
barometer unless evacuated.

\textbf{24.} Nearly the same photon rate (\qty{2.1}{eV} against \qty{1.9}{eV});
the fringes depend on amplitudes and phases, the photon count only on
the noise.

\textbf{25.} Lamp: centimetres, limited by Doppler and collision
broadening, fringes up to about \qty{1}{cm}; cheap pointer: \qty{0.85}{mm},
limited by its several modes; stabilized laser: \qty{300}{m}, limited by
its line width.
\end{solution}
