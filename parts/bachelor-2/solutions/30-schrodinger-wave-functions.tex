\qty{8.6}{pm}$ exactly).

\textbf{3.} \qty{4.8}{ns}.

\textbf{4.} $\lambda/a = \qty{3e-8}{rad}$: \qty{12}{nm} --- $10^4$ times smaller than a

\textbf{5.} $\Delta\theta = \Delta p_y/p \sim \hbar/2ap = \lambda/4\pi a$: the same order.

\textbf{6.} $\tau = \qty{1.6}{ms} \gg \qty{4.8}{ns}$: unchanged.

\textbf{7.} $\dd\langle p_y\rangle/\dd t = eE$ exactly for a uniform field:

\textbf{8.} $v/2 = \qty{4.2e7}{m/s}$; only the \omterm{prop:b2:dispersion-wave-packets:group}{group velocity} is observable;
a plane wave's phase carries no signal (and depends on the arbitrary
zero of energy).

\textbf{9.} $\Delta p = \hbar/2\sigma_0$, $\Delta v = \hbar/2m\sigma_0$.

\textbf{10.} \qty{1.7e-16}{s}, \qty{1.7e-14}{s}, \qty{1.7e-8}{s}; proton \qty{3.2e-11}{s};
virus \qty{20}{s}.

\textbf{11.} It is in a \omterm{thm:b2:schrodinger-wave-functions:stationary}{stationary state}: the potential holds the
packet; $|\varphi|^2$ does not move.

\textbf{12.} $v = \qty{5.9e5}{m/s}$; doubling at $t = \sqrt3\tau = \qty{2.9e-14}{s}$:
\qty{17}{nm}, some fourteen wavelengths.

\textbf{13.} Their \omterm{def:b2:schrodinger-wave-functions:psi}{wave functions} extend over the whole crystal: a
definite momentum, no definite position.

\textbf{14.} Resolution $\sim\lambda/\alpha = \qty{4}{pm}/0.01 = \qty{0.4}{nm}$: the
aberrations of magnetic lenses limit the aperture, not the wavelength.

\textbf{15.} Compare $\lambda$ with the sizes in play (apertures, the
scale of $V$), and the spreading time with the observation time.

\textbf{16.} \qty{0.38}{}, \qty{1.50}{}, \qty{3.38}{eV}; $\nu_{21} = \qty{2.7e14}{Hz}$
(\qty{1.1}{\micro m}), $\nu_{31} = \qty{7.3e14}{Hz}$ (\qty{410}{nm}).

\textbf{17.} The phasor has modulus one; at $x = L/2$.

\textbf{18.} At $t = 0$ the density piles up on the left half, at
$T_{21}/2$ on the right.

\textbf{19.} $\langle x\rangle = L/2 - (16L/9\pi^2)\cos\omega_{21}t$: amplitude
$0.18L = \qty{0.18}{nm}$.

\textbf{20.} At $\nu_{21}$: the spectral line; a \omterm{thm:b2:schrodinger-wave-functions:stationary}{stationary state} has no
\omterm{thm:b2:dipole-radiation:field}{oscillating dipole} and is stable; the superposition radiates its way
down to $\varphi_1$.

\textbf{21.} Not definite: $\langle E\rangle = (E_1 + E_2)/2 = \qty{0.94}{eV}$; a
measurement gives $E_1$ or $E_2$, each with probability one half.

\textbf{22.} Random: where each electron lands; not random: the
pattern $|\psi|^2$ that the landings build.

\textbf{23.} $j = |A|^2\hbar k/m$: with $|A|^2 = n$ electrons per unit length,
$nv$ electrons per second.

\textbf{24.} First order so that $\psi(x,0)$ is a complete description
that fixes the future; complex so that a definite energy has the
single time dependence $\eu^{-\iu Et/\hbar}$ and $E = p^2/2m$ comes out.

\textbf{25.} $\psi$ is the amplitude whose square is the probability; it
obeys Schrödinger's linear equation; \omterm{thm:b2:schrodinger-wave-functions:stationary}{stationary states} carry a
phasor and fixed densities; a free packet moves at $p/m$ and spreads
in $2m\sigma_0^2/\hbar$; Ehrenfest gives Newton back for the large.
\end{solution}
