\chapter{Two-Wave Interference}\label{ch:b2:two-wave-interference}

A soap bubble is made of a colourless liquid, yet it shimmers with
colour; a film of oil on a wet road shows the same rainbow bands; the
wings of a dragonfly glint green and violet. None of these colours
comes from a pigment: they come from two copies of the same light wave,
reflected at the two faces of a thin film, arriving with a delay and
either reinforcing or cancelling each other --- the \emph{interference}
of two waves. Young made it the proof that light is a wave in 1801,
with two slits and a candle; this chapter sets out the formula, the
geometry of Young's fringes, the two conditions --- temporal and
\omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{spatial coherence} --- under which fringes survive, and the thin-film
colours.

\section{The interference formula}

\begin{theorem}[Two-wave interference]\label{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}
\index{interference}\index{path difference}\index{order of interference}\index{contrast (fringe)}
Two coherent \omterm{def:b2:scalar-light-model:scalar}{monochromatic waves} of intensities $I_1$, $I_2$ and phase
difference $\Delta\varphi$ at a point $M$ give there the intensity
\[
I(M) = I_1 + I_2 + 2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\,\cos\Delta\varphi(M) , \qquad
\Delta\varphi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda_0}\,\delta(M) ,
\]
where $\delta$ is the \emph{optical \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{path difference}} between the two
routes from the source to $M$. The intensity is maximal ($I_1 + I_2 +
2\sqrt{I_1I_2}$) where $\delta = p\lambda_0$, $p$ an integer --- the \emph{\omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{order of
interference}} --- and minimal ($I_1 + I_2 - 2\sqrt{I_1I_2}$) where $\delta = (p +
\tfrac12)\lambda_0$. The \emph{contrast} (visibility) of the fringes is
\[
V = \frac{I_{\max} - I_{\min}}{I_{\max} + I_{\min}} = \frac{2\sqrt{I_1I_2}}{I_1 + I_2} ,
\]
equal to $1$ for equal intensities, where the minima are perfectly dark:
$I = 2I_0(1 + \cos\Delta\varphi) = 4I_0\cos^2(\Delta\varphi/2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$s = a_1\cos(\omega t - \varphi_1) + a_2\cos(\omega t - \varphi_2)$; $\langle s^2\rangle$ as in
\cref{ch:b2:scalar-light-model}, with $\Delta\varphi$ fixed during the
detection; $I_i = \tfrac12Ka_i^2$. The phase difference of two waves
that left the source together and travelled \omterm{def:b2:scalar-light-model:path}{optical paths} $L_1$, $L_2$ is
$2\pi(L_2 - L_1)/\lambda_0$ (plus $\pi$ for each reflection that reverses the
sign, \cref{ch:b2:wave-interfaces}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The three conditions]\label{rem:b2:two-wave-interference:conditions}
\index{coherence (spatial)}\index{coherence (temporal)}
Fringes exist only if (1) the two waves come from the \emph{same
source} (two copies of one vibration, otherwise $\Delta\varphi$ is random); (2)
the \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{path difference} is smaller than the \omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length}, $|\delta| < \ell_c$
(\emph{temporal coherence}: beyond it the copies belong to different
\omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{wave trains}); (3) the source is small enough that its different points
do not produce displaced fringe systems washing each other out
(\emph{\omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{spatial coherence}}, below). And a fourth, usually satisfied: the
two waves must not have perpendicular polarizations (they would add
in intensity, like the torches).
\end{remark}

\section{Young's slits}

\begin{proposition}[Division of wavefront: Young's experiment]\label{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}
\index{Young's slits}\index{interfringe}\index{division of wavefront}
A point source $S$ (or a slit) illuminates two narrow parallel slits
$S_1$, $S_2$ a distance $a$ apart, equidistant from $S$; a screen stands at
the distance $D \gg a$. At the point of the screen at height $x$ (parallel
to $a$) the \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{path difference} is
\[
\delta = S_2M - S_1M \approx \frac{ax}D ,
\]
and the screen shows straight, equidistant fringes, bright at $x_p =
p\lambda D/a$, with the \emph{interfringe}
\[
i = \frac{\lambda D}a .
\]
The fringes exist everywhere behind the slits (they are \emph{not
localized}); with a lens of focal length $f$ after the slits, they are
seen in its focal plane with $i = \lambda f/a$ (fringes "at infinity").
The intensity is $4I_0\cos^2(\pi ax/\lambda D)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$S_2M^2 - S_1M^2 = [(x + a/2)^2 - (x - a/2)^2] = 2ax$ and $S_2M + S_1M \approx 2D$,
so $\delta \approx 2ax/2D$. Two orders differ by $\lambda$, i.e.\ by $\Delta x = \lambda D/a$.
With a lens the two waves leaving the slits at the angle $\theta$ from the
axis have the \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{path difference} $a\sin\theta \approx a\theta$ and meet at $x = f\theta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Numbers, and white light]\label{ex:b2:two-wave-interference:numbers}
$a = \qty{0.5}{mm}$, $D = \qty{2}{m}$, $\lambda = \qty{600}{nm}$: $i = \qty{2.4}{mm}$ --- large
enough to see, which is why \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}{Young's slits} must be close and the screen
far. With white light every colour makes its own fringes, all
coinciding at the centre ($\delta = 0$): a white central fringe, then
coloured fringes as the orders drift apart (red $i$ is $1.8$ times the
violet), and after a few orders a uniform whitish field --- the
\omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length} of white light is a micrometre, two or three
wavelengths. A spectrometer pointed at that "white" field still sees
dark bands at the wavelengths for which $\delta = (p + \tfrac12)\lambda$: a
\emph{channelled spectrum}.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Spatial coherence: the source must be small]\label{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}
\index{spatial coherence}\index{extended source}
Each point of an \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{extended source} produces its own fringe system; a
source point displaced transversally by $b$ at the distance $L$ before
the slits shifts the fringes by $x = bD/L$ (the \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{path difference} gains
$ab/L$). A source of width $b$ spreads the fringes over one interfringe,
and the contrast vanishes, when
\[
\frac{ab}L \approx \lambda , \qquad\text{i.e. when the slits see the source under an angle } \frac bL \gtrsim \frac\lambda a .
\]
The two slits must be within the \emph{coherence area} of the light ---
the region over which the wave from the source has a well-defined
phase, of width $\lambda L/b$. Sunlight ($b/L = \qty{0.5}{\degree} = \qty{9}{mrad}$)
gives a coherence width of $\lambda/0.009 \approx \qty{60}{\micro m}$: Young needed a
pinhole to widen it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a source point at height $b$, the extra path $SS_2 - SS_1 \approx -ab/L$
adds to $ax/D$: the pattern shifts by $x = bD/L$. Integrating the
displaced $\cos^2$ patterns over a source of width $b$ gives a contrast
$|\operatorname{sinc}(\pi ab/\lambda L)|$, zero at $ab/L = \lambda$.
\end{proof}

\section{Thin films: division of amplitude}

\begin{proposition}[Interference in a thin film]\label{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:film}
\index{thin-film interference}\index{division of amplitude}\index{fringes of equal inclination}\index{fringes of equal thickness}
A film of index $n$ and thickness $e$ in air, lit at incidence $i$
(refraction angle $r$), returns two waves reflected at its two faces,
with the \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{path difference}
\[
\delta = 2ne\cos r \ (+\tfrac{\lambda_0}2) ,
\]
the extra half wavelength counting the sign reversal of the reflection
on the denser medium at the first face and not at the second
(\cref{ch:b2:wave-interfaces}). The fringes are bright for $2ne\cos r =
(p + \tfrac12)\lambda_0$, dark for $2ne\cos r = p\lambda_0$ --- a film of vanishing
thickness reflects \emph{nothing} (the black top of a draining soap
film). For a film of uniform thickness the fringes depend on $i$ only
and are seen at infinity (rings of \emph{equal inclination}); for a
wedge of slowly varying thickness seen at near-normal incidence they
follow the contours of equal $e$ and are localized on the film
(fringes of \emph{equal thickness}): an air wedge of angle $\alpha$ shows
straight fringes spaced $\lambda_0/2\alpha$; a lens of radius $R$ on a flat gives
\emph{Newton's rings}, dark at $r_p = \sqrt{p\lambda_0R}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Geometry: the wave refracted at the first face crosses the film twice
($2e/\cos r$ in glass, \omterm{def:b2:scalar-light-model:path}{optical path} $2ne/\cos r$) while the wave reflected
at the first face advances $2e\tan r\sin i$ in air; the difference is
$2ne/\cos r - 2e\tan r\sin i = 2ne\cos r$ with $\sin i = n\sin r$. For the
wedge, $e = \alpha x$ and successive dark fringes at $2\alpha x = p\lambda$; for the
lens, $e \approx r^2/2R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Soap, oil, dragonflies]\label{ex:b2:two-wave-interference:colours}
A soap film ($n = 1.33$) \qty{300}{nm} thick seen at normal incidence:
bright for $2ne = (p + \tfrac12)\lambda$, i.e.\ $\lambda = 798/(p + \tfrac12)$ nm: \qty{532}{nm}
(green, $p = 1$) within the visible, and a dark band at \qty{400}{nm}; the
film looks green. As it drains and thins, the colour runs through the
sequence, ending black. An oil film (\qty{400}{nm}, $n = 1.45$) on water
($1.33$): the second reflection, toward a lower index, does not reverse
the sign, so again $\delta = 2ne + \lambda/2$ and the film is bright at $2ne =
(p + \tfrac12)\lambda$: \qty{464}{nm} ($p = 2$), blue. The iridescence of a
butterfly's wing, a beetle's shell or a compact disc are interferences
of the same kind (the disc's, of many waves: \cref{ch:b2:gratings}),
which is why they change with the angle --- $\delta$ depends on $\cos r$ ---
while a pigment's colour does not.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Solving an interference problem]\label{met:b2:two-wave-interference:method}
(1) Identify the two routes from the source to $M$ and compute the
optical \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{path difference} $\delta(M)$, adding $\lambda/2$ for each reflection
with sign reversal. (2) $I = I_1 + I_2 + 2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos(2\pi\delta/\lambda)$;
bright at $\delta = p\lambda$. (3) The fringe spacing from $\delta(M + \Delta M) - \delta(M) =
\lambda$. (4) Check temporal coherence ($\delta_{\max} < \ell_c$, number of
visible fringes $\sim\lambda/\Delta\lambda$) and \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{spatial coherence} (source angle
$< \lambda/a$). (5) Say where the fringes are localized (everywhere for two
point sources; on the film for equal-thickness fringes; at infinity
for equal-inclination ones).
\end{method}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:1}
\omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}{Young's slits} $a = \qty{0.40}{mm}$ apart, screen at $D = \qty{1.5}{m}$, $\lambda =
\qty{633}{nm}$: interfringe; order of the fringe at $x = \qty{1.0}{cm}$; position
of the tenth dark fringe; interfringe with the slits \qty{2}{mm} apart,
and in water ($n = 1.33$).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:2}
Two waves of intensities $I_0$ and $I_0/4$ interfere: $I_{\max}$, $I_{\min}$,
contrast. Same for $I_0$ and $I_0/100$: why are fringes still visible
with such unequal beams? One slit of Young's pair is covered by a
grey filter transmitting $50\%$: new contrast.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:3}
A soap film \qty{250}{nm} thick ($n = 1.33$) seen at normal incidence:
visible wavelengths reflected most and least; its colour. A film of
\qty{1.0}{\micro m}: how many bright wavelengths in the visible, and what
does the eye see?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:4}
Newton's rings between a lens of radius $R = \qty{2.0}{m}$ and a flat, in
sodium light: radii of the first three dark rings; number of rings
within a radius of \qty{1}{cm}; is the centre bright or dark, and why?
What changes if the gap is filled with water?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:5}
\emph{White light.} \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}{Young's slits} with white light ($400$--\qty{700}{nm}),
$a = \qty{0.5}{mm}$, $D = \qty{1}{m}$. (a) Interfringe for violet and for red;
position of the second red and the third violet bright fringes. (b)
Beyond which order do the red and violet fringes of successive orders
overlap? (c) At $x = \qty{5}{mm}$, which wavelengths are missing
(channelled spectrum)? (d) Explain the sequence of colours seen from
the centre outward.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:6}
\emph{Source size.} A sodium lamp lights \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}{Young's slits} ($a = \qty{1.0}{mm}$)
through a source slit at $L = \qty{40}{cm}$. (a) Maximum width of the
source slit for good contrast (take $ab/L < \lambda/4$). (b) The source slit
is widened to \qty{0.5}{mm}: contrast (use $|\operatorname{sinc}(\pi ab/\lambda L)|$).
(c) Why can one not simply use the bare lamp (width \qty{1}{cm})? (d) The
Sun as a source, \qty{9}{mrad} across: maximum slit separation that
gives fringes.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:7}
\emph{Lloyd's mirror.} A point source at height $h = \qty{0.2}{mm}$ above a
plane mirror, screen at $D = \qty{1.0}{m}$, $\lambda = \qty{500}{nm}$: the direct wave
interferes with the one reflected by the mirror (a virtual source at
$-h$). (a) Interfringe. (b) The fringe at the mirror's edge ($x = 0$) is
dark: what does that tell about the reflection? (c) Number of fringes
visible if the source's \omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length} is \qty{50}{\micro m}. (d) Why is
this arrangement the radio astronomer's "sea interferometer" for a
rising star?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:8}
\emph{Air wedge.} Two glass plates \qty{10}{cm} long touch at one end and
are separated at the other by a hair of diameter $d$; in sodium light
$42$ dark fringes are counted. (a) $d$. (b) Fringe spacing. (c) The gap
is filled with water: number of fringes. (d) The plates are pressed
slightly at the far end: what do the fringes do, and what precision on
$d$ does counting a quarter of a fringe give?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:9}
\emph{Oil on water.} A film of oil ($n = 1.45$) of thickness $e$ floats
on water ($n = 1.33$). (a) Which of the two reflections reverses the
sign? \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{Path difference}. (b) For $e = \qty{400}{nm}$ at normal incidence:
reflected wavelengths reinforced and cancelled; colour. (c) Thinnest
film that looks violet (\qty{420}{nm}). (d) Why do the colours shift toward
blue when the film is viewed obliquely? (e) What changes for a film
whose index lies \emph{between} those of the two media (a coating on
glass)?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:10}
\emph{A slide on one slit.} A glass plate of thickness $e = \qty{20}{\micro m}$
and index $1.5$ covers slit $S_1$ of a Young's pair ($a = \qty{1}{mm}$, $D =
\qty{2}{m}$, $\lambda = \qty{500}{nm}$). (a) Extra \omterm{def:b2:scalar-light-model:path}{optical path}; by how many fringes
and in which direction does the pattern shift? (b) With white light,
where does the (now displaced) white central fringe go --- is it still
visible? \omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{Coherence length} \qty{1.5}{\micro m}. (c) Use the white-light
pattern to measure $e$: how? (d) The plate is tilted: the shift grows
--- show that to first order it grows as $e(n - 1)\theta^2/2 \times (1 + 1/n)$
and estimate it for \qty{5}{\degree}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:11}
\emph{The stellar interferometer.} Two mirrors a distance $a$ apart
collect the light of a star and bring it to interfere. The star is a
uniform disk of angular diameter $\theta$; each point of it shifts the
fringes, and the contrast vanishes for $a = 1.22\lambda/\theta$ (admitted, the
disk's version of $ab/L = \lambda$). (a) Betelgeuse, $\theta = \qty{0.047}{''}$
(seconds of arc), $\lambda = \qty{570}{nm}$: baseline at which the fringes
disappear (Michelson, 1920, Mount Wilson). (b) Why can a single
telescope of \qty{2.5}{m} not resolve the disk (its limit is $1.22\lambda/d$)?
(c) Modern interferometers link telescopes \qty{100}{m} apart: smallest
measurable diameter. (d) Why must the two paths be equal to within the
\omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length}, and how long is that for a \qty{10}{nm} filter?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:two-wave-interference:12}
\emph{Equal-inclination rings.} A parallel plate of thickness $e =
\qty{2.0}{mm}$ and index $1.5$ is lit by a broad sodium source; the
reflected light is focused by a lens of $f = \qty{50}{cm}$. (a) Show that the
rings are at the angles $i_p$ with $2ne\cos r_p = (p + \tfrac12)\lambda$ (bright)
and that, near the centre, $\cos r \approx 1 - i^2/2n^2$. (b) Order at the
centre; is the centre bright? (c) Radii of the first three bright
rings in the focal plane. (d) Why are these rings seen with an
\omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{extended source} while Young's fringes need a narrow one? (Localization
at infinity.)
\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: Fresnel's mirrors and an oil film}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- an interferometer of 1816 and a
rainbow on a puddle: two ways of splitting one wave and the two
coherences that limit both}]
\label{pb:b2:two-wave-interference:1}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Fresnel's mirrors.}
Two plane mirrors touching along a line make a very small angle $\alpha =
\qty{5.0e-3}{rad}$; a slit source $S$, parallel to that line, is at $d =
\qty{20}{cm}$ from it, and the screen at $D = \qty{1.5}{m}$ beyond. $\lambda =
\qty{589}{nm}$ (sodium).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Show that the two mirrors give two virtual images $S_1$, $S_2$ of
        $S$ on a circle of radius $d$ centred on the line of contact,
        separated by $a = 2\alpha d$; value.
  \item Why do the waves from $S_1$ and $S_2$ interfere, whereas those of
        two separate lamps would not?
  \item Interfringe on the screen (distance from the sources to the
        screen $\approx d + D$); number of fringes across the
        \qty{5}{mm}-wide region where the two reflected beams overlap.
  \item \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{Order of interference} at the edge of that region; is it below
        the limit set by the lamp's \omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length} (\qty{2}{cm})?
  \item The source slit is \qty{20}{\micro m} wide: shift of the fringes
        produced by its two edges (each source point is imaged in two
        virtual points; the fringe system shifts by $bD'/d$ with $D' =
        d + D$); is the contrast preserved (compare with $i$)?
  \item The slit is widened to \qty{0.1}{mm}: contrast, using
        $|\operatorname{sinc}(\pi ab/\lambda d)|$.
  \item The two mirrors reflect $90\%$ and $80\%$ of the light: contrast
        of the fringes.
  \item A glass plate \qty{10}{\micro m} thick ($n = 1.5$) is placed in
        front of one mirror: shift of the pattern, in fringes; can it
        be followed with sodium light? With white light?
  \item Replace the sodium lamp by white light: describe the screen;
        how many coloured fringes on each side of the centre?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The oil film.}
A film of oil ($n = 1.45$) spreads on a wet road ($n_{\text{water}} = 1.33$),
lit by the overcast sky and viewed at near-normal incidence.
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Reflections at the two faces: which reverse the sign? \omterm{thm:b2:two-wave-interference:formula}{Path
        difference}; condition for a bright colour.
  \item Thickness of the thinnest region that looks red ($\lambda =
        \qty{650}{nm}$); green (\qty{550}{nm}); violet (\qty{420}{nm}).
  \item A region \qty{600}{nm} thick: which visible wavelengths are
        reinforced, which cancelled; its colour.
  \item Why does the film show bands of colour (what varies along the
        road), and why do the bands follow the contours of thickness?
  \item The film thins by evaporation: describe the change of the
        colour sequence at a given point; what is seen when $e \to 0$
        here --- bright or dark? Compare with a soap film in air.
  \item The top of a draining soap film turns black just before it
        bursts: thickness there (a few nanometres) and why black.
  \item Viewed at \qty{60}{\degree} incidence: refraction angle in the oil,
        and the shift of the bright wavelengths of question 11.
  \item Beyond what thickness do the colours fade into a uniform
        grey, for daylight of \omterm{prop:b2:scalar-light-model:coherence}{coherence length} about \qty{1}{\micro m}?
  \item The light from the sky is unpolarized and arrives from many
        directions: why does the pattern not wash out as an \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{extended
        source} washes out Young's fringes? (Localization on the film.)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part III --- Two coherences.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Temporal coherence: for the Fresnel mirrors with sodium light,
        the maximum order visible; with a laser ($\ell_c = \qty{10}{m}$).
  \item \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{Spatial coherence}: the maximum source width for the mirrors
        (from $ab/d < \lambda/4$) and for \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}{Young's slits} with $a = \qty{1}{mm}$ at
        $L = \qty{40}{cm}$.
  \item Explain why the thin film tolerates an \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:spatial}{extended source} while
        \omterm{prop:b2:two-wave-interference:young}{Young's slits} do not: sketch the two rays of the film for two
        different source points and show they still meet on the film.
  \item The sodium line itself is \qty{0.02}{nm} wide: how many fringes
        can the mirrors show at most (use $N \approx \lambda/\Delta\lambda$)?
  \item A student replaces the sodium lamp by a green LED ($\Delta\lambda =
        \qty{30}{nm}$): number of fringes visible with the mirrors.
  \item With a laser the fringes extend over the whole overlap region
        with full contrast but the screen is "speckled": why?
  \item In one table, for the mirrors and the film: how the wave is
        split, where the fringes are localized, what limits their
        number, and what a wider source does.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
