\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g2:ice-water-steam} --- Ice, Water, Steam}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:1}
Icicle: solid. The sea: liquid. The invisible water above the spout:
gas (water vapor). Frost: solid. Rain: liquid.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:2}
The snowman \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{melts}; the puddle \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:evapcond}{evaporates}; the mirror mist \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:evapcond}{condenses};
the pond \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{freezes}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:3}
Warmth pushes toward the gas (solid to liquid to gas); cold pushes
back toward the solid (gas to liquid to solid).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:4}
No --- glass does not leak. The invisible water vapor in the room's
air condensed on the cold outside wall of the can, turning into

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:5}
Breath always carries invisible water vapor. Freezing air \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:evapcond}{condenses}
it at once into tiny droplets --- a visible cloud. Warm air lets it
stay evaporated, so there is nothing to see.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:6}
Evaporation. The Sun adds warmth, and warmth is what sends water
into the air as a gas faster.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:7}
The plume is burning hot --- hotter than bath-hot water. The rule:
never put a hand in the plume, lift lids away from you, and only
with an adult nearby.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:8}
The Sun warms the sea and some water \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:evapcond}{evaporates}, flying up invisibly.
High up, in the cold, it \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:evapcond}{condenses} into cloud droplets. The droplets
grow heavy and fall as rain, which runs back into the sea --- ready
to go around again.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:9}
The clear gap holds water in its gas state --- water vapor,
invisible. Only a little higher, cooling air \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:evapcond}{condenses} it into the
white droplets of the plume. So the real ``steam'' cannot be seen;
the white we call steam is already tiny drops of liquid water.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:ice-water-steam:10}
The water evaporated into the air of the room, a little every day.
To clear the cat: cover an identical glass with a plate or plastic
film and leave both side by side. After a week the covered glass is
still full while the open one has sunk --- the water escapes into
the air, and no cat can drink through a lid.
\end{solution}
