\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g2:moon-in-the-sky} --- The Moon in the Sky}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:1}
Much smaller than the Earth, and much farther than the clouds ---
farther than any airplane flies. We know people can visit because
astronauts did: the trip took them three days each way.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:2}
Craters --- bowl-shaped scars made by rocks from space that crashed
into the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}. They remain because the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} has no \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} and no rain
to wear them away.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:3}
A light-catcher. Moonlight is sunlight bouncing off the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s gray
rock, the way a white wall \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} a room without being a lamp.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:4}
Always the half facing the Sun --- the sunny side is bright, the far
side is in its own night.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:5}
Yes --- on many afternoons a pale \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} hangs in the blue sky. The
best proof for a doubting friend: go outside on such an afternoon
and point at it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:6}
\omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{Crescent}, half, almost full, \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{full moon} --- then back down through
almost full, half, \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescent}, to the hidden ``new'' \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}. The whole
show takes about a month.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:7}
A shout would make no sound --- sound needs \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}. A kite could not
fly: no \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} means no \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}, and nothing for the kite to lean on.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:8}
The same hour and the same honest drawing each evening (same place
helps too): a fair diary changes only one thing at a time --- the
night.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:9}
The houses are near, so driving past them changes how Rosa sees them
--- they slide by and are gone. The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} is so far that the whole
drive changes her view of it not at all: it stays at her shoulder,
seeming to follow. The champion of far always ``follows''.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:moon-in-the-sky:10}
The Earth itself. Sunlight bounces off the bright Earth onto the
\omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s dark side and faintly \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} it --- earthshine: our planet
returning the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s favor.
\end{solution}
