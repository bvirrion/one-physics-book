\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g2:air-around-us} --- Air Around Us}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:1}
For example: the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} bending trees or rustling leaves; a kite or
flag flying; breath-clouds on a cold morning; bubbles when blowing
through a straw; a door slamming in a draught.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:2}
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{Air} is trapped inside. Squeezing tries to push the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} into less
room, and the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} pushes back --- something truly empty could not.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:3}
The glass is full of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, and the trapped \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} keeps its room: two
things cannot be in the same place at once, so the water cannot rise
to the towel.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:4}
Do not tilt it. Tilted, the trapped \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} escapes upward as big
bubbles, water takes its room --- and the towel gets soaked.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:5}
Each glug is a parcel of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} escaping as a bubble while a gulp of
water takes its place. They take turns because the bottle's neck is
narrow and \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} and water cannot pass through it at the same time ---
and neither can share the same room inside.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:6}
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{Air} --- right up to the brim. ``Empty'' only means ``no drink left'';
the bottle is full of invisible stuff.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:7}
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{Wind} is \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} on the move. Jobs: filling sails, flying kites, drying
laundry, spinning \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} toys --- any two.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:8}
The \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} rushes out backward through the neck, and the balloon shoots
forward, the opposite way.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:9}
The bottle is already full of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, and the sealed funnel leaves the
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} no way out --- so the water cannot get in. The fix: loosen the
seal (or lift the funnel a moment) so the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} can escape past it;
then the water glugs down happily.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:air-around-us:10}
Yes! Tip the air-filled glass so its mouth is under the other glass's
mouth: the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} leaves as big silver bubbles that rise \emph{up} into
the water-filled glass and gather at its top, pushing the water out
downward. \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{Air} climbs and water falls because \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} always \omterm{def:g1:floating-and-sinking:words}{floats} up
through water --- pouring, upside down.
\end{solution}
