\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g2:measuring-length} --- Measuring Length}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:1}
Nearby: stand back to back. Far away: \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:measure}{measure} yourself in
\omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetres} and send the number --- a number can travel in a letter,
a back-to-back comparison cannot.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:2}
Neither counted wrong. Mara's steps are shorter, so more of them fit
in the same classroom. Different \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:measure}{units} give different numbers for the
same length.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:3}
The \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetre} is the same for everyone --- it belongs to nobody's
body. A measurement in \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetres} can be checked by any other
person with any ruler.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:4}
The ant: \unit{cm} (even less!); the door: \unit{m}; the schoolyard:
\unit{m}; the width of this book: \unit{cm}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:5}
No --- there is usually a small blank space between a ruler's edge
and its zero mark, so the pencil is a little shorter than $13$
\omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetres}. Tim forgot to put the \emph{zero mark}, not the edge, at
the pencil's end.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:6}
Answers vary; typical: hand about \qty{7}{cm} wide, spoon about
\qty{15}{cm}, mug about \qty{9}{cm}. The important part: each number
carries its \unit{cm}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:7}
$100 + 100 = 200$: the door is \qty{200}{cm} tall.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:8}
Answers vary; most beds are close to \qty{2}{m}. The point is to
compare the guess with the measured number.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:9}
The \qty{45}{cm} ribbon is longer: $45 - 38 = 7$, so by \qty{7}{cm}.
Without numbers, laying them side by side also finds the longer one
--- but ``\qty{45}{cm}'' fits in a letter, and ``side by side'' does
not.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:measuring-length:10}
Nobody else has Grandpa's legs: a small child pacing the same garden
might count $60$ little paces, and a visitor could not check ``$30$
paces'' without Grandpa there. Measured in \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} --- the same for
everyone --- the garden's length can be written down, sent, and
checked by anybody.
\end{solution}
