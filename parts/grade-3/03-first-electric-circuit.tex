\chapter{A First Electric Circuit}\label{ch:g3:first-electric-circuit}

Click --- the flashlight throws its beam across the dark room. Inside
that plastic tube live a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, a little \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} and two strips of
metal, playing a game with strict rules. Today we take the game
apart: with a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} and two wires, we will make \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} with
our own hands.

\section{A bulb, a battery, two wires}

\begin{definition}[The battery]\label{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}
A \emph{battery}\index{battery} is a small store of electricity. It
has two different ends, called its
\emph{terminals}\index{terminal}: one marked $+$ (plus), the other
marked $-$ (minus). Both ends matter --- electricity plays only when
the two of them join the game.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The bulb]\label{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}
A \emph{bulb}\index{bulb} is a little lamp with a thin thread inside
its glass that glows white-hot when electricity flows through it.
Look closely at its metal foot: the bulb also has \emph{two}
contact places --- the tip at the very bottom, and the metal side of
its foot. Two \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} on the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, two contacts on the bulb:
that is no accident.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[Lighting the bulb]\label{met:g3:first-electric-circuit:light}
With one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} and two wires:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item attach one wire from the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $+$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} to the tip
        of the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s foot;
  \item attach the other wire from the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $-$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} to the
        metal side of the foot;
  \item the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} --- you have built a circuit!
\end{enumerate}
If it stays dark, check the usual suspects: a wire touching the
glass instead of the metal contact, a loose connection, or a
worn-out \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}.
\end{method}

\section{The circuit must close}

\begin{definition}[Electric circuit]\label{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:circuit}
An \emph{electric circuit}\index{electric circuit} is a complete
loop for electricity: out of one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} of the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, through
the wires and the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, and back into the other \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}. When the
loop is complete we say the circuit is
\emph{closed}\index{closed circuit}; if it has a gap anywhere, it is
\emph{open}\index{open circuit}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The loop law]\label{prop:g3:first-electric-circuit:loop}
The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} only when the circuit is closed --- one unbroken
loop from one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}, through the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, back to the other
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}. One single gap, anywhere in the loop, and the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} goes
dark: electricity does not jump gaps, and it does not flow into
dead ends.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Why both ends?]\label{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:bothends}
Connect \emph{two} wires from the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $+$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} to the
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s two contacts, leaving $-$ alone: nothing. One wire from $+$
and one from $-$, but both to the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s tip: nothing again. The
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} only when the loop visits the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s two different
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} \emph{and} the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s two different contacts --- a full
round trip.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The switch]\label{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}
A \emph{switch}\index{switch} is a door in the circuit: a little
metal bridge that the click lays down (closed --- the loop is
complete, \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}!) or lifts open (a gap --- dark). The flashlight's
button, the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} switch on the wall, the button of a doorbell: all
of them are doors in a loop. You can build one from a clothes-peg
and a strip of kitchen foil folded over its jaws.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Tidy drawings]\label{rem:g3:first-electric-circuit:symbols}
Instead of drawing every \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} bump and \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} screw, electricians
use \emph{symbols}: two unequal bars for the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} (the long one
is $+$), a circle with a cross for the lamp, a little drawbridge for
the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}, and straight lines for the wires. A symbol drawing of a
circuit is quicker to draw, and anyone in the world can read it ---
we will use these drawings for many years.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Battery yes, wall socket never]\label{rem:g3:first-electric-circuit:safety}
Everything in this chapter is played with small round or flat
batteries: their push is tiny and safe for experiments. The
\emph{wall socket} is another world --- its push is hundreds of
times mightier, enough to hurt or kill. Never put wires, fingers or
anything else into a wall socket, and never take apart anything that
plugs into one. \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{Battery} games: yes. Socket: never.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:1}
Name the two \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} of a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} and the two contact places of a
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:2}
What is a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:circuit}{closed circuit}? What is an \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:circuit}{open circuit}? In which one does
the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:3}
Lea connects the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $+$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} to the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s tip with one
wire, and stops there. The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} stays dark. What is missing, in the
words of the loop law?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:4}
What job does a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} do in the loop? What lies inside a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}
when it is closed?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:5}
Draw, with the tidy symbols of
\cref{rem:g3:first-electric-circuit:symbols}, a circuit with one
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, one closed \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} and one lamp. Which symbol is which?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:6}
The flashlight goes dark in the middle of a camping trip. Give three
possible gaps or faults to check, from
\cref{met:g3:first-electric-circuit:light}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:7}
In the doorbell circuit, where is the ``\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}''? Why does the bell
ring only while the button is pressed?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:8}
Why must you never poke anything into a wall socket, when playing
with a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} is fine? (See
\cref{rem:g3:first-electric-circuit:safety}.)
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:9}
A \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} held with its tip on the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $+$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}, and one wire
from the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $-$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} pressed against the metal side of
the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s foot: will it \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}? Follow the loop with your finger to
decide.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:10}
Sam builds a loop: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, wire, \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, wire, back to the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}
--- but he attaches both wires to the same \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}, $-$, ``because
it was nearer''. The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} stays dark although the loop looks
complete. Explain what his loop is missing, using
\cref{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:bothends}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:11}
A string of party \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} has one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} missing, and the \emph{whole}
string is dark, though all the other \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} are fine. What does this
tell you about how the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} are arranged in the loop? (Next year's
circuits chapter will give this arrangement a name.)
\end{exercise}
