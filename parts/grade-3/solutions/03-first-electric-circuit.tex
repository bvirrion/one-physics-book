\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g3:first-electric-circuit} --- A First Electric Circuit}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:1}
The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals}: $+$ (plus) and $-$ (minus). The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s
contacts: the tip at the bottom of the foot, and the metal side of
the foot.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:2}
Closed: one unbroken loop from \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} to \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}. Open: a loop
with a gap somewhere. The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} only in the closed one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:3}
The return trip. One wire is only half a loop: the electricity must
go out of one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}, through the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, and \emph{back to the
other \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}} --- Lea needs a second wire from the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s side
contact to the $-$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:4}
The \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} is a door in the loop: closed, it completes the circuit;
open, it makes a gap. Inside a closed \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} a little metal bridge
joins the two wires.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:5}
A loop of straight lines joining: two unequal bars (the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, the
long bar being $+$), the drawbridge symbol lying closed (the
\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}), and a circle with a cross (the lamp).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:6}
A worn-out \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}; a loose connection (wire or \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} not screwed
tight, spring bent); a broken \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} --- its glowing thread snapped,
making a gap inside the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:7}
The button \emph{is} the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}. Pressing it lays the metal bridge
and closes the loop, so the bell rings; releasing it lifts the
bridge, the gap opens, and the ringing stops.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:8}
The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s push is tiny and safe; the wall socket's push is
hundreds of times mightier and can hurt or kill. Sockets are not for
experiments --- ever.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:9}
Yes, it \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights}. Follow the loop: $+$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}, into the tip, through
the glowing thread, out by the side contact, along the wire, back to
$-$. A complete round trip visiting both \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} and both
contacts.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:10}
His loop never visits the second \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}. A circuit must go from
one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} \emph{through the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} to the other} \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}: out of
$-$ and back into $-$ is a round trip to nowhere --- the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s
push needs both of its different ends in the game.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:first-electric-circuit:11}
The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} must all sit on one single loop, one after the other: the
missing \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} leaves a gap, and one gap darkens the whole string ---
the loop law. (Next year this one-after-the-other arrangement gets
its name: a series circuit.)
\end{solution}
