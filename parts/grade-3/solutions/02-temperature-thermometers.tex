\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g3:temperature-thermometers} --- Temperature and Thermometers}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:1}
The \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{thermometer} gives the same number for the same warmth, whoever
reads it, whenever. Hands were fooled by the three bowls (the same
lukewarm water felt warm and cool at once) and by metal feeling
colder than wood in the same shade.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:2}
Water \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{freezes} at \qty{0}{\celsius} and boils at \qty{100}{\celsius}
--- both in \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{degrees Celsius}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:3}
Warmed, the trapped \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid} swells and the column climbs; cooled, it
shrinks and the column \omterm{def:g1:floating-and-sinking:words}{sinks}. The tube is so thin that even a tiny
swelling pushes the column up visibly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:4}
Reading too soon (before the column has stopped moving), and reading
from an angle instead of with the eye level with the column's top.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:5}
Boiling water: \qty{100}{\celsius}. Cozy living room:
\qty{20}{\celsius}. Refrigerator: \qty{4}{\celsius}. Your body:
\qty{37}{\celsius}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:6}
The pot is hotter, by $100 - 35 = 65$ degrees --- far too hot to
touch: it is at boiling \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{temperature}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:7}
Minus three \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{degrees Celsius}, written \qty{-3}{\celsius}. It is
\emph{warmer} than \qty{-8}{\celsius}: eight marks below zero is
deeper into the \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{freeze} than three.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:8}
$14 - 6 = 8$: the morning warmed up by $8$ degrees.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:9}
The night went below \qty{0}{\celsius} --- water's freezing
milestone. Frozen puddles are a \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{thermometer} made of weather.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:10}
No --- \qty{16}{\celsius} is on the cool side of pleasant. Emma's
skin compares the hallway with the freezing garden she just left, so
it feels toasty to her. The family should trust the \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{thermometer}: it
compares with the fixed marks of its scale, not with where it has
just been.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:temperature-thermometers:11}
Boiling water stays at \qty{100}{\celsius} no matter how long it
boils --- the milestone is a ceiling: extra heating makes more
steam, not hotter water. That is exactly why \qty{100}{\celsius}
could be chosen as a reliable milestone in the first place.
\end{solution}
