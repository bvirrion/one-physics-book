\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g3:sound-around-us} --- Sound Around Us}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:1}
A very fast back-and-forth trembling. Guitar: the plucked string.
Drum: the struck skin. Voice: the two little folds in the throat.
Fly: its beating wings.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:2}
That a sounding skin is a trembling skin: the grains jump because
the skin under them moves rapidly up and down --- even when only a
shout, not a touch, sets it going.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:3}
The grip stops the cymbal's trembling, and no \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:vibration}{vibration} means no
\omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} --- the law ``no \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:vibration}{vibration}, no \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}''.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:4}
Pluck harder: the band swings wider --- a wider blur --- and the
twang is louder. Bigger trembling, bigger \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:5}
The short pinched band; the little flute; the thinnest string.
Short, small, thin things tremble faster and sing higher.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:6}
They feel a buzzing --- the throat's folds vibrating. The buzzing
stops the instant the humming stops: no \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:vibration}{vibration}, no \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:7}
The clapping hands set the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} trembling; the trembling spreads
through the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} like pond rings; it reaches the ear and sets the
ear's little skin trembling --- and the clap is heard.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:8}
Very loud \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sounds} shake the ear's tiny skin --- the ear's own
rice-grain drum, thinner than paper --- hard enough to damage it for
good; it never grows back.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:9}
The short teeth play the high notes, the long teeth the low ones:
short things tremble faster and sing higher.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:10}
The \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} inside the bottle is the trembler --- your breath sets it
shivering. Adding water leaves a \emph{shorter} column of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} above
the water, and short sings high: that is why the hoot rises. If the
glass itself were the singer, adding water would hardly change the
note.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sound-around-us:11}
\omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{Sound} needs a \emph{\omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:vibration}{vibration}}: a to-and-fro trembling fast enough
to shake the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, like the drum skin that made rice dance. The
pictures on a muted screen change, but nothing there trembles the
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} --- changing colors shake nothing, so there is nothing for an
ear to catch.
\end{solution}
