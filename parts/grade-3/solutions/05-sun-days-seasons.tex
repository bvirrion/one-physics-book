\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g3:sun-days-seasons} --- The Sun in the Sky: Days and Seasons}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:1}
Highest and longest in summer --- making long days; lowest and
shortest in winter --- making short days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:2}
At midday, when the Sun stands highest. The midday \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} also
points in the same direction every day of the year --- nature's most
reliable arrow.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:3}
In June the Sun travels its high road and stands nearly overhead at
midday, so the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} is a short stump. In December the Sun stays on
its low road; even at midday its \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} comes slanting, and the same
post throws a long \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:4}
The Sun sweeps the stick's \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} around the hour marks as it
travels its arc; you read the time from the mark the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} touches.
The moving hand is the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:5}
If a wall's shade swallows the stick at some hour, that hour can
never be marked or read. Under clouds the clock has no hand ---
no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} --- and at night, none either: a \omterm{def:g3:sun-days-seasons:sundial}{sundial} only works while
the Sun does.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:6}
One year --- about $365$ days. Four \omterm{def:g3:sun-days-seasons:year}{seasons} per lap: spring, summer,
autumn, winter.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:7}
About $365$ days in a year; $24$ \omterm{ex:g2:measuring-time:clock}{hours} in a day. The day is one
\emph{spin} of the Earth on itself; the year is one \emph{lap} of
the Earth around the Sun.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:8}
For example: how early the streetlamps come on (dark before dinner
means winter); how high the midday Sun stands; how long the midday
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} are; whether you can play outside in daylight after dinner.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:9}
It is summer for them, at the same moment. If the whole Earth were
farther from the Sun, \emph{everyone} would have winter at once ---
so the guess must be wrong. The real cause is the low road and its
slanted \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:10}
The stick has not moved --- the Sun's road has. From March to June
the arc climbed higher and the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} shrank and shifted, so the
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} now misses the pebbles laid down for March's road. A \omterm{def:g3:sun-days-seasons:sundial}{sundial}
must be read with its \omterm{def:g3:sun-days-seasons:year}{season}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:sun-days-seasons:11}
The Sun at its highest means she woke at midday. If only one hour
later the tent's \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} is already longer than the tent, the Sun
must be hanging very low even near midday --- the low winter road:
she is closer to midwinter.
\end{solution}
