\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g6:light-propagation} --- Rectilinear Propagation of Light}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:1}
In clear \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} (or any clear, well-mixed material) \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} travels in
straight lines. Rectilinear: straight-lined; propagation: the
traveling of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} from place to place.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:2}
Diverging: rays spreading from a point (a candle, a bare \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}).
Parallel: rays side by side (sunlight --- the Sun is so far that
its rays reach us marching together). Converging: rays closing
toward a point (\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} gathered by a magnifying instrument).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:3}
A ray of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} plays the ruler: eye, edge and far end are judged
aligned exactly when \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} from the far end reaches the eye
grazing the whole edge --- \omterm{prop:g6:light-propagation:law}{rectilinear propagation} is the oath.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:4}
The landing points of the rays that just graze the blocker's
edges. An eye inside the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} cone cannot see the lamp --- no
straight ray from the lamp reaches it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:5}
Halfway: the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} is twice the ball --- \qty{12}{cm}. Close to
the screen: the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} shrinks toward the ball's true
\qty{6}{cm}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:6}
At the screen's bottom: a straight line from a high point through
the low gate must keep going downward.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:7}
Upside down, and left-right swapped: on the screen the hand waves
low instead of high, and on the opposite side. Both reversals come
from the threads crossing at the gate.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:8}
Astronomers: watching eclipses safely on the screen instead of the
sky. Painters: tracing the projected scene to master perspective.
(Modern kin: every camera, and the eye itself.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:9}
The \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} shrinks toward the coin's true size: with the lamp
farther away, the grazing rays leave it less steeply --- a
narrower fan --- and land closer together. The construction shows
it: flatter lines, tighter spread.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:10}
The Sun is compact and far: one tight fan of near-parallel rays,
one crisp boundary --- sharp \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows}. A gray cloud layer is a lamp
the size of the whole sky: \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} arrives from every direction at
once, every point of ground is reached by some of it, and \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows}
wash away to almost nothing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:11}
Brighter: a wide gate admits more threads from every scene-point.
Blurred: through a wide gate, each scene-point's threads reach a
whole \emph{patch} of screen instead of one spot --- countless
overlapping patches smear the picture. The pinhole's sharpness
\emph{is} its smallness.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:light-propagation:12}
Each gap between leaves is a natural pinhole; each bright patch on
the ground is that pinhole's \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} of the Sun --- round on an
ordinary day because the Sun is round. When the eclipse turns the
Sun into a \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescent}, every leaf-gap faithfully projects the
\omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescent}: hundreds of little cameras, one celestial subject.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g6:light-propagation:1}
\textbf{1.} Against the screen, the grazing rays have no distance
left to spread: the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} hugs the puppet's own outline.
\textbf{2.} Halfway doubles it: \qty{40}{cm}.
\textbf{3.} Four times $20$ is exactly \qty{80}{cm} --- it
matches; the puppet stands at one quarter of the lamp--screen
distance.
\textbf{4.} A broad glowing globe is many lamps at once: each
part throws its own slightly shifted \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}, and the dragon's
edges smear soft and gray. Crisp theater needs a small, bare,
point-like source.
\textbf{5.} Toward the lamp: the fan of grazing rays widens and
the wolf grows; the arm stays low, out of the fan. As the puppet
nears the small-but-real lamp, edge softening grows a little ---
the lamp's width matters more, close up.
\textbf{6.} The straight lines from the lamp through the two
puppets must run to separate regions of the screen --- the
puppets stand far enough apart sideways that neither sits in the
other's \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} cone.
\textbf{7.} The hand's: closer to the lamp, at a third of the
way, its grazing rays fan three times wide, while the mid-stage
dragon's only double --- near the lamp, even small hands play
giants.
\textbf{8.} Move the villain flat against the screen and it
shrinks to its plain self (or out of the \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam} entirely); or slip
a second blocker close to the lamp --- its giant \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} swallows
the villain's, and the villain ``vanishes'' inside another
darkness.
\textbf{9.} Low on the sheet: from the high lamp, the straight
thread through the hole continues downward --- the pinhole
inverts.
\textbf{10.} From right to left: the gate swaps left and right
along with up and down.
\textbf{11.} Brighter but hopelessly blurred: each point of the
street now paints a coin-sized patch, the patches overlap, and
the picture dissolves --- the finale dies with the sharpness.
\textbf{12.} ``Tonight's entire program --- every \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}, every
giant, and one upside-down street --- was performed by a single
law: \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} travels in straight lines.''
\end{solution}
