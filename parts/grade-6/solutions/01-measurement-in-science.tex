\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:2}
\qty{320}{cm}; \ \qty{0.75}{kg}; \ \qty{240}{s}; \ \qty{5.6}{km}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:3}
Its steps go by $60$, not by $10$, so the decimal staircase lies:
$\qty{3.5}{min} = 3 \times 60 + 30 = \qty{210}{s}$, while the
staircase would have wrongly given \qty{350}{s}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:4}
Each step: $200 \div 5 = \qty{40}{mL}$. Two steps above $300$:
$300 + 80 = \qty{380}{mL}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:5}
Yeast: the kitchen scale (\omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{gram} steps --- the bathroom scale would
not notice a pinch). Heartbeats: a stopwatch (second steps and
finer). Page width: a ruler (millimetre steps). Schoolyard: a long
tape \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measure} or surveyor's wheel (metre-scale range).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:6}
The judging step: a pencil is estimated at under \qty{20}{cm}, so
\qty{17.4}{m} fails the sanity check at once. She read \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetres}
and wrote \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres}: the true value is \qty{17.4}{cm}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:7}
Honest: ``between $18$ and \qty{19}{\celsius}, nearer $19$'' (or
``about \qty{18.8}{\celsius}''). Dishonest: ``\qty{18.7643}
{\celsius}'' --- precision the marks cannot promise.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:8}
No one is at fault: fingers start and stop a hair apart, and small
scatter is measurement's normal breath. Wise report: the readings
huddle around \qty{6.2}{s} --- ``about \qty{6.2}{s}''.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:9}
A quarter of \qty{450}{g}: about \qty{112}{g} (\qty{113}{g} is
fine). The errand shows why shared \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{units} matter: recipes written in
one country's \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{units} must be translated before another country's
instruments can obey them.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:10}
The stamp spans from the $1$ mark to the $3.4$ mark: $3.4 - 1 =
\qty{2.4}{cm}$. The habit: never trust an instrument's edge ---
start from a chosen mark and subtract, and worn rulers lose their
power.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:11}
The two readings scatter by \qty{0.10}{m}, so the last digit of
either is not to be trusted; they huddle around $18.3$. Writing
``\qty{18.3}{m}'' keeps every digit the tape can honestly promise
and no more.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:measurement-in-science:12}
Disasters: no two towns' ``feet'' agree, so beams cut in one town
never fit houses in another; and when the son inherits, the \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit}
itself changes length --- every measurement in the kingdom expires
with the king. The metal bar fixed both: one unchanging length,
copied for all towns, outliving all kings. Its own flaw: a single
object can be scratched, stolen or slowly altered, and the world
had to trek to its vault to check copies --- until \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{units} were
rebuilt on nature's constants, which cannot wear out and are on
duty everywhere at once.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g6:measurement-in-science:1}
\textbf{1.} Every weighing adds the load to whatever the needle
already showed; a non-zero start poisons all the day's readings ---
the fair-start check must come first.
\textbf{2.} $500 + 200 = \qty{700}{g}$.
\textbf{3.} It reads \qty{20}{g} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}. Pouring flour until the
needle says \qty{700}{g}, the baker has really poured
\qty{720}{g}: the scale makes the baker hand over more goods than
the customer pays for --- it flatters the customers, and the baker
loses on every sale.
\textbf{4.} $500 + 200 + 50 = \qty{750}{g}$.
\textbf{5.} No: a \qty{2}{g} stamp vanishes below the scale's
\qty{5}{g} steps --- outside its territory.
\textbf{6.} $200 \div 4 = \qty{50}{mL}$ per step.
\textbf{7.} Water at $600 + 50 = \qty{650}{mL}$; ``about
\qty{700}{mL}'' rounds away a full step --- too vague for an
instrument this good.
\textbf{8.} Eye level with the mark: reading from below shifts the
apparent water line --- the squarely-in-front rule was broken.
\textbf{9.} Off by \qty{10}{mL} --- better (smaller) than its own
\qty{50}{mL} step: acceptable for a school jug.
\textbf{10.} Fast, by $2$ minutes.
\textbf{11.} $2 \times 7 = 14$ minutes ahead after a week.
\textbf{12.} First clock: set back $2$ min; second: forward $2$
min; third: nothing --- it is right. (Averaging the three would
``fix'' nothing and falsify the one honest clock.)
\textbf{13.} For example: \emph{Zero}: check the empty instrument
reads zero before trusting anything it says. \emph{Reading}: learn
the step's worth and read squarely in front, between marks with
honest modesty. \emph{\omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{Units}}: a number without its \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit} is not a
measurement --- write the \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit}, always.
\end{solution}
