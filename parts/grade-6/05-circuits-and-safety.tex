\chapter{Electric Circuits and Safety}\label{ch:g6:circuits-and-safety}

Three years of loops, \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} and \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches} have made you a builder of
circuits. This year you become something rarer: a \emph{writer} of
circuits. Electricians in every country read and draw the same
wordless language --- and its grammar, plus a chapter of hard-won
safety wisdom, is what stands between clever wiring and burnt
fingers.

\section{The wordless language}

\begin{definition}[Circuit diagram]\label{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:diagram}
A \emph{circuit diagram}\index{circuit diagram} is the standard
drawing of a circuit: each device is shown by its agreed
\emph{symbol}\index{symbol}, and the wires by straight lines drawn
with square corners. A diagram shows how the circuit is
\emph{connected} --- who feeds whom, what forks where --- and
deliberately ignores how the real wires snake across the table.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[From table to paper, and back]\label{met:g6:circuits-and-safety:translate}
To \emph{draw} a built circuit:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item list the devices, and pick each one's \omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:diagram}{symbol};
  \item follow the real loop with a finger from the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $+$,
        noting the order of devices and every fork;
  \item redraw as a tidy rectangle: \omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:diagram}{symbols} spaced out, wires
        straight, corners square --- same connections, no snakes.
\end{enumerate}
To \emph{build} from a diagram, run the translation backward ---
and afterward, check as always: every loop traced from $+$ must
reach $-$ through at least one device.
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Same circuit, two portraits]\label{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:portraits}
On the table: a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, a tangle of alligator-clip wires looping
over a pencil case, a lamp leaning on a book, a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} dangling.
On paper: a calm rectangle --- \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} on the left, switch on top,
lamp on the right. The tangle and the rectangle are the \emph{same
circuit}: every connection identical. To decide whether two
circuits are the same, ask one question only: who is connected to
whom?
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Motors and buzzers join the game]\label{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}
Two newcomers to your kit behave just like lamps in a diagram: the
\emph{motor}\index{motor} (\omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:diagram}{symbol}: M in a circle) spins whenever
current flows through it --- fans, toy cars, drills; the
\emph{buzzer}\index{buzzer} (a struck circle) \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sounds} whenever fed
--- doorbells, alarms. Series and parallel rules apply to them
unchanged: a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series} commands them; parallel \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches}
make them independent.
\end{example}

\section{Old rules in the new language}

\begin{example}[Reading a diagram like a pro]\label{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:reading}
Given any diagram, interrogate it with the finger-walk of the
parallel-circuits chapter: from $+$, walk every road to $-$. All
devices on one road: series --- one gap stops all, order free,
brightness shared. Roads that fork at \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{junction} dots: parallel ---
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches} independent, full brightness each, \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} placement now a
real decision. The language changed; the laws did not.
\end{example}

\section{The chapter of scars}

Electricity's safety rules were paid for in fires and worse. Here
they are, with their reasons --- a professional knows both.

\begin{definition}[Short circuit]\label{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:short}
A \emph{short circuit}\index{short circuit} happens when a bare
conducting road connects a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s (or socket's) two sides
\emph{directly}, skipping every device. With nothing useful in its
way, the current rushes wildly --- wires \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat}, insulation \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{melts},
batteries are ruined, and mains wiring can catch fire. Next year's
circuits chapter studies the villain closely; this year, learn its
face and keep it out of your builds.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The house's bodyguards]\label{rem:g6:circuits-and-safety:guards}
Look inside the fuse box at home: rows of small \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switches} ---
\emph{circuit breakers} --- one per \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} of the house's great
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{parallel circuit}. Each stands guard over its \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}: if a fault
lets the current rush --- a \omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:short}{short circuit}, an overloaded socket
--- the guard snaps itself off in a blink, cutting the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}
before wires overheat. When a breaker ``trips'', it is not
misbehaving: it has just done its one job. Find the fault before
resetting it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The rules, with reasons]\label{rem:g6:circuits-and-safety:rules}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{Battery} experiments only; the socket, never.} The
        mains pushes hundreds of times harder --- through damp
        skin it can stop a heart.
  \item \emph{No bare wire straight across a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}.} That is a
        \omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:short}{short circuit}: ruined cells, hot wire, burnt fingers.
  \item \emph{Water and mains electricity never share a room's
        reach:} no gadgets by the bath, no wet hands on plugs ---
        damp skin is a \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductor}.
  \item \emph{Never poke anything into a socket.} The guard in
        the fuse box is fast, but you must never bet on it.
  \item \emph{Damaged cords go to the bin,} not the drawer:
        cracked insulation is a fence with a gap.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:1}
What does a \omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:diagram}{circuit diagram} show faithfully, and what does it
deliberately ignore?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:2}
Draw from memory the \omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:diagram}{symbols}: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}; \ lamp; \ open \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}; \
closed \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}; \ \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor}; \ \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{buzzer}. Which mark tells the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s
$+$ side?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:3}
Draw the diagram of a doorbell: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{push-switch} and \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{buzzer} in
one loop. Why does the bell stop when the finger lifts?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:4}
Two circuits are built from the same box of parts: are they
``the same circuit''? What single question decides?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:5}
In the chapter's lamp-and-motor diagram, what happens to the fan
if the lamp burns out? And to both if the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} opens? Which old
rules answer?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:6}
What is a \omterm{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:short}{short circuit}, and why does it \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat} wires? Name two of
its victims.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:7}
A circuit breaker in the fuse box has tripped. What has it just
done for the house? What should be done before resetting it?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:8}
Give the reason behind each rule: no gadgets near the bath; \
damaged cords to the bin; \ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} games yes, socket never.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:9}
Translate to a diagram (in words or drawing): a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}; from its
$+$, a wire to a closed \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}; from the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} a fork --- one
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} through a \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{buzzer}, one through a lamp; \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches} rejoin and
return to $-$. Predict what is heard and seen, and what changes if
the \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{buzzer}'s \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} alone gets its own extra \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}, opened.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:10}
A toy fan is wired: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} $+$ to \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor}, \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor} to \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} $-$,
and --- ``for solidity'' --- one extra bare wire directly from
$+$ to $-$. The fan barely turns and the extra wire grows hot.
Diagnose with \cref{def:g6:circuits-and-safety:short}: what road
is the current preferring, and why is the build dangerous?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:11}
Grandpa's workshop trick: before touching any \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} fixture, he
switches off its wall \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} \emph{and} the whole \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}'s
breaker in the fuse box. Using series thinking, explain what each
cut achieves --- and why the careful man makes both.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:g6:circuits-and-safety:12}
Design the wiring of a puppet theater: one master \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} that
darkens everything; two stage lamps that must fail independently;
a curtain \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor} and a showtime \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{buzzer}, each with its own control
\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}. Describe (or draw) the diagram --- which devices ride
common road, which ride \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches}, where each \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} sits --- and
check your design against every rule of series and parallel you
own.
\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: Wiring the Garden Shed}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- a family wires its garden
shed; diagrams before screwdrivers; the inspector's
checklist}]\label{pb:g6:circuits-and-safety:1}
The family plans the shed's little battery-powered network on
paper first --- as professionals do.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The plan on paper.}
The wish list: one ceiling lamp switched at the door; a workbench
lamp with its own \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}; a small ventilation fan (\omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor}) with its
own \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Why must the three devices ride parallel \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches} rather
        than one series loop? Give two comforts that would break
        \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series}.
  \item Each \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} carries its own \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}. What does each \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}
        command, and what does none of them command?
  \item Where must a master \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} sit to darken and silence the
        whole shed at once?
  \item Draw (or describe precisely) the full diagram: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery},
        master \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}, three \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches}, four \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switches} in all.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The build, inspected.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Built and switched on, the ceiling lamp shines --- but
        dimly, and the workbench lamp shines only when the
        \emph{fan's} \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} is closed. The finger-walk finds the
        bench lamp and fan sharing one \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}, one behind the
        other. Name their arrangement, and explain both symptoms
        with the old rules.
  \item Give the one-wire repair that restores the plan.
  \item During tidying, a screwdriver briefly lies across the
        \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s two bare \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} and grows warm. Name the
        event, and state what the family should check about the
        \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} afterward.
  \item The inspector-parent adds a rule: ``every joint wrapped,
        no bare copper anywhere.'' Which two dangers is that one
        rule guarding against?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The night of the storm.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item A rainy night: the shed roof leaks onto the workbench.
        Why must the family not touch the wiring with wet hands,
        even in a battery-powered shed --- and why would the same
        touch be far graver in the mains-powered house?
  \item The house's own \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} flicker and one breaker trips.
        What has the breaker detected, and what is the wise order
        of actions?
  \item Morning: the family debates adding a doorbell --- \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{buzzer}
        at the shed, \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{push-switch} at the house, long twin wire
        between. Series or parallel with the shed's network?
        Where does its current come from? Sketch the loop.
  \item Write the family's three-line safety charter for the
        shed, one line each: sockets, water, bare wires.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
