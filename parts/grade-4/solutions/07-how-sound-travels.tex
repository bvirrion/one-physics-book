\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g4:how-sound-travels} --- How Sound Travels}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:1}
Carriers: \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, water, \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:solid}{solids} (wood, brick, steel, a taut string).
No carrier at all: empty space --- like the airless \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}, or the
pumped-out jar.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:2}
The \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} --- the \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}'s carrier --- was pumped away. The trembling
bell had nothing left to tremble, proving that \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} travels only
through stuff, never through nothing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:3}
No carrier, no \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}: the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} has no \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, so a shout's trembling
has nothing to travel through --- it dies at the lips.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:4}
The taut string carries it: voice shakes the cup, the cup shakes
the string, the string replays it in the far cup. Rules: keep the
string stretched tight, and let it touch nothing (no sagging, no
pinching fingers).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:5}
About \qty{340}{m} in one second --- roughly a kilometre in three.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:6}
$9 \div 3 = 3$: about \qty{3}{km} away. Three seconds later means
about \qty{1}{km}: the storm has come much closer --- head indoors.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:7}
The wood is a far better carrier than \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}: the faint scratch is too
weak for the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} to deliver across the table, but the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:solid}{solid} tabletop
carries the trembling loud and crisp straight into your ear.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:8}
An \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo} is your own \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} bounced off a large hard wall and
returning late enough to hear separately. A farther cliff means a
longer round trip for the \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}, so the answer comes later.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:9}
The ship clicks downward and times the \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo} from the sea floor:
quick \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo}, shallow water; late \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo}, deep water. Dolphins do it in
dark water, bats in pitch-black caves.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:10}
First, steel carries \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} far better than \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, so the rumble
survives the long trip. Second, the rail guides the \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} straight
along one road to the scout's ear, while in the open \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} the
train's noise spreads out in every direction like pond rings,
thinning with every \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metre} --- so the rail's message arrives both
stronger and clearer, long before the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}'s.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:how-sound-travels:11}
In one second \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} covers about \qty{340}{m} --- but that second
paid for going \emph{and} coming back. The wall stands at half:
about \qty{170}{m} away.
\end{solution}
