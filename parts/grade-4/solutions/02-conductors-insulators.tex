\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g4:conductors-insulators} --- Conductors and Insulators}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:1}
A \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductor} lets electricity travel through it; an \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:insulator}{insulator} blocks
it. The \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductor} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} --- it closes the loop.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:2}
\omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{Conductors}: the steel spoon, the kitchen foil, the copper coin.
\omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:insulator}{Insulators}: the cork, the glass, the plastic brick.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:3}
Touch the two free ends directly together: the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} must \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}. If
the tester itself is faulty, every object would seem to be an
\omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:insulator}{insulator} --- the check protects against blaming the innocent.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:4}
Either the object is an \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:insulator}{insulator} --- or the testing was sloppy: a
wire end touching paint, varnish or dirt instead of pressing firmly
on the object's bare stuff (or a connection came loose).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:5}
The copper conducts: it is the current's road to the lamp. The
plastic insulates: it keeps the current in the wire and fingers
safely out.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:6}
The little metal bridge conducts --- it must close the loop when
the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} is on. The case insulates --- fingers press it all day.
Without the bridge, no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}; without the case, no safety.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:7}
Yes --- aluminium is a metal, and all metals conduct. The warning:
magnetic and conducting are different talents; the \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet}'s
iron-only rule says nothing about whether it conducts.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:8}
The wood (and paint) is an \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:insulator}{insulator}, but the graphite lead inside
is a \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductor} --- a rare non-metal \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductor},
running hidden through the pencil from tip to tip.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:9}
The \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:insulator}{insulator} is protecting the human: if the tool's metal
ever touches something electric, the thick plastic handle keeps the
current from crossing into the hand.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:10}
Current must travel the whole road. In the chain, metal link touches
metal link: a complete metal road. In the beaded necklace, between
one metal bead and the next lies a stretch of cotton knot --- an
\omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:insulator}{insulator} --- so the road is broken at every bead: dark, despite all
those \omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductors}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:conductors-insulators:11}
Damp skin conducts much better than dry skin, so wet hands offer
the current an easy road through the body. The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} tester is
safe because a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s push is tiny; the wall socket's push is
hundreds of times mightier --- through damp skin it can be deadly,
which is why the rules allow \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} games and forbid sockets,
always.
\end{solution}
