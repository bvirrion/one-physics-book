\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g4:magnets-and-compass} --- Magnets and the Compass}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:1}
The \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} are the \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet}'s two strong ends: the \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{north pole} (N,
often red) and the \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{south pole} (S). Paperclips cluster at the \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles}
--- the ends --- not the middle.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:2}
Opposite \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{attract}; like \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} repel. A snap means opposite
\omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} were facing: an N toward an S.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:3}
N toward N: they push apart (like \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} repel). N toward S: they
pull together and snap.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:4}
Because a giant \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} is pulling its \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} into line: the Earth
itself is a \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet}, with magnetic \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} near the top and bottom of
the globe.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:5}
North, East, South, West. Facing north, East is on your right.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:6}
The needle is a tiny \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} and obeys the \emph{nearest} strong
pull. A \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} or big iron \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} nearby out-pulls the distant
\omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{Earth-magnet}, and the needle lies about north.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:7}
Stroke the needle with one \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{pole} of a \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet}, twenty times in the
same direction, lifting the \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} between strokes --- it becomes a
little \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet}. Lay it on a cork slice floating in a bowl of water:
the floating cork is a \omterm{def:g3:levers-and-scales:lever}{pivot} made of water, and the needle swings
to point north--south.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:8}
It points north. A \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:compass}{compass} whose needle agrees with the midday
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} is telling the truth --- and on a sunny day the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} alone
can serve as a rough \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:compass}{compass}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:9}
It works in fog, under clouds, on moonless nights and far from any
coast --- the needle asks nothing of the sky. Sailors could finally
hold a course across open oceans in any weather.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:10}
The friend's iron belt buckle out-pulls the \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{Earth-magnet} at close
range: the needle points at the buckle, not north. Trusting it, she
would drift --- the false ``north'' lies toward her left, so she
would bend her path leftward, to the west. The fix: read the
\omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:compass}{compass} away from the buckle (a few steps apart), then walk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:magnets-and-compass:11}
The short stroked needle has \emph{two} \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} --- its ends behave
like N and S (\omterm{def:g1:floating-and-sinking:words}{float} two such needles and they snap end to end,
opposite ends attracting). So shortening a \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} does not remove a
\omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{pole}, and cutting one in half just makes two complete short
\omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnets}: the hunt for a lone \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{pole} by cutting never ends --- each
cut hands you both \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:poles}{poles} again.
\end{solution}
