\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g4:weight-and-mass} --- Weight and Mass}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:1}
\omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{Mass}: how much stuff? \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{Weight}: how hard is it pulled down here?
\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{Weight} is the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} --- the Earth's downward pull.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:2}
Straight down, always. The \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:springscale}{spring scale} --- \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} stretches its
spring, and the pointer reports the stretch.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:3}
About four finger-widths --- double the stuff, double the pull,
double the stretch: the proposition ``more stuff, stronger pull''.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:4}
The stretch must be measured \emph{from the empty position}: the
band already hangs somewhat under the cup's own \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}, and only
the extra sinking below the zero mark \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:measure}{measures} the load.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:5}
Yes --- \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} travels unchanged: still \qty{20}{kg} of stuff. But it
is far easier to hold up: the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} pulls it about six times more
weakly, so its \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} is about a sixth of the Earth value.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:6}
Their legs push as hard as Earth-trained legs always do, but the
\omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s pull-back is six times weaker --- so every ordinary step
turns into a leap.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:7}
The \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:springscale}{spring scale}: it feels the local pull, and reads six times
less. The \omterm{def:g3:levers-and-scales:scale}{balance scale} compares the pack's pull with the blocks'
pull, and the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} weakens both alike --- the pans still tie at the
same \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:8}
True. \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{Weight} is the Earth's pull on a thing's stuff, and the pull
grows with the amount of stuff --- no stuff, nothing to pull:
\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} zero everywhere.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:9}
The spring inside feels \emph{\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}} --- the apples' pull on the
pan. On Earth the pull and the stuff travel strictly together
(more pull exactly when more stuff), so a scale marked in \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilograms}
of stuff still tells the truth --- here.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:10}
\omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{Mass} measurements travel unchanged: they describe the stuff itself,
not the place. Any weighing done by \emph{comparison} --- the
\omterm{def:g3:levers-and-scales:scale}{balance scale} against reference blocks --- reports \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} and remains
exactly right near the far planet. Only spring-style readings of
\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} will shrink out there, and those were never what the parts
list recorded.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:weight-and-mass:11}
The \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} is all there. Clues: shaking the wrench still takes real
effort --- her hand feels the stuff resisting the back-and-forth;
and thrown, it flies off no faster than her muscles can manage ---
a hammer of stuff is still a hammer to throw. \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{Weight} is gone;
stuff, never.
\end{solution}
