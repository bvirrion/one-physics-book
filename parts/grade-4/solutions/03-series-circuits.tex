\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g4:series-circuits} --- Series Circuits}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:1}
Players sit on one single loop, one after another, with no
branching --- like beads threaded on one necklace.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:2}
All or nothing (one gap stops everyone); order does not matter
(swapping players changes nothing); sharing (more \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} on the loop
means each glows dimmer).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:3}
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{Bulb} 1 goes dark too. The empty socket is a gap in the single loop,
and rule one --- all or nothing --- stops every player at once.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:4}
Nothing changes: the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} still commands both \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} exactly as
before. Rule two --- order on the loop does not matter.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:5}
Two \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs}: each noticeably dimmer than the single \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} was. Three:
dimmer still. The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s push is being shared along the whole
necklace.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:6}
Nose to tail: the $+$ of one touching the $-$ of the next, so both
push the same way around the loop. Drawing: $-\,[{+}\;{-}]\,[{+}\;
{-}]\,+$ queued in one line.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:7}
From $+$: through the case's metal strip to the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{button-switch},
through the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} to the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, through the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s glowing thread,
then back along the tube --- through the second \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, nose to
tail --- to the first \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $-$. One loop, no branching.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:8}
Anywhere on the same loop --- for instance right after the first
button. \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{In series}, both buttons must be closed to complete the one
road, so the bell rings only when both are pressed together.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:9}
Replace the first \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} with the known-good one; if the string
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights}, the removed \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} was the culprit. If not, put the original
back and move to the second socket, and so on down the string. At
some socket the string will \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} --- the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} just removed is the
broken bead.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:10}
In the dark, Tom slid one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} in backward. Nose to nose, the two
batteries' pushes fight instead of queuing, and the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} gets
nothing --- a full-strength tie is still a tie. Flipping one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}
restores the nose-to-tail queue and the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:series-circuits:11}
She is right: on a single loop, a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} anywhere commands
everything --- order does not matter. With branching roads, the
current could detour around her \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} through another branch, and
the motor might keep running; branched circuits need more careful
\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} placement (they are next year's parallel circuits).
\end{solution}
