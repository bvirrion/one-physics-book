\chapter{Gravitation}\label{ch:g9:gravitation}

An apple lets go of its \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} and falls. The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}, above the
same orchard, does not. For thousands of years these seemed two
unrelated facts --- until one insight of genius joined them:
the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} \emph{is} falling, endlessly, around the Earth, held
by the very pull that claims the apple. One law for orchard
and sky --- the boldest equation ever written to that day, and
your final year of this book opens with it.

\section{The universal attraction}

\begin{definition}[Gravitation]\label{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}
\emph{Gravitation}\index{gravitation} is the mutual attraction
between all masses: every portion of matter in the universe
pulls on every other. It is the no-touch \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} you met as
``the Earth's pull'' years ago --- revealed now as universal:
Earth pulls apple, apple pulls Earth, \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} pulls seas, Sun
pulls \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planets}, and two books on a shelf pull each other,
feebly, forever.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The law of universal gravitation]\label{prop:g9:gravitation:law}
Two bodies of masses $m_A$ and $m_B$ (in \unit{kg}), their
centers a distance $d$ apart (in \unit{m}), \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{attract} each other
with equal and opposite \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} along the line joining them, of
strength
\[
F = G \times \frac{m_A \times m_B}{d^2},
\]
$F$ in newtons (\unit{N}) --- the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit} whose full story
begins next chapter --- with
$G = \qty{6.67e-11}{N.m^2/kg^2}$, the \emph{gravitational
constant}, the same number everywhere in the universe. The
\omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} grows with each \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}, and dies with the \emph{square}
of the distance: twice as far, four times weaker; ten times
as far, a hundred times weaker.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Reading the law]\label{ex:g9:gravitation:reading}
Every feature of the formula speaks. Masses multiply: double
either partner, double the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force}. Distance squares in the
denominator: gravity is a rapidly fading whisper --- the
inverse-square dilution you will meet again with \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} and
\omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}, for it is the geometry of anything spreading into
space. And $G$'s minuscule size --- eleven zeros after the
decimal --- proclaims \omterm{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}{gravitation} the weakling of nature's
\omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces}: it rules the cosmos only because masses up there are
monstrous and nothing cancels it.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Why shelves do not slam together]\label{ex:g9:gravitation:books}
Two \qty{1}{kg} books, centers \qty{0.1}{m} apart:
\[
F = \num{6.67e-11} \times \frac{1 \times 1}{0.1^2}
= \qty{6.67e-9}{N}
\]
--- a few billionths of a newton, the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} of a fleck of
dust's fleck of dust. Now the Earth and the apple: replace one
book by \qty{5.97e24}{kg} of \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planet} and the distance by its
\qty{6.4e6}{m} radius, and the same law delivers the familiar
tug of an orchard afternoon. Universal means universal ---
only the masses decide who notices.
\end{example}

\section{The falling Moon}

\begin{example}[The cannon on the mountain]\label{ex:g9:gravitation:cannon}
The insight's own thought experiment. Fire a cannonball
horizontally from a high peak: it arcs and lands. Fire it
faster: it lands farther --- the Earth's pull bending its
path all the while. Now imagine firing it so fast that, as it
falls, the round Earth's surface \emph{curves away beneath it
at the same rate}: the ball falls endlessly and lands never
--- it circles the \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planet}. That perpetual falling has a
familiar name: an \emph{\omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbit}}. Nothing holds an orbiting
body ``up''; it is falling with style.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[What gravitation runs]\label{prop:g9:gravitation:runs}
One law, whole sky: the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} is the Earth's eternal
cannonball, falling around us once a month; the \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planets} fall
likewise around the Sun --- nearer ones faster, as the
inverse square demands; comets plunge in from the dark on
long falling loops; and every artificial satellite ---
weather eyes, navigation beacons, the crewed station circling
every ninety minutes --- rides the mountain cannon's
mathematics. Nothing in the heavens is suspended. Everything
falls, and misses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Tides: the Moon pulls back]\label{ex:g9:gravitation:tides}
Mutuality has a signature written in water. The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s pull
on the Earth is strongest on the ocean facing it and weakest
on the far side --- so the seas heap gently toward and away
from the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} at once, and as the Earth spins beneath the
heaps, coasts see the water rise and fall: the \emph{tides},
twice most days. The Sun adds its hand --- aligned at new and
\omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{full moon} for the great spring tides. Fishermen have read the
\omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} for millennia; the law reads it back.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[What the law does not say]\label{rem:g9:gravitation:notsay}
The great equation computes the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} to exquisite accuracy
and explains not one whit of \emph{how} two bodies grip
across empty nothing --- its own author called the question
open and left it, magnificently, unanswered. Deeper answers
exist --- the university years of this course reach the
modern one, where \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} curves the very geometry it inhabits
--- but every honest physicist since has repeated the
lesson you may take from this page: a law can be perfectly
useful, perfectly tested, and still guard its final secret.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:1}
State the law of universal \omterm{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}{gravitation} --- formula, \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{units},
and the two features (masses, distance) in words.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:2}
The Earth pulls the apple with \qty{1}{N}. With what \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force}
does the apple pull the Earth? Why does only one of the two
visibly accelerate?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:3}
Two satellites \omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbit} at distances $d$ and $2d$ from the
Earth's center. Compare the gravitational \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} on them
(equal masses). And at $10 d$?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:4}
Compute the attraction between two \qty{80}{kg} dance
partners \qty{0.5}{m} apart --- and comment on romance
versus \omterm{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}{gravitation}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:5}
In the mountain-cannon story, what two rates match exactly
for the orbiting shot? What is an \omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbit}, in three words?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:6}
``The astronauts \omterm{def:g1:floating-and-sinking:words}{float} because there is no gravity up
there.'' Demolish this with the cannonball: what are station
and crew both doing?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:7}
Why does \omterm{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}{gravitation}, nature's weakling, nonetheless govern
the cosmos? Two reasons from the chapter.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:8}
Whence the tides, and why twice most days? When do the
greatest ones strike?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:9}
The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} (\qty{7.3e22}{kg}) \omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbits} at \qty{3.8e8}{m} from
the Earth (\qty{5.97e24}{kg}). Compute their attraction,
scientific notation start to finish --- and admire how many
digits the answer's power of ten carries.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:10}
On the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s surface, your \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} shrank to a sixth
(remember the bouncing astronauts). Using the law's two
dials --- the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s smaller \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}, its smaller radius ---
explain how a sixth can emerge from a body eighty times
lighter than Earth.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:11}
Mercury laps the Sun in $88$ days, Neptune in $165$ years.
Read this pattern out of the inverse square: what does
gravity's fading with distance do to the Sun's grip, and so
to the outer family's pace?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:g9:gravitation:12}
A geostationary satellite hangs apparently motionless over
one point of the equator --- the dish antennas never move.
Reconcile the appearance with
\cref{prop:g9:gravitation:runs}: what must its orbital
period be, which way must it circle, and why does
``hanging'' here mean ``falling in perfect step with the
spinning Earth''?
\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: The Cannonball's Inheritance}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- from the orchard to
orbit; the apple, the cannon and the Moon audited with one
law; the satellite launch board}]\label{pb:g9:gravitation:1}
An evening at the space agency's outreach desk: one law,
$G = \qty{6.67e-11}{N.m^2/kg^2}$, Earth \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}
\qty{5.97e24}{kg}, Earth radius \qty{6.4e6}{m}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The apple's audit.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compute the Earth's pull on a \qty{0.1}{kg} apple at
        the surface (centers separated by one Earth radius).
  \item The apple pulls the Earth back with what \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force}?
        State the law's word for this two-way bookkeeping.
  \item Lift the apple to double the Earth's radius from the
        center. What becomes of the pull?
  \item An outreach visitor asks why the formula uses the
        distance to the Earth's \emph{center} rather than to
        the ground. Offer the honest short answer (the whole
        \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planet} pulls, and its scattered pulls add up as if
        from the center --- a theorem the great author
        himself needed years to prove).
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The launch board.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Today's satellite masses \qty{2000}{kg}. Its \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}
        at the surface, by the law? (Or by the shortcut the
        next chapter names: about \qty{9.8}{N} per
        \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilogram}.)
  \item In its working \omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbit} at \qty{1.28e7}{m} from the
        center --- two Earth radii --- what fraction of its
        surface \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} does gravity still exert on it?
  \item ``So there is still real gravity up there?''
        Confirm with the number --- and explain what the
        satellite is doing with that pull instead of
        crashing.
  \item The launch commentary says ``orbital \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed} must
        match the fall''. Retell the mountain-cannon story
        in two sentences for the visitors' brochure.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s ledger.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item With the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} at \qty{3.8e8}{m}, how many Earth
        radii out does it ride? (One decimal.)
  \item By the inverse square, gravity there has faded to
        roughly what fraction of its surface strength?
        (Square your previous answer.)
  \item The great insight, retold: the orchard apple falls
        about five \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} in its first second; the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon},
        each second, ``falls'' toward the Earth by barely a
        millimetre --- and yet never arrives. Connect the two
        facts through your answers 9 and 10: why is the
        \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s fall thousands of times gentler, and why
        endless?
  \item Close the outreach evening: one law, three
        performers --- apple, satellite, \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}. Write the
        board's closing sentence uniting them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
