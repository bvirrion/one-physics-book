\chapter{Weight vs Mass: Measuring Forces}\label{ch:g9:weight-vs-mass}

Five years ago, on a paper \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}, you split ``heavy'' in two:
\omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} for the stuff, \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} for the pull. The split was
wisdom; now it becomes arithmetic. \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{Forces} get their \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit} ---
named for the man of the falling apple --- \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} gets its
formula, and the rubber-band scale of your childhood returns
in professional dress.

\section{The newton}

\begin{definition}[The newton]\label{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}
\omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{Forces} --- pushes, pulls, \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weights}, \omterm{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}{gravitation}'s grip --- are
measured in \emph{newtons}\index{newton} (\unit{N}), honoring
the author of the law of \omterm{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}{gravitation}. Handy anchors: one
newton is about the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} of a small apple (history smiles);
your schoolbag weighs some \qty{50}{N}; a solid handshake
squeezes with around \qty{100}{N}; last chapter's Moon-lease
ran to $10^{20}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dynamometer]\label{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:dynamometer}
A \emph{dynamometer}\index{dynamometer} is the force-meter: a
calibrated spring in a case, stretching in proportion to the
\omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} applied, its pointer reading \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}{newtons} directly. It is
your rubber-band scale grown up --- and like the \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeter}
and \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:ammeter}{ammeter} before it, it retires another patch of ``feels
strong'' into honest numbers.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[Measuring a force]\label{met:g9:weight-vs-mass:measure}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item choose a \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:dynamometer}{dynamometer} whose range suits the job ---
        a \qty{2}{N} instrument for pencil cases, a
        \qty{100}{N} one for schoolbags;
  \item zero it in the position of use (hanging, if the load
        will hang --- the spring's own \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} shifts the
        zero);
  \item apply the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} steadily, along the instrument's
        axis, and read at eye level;
  \item record with the \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit} --- and, since \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} have
        directions, note the direction too when it matters.
\end{enumerate}
On diagrams, a measured \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} becomes an arrow drawn to a
declared scale --- ``one \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetre} per ten \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}{newtons}'' ---
pointing as the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} points: the childhood force-arrows,
now quantitative.
\end{method}

\section{The weight formula}

\begin{proposition}[Weight is proportional to mass]\label{prop:g9:weight-vs-mass:formula}
At any given place, an object's \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} $P$ (in \unit{N}) is
proportional to its \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} $m$ (in \unit{kg}):
\[
P = m \times g ,
\]
where $g$, the \emph{gravitational strength}\index{gravitational
strength} of the place, gives the \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}{newtons} of pull per
\omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilogram} of stuff. Near the Earth's surface,
\[
g \approx \qty{9.8}{N/kg}:
\]
each \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilogram} is pulled with just under ten \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}{newtons}. The
formula is last chapter's law in local dress: $g$ bundles the
Earth's \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} and radius into one number for
``here''.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[The formula at work]\label{ex:g9:weight-vs-mass:atwork}
A \qty{60}{kg} pupil: $P = 60 \times 9.8 = \qty{588}{N}$. A
\qty{450}{g} football: $P = 0.45 \times 9.8 \approx
\qty{4.4}{N}$ --- mind the \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilograms}. Backward: a crate
weighing \qty{343}{N} has \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} $m = P/g = 343 \div 9.8 =
\qty{35}{kg}$. And upside down: on a mountain where a
\qty{10}{kg} pack weighs \qty{97.7}{N}, the local $g$ is
$97.7 \div 10 = \qty{9.77}{N/kg}$ --- $g$ thins slightly
with altitude, exactly as the inverse square promised.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[One suitcase, many weights]\label{ex:g9:weight-vs-mass:planets}
Each world sets its own $g$, and the old astronaut story
becomes a table. Your faithful \qty{10}{kg} suitcase:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l|c|c}
place & $g$ (\unit{N/kg}) & suitcase \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} \\
\hline
Earth & $9.8$ & \qty{98}{N} \\
\omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} & $1.6$ & \qty{16}{N} \\
Mars & $3.7$ & \qty{37}{N} \\
Jupiter's cloud-tops & $24.8$ & \qty{248}{N}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Ten \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilograms} of stuff everywhere --- and a different \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} on
every world: the \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} is unchanged, while the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} follows the
local $g$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The two scales, judged for good]\label{rem:g9:weight-vs-mass:scales}
Now the childhood verdicts close with formulas. The balance
compares $m_1 g$ against $m_2 g$: the local $g$ multiplies
both pans and cancels --- balances \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measure} \emph{\omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}},
truthful on any world. The \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:springscale}{spring scale} reads $P = m g$
directly: it \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measures} \emph{\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}}, loyal to its location.
The bathroom scale is a \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:springscale}{spring scale} in disguise,
factory-marked in \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilograms} by dividing your \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} by the
\emph{Earth's} $g$ --- honest at home, a flatterer on the
\omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} (\qty{10}{kg} read for every \qty{60}{kg} of you), and
a slanderer on Jupiter.
\end{remark}

\section{Forces on paper}

\begin{example}[The book on the table, in newtons]\label{ex:g9:weight-vs-mass:book}
The oldest \omterm{def:g2:balance-equilibrium:equilibrium}{equilibrium} in this book, at last with numbers: a
\qty{2}{kg} dictionary rests on a desk. Its \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}: $P = 2
\times 9.8 = \qty{19.6}{N}$, drawn as an arrow \qty{2}{cm}
long at a scale of ten \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}{newtons} per \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetre}, pointing
straight down from the book. The desk's supporting push:
\qty{19.6}{N} straight up --- an equal arrow, opposite way.
The tie is exact, and stillness is its proof: the childhood
picture, now a measured budget.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[What forces do --- the missing law]\label{rem:g9:weight-vs-mass:dynamics}
This chapter \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measures} \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces}; it does not yet say what a
\emph{surplus} of \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} does to motion. The four childhood
jobs --- start, stop, turn, deform --- await their exact law,
and it is one of the greatest in all physics: the High School
volume opens its mechanics with it. This year finishes the
groundwork: \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} in \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}{newtons}, \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weights} by formula, arrows to
scale --- the grammar of the sentence to come.
\end{remark}

\section{Problem: The Interplanetary Outfitters}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- the interplanetary
outfitters' fitting room; one body, four worlds; scales,
dynamometers and the luggage desk}]\label{pb:g9:weight-vs-mass:1}
The outfitters equip travelers for the \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:family}{Solar System}'s ports
($g$, in \unit{N/kg}: Earth $9.8$; \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} $1.6$; Mars $3.7$;
Jupiter cloud-deck $24.8$). You staff the fitting room.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The fitting.}
A traveler of \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} \qty{70}{kg} steps up.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Their \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} at each of the four ports?
  \item At which port does the classic bathroom scale
        (marked in \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{kilograms}, calibrated for Earth) read
        their \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} correctly --- and what does it read at
        the other three? (Divide each \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} by Earth's
        $g$.)
  \item The outfitters' honest scale is a balance with
        marked masses. What does it report at each port,
        and why?
  \item The traveler's boots must never press the ground
        with more than \qty{2000}{N} (thin station floors).
        Any port where boots-plus-body (\qty{70}{kg} total)
        break the rule?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The luggage desk.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The Mars-bound trunk masses \qty{40}{kg}. Compute
        its \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} at departure (Earth) and at arrival
        (Mars).
  \item The porters' union caps hand-carried \emph{\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}}
        at \qty{200}{N} at every port. What maximum
        \emph{\omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}} may a porter carry at each?
  \item A crate labeled ``\qty{150}{N} on the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'' must
        clear Earth customs by \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}. Do the conversion.
  \item The desk's \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:dynamometer}{dynamometer} reads a hanging duffel at
        \qty{294}{N} on Earth. \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{Mass}, and its \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}
        for the manifest?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- Complaints and
curiosities.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item A bodybuilder returns from the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} furious:
        ``Six-fold strength gains, gone overnight!''
        Console them with the formula: what actually
        changed at each port, and what never did?
  \item A Jupiter-deck chef complains that whisking feels
        like lifting anvils, though the whisk ``masses a
        mere \qty{200}{g}''. Compute the whisk's deck
        \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} and adjudicate.
  \item The gift shop sells ``a \omterm{def:g9:weight-vs-mass:newton}{newton} of chocolate,
        anywhere''. At which port does the buyer get the
        most chocolate \emph{stuff}, and how much \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} at
        each of Earth, \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}, Jupiter?
  \item Write the outfitters' motto: one sentence
        separating what travels with you from what each
        world charges.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
