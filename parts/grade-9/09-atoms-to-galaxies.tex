\chapter{From Atoms to Galaxies: Scales of the Universe}\label{ch:g9:atoms-to-galaxies}

Nine years ago this book began with your five senses and a
ladybug's legs. It closes with a journey no senses can make
alone: forty-one powers of ten, from the heart of an \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atom} to
the rim of the observable universe --- with you, rather
exactly, in the middle. One last chapter, one last secret owed
from an old joke, and a ladder to stand on for all the physics
to come.

\section{Down: into matter}

\begin{definition}[Atoms]\label{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}
The \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} of your seventh year are themselves built:
every \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecule} is an assembly of \emph{atoms}\index{atom} ---
nature's alphabet, about a hundred kinds, whose combinations
spell every substance. A water \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecule}: two hydrogen atoms
and one oxygen. An atom, in turn, has architecture: a
minuscule, heavy, positively charged
\emph{nucleus}\index{nucleus} at the center, and, far outside
it, a cloud of \emph{electrons}\index{electron} --- almost
weightless carriers of negative charge. Atom sizes run near
$10^{-10}$ \unit{m}; their nuclei, near $10^{-15}$ ---
a hundred thousand times smaller again.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Matter is mostly emptiness]\label{prop:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:empty}
Scale an \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atom} up to a great stadium, and its \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{nucleus} is a pea
at the center circle --- the \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{electrons}, gnats in the highest
stands: everything between is empty. The marble table, the
iron anvil, your own hand: overwhelmingly void, their
solidity an electric refusal --- the \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atoms}' \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{electron} clouds
repelling each other into the illusion of fullness. It is
among physics' most astonishing sentences, and it is the
plain arithmetic of $10^{-10}$ against $10^{-15}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[The old joke, paid]\label{ex:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:electron}
The electricity chapters owed you a small joke, and here it
is. The marching charges in every wire of this book are
\emph{\omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{electrons}} --- and they are negative, so they march
from the \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's} $-$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} to its $+$: precisely
opposite to the conventional direction fixed by physicists a
century before anyone could ask the marchers. The convention
was kept --- every law of your circuits works perfectly with
it --- but the little irony stands: in every diagram you have
drawn, the real crowd walks the other way.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[And further down]\label{ex:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:deeper}
The \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{nucleus} has parts of its own, and their story --- how
they bind, why some nuclei split (the power station's \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat})
or merge (the Sun's) --- runs physics down to $10^{-15}$
\omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} and beyond, into the smallest structures humans have
probed. The High School volume opens the \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{nucleus} properly;
the university years go further still. The ladder's lower
rungs are still being built.
\end{example}

\section{Up: into the sky}

\begin{example}[The ascent]\label{ex:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:ascent}
Climb now, power of ten by power of ten, past the familiar
landmarks of nine years. You, about $10^{0}$ \unit{m}. The
schoolyard, $10^{2}$. The Earth, $10^{7}$ across --- the
marble of the sports-field model. The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s distance,
$4 \times 10^{8}$; the Sun's, $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ --- eight
light-minutes. The \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:family}{Solar System}'s breadth, roughly
$10^{13}$; the nearest \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:star}{star}, $4 \times 10^{16}$ --- four
\omterm{def:g8:speed-of-light:lightyear}{light-years}. The \omterm{ex:g5:stars-night-sky:milkyway}{Milky Way}, $10^{21}$ \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} of city; the
neighbor galaxy, $2 \times 10^{22}$ away; and the deepest
surveyed sky, near $10^{26}$ \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} --- the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} of its
galaxies older than the Earth beneath your chair.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Thinking in orders of magnitude]\label{met:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:oom}
The physicist's first tool on any new question:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item express the quantity in scientific notation;
  \item keep only the power of ten --- the \emph{order of
        magnitude}\index{order of magnitude};
  \item compare ladders, not digits: a \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:star}{star} at $10^{16}$
        \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} against a \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planet} at $10^{11}$ differs by five
        rungs --- a hundred-thousandfold, whatever the
        leading digits say;
  \item only then, if needed, sharpen the digits.
\end{enumerate}
Nine years of judging-steps --- ``is this answer sensible?''
--- mature into this habit. The High School volume's very
first chapter builds its measurement culture upon it.
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Ladder arithmetic]\label{ex:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:arithmetic}
How many \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atoms} span a pencil's width? Pencil, $10^{-2}$
\unit{m}; \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atom}, $10^{-10}$: eight rungs --- a hundred
million \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atoms}, shoulder to shoulder. How many Earths to the
Sun? $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ over $1.3 \times 10^{7}$: about
$10^{4}$ --- ten thousand marbles of the sports-field model,
laid in a row. The ladder answers in seconds what raw
kilometre-counts bury in zeros.
\end{example}

\section{The view from the middle rung}

\begin{remark}[What nine years built]\label{rem:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:built}
Look back down the ladder and count what you own. At
$10^{-10}$: \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} and \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atoms} --- with them, the three
states, \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat}, \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}'s relay, the current's marchers. Around
$10^{0}$: \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} and \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}, \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}'s straight rays, the
laws of circuits --- the human-sized physics of nine years
of experiments. From $10^{7}$ to $10^{22}$: the spinning
Earth, the tilted \omterm{def:g3:sun-days-seasons:year}{seasons}, the falling \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}, one law of
\omterm{def:g9:gravitation:gravitation}{gravitation} running orchard and galaxy alike. No other
schooling subject hands you a single ladder from the inside
of matter to the edge of the visible --- and every rung was
climbed with instruments, honesty, and arithmetic you can
check yourself.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What waits above]\label{rem:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:ahead}
And the unfinished business is the best of it. What law
exactly binds \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} to motion? What \emph{is} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}, that
it should race at $3 \times 10^{8}$ \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} a second and
carry colors inside it? What splits inside the \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{nucleus} ---
and what forges sunlight? Why does the tally of useful
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} always run downhill? Each question has been honestly
raised and honestly deferred in these pages; each has an
answer, and the answers are among the finest things our
species knows. They begin in the High School volume, one
year from now. Bring the ladder --- and the habit of
checking everything.
\end{remark}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:10}

\section{Problem: The Journey by Powers of Ten}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- the class films ``The
Journey by Powers of Ten''; one zoom, forty-one rungs; the
script conference}]\label{pb:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:1}
The class's film: one continuous zoom from a picnic blanket
outward to the deep sky, then inward to the \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{nucleus} ---
one power of ten per second of film. You write the
scientific script.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Outward.}
The zoom starts framing one \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metre} of blanket: $10^{0}$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item At which seconds does the frame first contain: the
        whole schoolyard ($10^{2}$); the whole Earth
        ($10^{7}$); the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s \omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbit} ($10^{9}$)?
  \item Which second frames the Sun's distance --- and what
        does the narrator say sunshine's age is, from the
        \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} chapter?
  \item Between which seconds does the frame cross the
        great emptiness where the \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:family}{Solar System} ends but the
        nearest \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:star}{star} has not yet entered? What should the
        screen honestly show there?
  \item The zoom ends at second $26$: what fills the frame,
        and how old is the oldest \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} in it compared with
        the Earth?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Inward.}
The zoom reverses and dives into a leaf on the blanket.
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item At which seconds does the film pass: an ant
        ($10^{-2}$, generously); a cell ($10^{-5}$); a
        \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecule} ($10^{-9}$); an \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atom} ($10^{-10}$)?
  \item Between the \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atom}'s \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{electron} cloud and its \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{nucleus},
        the film must zoom five more seconds through ---
        what? What does the narrator say about the
        stadium?
  \item The film's final frame, at $10^{-15}$: its subject
        --- and the narrator's honest closing line about
        the rungs below.
  \item Total the film's length: how many seconds of
        outward, how many of inward, from the blanket's
        \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metre}?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The script
conference.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item A teacher objects that the film spends one second
        on the step from house to street and one second on
        the step from galaxy to galaxy-cluster --- ``wildly
        unequal steps!'' Defend the equal seconds with the
        ladder's logic.
  \item The narrator wants one recurring line for both
        directions --- something true at every rung about
        emptiness. Draft it (the \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:family}{Solar System} and the \omterm{def:g9:atoms-to-galaxies:atom}{atom}
        must both fit under it).
  \item The credits must name the two instruments that
        conquered the two directions. Name them, and the
        chapters of this book that introduced their
        principles.
  \item The film's last words belong to the course itself:
        write the two-sentence epilogue --- nine years,
        one ladder, and what begins next year.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
