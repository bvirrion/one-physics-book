\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g9:alternating-current} --- Alternating Current}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:1}
DC flows always one way under a steady \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} --- the
\omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's} regime. AC reverses periodically under a swinging
\omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} --- the socket's regime.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:2}
$T$: the \omterm{def:g2:measuring-time:duration}{duration} of one full cycle; $f$: cycles per second;
$f = 1/T$ and $T = 1/f$. For \qty{50}{Hz}: $T =
\qty{0.02}{s}$. For $T = \qty{0.01}{s}$: $f = \qty{100}{Hz}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:3}
A hundred times: two reversals in every one of the fifty
cycles.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:4}
$T = 4 \times 5 = \qty{20}{ms} = \qty{0.02}{s}$; $f = 1/0.02
= \qty{50}{Hz}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:5}
\qty{230}{V} is the \omterm{prop:g9:alternating-current:effective}{effective voltage} --- the steady DC value
that would \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat} equally well, and what AC \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeters} read;
\qty{325}{V} is the peak, the crest the swing actually
touches, each way, fifty times a second.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:6}
Heating cares nothing for direction --- the coil warms on any
march, so kettles drink the swing directly. Charging is
chemistry run backward, and chemistry demands one consistent
direction: the brick converts the swing to steadiness first.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:7}
The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}: a flat line at \qty{9}{V}. The alternating
supply: a smooth wave swinging between $+9$ and
\qty{-9}{V}, one cycle per \qty{20}{ms}. The glance-test:
flat against wavy.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:8}
Spinning machines make AC naturally; and only AC feeds the
transformer, without which electricity could not travel the
country at high \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} and enter homes at a tame one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:9}
At low pedaling the dynamo's few cycles per second pulse the
lamp visibly --- each crest a flash, each zero a dip; faster
pedaling multiplies the pulses until the eye \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuses} them.
Cinema's $24$ pictures set the eye's rough fusion rate: below
about \qty{25}{Hz} of light-pulses, flicker annoys; above,
the glow reads steady.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:10}
Crests at about \qty{325}{V} --- and a hundred of them per
second, fifty positive and fifty negative.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:11}
$T = 1/60 \approx \qty{0.017}{s}$; peak $\approx 120 \times
1.4 \approx \qty{170}{V}$. Heaters and lamps adjust
indifferently; modern chargers read ``100--240 V, 50/60 Hz''
on their labels and convert happily --- the objectors are
old \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{single-voltage} devices, and any motorized clock that
counts the grid's cycles: it would run fast on sixty.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternating-current:12}
Brightening at both crests gives $2 \times 50 = 100$ pulses
per second. A $25$-frame camera samples every
\qty{0.04}{s} --- four pulses per frame, but not aligned
identically at every point of the rolling exposure: the
slight mismatch between the camera's rhythm and the lamp's
paints bands that creep across successive frames --- a beat
between two clocks. At \qty{60}{Hz} ($120$ pulses), the
mismatch against $25$ frames changes, and the bars change
width and \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed} --- the pattern betrays the local grid.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:alternating-current:1}
\textbf{1.} DC at $2.25 \times 2 = \qty{4.5}{V}$: the flat
pocket \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}.
\textbf{2.} AC; $T = \qty{20}{ms}$, $f = \qty{50}{Hz}$; peak
$3 \times 2 = \qty{6}{V}$.
\textbf{3.} DC at \qty{4.5}{V} connected \emph{backward} ---
the leads swapped: harmless on a scope, instructive in the
ledger.
\textbf{4.} AC; $T = \qty{10}{ms}$, $f = \qty{100}{Hz}$;
peak \qty{3}{V}. Its hundred-per-second doubled pulses sit
beyond the eye's fusion rate more comfortably than B's
fifty-cycle flicker.
\textbf{5.} The \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeter} reports the effective value ---
the DC-equivalent for heating --- which for a sinusoid sits
at about the peak divided by $1.4$: $6 \div 1.4 \approx
\qty{4.3}{V}$. Both numbers describe one swing.
\textbf{6.} Supply A alone (right way round --- C after
re-plugging). The AC supplies need a converter --- the
charger's rectifying brick --- between them and any
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}.
\textbf{7.} The mains' fifty swings a second lie near the
heart's own electrical rhythm and can disrupt it: \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{ampere}
for \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{ampere}, the swing endangers more than the steady march.
\textbf{8.} The sinusoid --- the perfect swing, drawn
naturally by a coil turning at constant \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed} in a magnetic
field: the alternator of the next chapter.
\textbf{9.} One cycle per $4$ squares (\qty{20}{ms}); crests
$325 \div 100 = 3.25$ squares above and below.
\textbf{10.} $f = 1/0.02 = \qty{50}{Hz}$; effective $\approx
325 \div 1.4 \approx \qty{230}{V}$ --- the label,
recovered from the screen.
\textbf{11.} A flat line at \qty{230}{V} --- steadiness of
equal heating power.
\textbf{12.} For example: ``A flat line is a \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's}
constancy --- one direction, one value. A wave is the
grid's swing --- direction and value in ceaseless cycle.
And the fair exchange rate between them is the \omterm{prop:g9:alternating-current:effective}{effective
voltage}: equal \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat}, equal worth.''
\end{solution}
