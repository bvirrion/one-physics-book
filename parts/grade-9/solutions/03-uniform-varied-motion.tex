\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g9:uniform-varied-motion} --- Motion: Uniform and Varied}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:1}
Marks of the object's positions at equal ticks of time. Two
makers: stepping through video frames; the dot-timer stamping
a pulled paper tape (or a strobe photograph).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:2}
Equal gaps: uniform. $v = 0.04 \div 0.1 = \qty{0.4}{m/s}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:3}
Growing gaps: accelerated. First interval: $0.01 \div 0.1 =
\qty{0.1}{m/s}$; last: $0.07 \div 0.1 = \qty{0.7}{m/s}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:4}
The radar reads \omterm{def:g9:uniform-varied-motion:instant}{instantaneous speed} --- the pace at the gate's
instant. ``Twenty minutes door to door'' computes the average
--- total distance over total time, stops and sprints blended.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:5}
In \omterm{def:g6:describing-motion:uniform}{uniform motions}: the pace never changes, so every interval
--- long or wink-short --- returns the same number, and the
two notions collapse into one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:6}
By about \qty{9.8}{m/s} each second --- numerically the local
$g$, \qty{9.8}{N/kg}: the year's deepest rhyme.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:7}
Balanced (or absent) \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} leave motion unchanged: rest stays
rest, and steady straight motion continues. The force-free
body coasts forever; the airliner's four-way tie --- thrust
against drag, lift against \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} --- is exactly why its
\qty{900}{km/h} holds steady.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:8}
The probe, force-free in the void, has coasted for decades
with cold engines --- motion needing no \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} to continue.
The bicycle that ``proves'' otherwise slows under friction
and \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, unbalanced backward \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} in disguise; on ice, with
the disguise thinned, it coasts far.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:9}
Shrinking gaps: decelerated. \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{Speeds}: $0.6$, $0.5$, $0.4$,
$0.3$, \qty{0.2}{m/s} --- falling by \qty{0.1}{m/s} per tick.
If the pattern holds, two more ticks (\qty{1.0}{cm}, then
nothing) bring the tape to rest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:10}
\qty{0.1}{m/s} per \qty{0.1}{s} is \qty{1}{m/s} gained per
second --- about a tenth of free fall's \qty{9.8}{m/s} per
second: a gentle push beside the Earth's.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:11}
First phase: \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} outpulls the young air resistance ---
unbalanced downward \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force}, \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed} grows. Canopy open: the
blossoming \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} drag rises until it ties the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} exactly
--- balanced \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces}, and the fall settles to the steady
\qty{5}{m/s} the sentence promises.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:uniform-varied-motion:12}
Record: film the release and step frames at \qty{0.1}{s} (or
tape the car to the dot-timer); \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measure} gap after gap down
ramp and across carpet. Numbers: interval \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speeds}, listed in
order. Expected verdict: act one, growing gaps --- accelerated
on the ramp (\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}'s unbalanced share along the slope); act
two, shrinking gaps --- decelerated on the carpet (friction's
unbalanced backward \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force}). One toy, both faces of the quiet
sentence.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:uniform-varied-motion:1}
\textbf{1.} Uniform; $v = 0.08 \div 0.1 = \qty{0.8}{m/s}
\approx \qty{2.9}{km/h}$ --- a stroll.
\textbf{2.} Accelerated: from $0.02 \div 0.1 = \qty{0.2}{m/s}$
to \qty{1.0}{m/s}; gaining \qty{0.2}{m/s} per tick ---
\qty{2}{m/s} per second.
\textbf{3.} Decelerated, steeply. The sand supplies a large
unbalanced backward \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} --- deep friction --- and the
\omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speeds} tumble: $1.0$, $0.7$, $0.45$, $0.25$,
\qty{0.1}{m/s}.
\textbf{4.} Three ticks of uniform gliding at \qty{0.5}{m/s},
then acceleration --- gaps leaping to $7.5$ and
\qty{10.0}{cm}: between ticks three and four something began
to push (a slope, a shove). The event lives at the third
gap's end.
\textbf{5.} Both can be true: the average blends waiting and
riding over the whole trip; the radar reads the crossing's
instant. Traffic law reads the radar --- limits govern
\omterm{def:g9:uniform-varied-motion:instant}{instantaneous speed}, and no gentle average excuses a fast
instant.
\textbf{6.} Uniformly accelerated. Equal speed-steps in equal
times point to a steady, unchanging net push --- constant
cause, constant growth.
\textbf{7.} The walker's forward push from the ground on the
shoes ties the backward drags of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} and ground on the body:
balanced, hence the steady \qty{0.8}{m/s}.
\textbf{8.} Growth: \qty{0.98}{m/s} per tick of \qty{0.1}{s}
--- \qty{9.8}{m/s} per second: the local $g$, reproduced by a
falling stone's tape.
\textbf{9.} The claim holds: \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speeds} $1.2$, $1.15$, $1.10$,
$1.05$, \qty{1.0}{m/s} --- slowing, gently, all along.
\textbf{10.} Exit $1.2 \times 3.6 = \qty{4.3}{km/h}$; entry
\qty{3.6}{km/h} --- both far below the cyclist's benchmark:
recklessness acquitted; steering, perhaps, another matter.
\textbf{11.} ``On flat ground nothing stops a rolling board
except unbalanced backward friction --- smooth yards supply
little, so the board keeps most of its motion, exactly as
the law of balanced \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} says it must.''
\textbf{12.} For example: ``Equal gaps, a pace at peace ---
no net \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} disturbs its lease. Changing gaps demand a
cause: some unbalanced push, by the quiet clause.''
\end{solution}
