\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g9:alternator} --- From Power Plant to Home: The Alternator}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:1}
Only change induces; faster motion, stronger \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} and more
turns give bigger \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage}; reversed motion reverses the
current. A \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} at rest, however strong, induces exactly
nothing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:2}
Each half-turn of the rotor brings a pole toward the coil
(inducing one way), then carries it off (inducing the other):
the \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} must swing sign with every half-rotation ---
alternation is built into the spinning.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:3}
Fifty turns per second (for the simple two-pole machine).
Every station must hold exactly the same rotation rhythm ---
the whole network spins in lockstep, sharing one \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:4}
The turbine and the \omterm{def:g9:alternator:alternator}{alternator}. Only the pusher of the blades
changes: flame's steam, atom's steam, falling lake, or \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:5}
Lake's stored height $\to$ falling water's motion $\to$
turbine's rotation $\to$ \omterm{def:g9:alternator:alternator}{alternator}'s electricity $\to$
transformer and grid $\to$ lamp. The Sun filled the lake:
evaporation lifted the sea, rain delivered it to the
mountains.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:6}
The lamp's \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} must come from somewhere, and the only
store on a bicycle is the rider: the dynamo converts leg-work
into \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}, so the lamp's \omterm{def:g9:kinetic-energy-safety:joule}{joules} appear as extra drag.
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{Energy} is converted, never conjured --- the oldest law of
the course.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:7}
\omterm{ex:g9:alternator:chains}{Renewable}: \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}, the lake (rain refills it), sunlight. Not:
coal, uranium --- spent forever on human timescales. The
sorting asks: does nature refill the store as we drain it?
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:8}
$P = U I$ lets one \omterm{def:g9:electric-power-energy:power}{power} travel as high \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} with small
current; the \omterm{prop:g8:electric-current-effects:three}{heating effect} makes the wires' loss grow with
the current. Small current, cool wires, saved \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} ---
so the grid ships high and steps down at the doorstep.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:9}
Slow cranking: small induced \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} (rule two) --- faint
lamp --- at few cycles per second: visible flicker, below
the eye's fusion rate. Fast cranking: bigger \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} ---
bright --- and a \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency} the eye \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuses} smooth. Two dials,
one crank.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:10}
At \qty{230}{V}: $I = \num{1e8} \div 230 \approx
\qty{4.3e5}{A}$ --- four hundred thousand \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{amperes}: no cable
on Earth. At \qty{400000}{V}: $I = \num{1e8} \div
\num{4e5} = \qty{250}{A}$ --- ordinary transmission cable.
The transformer's case, closed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:11}
Both chains read: store $\to$ \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat} $\to$ boiling water
$\to$ steam's motion $\to$ rotation $\to$ electricity ---
elaborate kettles feeding turbines. The chains differ at
exactly one link: what makes the \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat} --- burning coal's
chemistry, or splitting uranium's nuclei.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:alternator:12}
Upstream: the wall's \qty{230}{V} came through the
neighborhood transformer (\omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} stepped down), along
pylons at hundreds of kilovolts (transport form:
\omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{high-voltage} AC, leaking a little warmth), from an
\omterm{def:g9:alternator:alternator}{alternator} (rotation to electricity, by induction), spun by
a turbine (steam or water's motion), fed by a store --- say
the dam's lifted lake, lifted by the Sun's evaporation. So
the year-nine sentence: the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} in your room is sunshine
--- stored, fallen, spun and shipped.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:alternator:1}
\textbf{1.} Lake's height $\to$ water's rushing motion
$\to$ turbine's rotation $\to$ \omterm{def:g9:alternator:alternator}{alternator}'s induced
alternating \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} $\to$ the station's busbar.
\textbf{2.} The rotor \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} spins; the coils stand fixed.
Without motion, no change of magnetism through the coils ---
and without change, induction gives nothing at all.
\textbf{3.} The \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage}'s \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency} copies the rotation:
wrong \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed}, wrong \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency} --- and a machine out of step
with the grid's \qty{50}{Hz} fights every other \omterm{def:g9:alternator:alternator}{alternator}
on the network instead of helping them.
\textbf{4.} A stronger rotor \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} does induce more --- and
the turbine immediately labors harder to keep it spinning
against the stiffer magnetic drag: more electricity out
demands more water through, \omterm{def:g9:kinetic-energy-safety:joule}{joule} for \omterm{def:g9:kinetic-energy-safety:joule}{joule}. The lake, not
the \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet}, pays.
\textbf{5.} $I = \num{2e7} \div \num{2e5} = \qty{100}{A}$.
\textbf{6.} $I = \num{2e7} \div 230 \approx \qty{8.7e4}{A}$
--- eighty-seven thousand \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{amperes}: impossible cable. The
transformer's job is exactly this exchange --- \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} up,
current down, \omterm{def:g9:electric-power-energy:power}{power} unchanged.
\textbf{7.} More water through the gates. Underfed, the
loaded machines slow like a cyclist on a hill --- and the
grid's \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency} sags below fifty: the network's clocks and
machines all feel a hungry grid.
\textbf{8.} The first box only --- the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}, the blades'
push. Turbine, \omterm{def:g9:alternator:alternator}{alternator} and transformer stood perfectly
healthy, becalmed.
\textbf{9.} The grid is one great parallel network: many
roads join every station to every town, so a severed line's
share can flow by the surviving \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches} --- parallel
independence, at national scale.
\textbf{10.} The rerouted, overloaded lines let the
delivered \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} sag below nominal for a minute: the
warehouse's lamps dimmed and its \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motors} turned lazily ---
underfed, as any device below its \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:nominal}{nominal voltage} must be.
\textbf{11.} ``Tonight's every \omterm{def:g9:electric-power-energy:power}{watt} came from falling lake
water; the Sun evaporated the sea and the rain carried it to
our mountains. We spin sunshine here --- stored in a
lake, by way of the sky.''
\textbf{12.} For example: ``Motion past \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnets} made every
\omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{volt} of the night --- and at seven, ten thousand kettles
asked the lake to fall a little faster.''
\end{solution}
