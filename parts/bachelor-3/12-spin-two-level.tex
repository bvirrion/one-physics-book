\chapter{Spin and Two-Level Systems}\label{ch:b3:spin-two-level}

In 1922 Stern and Gerlach sent a beam of silver atoms through a
lopsided magnet, expecting either no deflection or a continuous
smear. The beam split cleanly in \emph{two}. No orbital angular
momentum can do that: $2l + 1$ is always odd. The atoms were
announcing a new, purely quantum possession --- \emph{\omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin}}, an
intrinsic angular momentum of $j = \tfrac12$, the half-integer rung
the algebra of \cref{ch:b3:quantum-angular-momentum} kept ready and
orbits could never use. \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{Spin} doubles every electron state (completing
the count behind the periodic table), carries the magnetism of iron
and of the proton, and is the cleanest two-level system nature
offers: the physics of this chapter runs the \omterm{prop:b3:spin-two-level:rabi}{magnetic resonance}
scanner in every hospital, the caesium clock that defines the second,
the 21-centimetre whisper by which galaxies are mapped, and the qubit.

\section{The experiment that found it}

\begin{example}[Stern--Gerlach]\label{ex:b3:spin-two-level:stern-gerlach}
A magnetic moment $\vect\mu$ in an \emph{inhomogeneous} field feels
the force $F_z = \mu_z\,\partial B_z/\partial z$: the deflection
measures $\mu_z$. Classical expectation: moments oriented at random,
a continuous fan. Quantum expectation for orbital momenta: $2l + 1$
spots: one, three, five --- always odd. Observed for silver (and
for hydrogen): \emph{two} spots, symmetric, nothing between. The
measured component takes exactly two values --- the signature of $j
= \tfrac12$, forbidden to orbits, and the direct display of
quantisation: the apparatus is a measuring device for one \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin}
component, and the beam splits into its two \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:observable}{eigenvalues}.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Spin one-half]\label{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}
\index{spin}\index{Pauli matrices}\index{spinor}
The electron (and the proton, the neutron, the quarks) carries an
intrinsic angular momentum with $j = \tfrac12$: a two-dimensional
state space spanned by $\ket\uparrow, \ket\downarrow$ (eigenstates
of $\hat S_z$ with $\pm\hbar/2$), on which
\[
\hat{\vect S} = \frac\hbar2\,\hat{\vect\sigma} , \qquad
\sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix} ,\;
\sigma_y = \begin{pmatrix}0&-\iu\\\iu&0\end{pmatrix} ,\;
\sigma_z = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
\]
--- the \emph{Pauli matrices}, realising the angular momentum
algebra in its smallest possible home. A general state
$\alpha\ket\uparrow + \beta\ket\downarrow$ is a \emph{spinor}. The
electron's magnetic moment is
\[
\hat{\vect\mu} = -g\,\frac{\mu_{\text{B}}}{\hbar}\,\hat{\vect S} ,
\qquad g \approx 2 :
\]
twice the orbital rate per unit angular momentum --- the anomaly
Einstein--de Haas had measured
(\cref{exo:b3:quantum-angular-momentum:12}) and the Dirac equation
would later predict. Spin is not a rotation of anything: no radius,
no ``spinning ball'' survives scrutiny --- it is intrinsic, like
charge.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Chained Stern--Gerlach filters]\label{ex:b3:spin-two-level:chained}
Select the $S_z = +\hbar/2$ beam, and measure $S_z$ again: all
atoms answer $+$. Measure $S_x$ instead: half and half --- the state
$\ket\uparrow$ is the superposition $(\ket{+x} + \ket{-x})/\sqrt2$.
Now keep the $S_x = +$ beam and measure $S_z$ once more: half and
half \emph{again} --- the $S_x$ measurement erased the previously
sharp $S_z$. Three magnets suffice to exhibit incompatibility,
collapse and Born's rule; this chain is
\cref{ex:b3:quantum-formalism:threepolarizers} performed with atoms.
\end{example}

\section{The Bloch sphere}

\begin{proposition}[Spin along any axis]\label{prop:b3:spin-two-level:bloch}
\index{Bloch sphere}
For the unit direction $\vect n = (\sin\theta\cos\varphi,
\sin\theta\sin\varphi, \cos\theta)$, the operator $\vect
n\cdot\hat{\vect\sigma}$ has \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:observable}{eigenvalues} $\pm1$, with
\[
\ket{+\vect n} = \cos\tfrac\theta2\,\ket\uparrow +
\eu^{\iu\varphi}\sin\tfrac\theta2\,\ket\downarrow .
\]
Every pure \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spinor} is $\ket{+\vect n}$ for exactly one direction: the
state space maps onto a sphere --- the \emph{\omterm{prop:b3:spin-two-level:bloch}{Bloch sphere}} --- with
orthogonal states at \emph{antipodes} (note the half-angles: opposite
directions, not perpendicular ones, are orthogonal). Rotations of
the sphere are exactly the evolutions a magnetic field generates:
the geometry of every qubit manipulation ever performed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Partial proof]
Diagonalise the $2\times2$ matrix $\vect n\cdot\vect\sigma =
\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & \sin\theta\,\eu^{-\iu\varphi}\\
\sin\theta\,\eu^{\iu\varphi} & -\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}$: trace $0$,
determinant $-1$, \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:observable}{eigenvalues} $\pm1$; the given \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spinor} is checked by
substitution (half-angle identities). Antipodes: $\theta \to \pi -
\theta$, $\varphi \to \varphi + \pi$ gives $\braket{+\vect
n'}{+\vect n} = 0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Spin in a field: precession and resonance}

\begin{proposition}[Larmor precession]\label{prop:b3:spin-two-level:larmor}
\index{Larmor precession}\index{gyromagnetic ratio}
In a static field $\vect B_0 = B_0\vect e_z$, the \omterm{def:b3:hamiltonian-mechanics:hamiltonian}{Hamiltonian} $\hat
H = -\hat{\vect\mu}\cdot\vect B_0$ splits the two levels by
$\hbar\omega_0$ and makes the mean \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} \emph{precess} about the
field:
\[
\frac{\dd\langle\hat{\vect S}\rangle}{\dd t} =
\gamma\,\langle\hat{\vect S}\rangle\wedge\vect B_0 , \qquad
\omega_0 = |\gamma|B_0 ,
\]
with the \omterm{prop:b3:spin-two-level:larmor}{gyromagnetic ratio} $\gamma$ ($\mu = \gamma S$). The Bloch
vector turns about $z$ at $\omega_0$, its angle to the field frozen
--- exactly the classical gyroscope picture, exact here because the
\omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:commutator}{commutators} are linear. For the electron: $\qty{28}{GHz/T}$; for
the proton (moment $2.79$ nuclear magnetons): $\gamma_p/2\pi =
\qty{42.6}{MHz/T}$ --- the licence plate of every MRI machine.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{prop:b3:quantum-formalism:evolution} with $\hat H =
-\gamma B_0\hat S_z$: the \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:commutator}{commutators} give $\dd\langle\hat
S_x\rangle/\dd t = -\gamma B_0\langle\hat S_y\rangle$,
$\dd\langle\hat S_y\rangle/\dd t = +\gamma B_0\langle\hat
S_x\rangle$ and $\dd\langle\hat S_z\rangle/\dd t = 0$, which
assemble into the stated vector product.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Magnetic resonance]\label{prop:b3:spin-two-level:rabi}
\index{magnetic resonance}\index{Rabi oscillation}
Add a small field $B_1$ rotating in the transverse plane at
frequency $\omega$. In the frame rotating with it, $B_0$ is
effectively reduced to $(\omega_0 - \omega)/|\gamma|$; \emph{at
resonance} ($\omega = \omega_0$) only $B_1$ survives, and the \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin}
precesses about it at the \emph{Rabi frequency} $\Omega_1 =
|\gamma|B_1$: starting from $\ket\uparrow$, the probability of
having flipped is
\[
\mathcal P_\downarrow(t) = \sin^2\Big(\frac{\Omega_1t}{2}\Big) .
\]
A pulse of duration $\pi/\Omega_1$ (a ``$\pi$ pulse'') inverts the
\omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin}; half of it (a ``$\pi/2$ pulse'') lays it in the equatorial
plane, where it precesses at $\omega_0$ and, by Faraday induction,
\emph{broadcasts} its frequency into any nearby coil. Off resonance
the flopping amplitude collapses as $\Omega_1^2/[\Omega_1^2 +
(\omega - \omega_0)^2]$: the response is sharply selective --- a
resonance --- which is what makes \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spins} addressable,
species by species and, with a field gradient, place by place
(\cref{pb:b3:spin-two-level:1}).
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Hyperfine structure and the 21 cm line]\label{ex:b3:spin-two-level:hyperfine}
\index{hyperfine structure}\index{21 cm line}
In hydrogen's ground state the electron's and proton's \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spins}
interact through their magnetic moments: the four \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} states split
into a triplet and a singlet separated by only $\Delta E =
\qty{5.9}{\micro eV}$ --- the \emph{hyperfine} splitting, $\nu =
\qty{1420}{MHz}$, $\lambda = \qty{21}{cm}$. The transition is
absurdly slow (one flip per ten million years), but the Galaxy holds
$10^{66}$ \omterm{thm:b3:hydrogen-atom:levels}{hydrogen atoms}: the \omterm{ex:b3:spin-two-level:hyperfine}{21 cm line} is bright enough to have
mapped the spiral arms, the warp of the disc, and --- through its
Doppler shifts --- the flat rotation curves that argue for dark
matter. The same physics, in caesium's ground state, splits levels
by exactly $\qty{9192631770}{Hz}$: since 1967, the \emph{definition
of the second} is a hyperfine \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} flip counted out
(\cref{exo:b3:spin-two-level:12}).
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Two-level craft]\label{met:b3:spin-two-level:method}
(1) Write any two-level \omterm{def:b3:hamiltonian-mechanics:hamiltonian}{Hamiltonian} in \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{Pauli matrices}: $\hat H =
\epsilon_0\mathbb 1 + \vect h\cdot\hat{\vect\sigma}$ --- then the
Bloch vector precesses about $\vect h$ at $2|\vect h|/\hbar$;
everything else is geometry. (2) Eigenstates along $\vect n$: use
half-angles. (3) Resonance problems: go to the rotating frame; at
resonance, only $B_1$ remains. (4) Pulses: $\pi/2$ to start a
precession signal, $\pi$ to invert (and to refocus --- the \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin}
echo). (5) Energy scales: $\mu_{\text{B}}B$ for electrons
($\qty{58}{\micro eV/T}$), three orders less for nuclei --- radio
frequencies, kelvin-free spectroscopy.
\end{method}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:1}
(a) Verify $\sigma_x^2 = \sigma_y^2 = \sigma_z^2 = \mathbb 1$ and
$\sigma_x\sigma_y = \iu\sigma_z$. (b) Deduce $[\hat S_x, \hat S_y]
= \iu\hbar\hat S_z$: the angular momentum algebra in dimension two.
(c) Show $\hat S^2 = \tfrac34\hbar^2\,\mathbb 1$ and check $j(j+1)$
for $j = \tfrac12$. (d) Show any $2\times2$ Hermitian matrix is a
real combination of $\mathbb 1$ and the three $\sigma_i$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:2}
(a) Find the normalised eigenstates of $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_y$.
(b) A \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} prepared in $\ket{+x}$ is measured along $z$: outcome
probabilities? (c) Along $y$? (d) Check both against the Bloch
picture (which angles $\theta, \varphi$?).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:3}
Three chained Stern--Gerlach magnets, axes $z$, then $\vect n$ at
angle $\theta$ in the $xz$ plane, then $z$ again; the $+$ beam is
kept each time. (a) Probability of surviving the second magnet. (b)
Of surviving all three. (c) Evaluate at $\theta = 90^\circ$ and
compare with \cref{ex:b3:spin-two-level:chained}. (d) For which
$\theta$ is the three-magnet survival maximal, and what does the
answer echo from \cref{exo:b3:quantum-formalism:5}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:4}
Compute the level splitting $2|\mu|B$ and the resonance frequency
in a \qty{1}{T} field for (a) the electron ($g = 2$); (b) the
proton ($\mu_p = 2.79\,\mu_{\text{N}}$, $\mu_{\text{N}} =
e\hbar/2m_{\text{p}}$); (c) compare both with $k_{\text{B}}T$ at

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:5}

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:6}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:7}

\end{exercise}

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:9}

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:10}

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:11}
Two \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spins} together. For two spin-$\tfrac12$ particles, the total
\omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} $\hat{\vect S} = \hat{\vect S}_1 + \hat{\vect S}_2$. (a) Show
the four product states reorganise into a \emph{triplet} ($S = 1$:
$\ket{\uparrow\uparrow}$, $(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow} +
\ket{\downarrow\uparrow})/\sqrt2$, $\ket{\downarrow\downarrow}$)
and a \emph{singlet} ($S = 0$: $(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow} -
\ket{\downarrow\uparrow})/\sqrt2$) --- verify the $S_z$ counts and,
for the two middle states, the effect of $\hat S^2$ (use $\hat
S^2 = \hat S_1^2 + \hat S_2^2 + 2\hat{\vect S}_1\cdot\hat{\vect
S}_2$ and $\hat{\vect S}_1\cdot\hat{\vect S}_2 = \tfrac12(\hat
S_{1+}\hat S_{2-} + \hat S_{1-}\hat S_{2+}) + \hat S_{1z}\hat
S_{2z}$). (b) Which of the four is entangled --- unwritable as a
product? (c) Hydrogen's hyperfine pair
(\cref{ex:b3:spin-two-level:hyperfine}) is exactly this
triplet/singlet: which is higher in energy, given that the line is
\emph{emitted} at 21 cm? (d) The singlet's \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spins} are perfectly
anticorrelated along \emph{every} axis: measure one along any
$\vect n$ and the other answers oppositely. Why does this
correlation, however striking, transmit no signal?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:spin-two-level:12}
The clock that defines the second. Caesium's ground-state hyperfine
splitting is exactly $\qty{9192631770}{Hz}$ (by definition of the
second). In a fountain clock, atoms get a $\pi/2$ pulse, fly freely
for $T = \qty{0.5}{s}$, and get a second $\pi/2$ pulse; the
transferred fraction oscillates as $\cos^2(\pi\,\delta\,T)$ with
detuning $\delta$ (Ramsey fringes --- accept this). (a) Explain in
Bloch language what each $\pi/2$ pulse and the free flight do. (b)
Width of the central fringe. (c) With signal-to-noise allowing the
fringe centre to be split by $10^4$, what fractional frequency
accuracy results? (d) Why do optical clocks (petahertz transitions)
beat microwave clocks at fixed fringe-splitting ability --- and by
what factor, roughly?
\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: Seeing inside the body}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- magnetic resonance imaging,
from spin to scan}]\label{pb:b3:spin-two-level:1}
Two-thirds of a human is water; every water molecule carries two
protons, each a spin-$\tfrac12$ magnet. Put a person in a strong
field, tickle the protons at their Larmor frequency, and listen:
that is \omterm{prop:b3:spin-two-level:rabi}{magnetic resonance} imaging, \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} physics as medicine. Data:
$\gamma_{\text{p}}/2\pi = \qty{42.58}{MHz/T}$; $B_0 = \qty{3.0}{T}$;
proton density of tissue $n \approx \qty{6.6e28}{m^{-3}}$;
$k_{\text{B}}T$ at \qty{310}{K} is \qty{26.7}{meV}; $h =
\qty{4.14e-15}{eV.s}$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{Spins} in the magnet.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compute the Larmor frequency at \qty{3.0}{T} and the photon
        energy $h\nu$ in eV.
  \item Compute the population imbalance between the two proton
        levels, $\Delta n/n \approx h\nu/2k_{\text{B}}T$.
  \item Only this excess --- parts per million --- contributes
        signal: how many ``useful'' protons per cubic centimetre of
        tissue?
  \item Why is thermal polarisation so feeble here where the
        Stern--Gerlach beam was fully split? (What does each
        experiment measure: single-atom \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:observable}{eigenvalues}, or a thermal
        average?)
  \item Doubling $B_0$ does what to the signal (two effects:
        polarisation and induced EMF at higher frequency)? Why do
        hospitals pay for bigger magnets?
  \item The magnet is superconducting and always on: why is a
        loose steel oxygen bottle in the room a lethal projectile
        (which force of \cref{ex:b3:spin-two-level:stern-gerlach}
        acts on it)?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Pulses and echoes.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item A transverse coil applies $B_1 = \qty{10}{\micro T}$ at
        resonance: compute the Rabi frequency and the durations of
        $\pi/2$ and $\pi$ pulses.
  \item After the $\pi/2$ pulse, what does the magnetisation do,
        and what does Faraday's law induce in the receiver coil (at
        which frequency)?
  \item The transverse signal decays with time constant $T_2$
        (\omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spins} dephase in each other's fields and local
        inhomogeneities); the longitudinal magnetisation regrows
        with $T_1$ (energy flows to the tissue). Typical values:
        $T_1 \approx \qty{1}{s}$, $T_2 \approx \qty{0.1}{s}$. Why
        can the coherence time never much exceed the energy
        relaxation time (what does every energy-exchanging flip do
        to the phase)?
  \item The \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} echo: after the $\pi/2$ pulse and a delay $\tau$,
        a $\pi$ pulse is applied and, at $2\tau$, the dephased
        \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spins} re-align and the signal returns. Explain the trick
        with runners on a track who are made to turn around.
  \item Which decay does the echo undo --- dephasing from
        \emph{static} field inhomogeneities, or from fluctuating
        molecular fields --- and why only that one?
  \item Different tissues have different $T_1$, $T_2$ (fat:
        short $T_1$; water/fluid: long; many tumours: longer $T_2$
        than their host tissue). In one sentence: how does timing
        the pulse sequence turn relaxation times into image
        \emph{contrast}?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- From signal to image.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Superimpose a gradient $G = \qty{40}{mT/m}$ along $z$: the
        Larmor frequency becomes position-dependent. Compute
        $\dd\nu/\dd z$ in kHz per millimetre.
  \item A shaped RF pulse containing only the band $\nu_0 \pm
        \qty{1}{kHz}$ excites which slab of the body? (Slice
        selection.)
  \item During readout, a gradient along $x$ makes each column of
        the slice broadcast its own frequency: what mathematical
        operation turns the received time-signal into a spatial
        profile (recall the Fourier toolbox of the Year 2 volume)?
  \item Estimate the total scan information: a $256 \times 256$
        image at 12 bits, and why acquiring it line by line
        (one echo per line, repetition time $\sim T_1$) makes a
        scan take minutes.
  \item The patient hears loud knocking: what is mechanically
        banging (think of the gradient coils switching in the
        \qty{3}{T} field --- which force)?
  \item X-ray imaging contrasts electron density; MRI contrasts
        proton density \emph{and} relaxation: why is MRI the tool
        of choice for soft tissue and the brain, and what ionising
        dose does it deliver?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- The \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin}'s other day jobs.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Chemists run the same experiment at \qty{10}{ppm}
        resolution: electron clouds shield each nucleus slightly,
        shifting its resonance (the ``chemical shift''). Why does
        this turn NMR into a molecular fingerprint?
  \item Functional MRI maps thinking: deoxygenated haemoglobin is
        paramagnetic and shortens the local $T_2^*$. Trace the
        chain from neural activity to image brightness.
  \item The same Larmor physics with the \emph{electron}'s moment
        runs at \qty{84}{GHz} at \qty{3}{T}: why is electron
        resonance useless inside a human but precious for studying
        radicals and defects?
  \item Compare the photon energy of item 1 with typical chemical
        bond energies: justify the phrase ``non-ionising'' and
        contrast with a \qty{60}{keV} X-ray photon.
  \item The ten-ppm polarisation can be boosted: laser-polarised
        xenon gas reaches order-one polarisation and is inhaled to
        image lung airspaces. By roughly what factor does the
        signal per nucleus rise, and why does the gas's low density
        still make the trick necessary?
  \item A single electron \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} in a silicon transistor-like trap
        is now read out and driven as a qubit with exactly this
        chapter's pulses: name the two properties of \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin} (size of
        its \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:state}{Hilbert space}; weakness of its coupling to noise) that
        recommend it.
  \item Summarise the named result: a \qty{128}{MHz} \omterm{def:b3:spin-two-level:spin}{spin}
        precession, a ten-parts-per-million thermal polarisation,
        two relaxation clocks and three field gradients suffice to
        photograph a living brain in slices --- Stern and Gerlach's
        two spots, grown into a hospital.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
