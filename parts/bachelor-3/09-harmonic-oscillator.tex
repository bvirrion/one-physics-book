\chapter{The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator}\label{ch:b3:harmonic-oscillator}

Almost nothing in nature is exactly a harmonic oscillator, and almost
everything is approximately one: any system nudged from stable
equilibrium --- a molecule's bond, an atom in a crystal, a bridge, a
mode of the electromagnetic field --- feels a restoring force
proportional to the displacement, because every smooth potential is a
parabola at the bottom of its well. Whoever solves the quantum
oscillator once therefore solves the small vibrations of the whole
world. This chapter solves it in the algebraic style that has become
the signature of quantum mechanics: two \omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:ladder}{ladder operators} climb and
descend a perfectly even staircase of levels $\big(n +
\tfrac12\big)\hbar\omega$, the half-step at the bottom --- the
\omterm{thm:b3:harmonic-oscillator:spectrum}{zero-point energy} --- being a theorem, not an option. The
consequences reach from why helium never freezes to how a carbon
dioxide molecule, ringing at its own $\hbar\omega$, intercepts the
Earth's outgoing heat.

\section{The ladder}

\begin{definition}[Ladder operators]\label{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:ladder}
\index{ladder operators}\index{annihilation operator}\index{creation operator}
For $\hat H = \hat p^2/2m + \tfrac12 m\omega^2\hat x^2$, introduce
the dimensionless, non-Hermitian pair
\[
\hat a = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\Big(\hat x +
\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\Big) , \qquad
\hat a^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\Big(\hat x -
\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\Big) .
\]
From $[\hat x, \hat p] = \iu\hbar$:
\[
[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger] = 1 , \qquad
\hat H = \hbar\omega\Big(\hat N + \tfrac12\Big) , \quad
\hat N = \hat a^\dagger\hat a .
\]
$\hat N$ is Hermitian; its \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:observable}{eigenvalues} will count \emph{quanta}, and
$\hat a$, $\hat a^\dagger$ --- the \emph{annihilation} and
\emph{creation} operators --- will remove and add one.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The spectrum, by algebra alone]\label{thm:b3:harmonic-oscillator:spectrum}
\index{zero-point energy}
The \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:observable}{eigenvalues} of $\hat H$ are exactly
\[
E_n = \Big(n + \tfrac12\Big)\hbar\omega , \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots
\]
--- an infinite ladder of equal steps $\hbar\omega$ above a ground
level that is \emph{not} zero: the \emph{\omterm{thm:b3:harmonic-oscillator:spectrum}{zero-point energy}}
$\tfrac12\hbar\omega$. The normalised eigenstates $\ket n$ are
connected by
\[
\hat a\ket n = \sqrt n\,\ket{n - 1} , \qquad
\hat a^\dagger\ket n = \sqrt{n + 1}\,\ket{n + 1} ,
\qquad
\ket n = \frac{(\hat a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,\ket0 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From the \omterm{def:b3:quantum-formalism:commutator}{commutator}, $\hat N\hat a = \hat a(\hat N - 1)$: if
$\ket\nu$ has $\hat N$-eigenvalue $\nu$, then $\hat a\ket\nu$ is an
eigenvector with $\nu - 1$ (or the zero vector). Each descent is
allowed only while $\nu \ge 0$, since $\nu = \braket{\nu}{\hat
N\nu} = \|\hat a\ket\nu\|^2 \ge 0$; the descent must therefore
terminate, and it terminates only on a state with $\hat a\ket{\nu_0}
= 0$, whence $\nu_0 = 0$. So $\nu$ runs over the non-negative
integers. The normalisations follow from $\|\hat a\ket n\|^2 = n$
and $\|\hat a^\dagger\ket n\|^2 = n + 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The states in space]\label{prop:b3:harmonic-oscillator:wavefunctions}
The ground state is the Gaussian
\[
\varphi_0(x) = \Big(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\Big)^{1/4}
\eu^{-m\omega x^2/2\hbar} ,
\]
obtained by solving the \emph{first-order} equation $\hat a\varphi_0
= 0$; it saturates Heisenberg's inequality, $\Delta x\,\Delta p =
\hbar/2$, with $\Delta x = \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}$. Applying
$\hat a^\dagger$ repeatedly generates $\varphi_n$: a polynomial of
degree $n$ (with $n$ nodes) times the same Gaussian. For large $n$,
$|\varphi_n|^2$ oscillates rapidly about the classical dwell-time
distribution, largest near the turning points --- the correspondence
principle in a picture.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Partial proof]
$\hat a\varphi_0 = 0$ reads $\varphi_0' = -(m\omega/\hbar)x
\varphi_0$: the Gaussian, normalised. Saturation: the Gaussian is
the equality case of \cref{thm:b3:quantum-formalism:uncertainty}.
The polynomial structure follows from $\hat a^\dagger$ being first
order in $x$ and $\dd/\dd x$; the large-$n$ statement is checked in
\cref{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:12}.
\end{proof}

\section{The zero-point energy is real}

\begin{remark}[Why the floor cannot be lower]\label{rem:b3:harmonic-oscillator:zeropoint}
A state of zero energy would need $\langle\hat p^2\rangle =
\langle\hat x^2\rangle = 0$: perfectly still \emph{and} perfectly
centred, forbidden by $\Delta x\,\Delta p \ge \hbar/2$. The ground
state is the best compromise the inequality allows, and
$\tfrac12\hbar\omega$ is the rent. It is not a bookkeeping constant:
zero-point motion smears X-ray diffraction patterns at absolute
zero; it gives lighter isotopes weaker effective bonds (H$_2$ and
D$_2$ dissociate at measurably different energies from the same
electronic well); and in helium it is so violent --- light atoms,
feeble attraction --- that the liquid \emph{never} freezes under its
own vapour pressure: the only element still liquid at absolute
zero, solidifying only under 25 atmospheres of help.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Scales of $\hbar\omega$]\label{ex:b3:harmonic-oscillator:scales}
Carbon monoxide bond ($\omega = \qty{4.1e14}{rad/s}$): $\hbar\omega =
\qty{0.27}{eV}$, ten times room temperature's $k_{\text{B}}T$ ---
molecular vibrations are frozen in everyday air, which is why
diatomic heat capacities puzzled the nineteenth century. A pendulum
($\omega = \qty{5}{rad/s}$): $\hbar\omega = \qty{3e-15}{eV}$,
hopelessly beyond resolution --- the correspondence limit. A LIGO
mirror of \qty{40}{kg}, suspended so that it swings at
\qty{100}{Hz}, has as an oscillator mode the zero-point amplitude $\Delta x =
\sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega} \approx \qty{5e-20}{m}$ --- and the observatory
routinely resolves displacements \emph{at} this quantum floor: the
zero-point motion of a forty-kilogram object is now an engineering
\omterm{def:b3:lagrangian-mechanics:coordinates}{constraint} (\cref{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:3}).
\end{example}

\section{Coherent states: the classical face of the quantum oscillator}

\begin{definition}[Coherent states]\label{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent}
\index{coherent state}
A \emph{coherent state} $\ket\alpha$ is an eigenvector of the
\omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:ladder}{annihilation operator}, $\hat a\ket\alpha = \alpha\ket\alpha$, with
$\alpha$ any complex number. Expanded on the ladder,
\[
\ket\alpha = \eu^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty
\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,\ket n :
\]
the quantum count is Poisson-distributed with mean $\langle\hat
N\rangle = |\alpha|^2$ and spread $\Delta N = |\alpha|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Why lasers are classical]\label{prop:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent-motion}
A \omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent}{coherent state} is the ground-state Gaussian displaced in \omterm{def:b3:hamiltonian-mechanics:hamiltonian}{phase
space}; under the oscillator's evolution it \emph{stays} coherent,
$\alpha(t) = \alpha\,\eu^{-\iu\omega t}$: its centre executes exactly
the classical motion, $\langle\hat x\rangle(t) =
x_0\cos\omega t + \cdots$, while its widths remain the minimal
$\Delta x\,\Delta p = \hbar/2$ forever --- a wave packet that never
spreads. \omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent}{Coherent states} are how a quantum oscillator impersonates a
classical one; the light of a laser is a \omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent}{coherent state} of a field
mode, its photon number Poissonian (the \emph{shot noise} of every
photodetector), its field oscillating like Maxwell said.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Partial proof]
The expansion follows by writing $\ket\alpha = \sum c_n\ket n$ in
$\hat a\ket\alpha = \alpha\ket\alpha$: $c_n = \alpha c_{n-1}/\sqrt
n$. Evolution: each $\ket n$ picks up $\eu^{-\iu(n + 1/2)\omega t}$,
which resums to a \omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent}{coherent state} of $\alpha\eu^{-\iu\omega t}$
(global phase apart). $\langle\hat x\rangle \propto
\operatorname{Re}\alpha(t)$ from $\hat x \propto \hat a +
\hat a^\dagger$. That the state is the displaced Gaussian is
admitted here.
\end{proof}

\begin{method}[Oscillator algebra]\label{met:b3:harmonic-oscillator:method}
(1) Express whatever is asked in $\hat a$, $\hat a^\dagger$: $\hat x
= \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}\,(\hat a + \hat a^\dagger)$, $\hat p =
\iu\sqrt{m\hbar\omega/2}\,(\hat a^\dagger - \hat a)$. (2) Move
$\hat a$'s to the right with $[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger] = 1$; use
$\hat a\ket0 = 0$. (3) Matrix elements: $\hat x$ connects only
neighbouring rungs --- the selection rule $\Delta n = \pm1$ of
vibrational spectroscopy. (4) Any quadratic \omterm{def:b3:hamiltonian-mechanics:hamiltonian}{Hamiltonian} (coupled
oscillators, circuits, field modes) diagonalises into independent
ladders: find the \omterm{prop:b3:lagrangian-mechanics:modes}{normal modes} first, quantise each. (5) Numbers
first: $\hbar\omega$ against $k_{\text{B}}T$ decides whether the
system is quantum or classical before any algebra.
\end{method}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:1}
(a) Verify $[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger] = 1$ from $[\hat x, \hat p] =
\iu\hbar$. (b) Verify $\hat H = \hbar\omega(\hat a^\dagger\hat a +
\tfrac12)$ by direct expansion. (c) Invert to express $\hat x$ and
$\hat p$. (d) Show $\hat N$ is Hermitian and explain why $\hat a$
alone could not be an observable.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:2}
On the eigenstate $\ket n$: (a) show $\langle\hat x\rangle =
\langle\hat p\rangle = 0$; (b) compute $\langle\hat x^2\rangle$ and
$\langle\hat p^2\rangle$; (c) deduce $\Delta x\,\Delta p = (n +
\tfrac12)\hbar$; (d) check the virial ratio $\langle E_k\rangle =
\langle E_p\rangle$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:3}
Zero-point amplitudes $\Delta x = \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}$: compute
for (a) the CO molecule ($\mu = \qty{1.14e-26}{kg}$, $\omega =
\qty{4.1e14}{rad/s}$), compared with the bond length
\qty{0.11}{nm}; (b) a hydrogen atom in a solid ($m =
\qty{1.7e-27}{kg}$, $\hbar\omega = \qty{0.1}{eV}$); (c) a LIGO
mirror ($m = \qty{40}{kg}$, $f = \qty{100}{Hz}$); (d) rank the three
as fractions of their systems' sizes and comment on who is
``quantum''.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:4}
(a) Using the ladder relations, compute $\bra m\hat x\ket n$ and
show it vanishes unless $m = n \pm 1$. (b) Deduce the vibrational
selection rule $\Delta n = \pm1$ for light absorption (the coupling
is $\propto\hat x$). (c) Why does a heteronuclear molecule (CO)
absorb infrared light while N$_2$ does not? (d) Real molecules show
weak ``overtone'' lines at $\approx 2\hbar\omega$: what does that
reveal about the potential?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:5}
(a) Solve $\hat a\varphi_0 = 0$ as a differential equation and
normalise. (b) Generate $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$ by applying
$\hat a^\dagger$. (c) Verify $\varphi_1 \perp \varphi_0$ by parity
alone. (d) Sketch the three densities and check the node count.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:6}
Boltzmann preview. A collection of identical oscillators at
temperature $T$ occupies level $n$ with probability $\propto
\eu^{-E_n/k_{\text{B}}T}$ (Year 2 volume, Boltzmann factor). (a)
Show the mean quantum number is $\langle n\rangle =
1/(\eu^{\hbar\omega/k_{\text{B}}T} - 1)$. (b) Evaluate for the CO
vibration at \qty{300}{K} and at \qty{2000}{K}. (c) Show the mean
energy tends to $k_{\text{B}}T$ at high temperature (equipartition
recovered) and to $\tfrac12\hbar\omega$ at low. (d) At what
temperature does a \qty{15}{\micro m} vibration (carbon dioxide's
bend) hold $\langle n\rangle = 0.1$?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:7}
Coherent-state statistics. (a) From the expansion of $\ket\alpha$,
show $\mathcal P(n)$ is Poisson with mean $|\alpha|^2$. (b) Show
$\langle\hat N\rangle = |\alpha|^2$ and $\Delta N = |\alpha|$. (c) A
\qty{1}{mW} laser at \qty{633}{nm}: photons per second, and the
relative fluctuation $\Delta N/\langle N\rangle$ in one second. (d)
Shot noise: show the photocurrent noise-to-signal falls as
$1/\sqrt{\langle N\rangle}$ --- why bright beams look smooth.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:8}
Evolution of a \omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent}{coherent state}. (a) Apply the evolution phases to the
expansion and show $\ket{\alpha(t)}$ with $\alpha(t) =
\alpha\eu^{-\iu\omega t}$ (up to a global phase). (b) Deduce
$\langle\hat x\rangle(t)$ and $\langle\hat p\rangle(t)$ and compare
with the classical solution. (c) Why does an energy eigenstate,
despite being stationary, \emph{not} describe a swinging pendulum
--- which feature of the \omterm{def:b3:harmonic-oscillator:coherent}{coherent state} does? (d) Estimate $|\alpha|$
for a real pendulum (\qty{10}{g}, \qty{10}{cm} amplitude,
\qty{1}{Hz}) and comment.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:9}
The quantum LC circuit. A superconducting loop with $L =
\qty{10}{nH}$ and $C = \qty{0.4}{pF}$ oscillates charge and flux
like $x$ and $p$. (a) Its resonance frequency (Year 1 volume) and
the quantum $\hbar\omega$ in $\unit{\micro eV}$. (b) Below what
temperature is $k_{\text{B}}T \ll \hbar\omega$, so the circuit sits
in its ground state? (c) Why are superconducting qubits operated in
dilution refrigerators at \qty{20}{mK}? (d) The zero-point voltage
fluctuation $\Delta V = \Delta q/C$ with $\Delta q =
\sqrt{\hbar\omega C/2}$: evaluate it.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:10}
Van der Waals from zero-point motion. Model two neutral atoms at
distance $R$ as two identical dipole oscillators (charge $e$, mass
$m$, frequency $\omega_0$) whose displacements couple by the dipole
energy $\lambda\,x_1x_2$ with $\lambda = e^2/2\pi\varepsilon_0R^3$.
(a) Show the normal coordinates $(x_1 \pm x_2)/\sqrt2$ oscillate at
$\omega_\pm = \omega_0\sqrt{1 \pm \lambda/m\omega_0^2}$. (b) The
ground energy is $\tfrac\hbar2(\omega_+ + \omega_-)$: expand to
second order in $\lambda$ and show the interaction energy is
\[
\Delta E = -\frac{\hbar\lambda^2}{8m^2\omega_0^3}
\ \propto\ -\frac{1}{R^6} .
\]
(c) Why is this attraction universal --- present between atoms with
no permanent dipoles at all? (d) The $1/R^6$ law is the van der
Waals force of the Year 1 volume's real-gas corrections: what,
microscopically, is ``fluctuating'' --- and what would happen to
this force in a world with $\hbar = 0$?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:11}
Sidebands of a trapped ion. An ion in a harmonic trap ($f =
\qty{1}{MHz}$) absorbs laser light. Because the ion moves, its
absorption spectrum shows the electronic line at $\nu_0$ flanked by
lines at $\nu_0 \pm f$: transitions that change the \emph{motional}
quantum number by $\mp1$ alongside the electronic one. (a) What is
$\hbar\omega_{\text{trap}}$ in $\unit{neV}$, and why does resolving
sidebands need a very narrow line? (b) Driving the $\nu_0 - f$ line
removes one motional quantum per cycle: explain \emph{sideband
cooling} to the ground state. (c) Once $\langle n\rangle \approx
0$, the lower sideband disappears entirely --- why is that
asymmetry a proof of reaching the quantum ground state? (d) This is
how the motional ground state of a single atom --- and of
kilogram-scale LIGO mirrors, by other means --- is certified: state
what ``temperature'' the ion has reached for $f = \qty{1}{MHz}$ and
$\langle n\rangle = 0.05$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:12}
The classical limit, quantitatively. A classical oscillator of
amplitude $A$ spends in $[x, x + \dd x]$ the fraction $\dd
t/T = \dd x/\pi\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$. (a) Derive this dwell-time
distribution. (b) For the quantum state $n$, take $A_n$ from $E_n =
\tfrac12 m\omega^2A_n^2$ and compare the classical distribution with
the (given) locally averaged $|\varphi_n|^2$: where do they agree
and where must they differ? (c) Show the fractional spacing between
adjacent levels, $\Delta E/E$, vanishes as $1/n$: energy becomes
effectively continuous. (d) For the pendulum of
\cref{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:8}(d), estimate $n$ and $\Delta
E/E$, and conclude the correspondence argument in one sentence.
\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: The molecule that warms the Earth}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- carbon dioxide's quantum ladder
and the greenhouse effect}]\label{pb:b3:harmonic-oscillator:1}
A carbon dioxide molecule is a linear O=C=O chain: a few quantised
oscillators. That its bending mode's $\hbar\omega$ happens to sit in
the middle of the Earth's outgoing thermal glow is why this trace
gas --- four molecules in ten thousand --- steers the planet's
climate. Data: bend wavenumber $\tilde\nu_2 = \qty{667}{cm^{-1}}$
(the spectroscopist's unit: $E = hc\tilde\nu$, $\qty{1}{cm^{-1}} =
\qty{1.24e-4}{eV}$); asymmetric stretch $\tilde\nu_3 =
\qty{2349}{cm^{-1}}$; symmetric stretch $\tilde\nu_1 =
\qty{1388}{cm^{-1}}$; $k_{\text{B}}T$ at $\qty{288}{K}$ is
\qty{24.8}{meV}; Wien's law (Year 2 volume): $\lambda_{\max}T =
\qty{2898}{\micro m.K}$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The modes of a linear molecule.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Three atoms have nine \omterm{def:b3:lagrangian-mechanics:coordinates}{degrees of freedom}: how many are
        translations of the whole molecule, how many rotations (the
        molecule is \emph{linear}), and how many vibrations remain?
  \item Describe the four vibrations: symmetric stretch, asymmetric
        stretch, and a doubly degenerate bend --- why does the bend
        come twice?
  \item Light couples to a vibrating \emph{electric dipole}
        (\cref{exo:b3:harmonic-oscillator:4}). Which modes of
        O=C=O modulate the dipole moment, and which one is
        infrared-silent?
  \item Convert the three wavenumbers to photon wavelengths, and
        place them: which are in the thermal infrared?
  \item Why do the two main air gases, N$_2$ and O$_2$, absorb
        essentially no infrared at all --- with what consequence for
        the atmosphere's transparency?
  \item Water vapour, bent and dipolar, absorbs across much of the
        infrared: in which spectral ``window'' does carbon dioxide's
        bend operate largely alone (compare \qty{15}{\micro m} with
        water's strong bands below \qty{8}{\micro m} and above
        \qty{20}{\micro m})?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The quantum ladder of the bend.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Compute $\hbar\omega_2$ in meV, and the ladder $E_n$.
  \item What fraction of molecules occupies $n = 1$ at
        \qty{288}{K} (relative to $n = 0$, Boltzmann factor)? And
        $n = 2$?
  \item Which photon wavelength drives $n = 0 \to 1$? Why does the
        \emph{same} wavelength dominate emission?
  \item Justify from the harmonic ladder that one wavelength serves
        the whole ladder ($n \to n + 1$ for every $n$): what
        property of the level spacing is at work?
  \item The molecule also rotates, adding fine structure: the
        \qty{15}{\micro m} feature is really a \emph{band} some
        \qty{1}{\micro m} wide. Why does band \emph{width} matter
        for a greenhouse gas (think of what happens once the band
        centre is opaque)?
  \item A vibrationally excited CO$_2$ in air is far more likely to
        lose its quantum by collision than by radiating (radiative
        lifetime $\sim\qty{1}{s}$, collision time $\sim\qty{e-9}{s}$):
        where does the absorbed radiant energy actually go?
  \item Conversely, air at \qty{288}{K} keeps a thermal population
        in $n = 1$ (question 8): what does that population do that
        matters for the energy budget?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The planet's radiation ledger.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The Sun radiates as a \qty{5800}{K} body: compute its Wien
        peak. Does CO$_2$'s bend intercept much sunlight?
  \item The ground radiates as a \qty{288}{K} body: compute its
        Wien peak, and locate \qty{15}{\micro m} on that thermal
        curve.
  \item Explain the greenhouse mechanism in four sentences:
        sunlight in, thermal infrared out, interception at
        \qty{15}{\micro m}, re-emission both up \emph{and} down.
  \item The re-emission that escapes to space comes from high, cold
        layers ($\sim\qty{220}{K}$): why does emitting from a colder
        layer reduce the planet's outgoing power at those
        wavelengths (recall that thermal emission grows with $T$)?
  \item More CO$_2$ pushes the emitting layer higher and colder:
        state in one sentence why the surface must then warm to
        rebalance the books.
  \item The band centre is already opaque; the effect of added
        CO$_2$ works in the band's \emph{wings}, giving a
        logarithmic growth of forcing with concentration: connect
        this to question 11.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- Isotopes: the quantum
fingerprint.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item An oscillator's frequency scales as $\sqrt{k/\mu}$: for the
        asymmetric stretch, replacing $^{12}$C by $^{13}$C changes
        the effective mass; the observed line shifts from
        \qty{2349}{cm^{-1}} to about \qty{2283}{cm^{-1}}. Check the
        order of magnitude of this $\approx 3\%$ shift from the
        masses.
  \item Lasers tuned to these two lines count $^{13}$CO$_2$ and
        $^{12}$CO$_2$ separately in a gas sample: explain why the
        quantised ladder makes such isotope-resolved detection
        possible at all.
  \item Plants prefer the lighter isotope, so fossil carbon is
        $^{13}$C-poor: what has the measured isotopic ratio of
        atmospheric CO$_2$ done as its concentration rose --- and
        what does that prove about the \emph{source} of the added
        gas?
  \item The same \qty{15}{\micro m} physics operates on Venus
        ($96\%$ CO$_2$, \qty{90}{bar}): what does its
        \qty{737}{K} surface illustrate?
  \item Mars also breathes nearly pure CO$_2$, but at \qty{6}{mbar},
        and is frigid: what does the Venus--Earth--Mars trio
        demonstrate about which variable controls the strength of
        the effect?
  \item Summarise the named result: a quantum of \qty{83}{meV} ---
        $\hbar\omega$ of a bending triatomic --- parked at
        \qty{15}{\micro m} on a \qty{288}{K} planet's thermal
        spectrum, absorbed, thermalised in nanoseconds and
        re-emitted from cold altitudes, is the mechanism by which
        $0.04\%$ of the air sets the temperature of the Earth.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
