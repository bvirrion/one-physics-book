\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:grand-canonical} --- The Grand Canonical Ensemble}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:1}
(a) From the denser box to the thinner: from high $\mu =
k_{\text{B}}T\ln(n\lambda_T^3)$ to low. (b) Equal $T$ makes
$\lambda_T$ common: equal $\mu$ is equal $n$. (c)
$\mu_{\text{liquid}} = \mu_{\text{vapour}}$ --- the definition of
saturated humidity. (d) In dry air the vapour's $\mu$ (low $n$)
sits below the liquid's: water evaporates down the $\mu$-hill
regardless of never boiling.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:2}
(a) $\lambda_T = \qty{1.9e-11}{m}$. (b) $n\lambda_T^3 =
\num{1.7e-7}$: $\mu = k_{\text{B}}T\ln(\num{1.7e-7}) =
\qty{-0.40}{eV}$. (c) The entropy term: a new molecule opens
$\sim1/n\lambda_T^3$ states, and $-TS$ wins over any kinetic cost.
(d) $n = \lambda_T^{-3} \approx \qty{1.5e32}{m^{-3}}$ ---
thousands of times liquid density for air; $n\lambda_T^3 \sim 1$
is the border of quantum degeneracy
(\cref{exo:b3:grand-canonical:11}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:3}
$\Xi = 1 + \eu^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}$; $\bar n = k_{\text{B}}T\,
\partial_\mu\ln\Xi = \eu^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}/(1 +
\eu^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)})$, the stated form. Limits: $1$ deep
below $\mu$, Boltzmann tail far above, exactly $\tfrac12$ at
$\epsilon = \mu$. It is the thermal two-level occupation of the
Schottky solid --- the same curve, re-labelled.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:4}

$\approx\qty{290}{kJ/mol}$. (b) \omterm{thm:b3:grand-canonical:distribution}{Chemical potentials} differ by bond
energies --- electron-volts: batteries inherit chemistry's energy

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:5}
(a) Geometric series with ratio $\eu^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)} < 1$;
differentiate. (b) At $\mu \to \epsilon_{\min}$ the series
diverges: the level can absorb unbounded population --- the
macroscopic pile-up that becomes Bose--Einstein condensation. (c)
$\bar n = 9.51$, $0.58$, $0.052$ against Boltzmann's $0.90$,
$0.37$, $0.050$: identical in the dilute tail, wildly enhanced
near $\mu$. (d) Since walls create and destroy photons at will,
equilibrium cannot depend on their number: the free-energy cost of
one more photon is zero, $\mu = 0$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:6}
(a) $P = P_0$; $P = 9P_0$. (b) One independent site gives the
hyperbola $P/(P + P_0)$ --- myoglobin; haemoglobin's four
interacting sites load cooperatively, steepening the curve into
the S shape that loads fully in the lungs \emph{and} unloads in
the tissues. (c) $\eu^{0.13/0.0267} \approx 130$: at equal partial
pressures CO wins the sites a hundredfold --- a trace of CO
evicts oxygen from the blood. (d) Raising the O$_2$ partial
pressure raises its $\mu$ and re-tilts the competition: the
isotherm is fought over $\mu$'s, and hyperbaric oxygen simply
outbids.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:7}
(a) Differentiate $\langle N\rangle = k_{\text{B}}T\,\partial_\mu
\ln\Xi$ once more. (b) $\langle N\rangle \propto
\eu^{\beta\mu}$: $\partial_\mu\langle N\rangle = \beta\langle
N\rangle$, so $\operatorname{Var}N = \langle N\rangle$. (c) The
Gaussian with variance $\bar n$ of that exercise is this Poisson
statement, seen in a sub-volume. (d) $\operatorname{Var}n =
\bar n(1 - \bar n)$: zero when the level is surely empty or surely
full --- Pauli blocking silences fluctuations deep in a Fermi sea.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:8}
(a) Solute dilutes water's entropy, lowering its $\mu$; pressure
raises $\mu$: balance fixes $\Pi = ck_{\text{B}}T$. (b) $\Pi =
\num{3e26} \times \num{1.38e-23} \times 310 \approx
\qty{1.3}{MPa}$ --- thirteen atmospheres inside your veins,
routinely balanced. (c) Pure water's $\mu$ exceeds the cell
interior's: water floods in and the cells burst (haemolysis) ---
why infusions are saline, never pure water. (d) Root-to-leaf
$\mu$-gradients (solutes, evaporation at the leaf) pull water up
as osmotic and capillary pressure differences: tens of metres
without a single moving part.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:9}
(a) Uniform $\mu$: $k_{\text{B}}T\ln n(h) + mgh = $ const, i.e.\
the barometric exponential. (b) $n(r) \propto
\eu^{+m\omega^2r^2/2k_{\text{B}}T}$: heavy species pile at the
rim. (c) $\exp[\Delta m\,\omega^2r^2/2k_{\text{B}}T] =
\exp[\num{5e-27} \times \num{3.6e5}/(2 \times \num{4.4e-21})]
\approx \eu^{0.2} \approx 1.2$ per stage. (d) From natural
$0.7\%$ to reactor or weapons grade requires many
compounded factors of $1.2$: hence cascades of thousands of
centrifuges --- and why cascade counting is nuclear diplomacy.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:10}
(a) As in the text: exponentiate the $\mu$-balance with ideal-gas
\omterm{thm:b3:grand-canonical:distribution}{chemical potentials} and the atom's $-E_{\text{I}}$ offset. (b)
With $n_{\text{e}} = n_{\text{p}} = xn$ and $n_{\text{H}} =
(1-x)n$: the stated quotient. (c) At \qty{5800}{K}:
$\lambda_{T,\text{e}} \approx \qty{1.0}{nm}$,
$\eu^{-27.2} = \num{1.5e-12}$, $1/n\lambda^3 \approx \num{1.1e4}$:
$x^2/(1-x) \approx \num{1.6e-8}$, $x \approx \num{1.3e-4}$ ---
the solar photosphere is $99.99\%$ neutral hydrogen. (d) At
\qty{10000}{K} (same $n$): $x \approx 6\%$ --- two and a half
orders in a factor $1.7$ of temperature; at the photosphere's
lower electron densities the rise comes earlier still. Saha
transitions are cliffs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:11}
(a) $\num{1.7e-7}$: seven orders below threshold --- air is
safely classical. (b) Set $n\lambda_T^3 = 1$: $T_{\text{deg}} =
h^2n^{2/3}/2\pi mk_{\text{B}} \approx \qty{6e4}{K}$ for copper's
electrons --- every metal you have ever touched carries a
deep-quantum electron gas. (c) For rubidium at \qty{e20}{m^{-3}}:
$T_{\text{deg}} \sim \qty{e-7}{K}$ --- the hundred-nanokelvin
frontier of \cref{ch:b3:quantum-statistics}. (d) Electrons in
metals: always quantum; laboratory atoms: only at nanokelvins;
air: never --- degeneracy goes as $n^{2/3}/m$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:grand-canonical:12}
(a) With $\mu = 0$, the pair density is the Boltzmann-suppressed
$\lambda_T^{-3}\eu^{-\beta m_{\text{e}}c^2}$ once pairs are
non-relativistic. (b) $m_{\text{e}}c^2/k_{\text{B}} =
\qty{5.9e9}{K}$, reached about one second after the beginning.
(c) Cooling from $\qty{6e9}{K}$ to $\qty{2e9}{K}$ multiplies the
exponent by three: the density collapses by $\eu^{-2} \to$
$\eu^{-6}$ in the exponential alone and by orders more with the
prefactor --- within seconds the positrons are gone, annihilated
against all but one part in a billion of the electrons. (d) The
annihilation photons joined and slightly reheated the radiation
bath whose stretched remnant is the cosmic microwave background
--- the \qty{2.7}{K} receipt of the event.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:grand-canonical:1}
\textbf{1.} $n_2/n_1 = 4\,\eu^{-10.2\,\text{eV}/k_{\text{B}}T}$.
\textbf{2.} $\num{2e-10}$, $\num{2.9e-5}$, $\num{1.1e-2}$: rising
steeply --- hotter is better for exciting $n = 2$.
\textbf{3.} Saha ionises hydrogen away: above
$\sim\qty{10000}{K}$ the \emph{neutral} fraction collapses, taking
the Balmer lines with it.
\textbf{4.} One factor rises with $T$, the other falls: their
product must peak between the two regimes.
\textbf{5.} Setting $\lambda_{T,\text{e}}^{-3}\eu^{-E_{\text{I}}/
k_{\text{B}}T} = n_{\text{e}} = \qty{e20}{m^{-3}}$ gives $T
\approx \qty{9600}{K}$.
\textbf{6.} Near \qty{10000}{K} --- exactly the A stars, whose
spectra are hydrogen's showcase.
\textbf{7.} At \qty{3000}{K} nothing reaches $n = 2$
($\eu^{-39}$), while fragile molecules like TiO survive and
absorb in bands: cool stars are molecular, hydrogen-dark.
\textbf{8.} Hydrogen is ionised to invisibility; helium, with its
\qty{24.6}{eV} ladder, only now has $k_{\text{B}}T$ and Saha
conditions to populate and retain its absorbing states.
\textbf{9.} Calcium's \qty{6.1}{eV} ionisation is far cheaper
than hydrogen's \qty{13.6}{eV}: at \qty{5800}{K} Saha ionises
calcium nearly completely (the Ca$^+$ lines then absorb from its
ground state --- no Boltzmann tax), while neutral hydrogen sits
in $n = 1$, Balmer-blind.
\textbf{10.} That the Sun is mostly calcium and iron, resembling
the Earth's crust --- the reigning assumption Payne inherited.
\textbf{11.} Dividing each strength by its visibility factor,
the wild differences collapsed: most elements came out in similar
proportions --- except hydrogen (and helium), which emerged
about a \emph{million} times more abundant.
\textbf{12.} Cosmic chemistry was assumed to mirror terrestrial
rock; Russell pressed her to call the hydrogen result ``almost
certainly not real'' --- and published the same conclusion
himself, with credit, four years later. Payne was right.
\textbf{13.} $4\eu^{-10.2/0.827} = 4\eu^{-12.3} \approx
\num{1.8e-5}$.
\textbf{14.} Products $\approx \num{9e-6}$, $\num{1.5e-6}$,
$\num{3.5e-7}$: the maximum sits at $\approx\qty{9600}{K}$ --- the
A-star peak, reproduced with two exponentials.
\textbf{15.} On the hot side the Saha exponential annihilates the
neutral population outright; on the cool side the Boltzmann
factor merely dwindles: cliff above, slope below.
\textbf{16.} Higher $n_{\text{e}}$ pushes recombination (more
partners to capture): half-ionisation moves \emph{hotter} ---
squeezing the reaction toward the bound side, Le Chatelier in
$\mu$-language.
\textbf{17.} Surface gravity: compact dwarfs (high pressure) and
bloated giants (low) separate at one temperature --- luminosity
classes read from line physics.
\textbf{18.} Equal a priori states $\to$ Boltzmann populations
$\to$ Saha ionisation $\to$ line-strength ratios inverted for
$T$: a thermometer with no moving parts at any distance.
\textbf{19.} Temperature --- one axis, from M's \qty{3000}{K} to
O's \qty{40000}{K}, replaces the whole alphabet.
\textbf{20.} Visibility is populations, and populations are
exponentials in $T$: a spectral line's strength measures the
star's thermodynamics first and its chemistry only after
correction.
\textbf{21.} Hydrogen fusion as the stellar power source, and
the 21 cm cartography of the galaxies: both stand on Payne's
abundance.
\textbf{22.} With two ensemble formulas she converted forty years
of spectral taxonomy into thermometry and re-weighed the
universe's composition --- in a doctoral thesis.
\textbf{23.} Cosmic recombination: the universe's own
half-ionisation at $\sim\qty{3000}{K}$, whose released light,
stretched a thousandfold, is the \qty{2.7}{K} microwave
background.
\textbf{24.} The electrons (hot, \qty{e4}{K}-scale) and the gas
plus walls (cool) exchange energy too slowly to equilibrate: two
temperatures coexist in one tube --- a non-equilibrium steady
state, which is why the lamp glows without melting.
\textbf{25.} One condition, $\mu_{\text{H}} = \mu_{\text{p}} +
\mu_{\text{e}}$, orders OBAFGKM by temperature alone, parks the
Balmer maximum on the A stars near \qty{9600}{K}, and --- once
strengths are corrected for visibility --- reveals a universe of
hydrogen.
\end{solution}
