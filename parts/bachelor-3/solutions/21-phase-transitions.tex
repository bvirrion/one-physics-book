\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:phase-transitions} --- Phase Transitions}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:1}
(a) First-order: density jumps, latent heat flows; no symmetry
differs between liquid and gas --- which is why their line can
\emph{end}. (b) Continuous; \omterm{def:b3:phase-transitions:order}{order parameter} $m$; the up--down (and
rotational) symmetry breaks. (c) Continuous; the condensate's
macroscopic wave function (a phase angle breaks). (d) Continuous:
at the \omterm{prop:b3:phase-transitions:vdw}{critical point} the jump has shrunk to zero --- the one
boiling with no latent heat.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:2}

(b) $2.7\,k_{\text{B}}$. (c) Roughly $k_{\text{B}}\ln$ of the

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:3}
(a) $3/8$ regardless of substance --- the model's law of
corresponding states; real fluids' $0.29$ shows vdW is a caricature
near the \omterm{prop:b3:phase-transitions:vdw}{critical point}, where correlations rule. (b) $b =
k_{\text{B}}T_{\text{c}}/8P_{\text{c}} = \qty{7.1e-29}{m^3}$; $a =
27b^2P_{\text{c}} = \qty{1.0e-48}{J.m^3}$. (c) $b^{1/3} \approx
\qty{0.41}{nm}$ --- the molecular size, extracted from a steam
table. (d) Liquefaction by pressure alone requires $T <
T_{\text{c}}$: true for CO$_2$ at room temperature, false for
N$_2$ --- hence CO$_2$ cylinders hold liquid, nitrogen cylinders
only compressed gas.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:5}
(a) One spin in $B_{\text{eff}} \propto qJm$: $\langle s\rangle =
\tanh(\beta qJm)$; self-consistency closes the loop. (b) Nonzero
crossing exists iff the initial slope $\beta qJ > 1$. (c) $m =
\beta qJm - \tfrac13(\beta qJm)^3$ gives $m^2 = 3(1 -
T/T_{\text{c}})$ near $T_{\text{c}}$. (d) $J =
k_{\text{B}}T_{\text{c}}/q \approx \qty{11}{meV}$ per bond ---
the electron-volt-class exchange of
\cref{exo:b3:identical-particles:12}, shared over eight
neighbours: electrostatics, not magnetism, heats iron's Curie
point to a thousand kelvin.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:6}
(a) Linearising, $m = \beta(qJm + \mu B)$: $\chi =
\mu\beta/(1 - T_{\text{c}}/T) \propto 1/(T - T_{\text{c}})$. (b)
Both give straight $1/\chi$ lines; the free-spin line passes
through the origin, the ferromagnet's intercepts the axis at
$+T_{\text{c}}$: the intercept \emph{is} the coupling
diagnosis. (c) Nickel orders at \qty{631}{K}: its high-temperature
susceptibility already announces the Curie point it will reach.
(d) A negative intercept means $J < 0$: neighbours prefer
anti-alignment --- an antiferromagnet in waiting.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:7}
(a) $\dd P/\dd T = L/T\Delta v = \qty{3.6}{kPa/K}$. (b)
$\ln(0.7) = -4900(1/T - 1/373)$: $T \approx \qty{363}{K} =
\qty{90}{\celsius}$. (c) $\dd T/\dd P = T\Delta v/L =
\qty{-7.4e-8}{K/Pa}$: five kelvin needs $\sim\num{7e7}\,\unit{Pa}$
--- almost seven hundred atmospheres. (d) The skater's
$\sim\qty{2e7}{Pa}$ buys only $-1.7$ K: pressure-melting fails on
a cold rink; the lubricating film is made by friction heating and
by ice's intrinsic premelted surface layer.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:8}
(a) $m^2 = a(T_{\text{c}} - T)/2b$. (b) $F_{\min} =
-a^2(T_{\text{c}} - T)^2/4b$: $C = -T\partial^2F/\partial T^2$
jumps by $a^2T_{\text{c}}/2b$ at the transition. (c) Mean field:
a clean step; helium's measured $\lambda$-shaped, near-logarithmic
peak is the fluctuations Landau ignores --- the very shape gave
the transition its name. (d) With $-hm$ the symmetry is
pre-broken: $m$ is never exactly zero and every singularity is
rounded --- spontaneous breaking needs an unbiased choice.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:9}
(a) $\dd/\dd r$ of $4\pi r^2\sigma - \tfrac43\pi r^3n\delta\mu = 0$
at $r^* = 2\sigma/n\delta\mu$. (b) Small embryos shrink (surface
wins): a clean phase can wait, metastable, until a fluctuation
crosses the barrier; dust, ions and scratches offer ready-made
surfaces that slash it. (c) The ion trail seeds droplets (cloud
chamber) or bubbles (bubble chamber) along the particle's path:
the track \emph{is} a line of catalysed nucleation. (d) Their
pores pre-seed bubbles, preventing superheating and the violent
``bumping'' of an unseeded flask.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:10}
(a) Only the one broken bond: $2J$, wherever it sits. (b)
$k_{\text{B}}\ln N$ placements. (c) $\Delta F = 2J -
k_{\text{B}}T\ln N \to -\infty$: at any positive temperature
walls invade and order dies --- $T_{\text{c}}(1\text{D}) = 0$. (d)
A length-$\ell$ wall costs $2J\ell$ against entropy
$\sim k_{\text{B}}\ell\ln3$: for $T < 2J/k_{\text{B}}\ln3 \approx
1.8\,J/k_{\text{B}}$ long walls are suppressed and order survives
--- the right magnitude beside Onsager's exact
$2.27\,J/k_{\text{B}}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:11}
(a) Both \omterm{def:b3:phase-transitions:order}{order parameters} are one real number choosing a sign
(up/down; liquid/gas about the critical density): the same
symmetry class. (b) The correlation length diverges: each spin's
``mean field'' is precisely as fluctuating as itself, and
replacing neighbours by averages becomes untenable. (c) More
neighbours average better; above four dimensions the fluctuations'
reach grows slower than the averaging improves, and mean field
becomes exact. (d) The renormalisation group averages out the
shortest scales repeatedly, flowing every microscopic model of the
same symmetry and dimension to one \omterm{def:b3:hamiltonian-mechanics:portrait}{fixed point}; exponents are
properties of the \omterm{def:b3:hamiltonian-mechanics:portrait}{fixed point} --- which is why steam and magnets
share decimals.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:phase-transitions:12}
(a) With $\phi_0^2 = \alpha(T_{\text{c}} - T)/2b$: $F''(\phi_0) =
4\alpha(T_{\text{c}} - T) > 0$ --- oscillations along the radial
direction have a stiffness, i.e.\ (in field language) a mass that
grows as the well deepens. (b) Around a circular rim the
potential is flat: a zero-stiffness, massless mode --- the
Goldstone modes realised as spin waves in magnets and phonons in
superfluids. (c) The vacuum's $\phi_0 \neq 0$ is the condensate;
particle masses are couplings to $\phi_0$; the radial oscillation
is the Higgs boson, found at \qty{125}{GeV}. (d) That broken
symmetry, condensates and massive/massless modes were laboratory
commonplaces first: particle physics imported a solid-state idea.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:phase-transitions:1}
\textbf{1.} $\mu_{\text{liquid}}(T, P) = \mu_{\text{vapour}}(T,
P)$: boiling is coexistence, pressure by pressure.
\textbf{2.} $\qty{3.6}{kPa/K}$.
\textbf{3.} $\ln2 = 4900(1/373 - 1/T)$: $T \approx \qty{394}{K} =
\qty{121}{\celsius}$.
\textbf{4.} Twenty-one degrees at ``double per ten'': about four
times faster --- the pressure cooker's entire business case.
\textbf{5.} $\ln0.65$: $T \approx \qty{361}{K} =
\qty{88}{\celsius}$ --- tea tannins extract poorly twelve degrees
short.
\textbf{6.} Solute lowers the \emph{liquid's} $\mu$
(\cref{exo:b3:grand-canonical:8}); matching the vapour then needs
a higher temperature: boiling-point elevation.
\textbf{7.} $\dd T/\dd P = \qty{-7.4e-8}{K/Pa}$: negative ---
pressure \emph{melts} ice.
\textbf{8.} Ice floats: the solid is the less dense phase, the
anomaly ($\Delta v < 0$) that tilts the line backwards --- rare
because most solids pack tighter than their melts.
\textbf{9.} $P = \rho gh \approx \qty{2.7}{MPa}$: only
$-0.2$ K --- yet combined with geothermal heat it maintains basal
meltwater, the lubricant of glacier surges.
\textbf{10.} Friction heating and the premelted surface film;
the pressure myth persists because the sign of the slope is real
--- only its magnitude is too small.
\textbf{11.} Ice would sink and lakes freeze from the bottom up
into solid blocks; no insulating lid, no liquid refuge, no
freshwater overwintering life.
\textbf{12.} A dozen crystalline ices means a free-energy
landscape with many nearly-tied packings: one bent molecule,
many compromises --- polymorphism as the rule.
\textbf{13.} Both shrink continuously to zero: at the endpoint
the two phases merge and the first-order line dies as a
continuous point.
\textbf{14.} Heat at $P > P_{\text{c}}$ to $T > T_{\text{c}}$,
then decompress: liquid to gas with never a meniscus --- around
the mountain instead of over it.
\textbf{15.} Compressibility diverges, so $\sqrt N$ density
fluctuations grow to optical scales and scatter light strongly:
the fluid becomes its own cloud.
\textbf{16.} Supercritical CO$_2$ pulls caffeine from beans;
venting drops it below $T_{\text{c}}$--$P_{\text{c}}$ and the
``solvent'' simply evaporates away entirely.
\textbf{17.} Above \qty{221}{bar} there is no liquid--vapour
interface: no bubbles, no film boiling, no burnout crises ---
heat flows into one smooth fluid.
\textbf{18.} \qty{400}{\celsius} exceeds $T_{\text{c}} =
\qty{374}{\celsius}$: at \qty{300}{bar} the vent water is
supercritical --- boiling is not suppressed, it is
\emph{undefined}.
\textbf{19.} Three lines meeting at the triple point
(\qty{0.01}{\celsius}, \qty{611}{Pa}); the vaporisation line
climbing to its end at (\qty{647}{K}, \qty{221}{bar}); the
melting line leaning left; sublimation below the triple point.
\textbf{20.} Freeze-drying works below \qty{611}{Pa}, where ice
sublimes; Mars's caps sublime seasonally for the same reason.
\textbf{21.} Three coexisting phases leave zero freedoms (Gibbs):
the state is a single point --- unreproducible thermometry's
dream, hence its long service defining the kelvin.
\textbf{22.} On a line the \omterm{def:b3:phase-transitions:order}{order parameter} (density) jumps and
latent heat flows; at the endpoint the jump has shrunk to zero
and fluctuations replace it.
\textbf{23.} Where a cooling surface drags the local vapour's
$\mu$ above the liquid's, water must condense: dew, fog and
misted windows are $\mu$-crossings made visible.
\textbf{24.} Evaporation (liquid $\to$ vapour) at the surface,
condensation (vapour $\to$ liquid) on the lid: the latent heat
carried up is repaid into the pot instead of the kitchen --- the
lid closes the heat loop.
\textbf{25.} $\dd P/\dd T = L/T\Delta v$: \qty{121}{\celsius}
under two atmospheres, \qty{88}{\celsius} at La Paz, a melting
line running backwards, and boiling itself expiring at
(\qty{647}{K}, \qty{221}{bar}) --- the kitchen, mapped.
\end{solution}
