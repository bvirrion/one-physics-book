\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:3}
(a) Isoceles triangle with apex angle $\theta$: $q =
2k\sin(\theta/2)$. (b) $q$ runs from $0$ to $2k = \qty{2e15}{
m^{-1}}$: structures down to $\sim\qty{5e-16}{m}$ are in
principle encoded (the Coulomb barrier, in practice, keeps alphas
outside). (c) $k = E/\hbar c = \qty{2.5e15}{m^{-1}}$: resolution
$\sim\qty{2e-16}{m}$ --- inside the proton. (d) Resolution
$\sim 1/q_{\max} \sim \hbar/p$: buying energy is buying a shorter
ruler.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:4}
(a) $f$ is a length; $|f|^2$ an area --- per steradian by
construction. (b) Integrate $(\hbar k/m)|f|^2/r^2$ over the
sphere: $\Phi\sigma$. (c) Probability conservation: the outgoing
wave must interfere \emph{destructively} with the forward beam to
pay for what scatters; that bookkeeping lives in $f$'s phase. (d)
The optical theorem states exactly that shadow-audit: total
removal $=$ forward interference deficit.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:5}
(a) The angular integral gives $4\pi\sin(qr)/qr$; then, since
$\int_0^\infty\eu^{-\mu r}\sin(qr)\,\dd r = q/(q^2 + \mu^2)$,
one finds $f = -2mg/\hbar^2(q^2 + \mu^2)$. (b) $\mu \to 0$: $|f|^2 =
(2mg/\hbar^2q^2)^2$; with $q = 2k\sin(\theta/2)$ and $E =
\hbar^2k^2/2m$ this is Rutherford's formula. (c) The $\theta \to
0$ divergence integrates to infinity: every passing particle is
deflected a little by the infinite-range force; in matter, atomic
electrons screen the nucleus beyond $\sim\qty{e-10}{m}$ and cut
the divergence. (d) $\hbar/m_\pi c = 197.3/140 = \qty{1.4}{fm}$
--- the nuclear force's reach, predicted from the pion's mass.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:6}
(a) $\sin(ka + \delta_0) = 0$ with the smallest choice: $\delta_0
= -ka$. (b) $\sigma = (4\pi/k^2)\sin^2(ka) \to 4\pi a^2$. (c) At
$\lambda \gg a$ the wave feels the sphere as a point defect and
rebuilds itself by diffraction all around it --- ``shadow'' is a
short-wavelength concept, and the wave answer counts the full
sphere surface $4\pi a^2$. (d) Even at short wavelengths,
removing a disc of wave requires diffractive filling-in --- the
shadow itself scatters ($\pi a^2$ of blocking $+$ $\pi a^2$ of
shadow diffraction).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:7}
(a) $\pi(3 \times 5.4^2 + 23.7^2)\,\unit{fm^2} = \pi \times 649
\,\unit{fm^2} = \qty{20.4}{b}$ --- the measured value. (b) The
singlet: its huge $|a_{\text{s}}|$ outweighs its $1/4$ weight. (c)
$a_{\text{t}}$ large and positive: a real bound state (the
deuteron) barely below threshold; $a_{\text{s}}$ large and
\emph{negative}: a ``virtual'' level barely above --- almost a

(equal masses), and twenty barns of elastic scattering per proton
makes water a superbly compact moderator.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:8}
(a) $\sigma_0 = (4\pi/k^2)\sin^2\pi = 0$: the $s$ wave exits
exactly as if no atom were there. (b) The wave is strongly
distorted \emph{inside}, but emerges with an integer number of
extra half-waves: no observable phase offset, no scattering ---
destructive interference of the scattered wavelets. (c) $KR
\approx \pi$ with $E \ll V_0$: $V_0 \approx \pi^2\hbar^2/2mR^2
\approx \qty{9}{eV}$ --- an atomic-scale well. (d) Their
shallower wells never wind the phase through $\pi$ at eV
energies. The effect is un-mimickable classically: a transparent
window in a strong attraction exists only for waves.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:9}
(a) $\sigma = 4\pi\sin^2\delta_0/k^2$. (b) $\operatorname{Im}f(0)
= \sin^2\delta_0/k$: $(4\pi/k)\operatorname{Im}f(0) = \sigma$ ---
verified. (c) $\sin^2\delta_0 \le 1$: ceiling $4\pi/k^2 \approx
\qty{1e-20}{m^2} = \num{e8}$ barns for thermal neutrons ---
room above even the most monstrous absorbers (xenon-135's
millions of barns). (d) The ceiling is unitarity: a wave cannot
remove more flux than interference allows; strength saturates the
sine, never exceeds it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:10}
(a) $\tau = \hbar/\Gamma = \num{6.58e-16}/0.027 =
\qty{2.4e-14}{s}$ --- long on nuclear timescales: a compound
nucleus that ``forgets'' its formation. (b) $4\pi/k^2 \approx
\num{3.9e5}$ b; the observed $\num{2.3e4}$ b is the ceiling times
the branching fraction of the entrance channel ($\Gamma_n/\Gamma
\sim 0.06$) --- resonances sell tickets by partial widths. (c)
$\Gamma/k_{\text{B}} \approx \qty{310}{K}$: heating the fuel
Doppler-widens the resonance, catching more neutrons during
slow-down --- absorption grows with temperature, a prompt
\emph{negative} feedback built into uranium itself. (d) Between
thermal and fast energies, $^{238}$U's resonance forest devours
neutrons; fuel geometry and moderators are designed to sneak
neutrons past it, and its Doppler feedback is a pillar of
reactor safety.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:scattering-theory:12}
(a) $F(0) = \int\rho/Q = 1$. (b) Expand the exponential; the

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:scattering-theory:1}
\textbf{1.} $\theta \sim \num{8e-4}$ rad per atom; even a random
walk through $10^4$ atomic layers accumulates only a few degrees:
backward scattering is impossible in the pudding.
\textbf{2.} $E = k_CzZe^2/d_0$: $d_0 = k_CzZe^2/E =
\qty{4.1e-14}{m}$ --- forty femtometres.
\textbf{3.} That the full positive charge sits inside
$\sim\qty{4e-14}{m}$: ten thousand times smaller than the atom.
\textbf{4.} $b \to 0$: $\theta \to 180^\circ$; $b \to \infty$:
$\theta \to 0$ --- head-on rebounds, distant grazes.
\textbf{5.} $\theta > 90^\circ$ needs $b < d_0/2 =
\qty{2.1e-14}{m}$: fraction $nt\,\pi b^2 \approx \num{3e-5}$ ---
one alpha in thirty thousand for this foil.
\textbf{6.} The same order as Geiger and Marsden's one in eight
thousand (their foils were thicker): the ``impossible'' rebounds
arrive exactly as often as a point nucleus demands.
\textbf{7.} $\dd\sigma = 2\pi b|\dd b|$ with $b =
(d_0/2)\cot(\theta/2)$: $|\dd b/\dd\theta| = (d_0/4)/\sin^2(
\theta/2)$, and $\dd\Omega = 2\pi\sin\theta\,\dd\theta$;
assembling, the $\sin\theta = 2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2)$
cancellations leave $(d_0/4)^2\sin^{-4}(\theta/2)$.
\textbf{8.} $d_0/4 = k_CzZe^2/4E = Q_1Q_2/16\pi\varepsilon_0E$:
the Born result --- the $1/r$ coincidence.
\textbf{9.} $(d_0/4)^2 = \qty{1.06}{b/sr}$: $236$, $17$ and
$\qty{1.9}{b/sr}$ at $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $120^\circ$.
\textbf{10.} $(\sin60^\circ/\sin15^\circ)^4 \approx 125$: their
counts tracked such ratios across five decades of rate --- the
law, not a trend.
\textbf{11.} The rate scales as $Z^2$ at fixed geometry:
comparing foils calibrates the nuclear charge itself, feeding the
identification of $Z$ with the atomic number.
\textbf{12.} Every rate drops fourfold; the angular \emph{shape}
is untouched --- a clean experimental signature of $1/E^2$.
\textbf{13.} $d(180^\circ) = d_0 = \qty{41}{fm}$; $d(60^\circ) =
(d_0/2)(1 + 2) = \qty{62}{fm}$.
\textbf{14.} The formula held at the largest angles: the gold
nucleus is smaller than $\sim\qty{4e-14}{m}$.
\textbf{15.} The projectile begins to touch the nucleus: the
short-range \emph{strong} force (and absorption) sets in at
femtometre distances --- the departure measures the nuclear
edge.
\textbf{16.} $R = 1.2 \times 197^{1/3} = \qty{7.0}{fm}$ ---
comfortably inside Rutherford's bound.
\textbf{17.} Volume $\propto A$: nuclear matter has a fixed
density ($\sim\qty{2e17}{kg/m^3}$) --- nuclei are droplets of an
incompressible liquid, the starting picture of
\cref{ch:b3:nuclear-physics}.
\textbf{18.} Spectroscopy interrogated the electrons; only a
projectile that penetrates the atom could testify where the
\emph{positive} charge and the mass sit.
\textbf{19.} Electrons at $20$ GeV scattering at improbably
large angles and energy losses --- too many hard events for a
soft proton: Rutherford's rebound, re-enacted.
\textbf{20.} Electrons feel no strong force and have no known
substructure: a point probe reading charge alone, with none of
the alpha's own compositeness.
\textbf{21.} The elastic form factor's collapse says the proton
is a diffuse cloud; the persistence of hard \emph{inelastic}
scattering says the cloud contains point-like constituents ---
quarks (partons).
\textbf{22.} 1911: $\sim\qty{e-14}{m}$; SLAC: $\hbar c/q \sim
\qty{0.2}{fm} = \qty{2e-16}{m}$; LHC: $\sim\qty{2e-19}{m}$ ---
five orders of magnitude of ruler in one century.
\textbf{23.} Hydrogen positions (in ice, polymers, proteins) and
magnetic structures (antiferromagnets, spin spirals) --- both
nearly invisible to X-rays, both bread and butter for neutrons.
\textbf{24.} The processes worth discovering occur once per
$10^{15}$ ordinary encounters: only colossal luminosity turns
femtobarns into events per year --- collider design is
\omterm{def:b3:scattering-theory:cross-section}{cross-section} arithmetic.
\textbf{25.} A $\sin^{-4}(\theta/2)$ law, checked flash by flash,
concentrated the atom's mass into $10^{-14}$ of its volume; run
at ever higher $q$, the same experiment found the nucleus's size,
then its constituents, and is still looking.
\end{solution}
