\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter} --- Electromagnetism in Matter}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:1}
(a) $+P$ on the face $\vect P$ points at, $-P$ on the other. (b)
Two infinite sheets $\pm P$ produce $P/\varepsilon_0$ between them,
directed from $+$ to $-$, i.e.\ \emph{against} $\vect P$. (c)
$\vect D = \varepsilon_0\vect E + \vect P = 0$ --- as it must be:
no free charge, and $\vect D$'s normal component is continuous from
the vacuum outside. (d) A bar magnet: frozen-in dipole density,
field sustained by its own bound sources.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:2}
(a) $C = \varepsilon_{\text{r}}C_0 = \qty{400}{pF}$. (b) $Q =
\qty{1.2}{nC}$ is trapped: $V = Q/C = \qty{3}{V}$. (c)
$\tfrac12C_0V_0^2 = \qty{7.2}{nJ}$ before, $\qty{1.8}{nJ}$ after:
the missing \qty{5.4}{nJ} became mechanical work --- the fringing
field \emph{pulls the slab in}. (d) Battery connected: $V$ stays
\qty{12}{V}, $Q$ quadruples, energy rises to \qty{28.8}{nJ} ---
the battery pays for both the new field energy and the pull.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:3}
Water: diamagnetic --- all electrons paired, only Larmor-induced
opposition. Aluminium: paramagnetic (conduction electrons' spins).
Liquid oxygen: strongly paramagnetic --- O$_2$ carries \emph{two
unpaired} electrons (Hund's rule filling of its antibonding
orbitals, \cref{ch:b3:identical-particles}); it visibly sticks
between magnet poles. Nickel: ferromagnetic. The magnet visibly
attracts nickel and liquid oxygen, and ever-so-slightly repels
water --- the dimple a strong magnet makes in a water surface.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:4}
(a) $H = nI = \qty{3000}{A/m}$. (b) $M = B/\mu_0 - H =
\qty{1.27e6}{A/m}$. (c) $B/\mu_0H \approx 420$. (d) The
equivalent bound sheet current is $M \approx \qty{1.27e6}{A}$ per
metre --- four hundred times the coil's own $nI = \qty{3000}{A/m}$:
the iron is doing almost all the work.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:5}
(a) A cylindrical Gauss surface encloses only free charge:
$D = \lambda/2\pi r$, radial. (b) $E =
\lambda/2\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\text{r}}r$, $P =
(\varepsilon_{\text{r}}-1)\lambda/2\pi\varepsilon_{\text{r}}r$.
(c) $\sigma_{\text{b}}(a) = -P(a)$: per unit length
$-(\varepsilon_{\text{r}}-1)\lambda/\varepsilon_{\text{r}}$;
at $r = b$, $+P(b)$: per unit length
$+(\varepsilon_{\text{r}}-1)\lambda/\varepsilon_{\text{r}}$ ---
sum zero, as \omterm{def:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:polarisation}{bound charge} must. (d) $V = \int E\,\dd r =
(\lambda/2\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\text{r}})\ln(b/a)$, so
$C/\ell = 2\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\text{r}}/\ln(b/a)$:
multiplied by $\varepsilon_{\text{r}}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:6}
(a) $n = P/k_{\text{B}}T = \qty{2.4e25}{m^{-3}}$, so
$\alpha/\varepsilon_0 = \chi/n \approx \qty{2.2e-29}{m^3}$ ---
the volume of a sphere of radius $\approx\qty{1.7}{\angstrom}$:
polarisability \emph{is} atomic volume, in $\varepsilon_0$ units.
(b) $n\alpha/3\varepsilon_0 = 0.15$, so $(\varepsilon_{\text{r}} -
1)/(\varepsilon_{\text{r}} + 2) = 0.15$ gives
$\varepsilon_{\text{r}} = 1.53$ --- dead on the measured value.
(c) The local-field correction is a 15\,\% effect here; argon is
nonpolar, so there is no orientation contribution to run away. (d)
Polar molecules (water, HCl): permanent dipoles dominate and
interact; Langevin--Debye (and, for dense liquids, Onsager) theory
replaces the simple cavity argument.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:7}
(a) $\chi_{\text{e}} = np^2/3\varepsilon_0k_{\text{B}}T \approx
11.5$. (b) $E = k_{\text{B}}T/p \approx \qty{6.7e8}{V/m}$ ---
ten times the breakdown field: water always operates deep in the
linear regime. (c) At \qty{2.45}{GHz} the dipoles almost keep up
but lag: the out-of-phase part of the response does net work on
the water every cycle --- \omterm{def:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:polarisation}{dielectric} heating. In ice the molecules
are locked to the lattice; their rotational relaxation sits at
kilohertz, so at gigahertz ice barely absorbs (defrost cycles
pulse gently, letting melted water do the heating). (d) Water:
negative ($1/T$ orientation response); argon: essentially zero ---
induced polarisability does not care about temperature.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:8}
(a) The Larmor shift changes the circulating current by $\Delta I
= -e\Delta\omega/2\pi = -e^2B/4\pi m_{\text{e}}$, hence
$\Delta\mu = \Delta I\cdot\pi r^2 = -e^2r^2B/4m_{\text{e}}$,
opposing $\vect B$ whichever way the electron orbits ---
Lenz's law at the atomic scale. (b) With the stated numbers,
$\chi_{\text{m}} \approx -9.5\times10^{-6}$: the measured
$-9\times10^{-6}$, from a ring model. (c) The induced moment
comes from orbit distortion, not from a Boltzmann competition
between alignment and disorder --- no $T$ anywhere. (d) Water is
diamagnetic, so the frog is pushed toward weak field; the force
density $\propto \chi\,\nabla(B^2/2\mu_0)$ vanishes in a uniform
field --- levitation needs $B\,\dd B/\dd z$ large enough to
balance $\rho g$, hence the \qty{16}{T} bore.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:9}
(a) $\chi_{\text{m}} = \mu_0n\mu_{\text{B}}^2/3k_{\text{B}}T
\approx 1.7\times10^{-5}$ at \qty{300}{K}, $5\times10^{-3}$ at
\qty{1}{K}. (b) Extrapolating, $\chi \to 1$ near \qty{5}{mK} ---
but dipolar and exchange couplings order (or freeze) real salts
first: Curie's law is a high-temperature law. (c) Magnetised at
\qty{1}{K}, the spin entropy is squeezed out into the bath;
isolated, $S$ is fixed, and lowering $B$ lets the spins reclaim
their $k_{\text{B}}\ln 2$ each --- the energy comes from the
lattice, whose temperature falls (millikelvins in practice). (d)
A ferromagnet's spins order themselves below $T_{\text{c}}$: their
entropy is no longer field-controlled, and \omterm{def:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:hysteresis}{hysteresis} would
dissipate instead of cool.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:10}
(a) $NI = B(\ell/\mu_0\mu_{\text{r}} + e/\mu_0)$: with
$\ell/\mu_{\text{r}} = \qty{0.15}{mm}$-equivalent versus $e =
\qty{4}{mm}$, $NI \approx 3960$ ampere-turns, $I \approx
\qty{7.9}{A}$. (b) $4/4.15 \approx 96\,\%$ of the effort crosses
the gap. (c) $\Phi = NI/(\mathcal R_{\text{iron}} +
\mathcal R_{\text{gap}})$ with $\mathcal R = \ell/\mu A$:
magnetomotive force $NI$ plays voltage, flux plays current,
reluctance plays resistance --- here $\mathcal R_{\text{gap}}
\approx 2.0\times10^{6}\,\unit{H^{-1}} \approx
27\,\mathcal R_{\text{iron}}$. (d) Past \qty{1.8}{T} the iron's
incremental $\mu_{\text{r}}$ collapses toward 1: its reluctance
soars, extra current buys almost no extra flux, and a motor pushed
there stops gaining torque while its windings cook.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:11}
(a) $P = wfV = 40\times50\times\num{4e-3} = \qty{8}{W}$ ---
acceptable. (b) $\qty{1.2}{kW}$: the core would glow; hard steel
is disqualified from AC service by its own virtue. (c) Eddy EMFs
scale with loop area and $f$; laminating (or powdering, or using
insulating ferrites) chops the loops, cutting the $f^2$ loss ---
which is why ferrites own the megahertz range. (d) Memory wants
the stored bit's barrier $\sim\mu_0H_{\text{c}}M_{\text{s}}V \gg
k_{\text{B}}T$ for a decade (the superparamagnetic limit), i.e.\
$H_{\text{c}}$ as large as writable; a transformer wants
$H_{\text{c}} \to 0$ for a thin loop. One material cannot sit at
both ends: soft magnets for machines, hard magnets for memory.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:12}
(a) A long axially magnetised needle is a long solenoid of bound
current: inside, $H \approx 0$ (all $B$ comes from $M$). A thin
plate magnetised across its faces is the shortest, fattest
solenoid possible: its interior return field gives $H = -M$. (b)
The stubby magnet works at $H = -N_{\text{d}}M$, well down its
demagnetisation curve: wall motion nibbles the \omterm{def:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:hysteresis}{remanence} away; a
needle sits at $H \approx 0$ and keeps what it has. (c) Hence
compass \emph{needles}, and the elongated single-domain grains of
magnetic tape and early hard disks --- shape anisotropy as memory
insurance. (d) Flux conservation and $\oint\vect H\cdot
\dd\vect\ell = 0$ give the interior of a sphere $H_{\text{in}} =
H_0 - M/3$; as $\mu_{\text{r}}\to\infty$ the iron drives
$H_{\text{in}}\to0$, so $M \to 3H_0 = 3B_0/\mu_0$: the sphere can
concentrate the field only threefold, however ``good'' the iron.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:1}
\textbf{1.} $B \approx \mu_0NI/\ell = \qty{13}{mT}$ --- a
refrigerator-magnet field from \qty{250}{W} of coil.
\textbf{2.} The coil's small $H$ unpins and rotates domains whose
bound currents then circulate in step with the coil's: the iron
adds $\mu_0M \gg \mu_0H$ of its own.
\textbf{3.} $\mu_0M_{\text{s}} = \qty{2.1}{T}$: no coil can pull
more from iron's poles.
\textbf{4.} $M = 2.2\mu_{\text{B}}n = 2.2\times\num{9.27e-24}
\times\num{8.5e28} \approx \qty{1.7e6}{A/m}$ --- consistent.
\textbf{5.} Any rust or grit is an extra series air gap in the one
place reluctance matters most; a tenth of a millimetre of scale
measurably eats the force.
\textbf{6.} Flux leaves the central north pole, crosses the
contact gap into the bale, runs through the steel, and returns
through the outer annular south pole and the yoke --- the load
closes the magnetic circuit.
\textbf{7.} $NI = H_{\text{iron}}\ell + 2H_{\text{gap}}e =
B(\ell/\mu_0\mu_{\text{r}} + 2e/\mu_0)$.
\textbf{8.} Per unit area: $\ell/\mu_{\text{r}} = \qty{0.6}{mm}$
versus $2e = \qty{3}{mm}$: the three millimetres of air take
$\sim83\,\%$ of the ampere-turns.
\textbf{9.} The linear formula gives $B = \mu_0NI/(\ell/
\mu_{\text{r}} + 2e) \approx \qty{3.5}{T}$ --- \emph{above} the
question-3 ceiling: the pot saturates and delivers $B \approx
\qty{1.7}{T}$, coil straining notwithstanding.
\textbf{10.} $B^2/2\mu_0 \approx \qty{1.15e6}{Pa} \approx
\qty{1150}{kPa}$ --- eleven atmospheres of pull.
\textbf{11.} $F = \num{1.15e6}\times0.12 \approx \qty{140}{kN}
\approx 14$ tonnes.
\textbf{12.} Yes: 14 tonnes against a 4-tonne requirement
(2 tonnes $\times$ safety factor 2) --- the margin exists for
question 13's sake.
\textbf{13.} With $e = \qty{4}{mm}$ and $A_{\text{eff}} =
\qty{0.04}{m^2}$: $B \approx \qty{1.5}{T}$, pressure
$\approx\qty{870}{kPa}$, force $\approx\qty{35}{kN} \approx 3.5$
tonnes --- the twelve-tonne ``flat plate'' rating shrinks to
barely the spec on real scrap, which is why capacity is always
quoted on ground plate.
\textbf{14.} \omterm{def:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:hysteresis}{Remanence}: switch off and the iron sits at
$B_{\text{r}}$ on its loop --- the pot is now a weak permanent
magnet, and light loads hang on.
\textbf{15.} Soft iron's thin loop makes $B_{\text{r}}$ and
$H_{\text{c}}$ small: the magnet must \emph{forget} on command;
hard steel would turn the lifter into a permanent magnet with a
switch that does nothing.
\textbf{16.} A calibrated reverse pulse drives the material to
$-H_{\text{c}}$, the loop's zero-$B$ crossing, and releases the
load.
\textbf{17.} A decaying AC sweep traces ever-smaller nested loops
spiralling into the origin: the demagnetised state --- the same
degaussing used on ship hulls and old CRT screens.
\textbf{18.} \qty{800}{\celsius} is \qty{1073}{K} $> T_{\text{c}}$:
the slab is \emph{paramagnetic} --- $\mu_{\text{r}} \approx 1$,
the circuit opens, the force collapses; hot mills move slabs with
tongs, not magnets.
\textbf{19.} Doubling $e$ halves $B$ and quarters the force
(gap-dominated circuit); doubling $NI$ restores both --- but
$P = RI^2$ quadruples: gaps are paid for in copper heat.
\textbf{20.} $R = \rho L/A = \num{1.7e-8}\times1000/
\num{1.6e-5} \approx \qty{1.1}{\ohm}$.
\textbf{21.} $P = RI^2 \approx \qty{660}{W}$; over
$8\,\text{h}\times0.6$: $\approx\qty{3.2}{kWh}$ per shift ---
a few euros of electricity to move hundreds of tonnes.
\textbf{22.} Gap volume $2Ae = \qty{3.6e-4}{m^3}$ at
\qty{1.15e6}{J/m^3}: $\approx\qty{410}{J}$ --- less than one
second of coil heating. Steady-state, essentially all electrical
input becomes copper heat; the field, once built, costs only its
maintenance current.
\textbf{23.} Opening the circuit forces $LI$ to zero abruptly:
the coil answers with a huge inductive spike that arcs the
contacts; a freewheel diode (or resistor) lets the stored field
energy die quietly as heat --- and the loop's area is dissipated
in the iron each cycle regardless.
\textbf{24.} The lifting force needs current: the bale drops.
\omterm{def:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter:hysteresis}{Remanence} retains only a token force --- kilograms, not tonnes.
Hence the walkway rule, and battery-backed (or permanent-magnet
plus release-coil) lifters for anything that dangles over people.
\textbf{25.} Ceiling: $\mu_0M_{\text{s}} \approx \qty{2}{T}$.
Force: $B^2A/2\mu_0$, ruled by millimetres of air gap. Control:
soft iron, small $B_{\text{r}}$, degauss pulse to drop. Cost:
$RI^2$, a kilowatt-scale heater --- the chapter, hanging from a
crane.
\end{solution}
