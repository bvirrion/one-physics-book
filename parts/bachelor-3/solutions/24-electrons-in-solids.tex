\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:electrons-in-solids} --- Electrons in Solids}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:1}
(a) $\tau = m_{\text{e}}/ne^2\rho = \qty{2.5e-14}{s}$. (b) $\ell =
v_{\text{F}}\tau \approx \qty{39}{nm}$. (c) About 110 \omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{lattice
constants}. (d) An electron sailing past a hundred ions without
scattering cannot be bouncing off the ions themselves --- the
\omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{lattice} must be transparent to it, exactly what Bloch's theorem
(\cref{thm:b3:electrons-in-solids:bloch}) later proves.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:2}
(a) $v_{\text{d}} = I/neA = \qty{7.3e-4}{m/s}$ --- under a
millimetre per second. (b) About 23 minutes. (c) The \emph{field}
establishes itself along the wire at nearly light speed and sets
the whole electron sea drifting at once: the marchers are slow,
the order to march is not. (d) $v_{\text{d}}/v_{\text{F}} \sim
10^{-9}$: conduction is an imperceptible bias on a violent
quantum motion, not a calm classical flow.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:3}
(a) Half-filled band: \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:classes}{metal}, $\dd\rho/\dd T > 0$. (b) Two
electrons would fill its band exactly --- magnesium conducts
because its full band \emph{overlaps} the next empty one: \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:classes}{metal},
$\dd\rho/\dd T > 0$. (c) Full band, \qty{5.5}{eV} gap: \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:classes}{insulator}
(formally $\dd\rho/\dd T < 0$, but with essentially no carriers
to count). (d) \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:classes}{Semiconductor}: $\dd\rho/\dd T < 0$, the
exponential sign flip that betrays the class.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:4}
(a) $\lambda = hc/E_{\text{g}} = \qty{1.1}{\micro m}$: silicon is
transparent in the infrared beyond that --- which is why infrared
cameras can be lensed in silicon. (b) \qty{225}{nm}, in the
ultraviolet: no visible photon can be absorbed, so diamond is
water-clear. (c) \qty{365}{nm}: a gap wide enough for blue
(\qty{2.8}{eV}) exists in nitrides and almost nowhere else
practical --- the blue LED waited decades on materials growth.
(d) Its gap fixes its photon: overdriving adds heat, not gap
width, and the diode cooks dark --- an LED's colour is a
material constant, not a brightness setting.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:5}
(a) $\kappa/\sigma T = 400/(5.9\times10^7\times300) =
\qty{2.3e-8}{W.\ohm/K^2}$. (b) Within ten percent of $L$. (c)
The same Fermi-surface electrons carry both the charge and the
heat, so their scattering time cancels in the ratio. (d) The
steel pan conducts heat to the food because its electrons move;
the wooden handle, an \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:classes}{insulator} in both senses, keeps them ---
and the heat --- out of your hand.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:6}
(a) Fifty percent extra --- flagrantly absent from experiment.
(b) $T/T_{\text{F}} \approx 0.004$: the electronic term is
throttled to under a percent. (c) The \omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{lattice}'s $T^3$ collapses
faster than the electrons' $T$: below a few kelvin the
``negligible'' electrons are all that is left. (d) $C/T =
\gamma + \beta T^2$: the intercept $\gamma$ weighs the electrons,
the slope $\beta$ the \omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{lattice} --- one graph, both tenants.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:7}
(a) $k_{\text{F}} = (3\pi^2n)^{1/3} = \qty{1.36e10}{m^{-1}}$:
$\lambda_{\text{F}} = \qty{4.6}{\angstrom}$. (b) $2a =
\qty{7.2}{\angstrom}$: the same order --- copper's Fermi surface
reaches toward the zone boundary, and the gap-opening physics
happens \emph{at} the energies that matter. (c) One standing wave
piles its charge on the positive ions (lower electrostatic
energy), the other between them (higher): same wavelength, two
energies --- the gap. (d) The 110-lattice-constant free path:
Bloch waves do not scatter off a perfect \omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{lattice}, so only
vibrations and impurities remain to resist.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:8}
(a) $E_{\text{g}}/2k_{\text{B}} = \qty{6500}{K}$:
$\exp[6500(1/300 - 1/400)] \approx 230$. (b) The thermistor's
resistance plunges exponentially --- a steep, sensitive curve;
platinum's climbs gently and linearly (more phonons). One is a
thermometer by carrier count, the other by scattering. (c)
Carriers are born in electron--hole \emph{pairs}, and the
equilibrium $np = n_{\text{i}}^2$ splits the gap's cost between
the two --- hence $E_{\text{g}}/2$. (d)
$n_{\text{i}}$ reaches $\qty{5e21}{m^{-3}}$ near \qty{760}{K}:
the \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:doping}{doping} drowns and the circuit forgets its design. Real
devices give up earlier --- their reverse leaks, doubling every
ten kelvin, misbehave first.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:9}
(a) $\qty{5e22}{m^{-3}}$ --- five million times $n_{\text{i}}$.
(b) The same factor $\sim5\times10^6$: one speck per million
atoms owns the conductivity outright. (c) Because accidental ppm
would do the same uninvited: only a \omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{crystal} pure to ppb has a
conductivity that belongs to the designer. (d) $d \sim n^{-1/3}
\approx \qty{27}{nm}$, ten times the \qty{2.4}{nm} orbit: \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:doping}{donors}
are isolated hydrogens; push the \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:doping}{doping} a hundredfold and the
orbits touch --- an impurity band, and eventually a \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:classes}{metal}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:10}
(a) $E = 13.6\times0.26/11.7^2 \approx \qty{26}{meV}$ (the
measured phosphorus value, \qty{45}{meV}, keeps the scale
honest). (b) Comparable to $k_{\text{B}}T = \qty{26}{meV}$:
essentially all \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:doping}{donors} are ionised at room temperature --- the
premise of \cref{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:doping}. (c) $a =
\qty{0.53}{\angstrom}\times\varepsilon_{\text{r}}/(m^*/
m_{\text{e}}) \approx \qty{2.4}{nm}$. (d) An orbit spanning
dozens of cells sees the \omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{crystal} as a smooth medium --- which is
precisely the condition under which a bulk
$\varepsilon_{\text{r}}$ and a band-averaged $m^*$ are legitimate:
the approximation certifies itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:11}
(a) $V_{\text{bi}} = 0.0259\ln(10^{12}) \approx \qty{0.71}{V}$.
(b) $eV/k_{\text{B}}T = 19.3$: ratio $\eu^{19.3} \approx
2\times10^{8}$ --- the diode is a one-way street to eight
digits. (c) $I_0 \propto n_{\text{i}}^2 \propto
\eu^{-E_{\text{g}}/k_{\text{B}}T}$: the same exponential as
\cref{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:8}, hence the
rule-of-thumb doubling. (d) The four diodes form a diamond: each
half-cycle, whichever pair is forward-biased steers the current
through the load in the \emph{same} direction --- AC in, bumpy
DC out.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:electrons-in-solids:12}
(a) Narrow the gap and more photons clear it but each delivers
only the small gap energy; widen it and each photon pays more
but fewer qualify: harvest $\times$ voltage peaks in between.
(b) Just below the \qty{1.3}{eV} optimum: silicon's ideal
ceiling is $\sim30\,\%$ --- close enough that its cheapness wins.
(c) The wide-gap top cell takes the blue at high voltage, passing
the red to the narrow-gap cell below: each photon is harvested
near its own energy, thermalisation shrinks, and the stack's
ceiling climbs toward the forties. (d) Heat raises $I_0$
exponentially, dragging down the open-circuit voltage
$\sim(k_{\text{B}}T/e)\ln(I_{\text{L}}/I_0)$; and the gap itself
narrows slightly, trading voltage for current it cannot fully
recover.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:electrons-in-solids:1}
\textbf{1.} $\lambda_{\text{peak}} \approx \qty{500}{nm}$:
about \qty{2.5}{eV}.
\textbf{2.} $hc/E_{\text{g}} \approx \qty{1.1}{\micro m}$.
\textbf{3.} It sails through the cells (no final state to absorb
into) and ends as heat in the backing --- warming the roof, not
the wires.
\textbf{4.} $\qty{1.12}{eV}$ survives as the pair; the excess
\qty{1.4}{eV} drains into phonons within picoseconds --- faster
than any circuit could intercept.
\textbf{5.} Sub-gap transparency ($\sim20\,\%$ of the power) and
above-gap thermalisation ($\sim30\,\%$): the spectrum is taxed
half before the junction sees a single electron.
\textbf{6.} Absorption is a single-photon quantum jump needing a
real final state; at half the gap there is none to pause in, and
two-photon events are negligible at sunlight's photon densities.
\textbf{7.} Pairs created in or near the \omterm{prop:b3:electrons-in-solids:junction}{depletion zone} feel the
built-in field: electrons are swept to the n side, \omterm{def:b3:electrons-in-solids:doping}{holes} to the
p side, and the field separates them faster than they can find
each other to recombine --- charge accumulates until an external
circuit relieves it as current.
\textbf{8.} $V_{\text{bi}} = 0.0259\ln(10^{12}) \approx
\qty{0.71}{V}$.
\textbf{9.} $60\times\qty{0.6}{V} = \qty{36}{V}$.
\textbf{10.} $I = 400/36 \approx \qty{11}{A}$.
\textbf{11.} Panel-wide, above-gap light is
$\sim\qty{1400}{W}$ at $\sim\qty{2}{eV}$ each: $4\times10^{21}$
photons per second --- but the sixty cells are in \emph{series},
so the \qty{11}{A} (an electron flux $I/e \approx
7\times10^{19}\,\unit{s^{-1}}$) passes through each cell, whose
own share of photons is $4\times10^{21}/60 \approx
7\times10^{19}$ per second: nearly every absorbed photon yields
a collected electron. The quantum efficiency is excellent; the
losses are energetic, not numeric.
\textbf{12.} After the spectrum's 50\,\%, the cell pays the
voltage deficit ($0.6/1.12 \approx 54\,\%$) and a few points of
reflection and recombination: $0.5\times0.54 \approx 27\,\%$,
trimmed to the rated 20\,\%.
\textbf{13.} The leakage $I_0$: the open-circuit voltage
$(k_{\text{B}}T/e)\ln(I_{\text{L}}/I_0)$ stops where
photocurrent and diode leak balance --- about \qty{0.6}{V}, well
short of the gap.
\textbf{14.} $\sin30^\circ = 0.5$: half the noon rating from
geometry alone --- before clouds.
\textbf{15.} $\Delta T = \qty{40}{K}$: $-16\,\%$, leaving
$\approx\qty{335}{W}$ --- the sunniest days are not the best
days per watt.
\textbf{16.} Heat inflates $I_0$ exponentially, and the
open-circuit voltage --- a logarithm's rebuke --- falls a
fraction of a percent per kelvin; the slightly shrunken gap
finishes the job.
\textbf{17.} Series wiring forces one common current: the shaded
cell, generating none, is driven into reverse bias by its
fifty-nine colleagues and dissipates their power as a hot spot
--- the string throttles to the weakest cell.
\textbf{18.} The bypass diode gives the current a forward path
around the shaded sub-string, sacrificing its voltage instead of
the whole panel's output.
\textbf{19.} $\qty{400}{W}\times0.15\times\qty{8760}{h} \approx
\qty{530}{kWh}$ per year.
\textbf{20.} $\approx\qty{130}{euros}$ per year: payback in
roughly $300/130 \approx 2.5$ years, then two decades of profit.
\textbf{21.} $500/530 \approx$ one year: the panel repays its
manufacturing energy about as fast as its price.
\textbf{22.} Blackness is not a choice but a \omterm{thm:b3:electrons-in-solids:bloch}{band structure}:
absorption needs an empty state one photon-energy above a full
one, and below the gap silicon simply has none to offer ---
paint cannot add states.
\textbf{23.} Stack a wide-gap cell on top to take the blue at
high voltage and pass the red down: the tandem beats the
single-junction ceiling. The catch: the series stack must match
currents (and prices) between layers.
\textbf{24.} Whatever shortens $\tau$ and poisons junctions:
UV-created defects and in-diffused impurities scatter and trap
carriers, moisture corrodes contacts, and hot spots age the
diodes --- the slow entropy of a \omterm{def:b3:crystalline-solids:lattice}{crystal} asked to sit in the sun
for thirty years.
\textbf{25.} The gap harvests half the sun; the junction turns
pairs into \qty{36}{V} of ordered current; series strings,
shade and summer heat take their cut; and at
$\sim\qty{530}{kWh}$ a year against a one-year energy and
three-year money payback, the arbiter rules: roof it.
\end{solution}
