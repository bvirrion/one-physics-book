\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:1}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:2}
(a) $\int_0^{E_{\text{F}}}\epsilon\,g\,\dd\epsilon/\int g\,\dd
\epsilon = \tfrac35 E_{\text{F}}$ from $g \propto \sqrt\epsilon$.
(b) $v_{\text{F}} = \sqrt{2E_{\text{F}}/m_{\text{e}}} =
\qty{1.6e6}{m/s}$; r.m.s.\ $\sqrt{3/5}\,v_{\text{F}} \approx
\qty{1.2e6}{m/s}$. (c) $\tfrac32 k_{\text{B}}T = \tfrac35
E_{\text{F}}$: $T \approx \qty{3.3e4}{K}$. (d) There is nowhere to
go: every lower state is filled, so no photon can be emitted and no
collision can slow them --- speed without heat, by exclusion.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:3}

\qty{3.9e10}{Pa} \approx \num{4e5}$ atmospheres. (b) The Coulomb

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:4}
(a) $T_{\text{c}} \approx \qty{0.4}{\micro K}$ --- the JILA
condensate appeared at \qty{170}{nK}: same physics, slightly lower
peak density. (b) $T_{\text{c}} \propto 1/m$: $\times87 \approx
\qty{35}{\micro K}$. (c) At high density and such cold, every gas
but spin-polarised hydrogen \emph{solidifies}; three-body
collisions make molecules. The trick is a dilute, metastable gas
cold enough that $\lambda_T$ compensates the dilution. (d)
$\lambda_{T_{\text{c}}} = h/\sqrt{2\pi mk_{\text{B}}T_{\text{c}}}
\approx \qty{0.3}{\micro m}$: $n\lambda^3 = \num{e20} \times
\num{2.7e-20} \approx 2.7$, as the rule requires.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:5}
(a) $\gamma T = \beta T^3$ at $T^2 = \gamma/\beta$: for copper
$T \approx \qty{3}{K}$. (b) $C_{\text{el}}/C \approx
(\pi^2/2)(300/\num{8.2e4})/3 \approx 0.6\%$. (c) At \qty{0.1}{K}
the lattice term has collapsed as $T^3$: electrons carry
$\sim10^3$ times more --- calorimetry becomes electron
spectroscopy. (d) $C/T = \gamma + \beta T^2$ is a straight line in
$T^2$: intercept $\gamma$ measures the \omterm{prop:b3:schrodinger-three-dimensions:counting}{density of states} at the
Fermi level, slope $\beta$ the Debye temperature --- two numbers
per metal from one plot.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:6}
(a) $N_{\text{e}} = M/2m_{\text{n}} = \num{6e56}$; $n =
\num{5.5e35}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$; $E_{\text{F}} \approx

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:7}
(a) $\rho \approx \qty{5e17}{kg/m^3}$: about twice nuclear
density --- a nucleus the size of a city. (b) $n_{\text{n}}
\approx \qty{3e44}{m^{-3}}$: $E_{\text{F}} \approx \qty{90}{MeV}$,

neutrons. (d) $\qty{5}{cm^3} \times \qty{5e17}{kg/m^3} =

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:8}
(a) Cooling a cavity simply \emph{removes} photons (walls absorb
them): with no number to conserve, $\mu$ stays pinned at zero and
no surplus ever piles into the ground mode --- the gas dims
instead of condensing. (b) Atom number is conserved on every
laboratory timescale. (c) The dye repeatedly absorbs and re-emits
photons \emph{without losing them} on the thermalisation
timescale: an effectively conserved photon number (and an
effective mass from the cavity) --- and condensation duly appears.
(d) Phonons: created and destroyed freely, $\mu = 0$: no
condensation --- a crystal's ``phonon gas'' just freezes out.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:9}
(a) $T_{\text{c}}^{\text{ideal}} \approx \qty{3.1}{K}$ against the
measured $T_\lambda = \qty{2.17}{K}$. (b) Liquid helium is dense
and strongly interacting: ``ideal'' drops the interactions that
shift (and reshape) the transition. (c) $^3$He atoms are fermions:
no condensation without pairing; attractive interactions bind
Cooper-like pairs only below \qty{2.6}{mK}, and the paired liquid
then superflows. (d) Nine fermions: $^6$Li is a fermion (while
$^7$Li is a boson) --- the pair of lithium isotopes lets one
laboratory study a Fermi sea and a condensate in the same oven.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:10}
(a) Substitute $g \propto \sqrt\epsilon$ and scale out $\beta$.
(b) The integral's value gives $N_{\text{exc}} =
2.612\,V/\lambda_T^3$; setting $N_{\text{exc}} = N$ defines
$T_{\text{c}}$. (c) Below $T_{\text{c}}$, $N_{\text{exc}}(T) =
N(T/T_{\text{c}})^{3/2}$; the remainder is $N_0$. (d) In two
dimensions $g(\epsilon)$ is constant and $\int\dd\epsilon/(\eu^{
\beta\epsilon} - 1)$ diverges at the bottom: the excited states
can always absorb everyone --- no macroscopic ground-state
population at any $T > 0$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:11}
(a) $\dd\epsilon/\dd n = \tfrac23 An^{-1/3} - \tfrac13 Bn^{-2/3} =
0$ at $n^{1/3} = B/2A$: a genuine minimum. (b) With $A \sim
\hbar^2/m_{\text{e}}$ and $B \sim e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0$, the
equilibrium spacing $n^{-1/3} \sim \hbar^2 4\pi\varepsilon_0/
m_{\text{e}}e^2 = a_0$: metals are dense because the \omterm{thm:b3:hydrogen-atom:levels}{Bohr radius}
says so. (c) $\epsilon_{\min} \sim -B^2/A \sim -E_{\text{I}}$
--- electron-volt cohesion, as measured. (d) \omterm{thm:b3:quantum-statistics:fermi}{Degeneracy pressure}
is the repulsive half of the truce: without it the Coulomb term
would crush the lattice to a point --- matter's bulk is Pauli's.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:quantum-statistics:12}
(a) Active electrons: $g(E_{\text{F}})k_{\text{B}}T$; energy each:
$\sim k_{\text{B}}T$; differentiate $E_{\text{extra}} \sim
g k_{\text{B}}^2T^2$. (b) $g(E_{\text{F}}) = 3N/2E_{\text{F}}$
gives $C \sim 3Nk_{\text{B}}T/T_{\text{F}}$ --- the exact
$\pi^2/2$ replaces the 3. (c) Flippable spins $\propto T$; each
contributes alignment $\propto \mu^2B/k_{\text{B}}T$: the $T$'s
cancel --- Pauli's susceptibility is flat where Curie's
(\cref{exo:b3:canonical-ensemble:11}) falls as $1/T$: a
diagnostic of degeneracy. (d) Degenerate fermions respond only at
their surface: multiply any classical answer by $\sim
T/T_{\text{F}}$, or restrict it to the Fermi shell.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:quantum-statistics:1}
\textbf{1.} The energy released assembling the star from dispersed
matter --- negative, deeper for compactness.
\textbf{2.} $E_{\text{F}} \sim (\hbar^2/m_{\text{e}})
(N_{\text{e}}/R^3)^{2/3}$.
\textbf{3.} $E_{\text{k}} \sim N_{\text{e}}E_{\text{F}} \sim
\hbar^2N_{\text{e}}^{5/3}/m_{\text{e}}R^2$.
\textbf{4.} Minimising $A/R^2 - B/R$ gives $R = 2A/B$: $R \sim
\hbar^2N_{\text{e}}^{-1/3}/(Gm_{\text{e}}m_{\text{n}}^2\mu_e^2)$.
\textbf{5.} $N_{\text{e}} \propto M$: $R \propto M^{-1/3}$ ---
adding mass \emph{shrinks} the star, so density and $E_{\text{F}}$
grow without bound: the non-relativistic support is living on
credit.
\textbf{6.} $N_{\text{e}} = \num{6e56}$: $R \sim \qty{2000}{km}$
--- the right order beside Sirius B's \qty{5800}{km} (our scaling
dropped factors of order a few).
\textbf{7.} $E_{\text{F}} \sim m_{\text{e}}c^2$ at $n \sim
(m_{\text{e}}c/\hbar)^3 \approx \qty{2e37}{m^{-3}}$, i.e.\ $\rho
\sim \num{e10}$--$\num{e11}\,\unit{kg/m^3}$: the regime of the
heaviest dwarfs.
\textbf{8.} Each electron: $\epsilon \sim \hbar c\,n^{1/3}$;
total: $\hbar cN_{\text{e}}^{4/3}/R$.
\textbf{9.} With both terms $\propto 1/R$, radius drops out:
stability is decided by whether the kinetic coefficient exceeds
the gravitational one --- a pure mass criterion.
\textbf{10.} Equate: $N_{\text{e,crit}} \sim (\hbar c/
Gm_{\text{n}}^2\mu_e^2)^{3/2}$, and $M_{\text{Ch}} =
\mu_em_{\text{n}}N_{\text{e,crit}}$: the stated formula.
\textbf{11.} $\hbar c/Gm_{\text{n}}^2 = \num{1.7e38}$;
$M_{\text{Ch}} \sim (\num{1.7e38})^{3/2}m_{\text{n}}/4 \approx
\qty{9e29}{kg} \approx 0.5\,M_\odot$ --- the exact computation
sharpens the order-of-magnitude to $1.4\,M_\odot$.
\textbf{12.} $\hbar$ (the pressure is quantum), $c$ (its
relativistic softening), $G$ (the adversary): a star-sized number
built from three microscopic constants --- quantum mechanics
legislating for objects of $10^{57}$ particles.
\textbf{13.} Neutron degeneracy, at radius smaller by
$\sim m_{\text{e}}/m_{\text{n}}$ (times the $\mu_e$ bookkeeping):
kilometres --- the neutron star.
\textbf{14.} The same three constants with $m_{\text{n}}$
throughout give the same mass scale: neutron stars top out near
$2\,M_\odot$; beyond, no pressure law wins --- a black hole.
\textbf{15.} $GM^2/R \approx \qty{5e46}{J}$: several hundred times
the Sun's entire ten-billion-year output, released in seconds.
\textbf{16.} Supernova 1987A: two dozen neutrinos, arriving hours
before the light brightened, carrying just the predicted energy
--- the collapse heard directly.
\textbf{17.} Core collapse: a massive star's iron core implodes
past every pressure floor; the envelope is blown off. Type Ia: a
\omterm{ex:b3:quantum-statistics:dwarf}{white dwarf} fed to the threshold ignites its carbon in a
thermonuclear flash that unbinds the entire star.
\textbf{18.} Same trigger mass, same fuel, same energy release:
nearly identical light curves --- calibrated bombs.
\textbf{19.} Knowing the absolute brightness, the observed
brightness gives distance by the inverse square; without
``standard'' the ladder collapses.
\textbf{20.} Too faint means too far: the expansion has been
speeding up --- dark energy, resting evidentially on the candles'
uniformity (hence the care about their physics).
\textbf{21.} Exclusion principle $\to$ \omterm{thm:b3:quantum-statistics:fermi}{degeneracy pressure} $\to$ a
universal limiting mass $\to$ standardised explosions $\to$ the
measured acceleration of the universe.
\textbf{22.} Follow the mathematics wherever it leads: the
``absurd'' behaviour of stars beyond the limit turned out to be
neutron stars and black holes --- both now catalogued by the
thousand.
\textbf{23.} In 1915 no known physics allowed matter a tonne per
cubic centimetre; in 1926 Fermi--Dirac statistics made it the
default state of any cold dense gas: the observation waited for
the statistics, not the reverse.
\textbf{24.} That dwarfs exist stably across a range of masses
(the $R(M)$ branch), and that the branch \emph{ends} at
$1.4\,M_\odot$: both are drawn in that histogram.
\textbf{25.} $(\hbar c/Gm_{\text{n}}^2)^{3/2}m_{\text{n}} \approx
1.4\,M_\odot$: three constants fix the heaviest \omterm{ex:b3:quantum-statistics:dwarf}{white dwarf} ---
and thereby the trigger mass of the standard candles that weighed
the universe's fate.
\end{solution}
