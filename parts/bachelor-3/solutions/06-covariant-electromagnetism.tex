\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:covariant-electromagnetism} --- Covariant Electromagnetism}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:1}
(a) $\gamma = 1.25$: $a'^0 = 1.25(5 - 1.8) = 4.0$, $a'^1 = 1.25(3 -
3) = 0$; $a\cdot a = 25 - 9 = 16 = 16 - 0$. (b) The transformation is
linear, so it distributes over sums. (c) $(c, \vect v)$: no --- its
``time'' component is the same for all particles while the mixing
demands otherwise; $\gamma(c, \vect v) = \dd x^\mu/\dd\tau$: yes, a
\omterm{def:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:fourvector}{four-vector} divided by the invariant $\dd\tau$. (d) $\vect E$'s three
components mix with $\vect B$'s, not with any scalar: the fields fill
a two-index tensor, not a \omterm{def:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:fourvector}{four-vector}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:2}
(a) $u = I/nSe = 10/(\num{8.5e28} \times \num{e-6} \times
\num{1.6e-19}) = \qty{7.4e-4}{m/s}$. (b) Lattice: $(nec, \vect 0)$;
electrons: $(-nec, -ne\vect u)$ --- their current density
$\rho_-\vect u = -ne\vect u$ points against their drift, and that
\emph{is} the conventional current's direction. (c) Both are static and uniform: every term vanishes. (d)
Total: $(0, \vect\jmath)$ with $j = I/S = \qty{e7}{A/m^2}$ --- a
pure current with no charge, the configuration that makes the wire's
relativity subtle.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:3}
(a) $E_y$: $F^{20} = E_y/c$; $B_x$: $F^{32} = B_x$. (b) For $\vect B
= B\vect e_z$, only $F^{12} = -B$ and $F^{21} = +B$; for the wave,
$F^{20} = E/c$, $F^{12} = -E/c$ (and antisymmetric partners). (c)
$F^{12} = \partial^1A^2 - \partial^2A^1 = -\partial_xA_y +
\partial_yA_x = -(\operatorname{\vect{curl}}\vect A)_z = -B_z$:
matches the matrix. (d) The particle's rate of energy change is
$\propto F^{0\nu}u_\nu$; in the rest frame $u_\nu = (c, \vect 0)$ and
antisymmetry makes $F^{00} = 0$: a force that can never work on a
particle at rest --- the defining property of the magnetic part.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:4}
(a) Boost along the field: longitudinal components unchanged, $\vect
E' = E_0\vect e_y$, $\vect B' = \vect 0$. (b) Boost along $\vect
e_x$: $E'_y = \gamma E_0$, $B'_z = -\gamma vE_0/c^2$ --- the plates
now stream as surface currents $\pm\sigma'v$, and two opposite
current sheets enclose exactly such a field. (c) $E'^2 - c^2B'^2 =
\gamma^2E_0^2(1 - \beta^2) = E_0^2$; $\vect E'\cdot\vect B' = 0$:
both preserved. (d) The plates contract along $x$, so $\sigma' =
\gamma\sigma$, consistent with $E' = \sigma'/\varepsilon_0 = \gamma
E_0$; the separation, transverse to the boost, is untouched.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:5}
(a) The magnetic force $-e\vect v\wedge\vect B$ pushes electrons
along the rod: EMF $= vBL$. (b) In the rod's frame the rod is at
rest --- no magnetic force on stationary charges --- but the
transformation delivers $\vect E' = \gamma\,\vect v\wedge\vect B$:
an honest electric field does the driving. (c) $E'L = \gamma vBL
\approx vBL$ at order $v/c$. (d) It had to work because ``motional''
and ``transformer'' EMFs are one phenomenon read in two frames: the
flux rule is covariance wearing 1831 clothes --- Einstein's 1905
paper opens with exactly this magnet-and-conductor asymmetry.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:6}
(a) The $x$ components are untouched; for the transverse ones,
$E_y'^2 + E_z'^2 - c^2(B_y'^2 + B_z'^2) = \gamma^2[(E_y - vB_z)^2 +
(E_z + vB_y)^2 - c^2(B_y + vE_z/c^2)^2 - c^2(B_z - vE_y/c^2)^2]$;
the cross terms cancel and the squares collect $\gamma^2(1 -
\beta^2) = 1$ times the untransformed combination. (b) Same
bookkeeping on $E_xB_x + E_yB_y + E_zB_z$. (c) $E^2 - c^2B^2 =
3c^2B^2 > 0$: boost along $\vect E\wedge\vect B$ at $v = c^2B/E =
c/2$; there $B' = 0$ and the surviving field is $E' = E/\gamma =
\sqrt3\,cB$ --- which squares to the invariant, as it must. (d) A light wave has both invariants
zero: any frame must reproduce $E' = cB' \perp$, never a pure field.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:7}
(a) With $v_{\text{d}} = E/B$: $E'_y = \gamma(E - v_{\text{d}}B) =
0$; $B'_z = \gamma(B - v_{\text{d}}E/c^2) = B/\gamma$: pure, slightly
weakened magnetic field. (b) There: a circle at $qB'/\gamma m$; back
in the lab: that circle plus the uniform drift --- a cycloid creeping
at $E/B$ perpendicular to both fields, the trajectory of charges in
a magnetron. (c) A particle moving at exactly $v_{\text{d}}$ is at
rest in the drift frame, where the only field is magnetic and it
feels nothing: undeflected. (d) For $E > cB$ the required drift
exceeds $c$: no such frame; instead a pure-$E$ frame exists
(previous exercise) and the charge is accelerated without bound
along the field.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:8}
(a) The phase $\omega t - \vect k\cdot\vect r$ counts crest-crossing
events, an invariant number; it equals $k^\mu x_\mu$, and
invariance of the product for all $x^\mu$ forces $k^\mu$ to
transform four-vectorially. (b) $\omega'/c = \gamma(\omega/c -
\beta k_x)$ with $k_x = (\omega/c)\cos\theta$; for $\theta = 0$,
$\omega' = \omega\sqrt{(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)}$. (c) $\cos\theta' =
k'_x/(\omega'/c)$: the stated formula. (d) $\beta = \num{e-4}$:
$20.5''$; over a year the apparent position sweeps an ellipse of that
angular semi-axis --- Bradley's aberration.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:9}
(a) In the rest frame, Coulomb; boosting multiplies transverse
components by $\gamma$ (at the transverse plane, $E'_\perp = \gamma
q/4\pi\varepsilon_0b^2$) while the field along the motion, evaluated
ahead or behind at lab distance $r$, maps to a rest-frame distance
$\gamma r$: reduced by $1/\gamma^2$. (b) The pancake of angular width
$1/\gamma$ sweeps past at $v$: duration $\sim (b/\gamma)/v$. (c)
$E' = 7250 \times \num{1.6e-19} \times \num{9e9}/\num{e-4} \approx
\qty{0.1}{V/m}$, lasting $2b/\gamma c \approx \qty{9}{fs}$. (d)
$B' = vE'/c^2 \approx E'/c$ and both invariants are $O(1/\gamma^2)$:
locally indistinguishable from a light pulse --- the basis of the
``equivalent photon'' description of fast-charge collisions.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:10}
(a) $E'_y = \gamma(E - vB) = \gamma E_0(1 - \beta)\cos(\cdots) =
E_0\sqrt{(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)}\cos(\cdots)$, and $B'_z = E'_y/c$ by
the same algebra. (b) The phase transforms with the same Doppler
factor: same null wave, redder and weaker. (c) Energy density falls
as the Doppler factor squared; the photon number is unchanged ---
each photon's $h\nu$ carries the whole factor. (d) ``Riding
alongside'' means the factor $\to 0$ with $\beta \to 1$: the wave
never becomes static because its invariants are zero --- there is no
frame in which light stands still, which is where this book's
relativity began.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:11}
(a) Per turn, $\Delta E = P \times 2\pi r/c$: the stated result. (b)
$\gamma = \num{e11}/\num{5.11e5} = \num{1.96e5}$: $\Delta E \approx
\qty{2.9}{GeV}$ per turn --- three per cent of the beam energy
re-injected every lap; LEP's klystrons were the largest radio
transmitter on Earth. (c) Protons: $\gamma = 7250$, $\gamma^4$
smaller by $(m_{\text{p}}/m_{\text{e}})^4 \approx \num{e13}$: about
\qty{5}{keV} per turn --- negligible. (d) $\gamma^4 = (E/mc^2)^4$:
at equal energy the electron radiates $\num{e13}$ times more; hence
linear colliders (radiate once, not per turn) or gigantic rings for
electrons, while proton rings scale happily to \qty{27}{km}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:12}
(a) $|\vect p|$ is constant (no work); $\dot{\vect p} = q\vect
v\wedge\vect B$ turns $\vect p$ at rate $qvB/p = qB/\gamma m$. (b)
Slip per turn $2\pi(\gamma - 1)$; with $\gamma - 1$ growing linearly
to $\Gamma$, total slip $\approx \pi N\Gamma$; a quarter period is
$\pi/2$: $\Gamma \approx 1/2N$. For $N = 100$: $\Gamma =
\num{5e-3}$, $E_k \approx \qty{4.7}{MeV}$ --- the right scale; real
machines stretched it to $\sim\qty{20}{MeV}$ with high dee voltages
(fewer turns). (c) Synchrocyclotron: sweep the RF downward during
each pulse to follow $qB/\gamma m$. Isochronous cyclotron: let
$B(r)$ grow $\propto\gamma(r)$ so the ratio never changes and the
beam stays continuous. (d) $\gamma = 1 + 590/938 = 1.63$: the rim
field must exceed the central field by that factor.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:covariant-electromagnetism:1}
\textbf{1.} $u = I/nSe = \qty{7.4e-4}{m/s}$; $u^2/c^2 =
\num{6e-24}$.
\textbf{2.} $\lambda = I/u = nSe = \qty{1.36e4}{C/m}$ --- fourteen
kilocoulombs per metre in each stream.
\textbf{3.} $B = \mu_0I/2\pi d = \qty{2.0e-4}{T}$; $F = qvB =
\num{e-6} \times \num{e5} \times \num{2e-4} = \qty{2e-5}{N}$,
directed \emph{toward} the wire (parallel currents attract).
\textbf{4.} The two line charges cancel exactly: $\lambda_{\text{tot}}
= 0$, no field to zeroth order.
\textbf{5.} One excess electron per metre: $E = 2k\lambda_1/d =
\qty{2.9e-7}{V/m}$, force $\qty{2.9e-13}{N}$ --- $10^8$ times smaller
than the magnetic force. The wire could hide a hundred million
stray electrons per metre before electrostatics rivalled magnetism:
attributing the force to $\vect B$ is safe.
\textbf{6.} $\qty{2e-5}{N}$ is twenty sand-grain weights: easily
measurable.
\textbf{7.} Lattice: $-v$. Electrons (drifting at $u$ opposite $I$,
i.e.\ opposite the boost): speed $(u + v)/(1 + uv/c^2)$ --- faster
than the lattice.
\textbf{8.} $\lambda' = \gamma_v(\lambda_{\text{tot}} - vI/c^2) =
-\gamma_v vI/c^2$.
\textbf{9.} $\lambda' = -\num{e5} \times 10/\num{9e16} =
\qty{-1.1e-11}{C/m}$: about $7 \times 10^7$ excess electrons per
metre. The electron stream, faster in this frame, is the denser one
--- each stream contracts by its own $\gamma$.
\textbf{10.} $E' = 2k|\lambda'|/d = 2 \times \num{9e9} \times
\num{1.1e-11}/0.01 = \qty{20}{V/m}$, pointing at the wire; force
$qE' = \qty{2e-5}{N}$, attractive.
\textbf{11.} Identical to $qvB$ up to the factor $\gamma_v = 1 +
\num{5.6e-8}$: exactly the transformation law of a transverse force
(the four-force), $F_{\text{rest}} = \gamma F_{\text{lab}}$.
\textbf{12.} The wire still carries a (larger) current, hence $\vect
B' \neq 0$ --- but our charge is at rest here, and a magnetic field
grips only moving charges.
\textbf{13.} $|\lambda'|/\lambda = \gamma_v vu/c^2 = \num{8.2e-16}$.
\textbf{14.} It multiplies $\lambda/e \approx \num{8.5e22}$
elementary charges per metre: $\num{8.2e-16} \times \num{8.5e22}
\approx \num{7e7}$ electrons per metre --- macroscopic. Matter is so
enormously charged that its neutrality is a razor's edge; relativity
tips the razor.
\textbf{15.} $F/L = \mu_0I_1I_2/2\pi d = \num{2e-7} \times 100/0.01 =
\qty{2e-3}{N/m}$ --- the formula that once \emph{defined} the ampere.
\textbf{16.} $B = \mu_0nI = 4\pi\times10^{-7} \times \num{e4} \times
10 = \qty{0.13}{T}$; magnetic pressure $B^2/2\mu_0 \approx
\qty{6300}{Pa} \approx \qty{0.6}{N/cm^2}$: whole cars hang on
integrated relativity.
\textbf{17.} $c \to \infty$ kills $\lambda'$, and with it the force,
the field's magnetic part, motors, dynamos and scrapyard cranes:
magnetism has no non-relativistic existence.
\textbf{18.} At rest the charge sees the lab's neutral wire: the two
streams cancel to whatever precision matter is neutral --- known
experimentally to fantastic accuracy --- and no force acts.
\textbf{19.} That the ``given sources'' picture was one frame's
slice of a single tensor: $\vect B$ is the piece of the
electromagnetic field that a given observer's motion assigns to
currents rather than charges.
\textbf{20.} Electron spin: each electron is an elementary magnet
(an intrinsic, quantum ``current''), and iron is matter in which
these align --- \cref{ch:b3:spin-two-level} and
\cref{ch:b3:electromagnetism-in-matter} take this up.
\textbf{21.} In the beam's rest frame the repulsion is pure Coulomb
and \emph{maximal}; in the lab, the parallel currents' magnetic
attraction nearly cancels it. No contradiction: the lab's transverse
force is the rest frame's divided by $\gamma$, and slower dynamics
(\omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{time dilation}) completes the account.
\textbf{22.} Net lab force $= qE_{\text{lab}} - qvB_{\text{lab}} =
(1 - \beta^2)qE_{\text{lab}} = F_{\text{Coulomb}}/\gamma^2$ per the
transformation; in the rest frame the factor is transparent: pure
electrostatics, with the blow-up watched through dilated time.
\textbf{23.} The Poynting vector: energy streams through the
\emph{fields} around the conductors at nearly $c$, the electrons
merely marshalling it; the cord's copper is a guide, not a pipe.
\textbf{24.} $vu/c^2 \approx \num{8e-21}$ --- and yet Ørsted saw
his compass swing beside a wire in 1820: the multiplier of
$10^{22}$ charges was already at work a century before anyone could
name it.
\textbf{25.} A neutral \qty{10}{A} wire, viewed from \qty{e5}{m/s},
carries $\qty{-1.1e-11}{C/m}$: \omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:contraction}{length contraction}'s $10^{-16}$
bookkeeping difference, multiplied by $10^{23}$ charges per metre,
\emph{is} the magnetic force --- all of magnetism is relativity
audited at a snail's pace.
\end{solution}
