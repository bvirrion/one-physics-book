\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:relativistic-kinematics} --- Relativistic Kinematics}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:1}
$\gamma = 1.005$, $1.155$, $2.294$, $7.09$, $22.4$. ISS: $\beta =
\num{2.57e-5}$, $\gamma - 1 \approx \beta^2/2 = \num{3.3e-10}$; over
six months ($\num{1.6e7}{}\,\unit{s}$) the crew's clock runs
\emph{slow} by $\approx\qty{5}{ms}$ (velocity effect alone; at the
ISS's low altitude the gravitational effect reduces but does not
reverse this).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:2}
(a) $\Delta\tau = 2d/c$. (b) Each half-tick, the photon travels the
hypotenuse: $(c\Delta t/2)^2 = (v\Delta t/2)^2 + d^2$ with $d =
c\Delta\tau/2$; solve: $\Delta t = \Delta\tau/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$. (c)
If $d$ changed with motion, the Pythagoras step would be wrong. (d)
Let two identical rulers pass, each carrying a paintbrush at its tip
pointing at the other. If motion contracted transverse lengths, each
frame would predict its own ruler paints a mark \emph{beyond} the
other's tip --- two contradictory facts about the same brush strokes
at the same passing \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{event}. Contradiction at one \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{event} is not allowed:
transverse lengths cannot change.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:3}
$\gamma = 22.4$. (a) $\gamma\tau = \qty{49}{\micro s}$. (b) $v\gamma
\tau = \qty{14.8}{km}$. (c) $15/22.4 = \qty{670}{m}$. (d) Lab time of
flight $\qty{50}{\micro s}$: without relativity
$\eu^{-50/2.2} \approx \num{e-10}$ --- none; with relativity the
\omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{proper time} is $50/22.4 = \qty{2.2}{\micro s}$, fraction $\eu^{-1}
\approx 0.37$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:4}
(a) $c\Delta t = \qty{600}{m}$, $\Delta x = \qty{300}{m}$: $\Delta s^2
= (600^2 - 300^2)\,\unit{m^2} > 0$, timelike. (b) Yes: a signal at
$\Delta x/\Delta t = c/2$ suffices. (c) That same frame speed, $v =
0.5c$; \omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{proper time} $\Delta s/c = \sqrt{600^2 - 300^2}/c = 520/c =
\qty{1.73}{\micro s}$. (d) Now $c\Delta t = 300 < \Delta x = 600$:
spacelike; no causal link possible; no frame brings them to the same
place, but the frame at $v = c^2\Delta t/\Delta x = 0.5c$ makes them
simultaneous.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:5}
(a) $\beta = \num{1.29e-5}$: $\gamma - 1 = \num{8.3e-11}$. (b)
$86400\,\unit{s} \times \num{8.3e-11} = \qty{7.2}{\micro s}$ per day.
(c) $c \times \qty{7.2}{\micro s} = \qty{2.2}{km}$ --- navigation
would die within a day. (d) The net $+38\,\unit{\micro s}$ means the
gravitational blueshift ($+45.7$) outweighs the kinematic slowing
($-7.2$): at \qty{20200}{km}, sitting higher in the Earth's potential
speeds a clock more than orbital speed slows it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:6}
(a) $1.6c/1.64 = 0.976c$. (b) $1.8c/1.81 = 0.994c$. (c) $c - u =
\dfrac{(c - u')(c - v)}{c\,(1 + u'v/c^2)}$: both factors positive, so

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:7}
(a) Shorter wavelength: approaching. $f/f_0 = 589/575 = 1.024$;
$(1+\beta)/(1-\beta) = 1.049$: $\beta = 0.024$, about
\qty{7200}{km/s}. (b) Ratio $2$: $(1+\beta)/(1-\beta) = 4$, $\beta =
0.6$. (c) Expanding the square root, $\Delta\lambda/\lambda \approx
\beta$; at \qty{600}{nm}, $\qty{1}{\angstrom} = \qty{0.1}{nm}$ gives
$\beta = \num{1.7e-4}$: about \qty{50}{km/s} per angström --- the
astronomer's reflex. (d) The transverse Doppler effect: pure \omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{time
dilation}, of order $\beta^2$ --- measurable only with atomic
precision.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:8}
(a) $20/2 = \qty{10}{m}$: it fits, just, and both doors can be shut
simultaneously (barn frame). (b) In the runner's frame the \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{events}
``front door shuts'' and ``back door opens'' are \emph{not}
simultaneous: the exit opens before the entrance closes, and the
\qty{20}{m} pole threads a \qty{5}{m} barn without ever being
enclosed. (c) Barn frame: $\Delta t = 0$ over $\Delta x =
\qty{10}{m}$. Runner frame: $|\Delta t'| = \gamma v\Delta x/c^2 =
2 \times 0.866c \times 10/c^2 = \qty{58}{ns}$. (d) ``The pole is
enclosed'' silently asserts that two separated \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{events} (both doors
shut) are simultaneous --- a frame-dependent statement, not a fact.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:9}
$\gamma = 1.25$ at $0.6c$. (a) Moving length $100/1.25 = \qty{80}{m}$
at \qty{1.8e8}{m/s}: \qty{444}{ns}. (b) $240/\num{1.8e8} =
\qty{1.33}{\micro s}$. (c) $|\Delta t'| = \gamma v\Delta x/c^2 =
\qty{200}{ns}$; from $t' = \gamma(t - vx/c^2)$, the clamp at larger
$x$ --- the forward one --- fires \emph{first} for the rocket. (d)
Station: $\Delta s^2 = 0 - 80^2 = -6400\,\unit{m^2}$. Rocket: $\Delta
x' = \gamma\,\Delta x = \qty{100}{m}$, $c\Delta t' = \qty{60}{m}$:
$60^2 - 100^2 = -6400\,\unit{m^2}$ --- invariant.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:10}
(a) Terra: \qty{20}{yr}; Stella: $20/\gamma = \qty{12}{yr}$. (b) The
distance contracts to $8/\gamma = \qty{4.8}{ly}$, covered in $4.8/0.8
= \qty{6}{yr}$ of her time --- consistent with \qty{12}{yr} for the
round trip. (c) \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{Simultaneity} with the turnaround \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{event} $(t =
\qty{10}{yr},\ x = \qty{8}{ly})$: outbound frame assigns Earth's
clock $t - vx/c^2 = 10 - 6.4 = \qty{3.6}{yr}$; inbound frame, $10 +
6.4 = \qty{16.4}{yr}$. Her ``now on Earth'' jumps by \qty{12.8}{yr}
at the kink; her ledger $3.6 + 12.8 + 3.6 = \qty{20}{yr}$ matches
Terra exactly. (d) Stella occupies \emph{two} \omterm{thm:b3:relativistic-kinematics:postulates}{inertial frames} joined
by an acceleration she can feel; Terra occupies one. The straight
worldline between the departure and reunion \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{events} carries the longest
\omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{proper time}; only Stella's is bent.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:11}
(a) If the map were nonlinear, a uniform motion would not look uniform
in the other frame, distinguishing points of homogeneous space and
time. (b) Substituting one relation into the other: $t' = \gamma t +
(1 - \gamma^2)x/\gamma v$. (c) Setting $x = ct$, $x' = ct'$:
$\gamma(c - v)t = c\gamma t + c(1 - \gamma^2)ct/\gamma v$, which
solves to $\gamma^2 = 1/(1 - v^2/c^2)$. (d) Relativity gave the same
$\gamma$ for the two directions (no preferred frame); the light
postulate turned the remaining free function into the definite
$\gamma(v)$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:12}
(a) With $\cosh\varphi = \gamma$, $\sinh\varphi = \gamma\beta$, the
transformation is exactly the hyperbolic rotation stated, and
$\cosh^2 - \sinh^2 = 1$ preserves $c^2t^2 - x^2$. (b)
$\tanh(\varphi_1 + \varphi_2) = (\tanh\varphi_1 + \tanh\varphi_2)/(1 +
\tanh\varphi_1\tanh\varphi_2)$: precisely the composition law ---
velocities do not add, rapidities do. (c) $\varphi = g\tau/c$, so
$\beta = \tanh(g\tau/c)$; $\operatorname{artanh}(0.99) = 2.65$ gives
$\tau = 2.65c/g \approx \qty{8.1e7}{s} \approx 2.6$ years of ship
time. (d) $\varphi$ ranges over all reals while $\tanh\varphi$
saturates at $1$: one can accelerate forever, gaining rapidity
linearly, while the speed only creeps toward $c$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:relativistic-kinematics:1}
\textbf{1.} All muons are strictly identical and decay at a fixed
statistical rate: the surviving fraction of a population measures
elapsed \omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{proper time} as surely as a clock hand.
\textbf{2.} $c\tau = \qty{3.00e8}{} \times \qty{2.2e-6}{} =
\qty{660}{m}$.
\textbf{3.} $t = \qty{15}{km}/c = \qty{50}{\micro s}$: fraction
$\eu^{-50/2.2} = \eu^{-22.7} \approx \num{1.4e-10}$.
\textbf{4.} Particles that ``cannot'' travel more than a kilometre
cross fifteen of them and arrive in force.
\textbf{5.} A hand $\sim\qty{100}{cm^2}$: a couple of muons per
second; a body $\sim\num{e3}\,\unit{cm^2}$ horizontal cross-section:
of order fifteen between two heartbeats --- relativity rains through
everyone, always.
\textbf{6.} $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - 0.995^2} = 10.0$.
\textbf{7.} Comparing two counts of the \emph{same} selected
population over a known height difference removes every assumption
about where and how many muons are born.
\textbf{8.} $t = 1907/(0.995 \times \num{3e8}) = \qty{6.39}{\micro s}$.
\textbf{9.} $\eu^{-6.39/2.2} = 0.055$: about $31$ per hour.
\textbf{10.} $t/\gamma = \qty{0.64}{\micro s}$.
\textbf{11.} $\eu^{-0.64/2.2} = 0.75$: about $420$ per hour.
\textbf{12.} Measured $408 \pm 9$: relativity's $\approx 420$ agrees
within the muons' slight slowing in the detectors' absorber;
classical physics' $31$ is off by a factor thirteen. The mountain
decides.
\textbf{13.} $408/563 = 0.725 = \eu^{-t_{\text{proper}}/\tau}$:
$t_{\text{proper}} = \qty{0.71}{\micro s}$, so $\gamma_{\exp} =
6.39/0.71 = 9.0$ --- squarely in Frisch and Smith's $8.8 \pm 0.8$.
\textbf{14.} $1907/10 = \qty{191}{m}$.
\textbf{15.} $191/(0.995c) = \qty{0.64}{\micro s}$: the same $25\%$
decay --- the muon's account of the same count.
\textbf{16.} A count of clicks is a set of local coincidence \omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{events};
\omterm{def:b3:relativistic-kinematics:event}{events} and their tallies are frame-invariant --- frames may disagree
about times and lengths, never about what a counter read.
\textbf{17.} $\Delta s^2 = (c \times \qty{6.39}{\micro s})^2 -
(\qty{1907}{m})^2 = (1917^2 - 1907^2)\,\unit{m^2} = (\qty{196}{m})^2$:
$\Delta s/c = \qty{0.65}{\micro s}$ --- the \omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{proper time}, computed
without ever leaving the mountain frame.
\textbf{18.} Decay rates would depend on altitude for every speed
alike; instead the survival tracks $\gamma$ --- slower selections
dilate less, exactly as $\gamma(v)$ prescribes.
\textbf{19.} Lifetimes of $\gamma\tau$ to a part in a thousand
(CERN muon storage rings); the ring makes the muon a perpetually
accelerated traveller --- the ``travelling twin'' stays young even
when the journey is one endless turnaround.
\textbf{20.} $\beta = \num{8.3e-7}$: $\Delta t = \tfrac12\beta^2
\times \qty{144000}{s} = \qty{50}{ns}$.
\textbf{21.} The aircraft's speed adds to or subtracts from the
rotating Earth's, while the altitude (gravitational) effect is the
same both ways: the eastward and westward flights split the two
contributions cleanly (the 1971 result matched both).
\textbf{22.} From \cref{exo:b3:relativistic-kinematics:5}:
\qty{7.2}{\micro s} per day.
\textbf{23.} $7 \times 7.2\,\unit{\micro s} \times c \approx
\qty{15}{km}.$
\textbf{24.} Positions would drift by hundreds of metres within
hours, kilometres within a day --- navigation would visibly break the
first morning.
\textbf{25.} In 1963 a mountain, two counters and \qty{2.2}{\micro s}
clocks measured $\gamma_{\exp} = 9.0$ against a predicted $10$ (with
slowing, $8.8 \pm 0.8$): \omterm{prop:b3:relativistic-kinematics:dilation}{time dilation} confirmed within $10\%$ ---
and the identical physics is silently corrected, every second, in
every navigation satellite above your head.
\end{solution}
