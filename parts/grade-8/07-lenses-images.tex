\chapter{Lenses and Images}\label{ch:g8:lenses-images}

The pinhole camera bought its sharp little \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} at a cruel
price: almost no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}. Yet your own eye paints a bright, crisp
world on its back wall all day long, and no pinhole is in
sight. The eye's secret is a disc of curved glassy stuff that
gathers a whole \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam} and bends it to order --- the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}, \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:star}{star}
of cameras, spectacles, magnifiers and this chapter.

\section{Two families of lens}

\begin{definition}[Converging and diverging lenses]\label{def:g8:lenses-images:families}
A \emph{lens}\index{lens} is a piece of \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{transparent} material
--- glass or clear plastic --- with curved faces, built to
bend \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} in a controlled way (\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} bends when it crosses
into glass; the exact law of that bending is High School
treasure). Two families:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{converging lenses}\index{converging lens} ---
        thicker in the middle than at the rim --- bend a \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam}'s
        rays \emph{toward} each other;
  \item \emph{diverging lenses}\index{diverging lens} ---
        thinner in the middle --- spread them apart.
\end{enumerate}
Run a fingertip across a spectacle lens: a middle bulge or a
middle hollow sorts every lens on Earth into its family.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Focal point and focal length]\label{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}
Send a bundle of parallel rays --- sunlight serves --- through
a \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lens}: the bent rays all cross at one point behind
it, the \emph{focal point}\index{focal point}. The distance
from \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} to focal point is the \emph{focal
length}\index{focal length} $f$: the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}'s one defining
number. Strongly curved, fat lenses bend hard --- short $f$;
gently curved ones bend little --- long $f$.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[Measuring a focal length]\label{met:g8:lenses-images:measure}
For any \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lens}, one minute and a far window:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item face a wall opposite a bright \emph{distant} scene ---
        the window and the world beyond;
  \item hold the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} near the wall and back it away slowly
        until the scene snaps into a small sharp picture on
        the wall (upside down --- as expected by now);
  \item \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measure} the lens-to-wall distance: for a distant
        scene, that distance \emph{is} the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal length} ---
        far-off rays arrive practically parallel, and
        parallel rays gather at the focus.
\end{enumerate}
A magnifier \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measures} around \qty{10}{cm}; spectacle lenses,
tens of \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetres}; a camera's stubby \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}, a few.
\end{method}

\begin{remark}[The burning glass]\label{rem:g8:lenses-images:burning}
At the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal point} of a \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lens}, a whole beam-width of
sunlight crowds into one blazing dot --- concentrated enough
to char paper and start fires within seconds. Treat it as
what it is: the solar cooker's principle in pocket form.
Never focus sunlight near anything flammable, never toward
skin or clothing --- and, twice over the old law, never look
at the Sun \emph{through} any \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}: the eye's own focusing
plus the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}'s makes an instant, painless, permanent burn.
\end{remark}

\section{The lens as image-maker}

\begin{proposition}[Real images]\label{prop:g8:lenses-images:realimage}
A \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lens} gathers the rays diffused by each point of
an object and delivers them to a matching point on the other
side: point by point, it assembles a genuine picture --- a
\emph{real image}\index{real image} --- that can be caught on
a screen. The \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} is inverted, like the pinhole's, but
bright: the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} harvests a whole lensful of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} per point
where the pinhole passed a single thread. Geometry's rules of
thumb: a distant object \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{images} small and near the focus; as
the object approaches the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}, its \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} retreats and
grows.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Camera and eye, one architecture]\label{ex:g8:lenses-images:cameraeye}
A camera is the image-maker boxed: \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lens} in front, a
light-sensitive screen behind, \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} formed inverted on it
(software politely rotates the file). Your eye is the same
machine in living tissue: a \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lens} behind the pupil,
the \emph{retina} as screen at the back, \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} inverted ---
the brain turns it over, as noted when the pinhole first
taught us. Focusing differs beautifully: the camera moves its
\omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}; the eye's soft \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} \emph{changes shape}, tugged fatter
for near things, relaxed thinner for far ones --- feel the
effort after an hour of close reading.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Spectacles]\label{ex:g8:lenses-images:spectacles}
When the eye's own focusing falls short, a \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} in front
tops it up. The short-sighted eye focuses too strongly ---
distant scenes assemble \emph{before} the retina and arrive
blurred: a \emph{diverging} spectacle \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} un-bends the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}
just enough. The long-sighted (and most older) eyes focus too
weakly for near work: a \emph{converging} \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} lends the
missing strength --- reading glasses are honest little
magnifier-cousins. Two families of \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}, two families of
blur.
\end{example}

\section{The magnifier}

{\small The magnifier at work: held closer than its \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal length}, the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} shows the print upright and enlarged.}
\end{omfigure}

\begin{example}[Inside the focal length]\label{ex:g8:lenses-images:magnifier}
Hold a \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lens} \emph{closer} to a stamp than its \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal
length}, and the picture changes character: no \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} lands on
any screen --- instead, looking through the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}, you see the
stamp upright, in place, and enlarged. The \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} bends the
stamp's rays so they reach the eye \emph{as if} diffused by a
grander stamp behind the glass --- a made-to-order illusion,
cousin to the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}'s phantom world. Watchmakers, stamp
collectors and your own past self with the childhood
magnifying glass have all used it; the full ray-geometry of
this trick is a jewel of the High School volume.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Instruments upon instruments]\label{rem:g8:lenses-images:instruments}
Stack lenses and the world opens. Two \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging lenses} make
a microscope: one throws a large \omterm{prop:g8:lenses-images:realimage}{real image} of a droplet,
the second magnifies that \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} --- \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules}' defenders got
their first direct allies this way. A large \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} (or curved
\omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}) plus a magnifier makes a telescope: the far galaxy's
faint parallel rays gathered into a \omterm{prop:g8:lenses-images:realimage}{real image}, then enlarged
into an astonished eye. Every optical instrument of
civilization is this chapter's two ideas --- gather, then
magnify --- in various arrangements.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:1}
Sort by touch: how does a fingertip tell a converging from a
\omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{diverging lens}? What does each do to a parallel \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:2}
Define \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal point} and \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal length}. What does a short \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal
length} say about a \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}'s curvature and bending power?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:3}
Describe the window-and-wall measurement of $f$. Why must the
scene be distant?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:4}
State two safety rules of the burning glass, and the reason
each exists.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:5}
What is a \omterm{prop:g8:lenses-images:realimage}{real image}? Give its two signature properties, and
the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}'s advantage over the pinhole.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:6}
Map camera onto eye: what plays the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}, the screen, and the
focusing mechanism in each?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:7}
Which spectacle family serves the short-sighted eye, and why?
The aging long-sighted eye?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:8}
What must be true of the stamp's position for the magnifier
trick --- and what kind of ``\omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image}'' does the eye then
enjoy?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:9}
A photographer's subject walks toward the camera. Which way
must the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} move to keep the \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} sharp on the sensor,
by the rules of thumb? What does the eye do instead in the
same situation?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:10}
On a sunny day, a \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} laid carelessly on dry leaves is a
fire waiting to happen --- but only at one particular
lens-to-leaf distance. Which distance, and why exactly
there?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:11}
Grandfather's reading glasses double as an emergency
magnifier and, on holidays, as a solar cigar-lighter ---
but his short-sighted grandson's glasses do neither. Explain
both family facts.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:g8:lenses-images:12}
The microscope and the telescope both stack two \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{converging
lenses} --- yet one magnifies droplets and the other
galaxies. Contrast their two stages: what does \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} one
receive and produce in each instrument (near object against
practically-parallel starlight), and why does the telescope
want \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} one \emph{large} above all, while the microscope
wants it \emph{strongly curved}?
\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: The Optician's Bench}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- apprenticed to the town
optician; focal lengths, fittings and one cracked
telescope}]\label{pb:g8:lenses-images:1}
A day at the optician's: lenses to sort, spectacles to
match, and a customer's telescope in pieces.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Sorting the tray.}
A drawer spill has mixed the stock.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Propose two quick tests --- one by touch, one with
        the window and wall --- to sort converging from
        \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{diverging lenses}.
  \item \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{Lens} A throws a sharp \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} of the street onto the
        wall at \qty{25}{cm}. Its \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:focus}{focal length} and family?
  \item \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{Lens} B refuses to throw any \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} on the wall at any
        distance, and things viewed through it look smaller.
        Its family?
  \item Lenses C and D are both converging; C makes its
        \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{window-image} at \qty{10}{cm}, D at \qty{40}{cm}.
        Which is the more strongly curved, harder-bending
        \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Fittings.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item A customer sees distant road signs blurred but reads
        menus effortlessly. Diagnose the eye and prescribe
        the family.
  \item Another holds the newspaper at arm's length and
        complains their arms are ``too short''. Diagnose and
        prescribe.
  \item The optician warns the second customer: ``your new
        reading glasses will blur the mountains.'' Why are
        reading spectacles for near pages only?
  \item A child asks why the optician's trial lenses make
        the eye-chart swim while their own eyes are
        ``fine''. What is a corrective \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} doing to a
        well-focused eye?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The cracked
telescope.}
A customer brings a stargazing telescope with its front \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}
cracked, asking whether the spare magnifier in the box can
replace it.
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Name the two lenses' jobs in the healthy instrument:
        what does the great front \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} receive from a \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:star}{star}
        and make of it, and what does the small \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{eye-lens} do
        with that?
  \item The front \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens} is wide as a saucer; the \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{eye-lens},
        coin-small. Why does the front \omterm{def:g8:lenses-images:families}{lens}'s \emph{width}
        matter so much for faint \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:star}{stars}?
  \item The spare magnifier is strongly curved but
        coin-small. Judge the replacement: what would be
        gained and lost at the front of the instrument?
  \item Close the day with the optician's one-line creed
        about every instrument on the shelf --- spectacles,
        magnifiers, cameras, telescopes --- and the two-verb
        summary of what lenses do for civilization.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
