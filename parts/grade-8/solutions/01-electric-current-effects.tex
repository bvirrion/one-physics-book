\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g8:electric-current-effects} --- Electric Current and Its Effects}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:1}
Heating (the toaster, the kettle, the filament); magnetic (the
\omterm{def:g8:electric-current-effects:electromagnet}{electromagnet}, the \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor}, the doorbell); chemical (electroplating,
splitting water, charging batteries).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:2}
Heating; magnetic; chemical; heating; magnetic.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:3}
That a current creates magnetism around its wire --- the first
bridge between electricity and magnetism. Reversing the current
reverses the twitch.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:4}
It can be switched off, switched on, and have its poles flipped
at will --- and its strength can be dosed (more turns, more
current). A stone \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} can do none of these.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:5}
Bare turns touching would let the current shortcut across the
windings instead of touring the coil --- insulation \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{forces} the
long magnetizing path. And the coil alone opposes the march very
little: left connected it is nearly a \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:device}{short circuit}, warming coil
and \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} --- the \omterm{prop:g8:electric-current-effects:three}{heating effect} chaperones.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:6}
By opening a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}: current gone, magnetism gone, car dropped. A
stone \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} of such strength would hold forever --- releasing
the car would demand machines to tear it free.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:7}
In use: stored chemical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} becomes electrical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} driving
the march (ending as \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}, motion, warmth). On the charger:
electrical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} forced backward through the cell rebuilds the
chemical store.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:8}
Because the march itself is invisible: only its deeds betray it.
Warming, twitching, bubbling are the current's only signatures
--- and next chapter's instrument refines the magnetic twitch
into a numbered reading.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:9}
Button pressed: current flows, the \omterm{def:g8:electric-current-effects:electromagnet}{electromagnet} snatches the
hammer, ding --- but the hammer's swing opens a contact in its
own supply; magnetism dies, the spring returns the hammer,
remaking the contact; current returns, snatch again --- and so
on, many times a second. Holding is impossible by design: the
snatch always cuts its own cause --- and the rattle \emph{is}
the ring.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:10}
The thermostat's frail contacts carry only the coil's small
current; the coil's magnetism closes stout contacts that carry
the heater's hungry current in a separate loop. Small commands
large, and the two circuits never touch --- the delicate hand
pulls a \omterm{def:g3:levers-and-scales:lever}{lever}, not the load.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:11}
Protocol: hold the \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:compass}{compass} flat against the wall, far from iron
furniture; sweep slowly; where the needle twitches as the
suspect appliance is switched on and off, a current-carrying
wire runs. A strong twitch means a close or strong current.
Honest limit: needles also answer iron pipes, \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnets} and the
Earth itself --- so only a twitch that follows the switch
on--off proves a current.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:electric-current-effects:12}
Current makes the coil a \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet}; unlike poles of coil and frame
\omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{attract}, like poles repel, and the coil turns toward alignment.
Without reversal it would reach alignment and stop --- one
half-turn, then a shudder into rest: a door-closer, not a \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor}.
Reversing the current at the aligned moment swaps the coil's
poles: yesterday's attraction is now repulsion, alignment
becomes the worst place to be, and the coil must keep chasing a
goal that flips away each half-turn --- perpetual pursuit,
which is to say: rotation.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g8:electric-current-effects:1}
\textbf{1.} All three demonstrations in \emph{series} on one
loop with the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}: one march then serves warm wire, \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:compass}{compass}
wire and jar in turn --- one \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}, three effects.
\textbf{2.} The series law: the \emph{same} current crosses
thick and thin wire alike. What differs is only the road --- the
thin wire suffers the march more and warms: same dose, different
patient.
\textbf{3.} Reverse the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} \emph{and} pass the wire on the
\omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:compass}{compass}'s other side (or flip the \omterm{def:g4:magnets-and-compass:compass}{compass}): two reversals cancel,
and the twitch stays east.
\textbf{4.} That water's two components are present in a
two-to-one proportion --- twice the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:gas}{gas} from one lead as the
other.
\textbf{5.} Remove the self-interrupting contact (the piece the
hammer's swing opens): the \omterm{def:g8:electric-current-effects:electromagnet}{electromagnet} then holds the felt
hammer against the bell in silence --- the visitor hears one
soft ``dum'' and nothing more.
\textbf{6.} At the bin the operator opens the coil's \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}:
magnetism vanishes, iron drops. Aluminium rides on because
\omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnets} --- stone or current-made --- grip only iron's family,
and aluminium never answered them.
\textbf{7.} That the poles of a current-made \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} follow the
current's direction: reverse one, reverse the other.
\textbf{8.} A \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} drives plating by \emph{spending} its
chemical store; rebuilding a store requires \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} forced in
from outside. A \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} that refills itself from the very work
it pays for is the forever-machine again --- the \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} bill
cannot pay itself.
\textbf{9.} Grab: \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} closes, coil magnetizes, trinket held.
Swing: current maintained, grip maintained --- the \omterm{prop:g8:electric-current-effects:three}{magnetic
effect} on duty. Release: \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} opens over the chute, magnetism
dies, prize falls.
\textbf{10.} That the \omterm{def:g8:electric-current-effects:electromagnet}{electromagnet} lives by its current: sagging
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, weaker march, weaker grip --- switchability's price is
dependence. (The stone \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} never sags --- and never lets
go.)
\textbf{11.} More turns, stronger \omterm{def:g1:magnets:magnet}{magnet} at the same current:
firmer grip. The other dose is the current's own strength ---
the \emph{intensity}, measured in the next chapter.
\textbf{12.} For example: ``Heating: the \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse}, dying first and
faithfully. Magnetic: the crane game, gripping and releasing on
command. Chemical: the plating jar, silvering spoons with a
\omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's} patience.''
\end{solution}
