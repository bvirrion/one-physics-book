\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g8:sound-pitch-loudness} --- Sound: Speed, Pitch, Loudness}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:1}
\omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{Frequency}: the number of complete swings per second, in \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{hertz}
(\unit{Hz}). Concert A: \qty{440}{Hz}; the octave above:
\qty{880}{Hz} --- doubled.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:2}
$4$ swings in \qty{0.02}{s}: \qty{200}{Hz}; $16$ swings:
\qty{800}{Hz} --- the second \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sounds} higher (two octaves, in
fact).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:3}
About \qty{20}{Hz} to \qty{20000}{Hz}. Below: \omterm{prop:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:range}{infrasound} ---
elephants' long-range rumbles. Above: \omterm{prop:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:range}{ultrasound} --- the bat's
hunting clicks.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:4}
Louder, same note: the peaks grow taller, spacing unchanged.
Higher, same loudness: the peaks crowd closer, height
unchanged. Two independent dials.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:5}
Each raises the \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency}: shorter, tighter and thinner
strings all tremble more times per second --- higher $f$,
higher pitch.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:6}
Leaves \qty{10}{dB}; traffic \qty{80}{dB}; front row
\qty{110}{dB}; pain near \qty{120}{dB}. The damage band opens
around \qty{85}{dB}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:7}
The whistle's pitch sits just above \qty{20000}{Hz} ---
outside the owner's window, inside the dog's. Broken to one
pair of ears, piercing to the other: windows differ by
species.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:8}
That the \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed} is one and the same for every pitch and every
loudness: different \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speeds} would smear distant chords into
arpeggios and delay the quiet behind the loud --- the park
concert refutes both nightly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:9}
$50000 \times 0.01 = 500$ swings per click. Fine painting
needs fine brushes: only \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:vibration}{vibrations} with very short swings
--- very high $f$ --- can feel out small details in their
\omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echoes}; coarse \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{low-frequency} rumbles wash over a moth
unnoticed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:10}
Both keys sit inside the window --- \qty{27}{Hz} just above
the \qty{20}{Hz} floor, \qty{4200}{Hz} comfortably below the
ceiling. But age erodes the window from the \emph{top}: the
tuner's own ear dulls first in the treble, so the analyzer is
summoned for the highest octaves before the lowest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:11}
Tenfold growth hiding in equal steps --- each few rungs
multiply the \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}'s strength, not add to it. So ``a bit
louder'' on the ladder is \emph{much} stronger on the ear:
budgets collapse from \omterm{ex:g2:measuring-time:clock}{hours} to minutes across what reads as
a small turn of the dial.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:12}
Piano check: analyzer beside the string, compare its readout
with $440$. Back-row loudness: stand there with the decibel
readout during the drum test. Ventilation: aim the analyzer's
\omterm{prop:g8:colors-spectra:mixture}{spectrum} display at the low end and look for a spike below
\qty{20}{Hz}. Least trustworthy: the \omterm{prop:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:range}{infrasound} verdict ---
phone microphones are built for voices and go deaf near the
window's floor, so a silent screen proves little down there.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:1}
\textbf{1.} The piano is flat --- every A trembling eight
swings a second too few. The pitch dial: a tuner's key, not
an amplifier.
\textbf{2.} \qty{41}{Hz} sits just above the window's
\qty{20}{Hz} floor: heard, deeply --- and with swings that
slow, the chest's own drum answers along: felt as much as
heard.
\textbf{3.} Same pitch (equal spacing, \qty{440}{Hz}),
three times the \omterm{prop:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:loudness}{amplitude}: the singer sings the fork's note,
much louder.
\textbf{4.} Only the youngest ears --- the window's ceiling
near \qty{20000}{Hz} belongs to children; most adults'
windows have already lowered, and no listener certifies
\qty{22000}{Hz}.
\textbf{5.} The rock set (\qty{100}{dB}) and the finale
(\qty{110}{dB}) both stand above the \qty{85}{dB} damage
line: minutes-only territory; the acoustic set passes.
\textbf{6.} About \qty{80}{dB} behind the plugs --- back
below the damage line: an evening's exposure made
survivable, which is why the wise wear them always.
\textbf{7.} The spreading shells: each squeeze is shared
over a vaster shell with distance --- loudness (\omterm{prop:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:loudness}{amplitude})
fades. Pitch rides unchanged: distance turns one dial only.
\textbf{8.} Playing softly turns the \omterm{prop:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:loudness}{amplitude} dial alone
--- every note's \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency}, high and low, stays exactly
put. (His ``duller'' is the ear's own trick at low
loudness, not the physics of the strings.)
\textbf{9.} $680 \div 340 = \qty{2}{s}$ flash-to-bang. Two
thumps per second: \qty{2}{Hz} --- far below the floor:
felt in the chest, never heard as tone.
\textbf{10.} The bass's lowest swings: near the window's
floor and below, exactly where the voice-built microphone
goes deaf --- the rumble was real; the phone never caught
it.
\textbf{11.} For example: ``Pitch is \omterm{def:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:frequency}{frequency} --- the count
of swings per second, and the tuner's whole business.
Loudness is \omterm{prop:g8:sound-pitch-loudness:loudness}{amplitude} --- the swings' size, the nurse's
whole ledger. And neither dial touches the \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed}: every
note tonight crossed the hall at the same \qty{340}{m/s}.''
\textbf{12.} The window proposition: repeller pitched above
\qty{20000}{Hz} --- outside every human window, inside the
mouse's. One trembling, two audiences, no contradiction.
\end{solution}
