\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g8:intensity-ammeter} --- Current Intensity and the Ammeter}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:1}
The current's flow rate --- charge passing per second, as a
river counts water per second. \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{Unit}: the \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{ampere}, \unit{A};
$\qty{250}{mA} = \qty{0.25}{A}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:2}
\qty{0.02}{A}: indicator lamp. \qty{0.3}{A}: flashlight \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}.
\qty{10}{A}: kettle. \qty{100}{A}: starting engine.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:3}
It counts only the march passing through itself, so it must
stand in the road --- \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series}. It opposes the current almost
not at all; wired straight across a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} it therefore becomes
a \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:device}{short circuit} with a dial.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:4}
\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{Switch} off and break the loop; insert the meter with the current
entering by the \unit{A} \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} and leaving by common; start
on the largest range and step down; switch on, read, write the
\omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit}. The largest range first, so an unexpectedly strong current
meets the sturdiest setting instead of wrecking a delicate one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:5}
In a \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{series circuit} the \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{intensity} is the same at every point of
the loop. It turned into numbers the old observation that two
identical series \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} glow exactly equally, wherever they sit.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:6}
\qty{0.45}{A}, and \qty{0.45}{A}: one loop, one \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{intensity}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:7}
No. The law promises one value \emph{around the loop at a given
moment}, not the same value after the loop is rebuilt. The
second lamp weakened the march --- equally everywhere: all
tollbooths now agree on \qty{0.17}{A}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:8}
Wired the meter backward --- current entering by the common
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal}. On a digital meter, harmless: the minus sign is the
whole penalty (a needle meter would have slammed its stop).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:9}
$0.60 - 0.35 = \qty{0.25}{A}$ --- the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{junction} must balance:
what flows in flows out. The second \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{junction}'s tollbooth reads
the reunited \qty{0.60}{A}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:10}
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{In parallel}, the near-effortless meter is a bypass: the lamp,
deserted, dims to nothing, while the meter carries a rushing
current limited by almost nothing --- a \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:device}{short circuit} through
the instrument, blowing its internal \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} if fortune smiles.
The student built the villain of the safety chapter.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:11}
The main road carries the sum: \qty{0.60}{A}. A third identical
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} brings it to \qty{0.90}{A} --- and the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, serving
three full marches, drains fastest of all.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:intensity-ammeter:12}
Currents to know: the main road's $I$, and each \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}'s
($I_1$, $I_2$) --- three in all (the loop segments beyond the
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{junctions} repeat these). Two measurements suffice: \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measure} any
two, and the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{junction} balance $I = I_1 + I_2$ hands over the
third. Safe order: circuit off; insert the meter into the
chosen road; largest range; on; read; off before moving the
meter --- and the meter never, at any step, bridges two points
of different roads.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g8:intensity-ammeter:1}
\textbf{1.} Off; break the loop between lamp and \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery};
insert the \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:ammeter}{ammeter}, current in by \unit{A}, out by common;
largest range, step down; on; read; write the \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit}.
\textbf{2.} \qty{0.28}{A}.
\textbf{3.} \qty{280}{mA} again --- one loop, one \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{intensity}:
the tollbooth counts the same march on either side.
\textbf{4.} Anywhere on the loop --- both \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series}, any
positions: both show \qty{280}{mA}.
\textbf{5.} The law vetoes the prediction: series lamps carry
the identical current. The difference lies in the lamps
themselves --- unequal builds turn the same march into unequal
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} (the next chapters name what differs).
\textbf{6.} Every point of the loop: \qty{150}{mA} --- before
A, after B, beside the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}.
\textbf{7.} B dies (deserted by the bypass) and the loop,
having lost one opposer, carries a stronger march: the meter
climbs --- and lamp A brightens accordingly.
\textbf{8.} ``Across B the bypass still leaves lamp A guarding
the loop; across the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} it would leave nothing at all ---
and an unguarded march is the fire chapter, not a
demonstration.''
\textbf{9.} $0.52 - 0.30 = \qty{0.22}{A}$.
\textbf{10.} $0.40 + 0.30 = \qty{0.70}{A}$ --- and the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}
\omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} (or breaker) guarding the \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor}'s line is listening
closely: jammed \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motors} are how guards earn their keep.
\textbf{11.} Lamp \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}: \qty{0}{A}; \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor} \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}: its own
\qty{0.40}{A} unchanged (parallel independence); main road:
\qty{0.40}{A}.
\textbf{12.} Generosity is paid at the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}: two healthy
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches} drink two full marches, and the summed main-road
current empties the store fastest.
\textbf{13.} For example: ``An \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:ammeter}{ammeter} stands in the road,
never across it. One loop guarantees one \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{intensity} ---
everywhere, always. And a \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{junction} balances its books: marches
in equal marches out, to the milliampere.''
\end{solution}
