\chapter{Voltage and the Voltmeter}\label{ch:g8:voltage-voltmeter}

Since the first \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} lit the first \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, one word has done
heavy, vague duty in this book: the ``push''. Batteries push
weakly, the mains pushes murderously, two batteries nose-to-tail
push together. The push now becomes a measured quantity with a
famous \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit} --- and its instrument, unlike last chapter's,
refuses to stand in the road.

\section{Voltage}

\begin{definition}[Voltage]\label{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}
The \emph{voltage}\index{voltage} $U$ between two points of a
circuit \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measures} the electrical push available between them ---
how hard the current is driven from one point to the other, as
a waterfall's height \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measures} how hard it drives the
millwheel. Voltage is always a \emph{between}: between the two
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} of a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, between the two sides of a lamp ---
never at a single point alone. Its \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit} is the
\emph{volt}\index{volt} (\unit{V}), honoring the inventor of
the first \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The volt family album]\label{ex:g8:voltage-voltmeter:values}
A single round cell: \qty{1.5}{V}. The flat pocket \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}:
\qty{4.5}{V} --- three cells nose-to-tail inside one wrapper.
The rectangular \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{nine-volt} with both \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} on top:
\qty{9}{V}, six hidden cells. A car \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}: \qty{12}{V}. The
wall socket: \qty{230}{V} --- more than a hundred and fifty
little cells' worth of push, which is the whole quantitative
content of ``the socket, never''. Lightning between cloud and
ground: hundreds of millions of \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{volts}, briefly.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Voltage without current]\label{prop:g8:voltage-voltmeter:nocurrent}
\omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{Voltage} and current are different quantities, and \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} comes
first. A \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} alone in a drawer, wired to nothing, drives no
current at all --- yet between its \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} the full
\qty{1.5}{V} stands ready, measurable, like a waterfall's
height on a day the mill is closed. Push can exist without a
march; the march never exists without a push.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Voltmeter]\label{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}
A \emph{voltmeter}\index{voltmeter} \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measures} the \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage}
between two points --- so it is connected \emph{across} them:
\emph{\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in parallel}} with the component of interest, one lead on
each side, like a surveyor holding a level between two heights.
Built opposite to the \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:ammeter}{ammeter} in every way, it opposes current
almost \emph{completely}: connected across anything, it draws
next to none --- so clipping it on disturbs nothing, and no
loop need ever be broken.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[Measuring a voltage]\label{met:g8:voltage-voltmeter:measure}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item leave the circuit intact --- nothing is broken for a
        \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeter};
  \item clip one lead to each side of the component: the
        \unit{V} lead where the conventional current enters,
        the common lead where it leaves;
  \item largest range first, step down to comfort;
  \item read, and write the \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{unit}.
\end{enumerate}
The two meters' manners, side by side: \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:ammeter}{ammeter} --- break the
road, stand in it, oppose nothing; \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeter} --- break
nothing, stand aside, oppose everything. Confusing the two
manners is the workshop's most expensive habit.
\end{method}

\section{Rated voltages}

\begin{definition}[Nominal voltage]\label{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:nominal}
Every lamp, \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor} and appliance carries a \emph{nominal
voltage}\index{nominal voltage} --- the push it is built for,
stamped on its base or plate: a flashlight \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} ``3.5 V'', a
car headlamp ``12 V'', a kettle ``230 V''. Fed its nominal
\omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage}, the device performs as designed; fed much less, it
sulks --- dim lamp, lazy \omterm{ex:g6:circuits-and-safety:motor}{motor}; fed much more, it burns out ---
brilliantly, once.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Matching lamp to battery]\label{ex:g8:voltage-voltmeter:matching}
A ``3.5 V'' \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} on the \qty{4.5}{V} flat \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}: slightly
overdriven --- bright, and short-lived. On a single
\qty{1.5}{V} cell: a sleepy orange glow. On the \qty{9}{V}
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}: one dazzling instant, then the thin thread parts ---
the filament's obituary. The label is a contract: honor it, and
the lamp lives its advertised life.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Why 230 V kills]\label{rem:g8:voltage-voltmeter:mains}
Now the old absolute rule gets its number. The body's damp
conducting road, offered \qty{4.5}{V}, passes a current too
small to feel. The same road at \qty{230}{V} --- fifty times
the push --- passes fifty times the current, well past the few
hundredths of an \omterm{def:g8:intensity-ammeter:intensity}{ampere} that stop a heart. The socket is not
``somewhat'' stronger than your \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} bench: it is two
orders of magnitude beyond it, and the rulebook's severity is
plain arithmetic.
\end{remark}

\section{First voltage readings}

\begin{example}[Around a simple loop]\label{ex:g8:voltage-voltmeter:readings}
One \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, one lamp, meters in hand. Across the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}:
\qty{4.5}{V}. Across the lamp: \qty{4.5}{V} --- the lamp
receives what the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} offers. Across a stretch of
connecting \emph{wire}: \qty{0}{V}, or as near as the meter can
say --- a good wire is a level road, spending none of the push.
The push lives across the \emph{workers}, not along the roads:
a reading worth remembering when next chapter's laws make it
official.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The open switch's confession]\label{ex:g8:voltage-voltmeter:openswitch}
Lamp dark, \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} open --- where did the push go? Measure:
across the dark lamp, \qty{0}{V}; across the \emph{open
\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}}, the full \qty{4.5}{V}. The \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's} push stands
entire across the gap, waiting --- the one place in the loop
where two points are truly ``far apart'' electrically. Close
the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} and the readings trade places. An open \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} is
where the \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} waits.
\end{example}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g8:voltage-voltmeter:7}

\section{Problem: The Battery Drawer Audit}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- the great battery drawer
audit; a voltmeter, a shoebox of cells, and the family's
gadget shelf}]\label{pb:g8:voltage-voltmeter:1}
Every house has the drawer: loose cells, mystery batteries,
gadgets that ``need new ones''. Audit day --- \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeter} in
hand.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Sorting the cells.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Describe the correct \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeter} connection to check a
        loose \qty{1.5}{V} cell --- and why no circuit needs
        building first.
  \item The audit's readings, fresh from the drawer:
        \qty{1.58}{V}, \qty{1.32}{V}, \qty{0.9}{V},
        \qty{0.1}{V}. Sort them: fresh, usable, tired, dead.
  \item A \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{nine-volt} \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} reads \qty{8.9}{V}. Roughly what
        does each of its six inner cells contribute?
  \item Why does a nearly dead cell still show \emph{some}
        \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} although it can no longer \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} anything? (Its
        push survives feebly; what has it lost the strength to
        drive?)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The gadget shelf.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The camera wants two cells nose-to-tail. What \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage}
        does it expect, and what will the pair of
        \qty{1.32}{V} survivors from question 2 deliver?
  \item The old radio takes four cells nose-to-tail and sulks
        below \qty{5}{V}. Which drawer cells, if any, can crew
        it?
  \item Little brother crams three cells into the two-cell
        torch ``for extra brightness'', wedging the third
        across the spring. The \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} is rated \qty{3}{V}.
        Predict the show, in \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{nominal-voltage} language.
  \item The doorbell transformer plate reads ``\qty{8}{V}''.
        Someone proposes wiring the doorbell straight to a
        \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{nine-volt} \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} ``since eight is almost nine''.
        Assess: fair match or slow murder?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The audit's laws.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item In the torch (\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} and cells \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series}, \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} open),
        the auditor \omterm{def:g6:measurement-in-science:measuring}{measures}: across the open \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch},
        \qty{3.1}{V}; across the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, \qty{0}{V}. Explain
        both, and predict both after the click.
  \item Across every stretch of the torch's wiring the meter
        says \qty{0}{V}, lit or dark. What does this declare
        about good wire, in push-language?
  \item The mains socket is \emph{not} audited, by standing
        family law. Restate the law with this chapter's
        number, and the arithmetic sentence behind it.
  \item File the audit report: three sentences --- what a
        \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltmeter}{voltmeter} is and is never; what a label's \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:nominal}{nominal
        voltage} promises and threatens; and where, in any
        idle circuit, the \omterm{def:g8:voltage-voltmeter:voltage}{voltage} waits.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
