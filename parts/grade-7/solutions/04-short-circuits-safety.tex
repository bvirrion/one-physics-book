\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:short-circuits-safety} --- Short Circuits and Electrical Safety}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:1}
Between two roads joining the same points, the current crowds
onto the one opposing it less. A working lamp opposes the march;
a bare thick wire barely does --- so the march deserts the lamp
for the wire.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:2}
Device short: a bypass across one device --- it stops working
while the loop continues. \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{Battery} short: a bare road joining the
\omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's} own \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} with no device at all --- the graver
case: nothing opposes the march, and the stampede \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heats} wire and
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} toward ruin and fire.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:3}
The bypass steals \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 2's entire march --- deserted, it goes
dark. With one opposer bypassed, the whole loop opposes the
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} less, the march strengthens, and \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 1 --- now carrying
the stronger current alone --- brightens.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:4}
A marching current always warms its road, and a stampede warms
it furiously. The lamp uses the warming on purpose: its thread
is built to run white-hot and shine.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:5}
A deliberately thin sacrificial wire that \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{melts} --- cutting the
loop --- when the current exceeds its rating; it sacrifices
itself. \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{In series}, because the stampede must be forced through
the checkpoint: a parallel guard would only watch it pass.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:6}
The breaker snaps open instead of \omterm{def:g6:water-states:changes}{melting}, and is reset by a
flick --- no spare parts. Before any reset or replacement, the
fault must be found and cured: the guard tripped for a reason.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:7}
An overload: three innocent appliances asked one \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} to carry
their three full marches at once, and the summed rush warms the
wiring. The breaker trips on the sum --- correctly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:8}
A nail never \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{melts}: it silences the guard while the disease
rages. The next stampede meets no checkpoint, and the house's
wall wiring itself becomes the \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} --- charring where no one
can see.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:9}
A \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:device}{short circuit} upstream of the device --- effectively a short
across the supply as seen from that cord. The lamp dies
(deserted), the stampede rushes through the frayed contact and
\omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heats} the cable --- and the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}'s guard, \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} or breaker,
cuts the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}. That cut is the system working.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:10}
Laid across both \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs}, the wire joins --- through plain wire
and the \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's} leads --- the \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:battery}{battery's} own two \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminals} with
no device on the bypass road: a \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:device}{short circuit} of the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}.
Both \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} die and the stampede \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heats} wire and \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}; even at
toy scale it ruins cells and warms fingers, so it is
demonstrated in words, not deeds.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:11}
The dying \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s fused support wires became a built-in bypass:
the loop stays closed \emph{through} the dead \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s short, so
the string survives its loss --- one opposer fewer, so slightly
brighter. But each such death strengthens the march through the
survivors, hurrying the next failure: the string is politely
preparing its own cascade.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:short-circuits-safety:12}
One main guard and master switch on the common road; then each
\omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} --- \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights}, tools, charger --- with its own \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} and
its own guard sized to its \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} (the tools' \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} carries the
heaviest marches and earns the stoutest breaker; the charger's
thin \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} wants the most delicate \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse}, for it would char at
currents the main guard ignores). Notice above the drawer, for
example: ``A \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} that blows is telling the truth --- find the
fault. Replace like with like; foil and nails guard nothing but
the fire's path.''
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:short-circuits-safety:1}
\textbf{1.} An overload: three heat-hungry appliances summed
their honest marches on one \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} --- no bare wire needed; the
sum alone warms wiring past safety.
\textbf{2.} A \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:device}{short circuit} at the bite: from that moment the
march deserted the lamp beyond and stampeded through the touching
\omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductors}, heating the cord.
\textbf{3.} It abolished it: foil conducts any stampede without
\omterm{def:g6:water-states:changes}{melting} --- the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} stood guardless.
\textbf{4.} Likely story: the overload came first, and the true
\omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} blew --- honestly --- perhaps more than once; someone
``cured'' the nuisance with foil (exhibit three); when the
chewed cord's short finally struck, no guard remained, and the
stampede heated the wall wiring into fire instead of dying at a
blown \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} in the dark.
\textbf{5.} A current warms its road always; rush harder, warm
fiercer. The lamp proves warming can be tamed --- its thread is
designed for white \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat} in a safe \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}. Missing here: any
designed place for the \omterm{def:g5:heat-and-insulation:heat}{heat} to live, and any guard to stop it.
\textbf{6.} Thin on purpose so that it, of all the circuit's
wires, \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{melts} first: the guard takes the fatal heating in its own
three \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{centimetres} --- dying so the \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} in the walls do not.
\textbf{7.} A guard must \emph{fail} at the right moment: its
virtue is a designed weakness. The foil's excellence as a
\omterm{def:g4:conductors-insulators:conductor}{conductor} was precisely the corruption of the checkpoint ---
perfect passage, no protection.
\textbf{8.} Safety does not add like courtesy: three lawful
marches on one road sum to one unlawful rush --- the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}
obeys arithmetic, not intentions.
\textbf{9.} The foil. Overloads and chewed cords are mishaps the
system is built to survive --- guards exist for exactly them;
silencing the guard converted survivable faults into
catastrophe.
\textbf{10.} With a true \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse}: the cord's short strikes, the
rush leaps, the \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{melts} within moments --- the kitchen goes
dark, the family returns to a blown \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} and a chewed cord,
grumbles, repairs both, and never learns how bad the night
nearly was.
\textbf{11.} Each heat-maker to its own wall socket on its own
adequately-guarded \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}; the multi-socket restored to lamps
and chargers only, under the standing rule: one hungry appliance
per socket, and no strip ever feeds a heater.
\textbf{12.} For example: ``The villain's method is the
effortless road: offer the march a bypass and it will take it,
heating as it rushes. The guard's duty is to stand \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series} and
fail first, honestly and cheaply. And no household may ever
improvise a guard: a \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} is replaced by a \omterm{def:g7:short-circuits-safety:fuse}{fuse} --- like for
like --- or the walls themselves will one day do the \omterm{def:g6:water-states:changes}{melting}.''
\end{solution}
