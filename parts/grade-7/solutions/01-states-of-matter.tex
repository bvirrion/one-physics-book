\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:states-of-matter} --- States of Matter and Changes of State}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:1}
\omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:solid}{Solid}: \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} packed in an orderly stack, trembling in place.
\omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{Liquid}: \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} touching but disordered, sliding past one
another. \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:gas}{Gas}: \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} far apart, flying fast in all directions.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:2}
In the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:solid}{solid} every \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecule} holds its post in the stack, so the
whole keeps its shape. In the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid} the \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} slide freely
past each other while still touching: the crowd slumps to fit the
container, until nothing can slide lower --- the level surface.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:3}
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{Air} is mostly empty space between flying \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules}: pushing packs
the flyers closer (and their hammering fights back --- the
spring). Water's \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} already touch; there is no emptiness to
squeeze away, so the plunger meets a wall.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:4}
That \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:gas}{gas} \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} fly far apart with great emptiness between ---
room-fulls can be packed together. Water, its \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} already
touching, is incompressible: no pump packs it tighter.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:5}
Warmth makes the stacked \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} tremble harder and harder; at
the \omterm{def:g4:changes-of-state:melting-point}{melting point} the trembling overpowers the stack's grip, the
ranks break, and the \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} begin to slide: the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:solid}{solid} has
become \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:6}
A change of state only rearranges the same \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} --- none are
created or destroyed, so the \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} cannot change. Losing \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} would
require \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} to vanish (or escape the container --- the
uncapped bottle's false alarm).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:7}
The liveliness of molecular motion. When hot touches cold, the
lively \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} batter the sluggish ones, handing over liveliness
collision by collision --- the lively calm down, the sluggish
quicken --- until both crowds share one pace: equal \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{temperatures}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:8}
Perfume \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} fly, collide with \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules}, rebound, and
wander --- zigzag by zigzag across the room. A breeze carries
whole crowds of them bodily: riding the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} beats staggering
through the crowd.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:9}
Only the \emph{fastest} \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} can tear free of the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid}'s
grip, so evaporation is a tax on liveliness: the escapees carry
away more than their share, and the crowd left behind is, on
average, slower --- that is, cooler. Sweat and the wet finger
chill by exporting their liveliest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:10}
In the sun the trapped \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} fly faster and hammer the skin
harder and more often: the hailstorm inflates the balloon until
the stretched skin balances it. In the cold the same \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules}
slow, the hammering weakens, and the outside \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}'s push squashes
the balloon smaller.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:11}
Freezing water locks into an unusually airy, open stack --- more
empty space than the sliding crowd had --- so ice takes more room
for the same \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules}: \omterm{def:g6:density-floating:density}{density} below the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid}'s, and it
\omterm{def:g1:floating-and-sinking:words}{floats}. Were water ordinary, ice would stack tight, \omterm{def:g1:floating-and-sinking:words}{sink} as it
formed, and ponds would \omterm{def:g2:ice-water-steam:meltfreeze}{freeze} from the bottom up --- with no
sheltered water left for the fish.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:states-of-matter:12}
Nothing stirred the water, yet the ink traveled: the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid}'s own
\omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} are in ceaseless motion, jostling the ink \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules}
step by random step through the crowd --- both \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquids} are alive
with motion at every instant, even when the glass looks
perfectly still. Warmth quickens every jostle, so the spreading
hurries. Patient ink thus made the invisible dance visible ---
evidence that convinced the doubters of \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} themselves.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:states-of-matter:1}
\textbf{1.} In neat rows, shoulder to shoulder, each jiggling on
the spot --- and none may swap places or leave their post.
\textbf{2.} Ranks dissolve: pupils stay shoulder to shoulder
(touching!) but now weave and slide past one another. They must
still keep contact with the crowd --- no one may run free.
\textbf{3.} Pupils scatter, running fast and straight until they
collide or hit a wall, rebounding endlessly --- and since nothing
holds them together, only walls stop them: the crowd claims every
corner the gym offers.
\textbf{4.} Yes --- the same thirty in every scene: \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} survives
changes of state, staged.
\textbf{5.} The \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:gas}{gas} scene allows it --- runners crowd into half
the gym (and hammer the herders harder). The \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid} huddle cannot
shrink: the pupils already touch. Staged: gases squeeze, \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquids}
do not.
\textbf{6.} Jiggle harder and harder at their posts; the scene
becomes the \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid} the moment jiggling bursts the ranks and
pupils begin to slide past each other.
\textbf{7.} The dial's \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} is spent breaking the stack's grip
--- rank by rank --- not on faster motion: the \omterm{def:g3:temperature-thermometers:temperature}{thermometer} critic
must report the average liveliness \emph{unchanged} until the
last rank breaks. The plateau, staged.
\textbf{8.} The \emph{fastest} pupils --- only they can tear
free. The remaining huddle's average liveliness drops: the
\omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid} cools itself by evaporating, exactly the chill of sweat
and wet fingers.
\textbf{9.} The actors keep colliding with the rope and shoving
it outward; faster actors shove harder and more often, and the
ring swells --- the balloon in the sun.
\textbf{10.} ``Stack in \emph{open} order --- arms' length,
airy'': water's \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:solid}{solid} takes more room than its \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:liquid}{liquid}, so the
ice-scene raft would \omterm{def:g1:floating-and-sinking:words}{float} on the liquid-scene crowd.
\textbf{11.} The blue shirt is jostled --- bumped step by random
step through the sliding crowd until it has wandered everywhere:
ink diffusing. Turn the dial up and every jostle quickens, so the
wandering hurries.
\textbf{12.} For example: ``Tonight's play stages one idea: all
matter is \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} in motion. Granted, two old laws come free
--- \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} surviving every change of state, and the \omterm{def:g6:water-states:changes}{melting}
plateau. What we cannot stage is the scale: real actors are
smaller than any seeing and more numerous than all the audiences
of the world.''
\end{solution}
