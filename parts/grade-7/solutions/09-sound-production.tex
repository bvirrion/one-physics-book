\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:sound-production} --- Sound: Production and Propagation}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:1}
The substance a \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} travels through --- \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}, water, wood,
steel, any molecular crowd. The \omterm{def:g7:sound-production:medium}{non-medium}: vacuum.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:2}
The relay travels: a racing pattern of squeezes handed from rank
to rank of the crowd. Each \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecule} only jiggles about its home
place --- the message moves, the messengers stay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:3}
No crowd, no relay: the pumped-out jar leaves the bell's
trembling walls shoving at nothing, so no message forms. Space
battles are silent for the same reason --- explosions between the
\omterm{def:g4:solar-system:star}{stars} have no crowd to carry their roar.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:4}
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{Air} about \qty{340}{m/s}; water about \qty{1500}{m/s}; steel
about \qty{5000}{m/s}. The tighter the \omterm{def:g7:states-of-matter:molecule}{molecules} touch, the
faster the shove is handed on: flying, touching, locked.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:5}
Steel's locked ranks relay nearly hand-to-hand: about fifteen
times the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}'s pace, so the rail's message arrives long before
the airborne rumble.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:6}
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{Light}'s trip across any field counts as instant beside \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}'s:
the seen strike marks the true start to far better precision than
the stopwatch hand can use.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:7}
$340 \times 1 = \qty{340}{m}$ round trip: the wall stands at
\qty{170}{m}. Answering \qty{340}{m} forgets that the second
paid for going \emph{and} coming back.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:8}
In water: $1500 \times 2 = \qty{3000}{m}$ round trip --- depth
\qty{1500}{m}. The slip: borrowing the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}'s \qty{340}{m/s} for
an underwater journey.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:9}
$d = 340 \times 2.5 = \qty{850}{m}$. The firework's flash gave a
true starting gun; the unseen jet offers none --- no moment of
emission to time from --- and worse, the heard roar left the jet
seconds ago: the ear points at where the jet \emph{was}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:10}
The underwater ear is served by water's \qty{1500}{m/s} relay,
the deck ear by \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}'s \qty{340}{m/s} --- and much of the
speaker's \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} never crosses the surface border into the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} at
all. Faster \omterm{def:g7:sound-production:medium}{medium}, private message: the swimmer wins despite
the distance.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:11}
One blow, two relays: through steel and through \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}. Steel:
$3000 \div 5000 = \qty{0.6}{s}$; \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}: $3000 \div 340 \approx
\qty{8.8}{s}$ --- the clang leads the boom by about
\qty{8.2}{s}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:sound-production:12}
One clack sends the same instant into both media; at
\qty{750}{m} the recorder captures the water arrival (on the
hydrophone) and the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} arrival (on the microphone). Expected
times: water $750 \div 1500 = \qty{0.5}{s}$, \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} $750 \div 340
\approx \qty{2.2}{s}$ --- a gap of about \qty{1.7}{s}. Run
backward: with $d$ and the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed} known, the measured gap
gives the water arrival time ($t_{\text{air}} -
\text{gap}$), and $v = 750 \div 0.5 = \qty{1500}{m/s}$ ---
water's pace measured without ever timing the silent clack
itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:sound-production:1}
\textbf{1.} $340 \times 4 = \qty{1360}{m}$ round trip: width
\qty{680}{m}.
\textbf{2.} $340 \times 1.5 = 510$; half: \qty{255}{m}.
\textbf{3.} First wall: $340 \times 1 \div 2 = \qty{170}{m}$;
second: $340 \times 2 \div 2 = \qty{340}{m}$; the canyon spans
$170 + 340 = \qty{510}{m}$ along that line.
\textbf{4.} The formula is exact; the hand is not: human
starting and stopping scatters by tenths of a second --- tens of
\omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} at \qty{340}{m/s}. Hence the old rule: repeat, watch the
readings huddle, report the huddle.
\textbf{5.} $1500 \times 0.2 = \qty{300}{m}$ round trip: depth
\qty{150}{m}.
\textbf{6.} $1500 \times 0.4 \div 2 = \qty{300}{m}$.
\textbf{7.} At the air--water border most of a shout is turned
back --- little enters the river, and the weak bottom \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo} must
cross the border again to reach an ear in \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}: almost nothing
survives. And even granting the \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo}, round trips of tenths of a
second defeat any by-ear timing.
\textbf{8.} The \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo} time lengthens abruptly as the boat crosses
the drop --- deeper bottom, longer round trip: the sonar draws
the cliff underwater.
\textbf{9.} Steel: $2000 \div 5000 = \qty{0.4}{s}$; \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}: $2000
\div 340 \approx \qty{5.9}{s}$.
\textbf{10.} Both relays start together, so the gap grows in
proportion to the distance: each kilometre adds a fixed extra
delay to the slow relay over the fast one. Measure the gap,
divide by the per-kilometre difference, and the distance falls
out --- one hammer blow, no stopwatch at the far end needed.
\textbf{11.} The \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{rail-sound}: fog is a crowd of droplets that
scatters \emph{\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}} hopelessly but barely troubles a relay
locked inside \omterm{def:g3:solids-liquids-gases:solid}{solid} steel (\omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{air-sound} too survives fog well ---
but the rail's message is the cleanest and fastest of the
three).
\textbf{12.} For example: ``Every \omterm{ex:g4:how-sound-travels:echo}{echo} pays for the round trip:
halve before believing. Every \omterm{def:g7:sound-production:medium}{medium} sets its own pace: choose
the \omterm{def:g7:motion-average-speed:formula}{speed} with the crowd, never by habit. And every timing in
this canyon started with the eye --- \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}'s trip counts as
instant here, which is why seeing the hammer fall may serve as
the starting gun.''
\end{solution}
