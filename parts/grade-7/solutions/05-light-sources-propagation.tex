\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:light-sources-propagation} --- Sources of Light and Propagation}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:1}
\omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:primary}{Primary sources}: the candle flame, the firefly, the red traffic
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}. Diffusers: the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}, this page, Venus --- the \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planet}
shines by diffused sunlight, however star-like it looks.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:2}
It re-sends part of the received \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} in all directions. In a
dark room it receives nothing, forwards nothing --- and is
invisible.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:3}
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{Light} must leave the object toward the eye. A perfect polish
refuses to \emph{diffuse}: it sends \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} on in one disciplined
direction only, so no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} says ``here is the surface'' to every
viewpoint --- the eye receives only the re-directed pictures of
other things.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:4}
The apple diffuses to all directions at once --- every eye gets a
share. The spoon's polish sends the lamp's \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} in one direction
only, and just one eye position sits on that single road.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:5}
Clean \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} forwards almost nothing sideways, so no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{beam-light}
enters an eye off the \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam}'s path. Mist fills the \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam} with tiny
diffusers, each scattering a share toward you: \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{diffusion} draws
the line.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:6}
\omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{Transparent}: window glass, shallow clear water. \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{Translucent}:
tracing paper, the frosted envelope. \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{Opaque}: the brick, the foil.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:7}
Brightness passes; \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{images} are stopped (scrambled). The bathroom
window admits daylight and denies onlookers; the shade converts a
blinding point-filament into a broad gentle glow.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:8}
The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} has no \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}: nothing above the astronauts diffused the
passing sunlight toward their eyes, so the sky offered nothing to
see --- black at noon. Earth's \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} forwards a share of sunlight
from every direction of the sky: our bright blue dome is
\omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{diffusion} overhead.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:9}
Each added layer scrambles and steals a little more: teams are
thickness-dependent. Water is the chapter's second example ---
\omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{transparent} by the glassful, \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{opaque} by the ocean. (Thin paper
\omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{translucent}, the closed book \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{opaque}.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:10}
Something for the \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam} to strike: theatrical haze or smoke ---
crowds of tiny diffusers that catch \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{beam-light} and scatter a
share to every seat. They are buying \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{diffusion}, the only way a
sideways eye ever sees a \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:11}
Fire (\omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:primary}{primary source}) $\to$ straight line to the face $\to$ the
face diffuses $\to$ straight line to your eye: one diffuser in
the chain. Via the lake: face-diffused \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} travels straight to
the still water, is mirror-bounced (not diffused --- the lake is
polished), and continues straight to your eye: still one
\emph{diffuser}, plus one mirror-bounce --- the face, then the
lake's disciplined fold.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:light-sources-propagation:12}
Sunlight crossing \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} loses a share to \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{diffusion} all along its
path --- the longer the path, the greater the theft. At \omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{sunset}
the Sun's \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} grazes the \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere} along its longest road, so
the direct \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam} reaches you robbed and weakened --- the tamed
orange disc. The stolen share has not vanished: it was
scattered along the way, delivered to other skies as their
daylight --- and some, scattered late, paints the high clouds
pink. (Which colors are stolen soonest is next year's story.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:light-sources-propagation:1}
\textbf{1.} Marble makes no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}: seeing needs \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} leaving
the object into the eye, and an unlit diffuser sends nothing.
White is a promise about \emph{forwarding}, worthless without a
delivery.
\textbf{2.} \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:primary}{Primary sources}: the lamps, alone. Diffusers:
statue, paintings, visitors --- and the case's glass faintly
(mostly it transmits).
\textbf{3.} \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{Diffusion}: marble's surface scatters received \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}
to all directions, so every position in the hall is served and
none is dazzled.
\textbf{4.} Mirror-manner \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{reflection}: the polished brass bounces
the lamp's \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} at equal angles into one direction --- the
dazzle lands only where that single road goes.
\textbf{5.} \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{Translucent}: the globe passes the brightness and
scrambles the \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} --- the filament's point-glare becomes a
soft ball of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}.
\textbf{6.} By the bounce rule, a lamp shows in the varnish when
lamp and viewer sit at equal angles on either side of the
painting's surface. The designer places lamps high and steep, so
their bounced road dives to the floor in front of the painting
--- never into the main viewing spot's eyes.
\textbf{7.} Glass mostly transmits but always mirror-bounces a
faint share --- clear windows are weak \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors}, seen against
darkness behind. The black niche gives the transmitted view a
dark ground so the faint \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{reflections} cannot compete --- ghost
sleeves fade.
\textbf{8.} Both: physically the threads are \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{diffusion} by dust
motes --- \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{beam-light} scattered to the visitors' eyes; and dusty
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} is indeed what makes them possible. The physics is with the
designer; the poetry with the curator.
\textbf{9.} No source, no leaving \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}, nothing to receive:
whiteness is a high forwarding rate applied to nothing.
Condition one fails for every object at once.
\textbf{10.} Soft, even, shadowless daylight over the hall ---
brightness without \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{images}: no cloud views, and no direct
sun-dazzle on the marble.
\textbf{11.} The sky itself: \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} diffusing sunlight. North \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}
is sunshine forwarded by a hemisphere of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} --- gentle,
sourceless, and still solar; only the direct \omterm{def:g6:light-propagation:ray}{beam} has been
declined.
\textbf{12.} For example: ``\omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{Diffusion} is our best friend: it
hands every artwork to every visitor without a single dazzle.
Mirror-manner surfaces --- varnish, brass, case glass --- must
be watched, for they deliver lamps to eyes along exact,
predictable roads. And each glass serves by team: \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{transparent}
for seeing in, \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{translucent} for glare and skylight, \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:coats}{opaque} for
the dark.''
\end{solution}
