\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:shadows-eclipses} --- Shadows and Eclipses}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:1}
\omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{Umbra}: the source is entirely hidden --- \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} from no part of it
arrives. \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{Penumbra}: the source is partly hidden --- \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} from
part of it still arrives, peeking over the blocker's edge.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:2}
A point has no ``parts'' to be partly hidden: every place either
sees it or does not --- sharp \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}, no fringe. An overcast sky
is a source so broad that almost every spot sees most of it:
nearly all \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{penumbra}, \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} washed away.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:3}
The rays from the source's \emph{edges}, grazing the blocker's
sides. The \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{umbra} is the region both edges' rays miss.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:4}
Solar: Sun--Moon--Earth, in that order --- the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} must be new
(between us and the Sun). Lunar: Sun--Earth--Moon --- the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}
must be full (opposite the Sun).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:5}
Totality: only those inside the \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{umbra}'s small racing spot. A
partial bite: the great penumbral surroundings. Nothing at all:
everyone the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} regions miss --- most of the \omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planet}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:6}
The \omterm{def:g7:shadows-eclipses:lunar}{lunar eclipse} is the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} itself dimming --- a change on an
object the whole night side can see at once. Solar totality
requires standing \emph{inside} the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s slender \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{umbra} where
it touches the ground: a spot, not a sky-wide event.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:7}
The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s \omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbit} tilts about five degrees against the Earth--Sun
plane: most new and \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{full moons} pass above or below the exact
line, and the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} miss. Only when phase and plane-crossing
coincide do the three worlds align.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:8}
The pinhole projection onto paper, and the leafy tree's ground
\omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescents} (or a colander); certified, undamaged eclipse glasses
for direct viewing. Bare eyes: only during totality itself, and
the first returning sliver ends the permission.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:9}
The Sun is about $400$ times the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s width at about $400$
times its distance, so the two discs match in the sky almost
exactly. Children of the fit: totality exists but only just ---
brief, with a tiny umbra-spot; and when the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} rides the far
part of its oval \omterm{def:g6:sun-earth-moon:axisorbit}{orbit}, its slightly smaller disc leaves the
Sun's rim uncovered --- the annular ring.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:10}
Every \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}'s penumbral hem is drawn by the Sun's edges, and the
construction shows the hem widening with the gap between blocker
and screen: rays from opposite solar edges spread farther apart
the longer they travel past the blocker. Feet stand at zero gap
--- crisp; the head's \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} lies two \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} of spreading away
--- soft.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:11}
Each leaf-gap is a natural pinhole camera; each ground-patch is
that pinhole's \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} of the Sun --- round on ordinary days.
During the partial eclipse the Sun itself is a \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescent}, so every
faithful little camera projects a \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescent} --- and all point the
same way because all copy the one \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescent} in the sky.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:shadows-eclipses:12}
Sunlight grazing the Earth's rim crosses the \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere} along its
longest possible roads --- sunset-length paths all around the
\omterm{def:g4:solar-system:planet}{planet} at once. The \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} diffuses away much of the passing \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}
(the share that paints Earth's skies), and the \omterm{def:g7:light-sources-propagation:diffusion}{diffusion} robs the
short-path colors soonest, so the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} that survives the long
grazing journey and bends on to the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} is the \omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{sunset}'s
survivor: red. The eclipsed \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} glows with the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} of every
\omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{sunrise} and \omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{sunset} on Earth falling on it together.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:shadows-eclipses:1}
\textbf{1.} The stripe is the path of the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{umbra} racing
across the ground; the broad flanking zone is the \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{penumbra}'s
footprint.
\textbf{2.} A partial eclipse: the town sits in the \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{penumbra} ---
a bite out of the Sun, watched through proper protection, and no
darkness.
\textbf{3.} Even a five-percent sliver of Sun is thousands of
times brighter than full moonlight: it keeps the sky lit and the
corona invisible. Night-at-noon belongs to the \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{umbra} alone ---
there is no ``almost totality''.
\textbf{4.} The umbra-spot is roughly round: a camp on the
centerline sits under the spot's full width as it races past,
while camps near the stripe's edge catch only a chord of the
circle --- a shorter crossing, a shorter totality.
\textbf{5.} Certified eclipse glasses block the Sun's burn;
sunglasses do not --- they dim glare while letting the
retina-burning share through, making comfortable staring
possible: worse than nothing.
\textbf{6.} Pinhole in one end, tracing-paper screen at the
other (or plain paper on the ground): stand with your \emph{back}
to the Sun, hole aimed over your shoulder, and watch the
projected disc --- never through the hole.
\textbf{7.} Rejected: water's \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{reflection} is a \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{mirror-image} of
the Sun, nearly as blinding as the Sun itself --- a bounced burn
is still a burn.
\textbf{8.} Dozens of \omterm{ex:g2:moon-in-the-sky:shapes}{crescent} \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{images} on the sheet: each hole is
a pinhole camera, and each projects the bitten Sun --- a colander
full of eclipses.
\textbf{9.} The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} --- invisible against the glare until its
disc's edge starts covering the Sun's; the camp has entered the
\omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{penumbra}.
\textbf{10.} The visible Sun is shrinking toward a thin sliver
--- an ever more point-like source. Smaller source, narrower
penumbral hems: every \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}'s soft edge tightens, and the world
turns strange and sharp.
\textbf{11.} The \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{umbra} itself --- the class stands inside
the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} cone's tip as it races over the camp. Around the
black disc hangs the corona, the Sun's pearly outer \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere},
visible only now; the first sliver of returning disc restores
full daylight rules --- glasses on.
\textbf{12.} For example: ``An eclipse is a \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} with worlds
for lamp, blocker and screen: the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon}'s \omterm{prop:g7:shadows-eclipses:umbra}{umbra} brushing our
ground, or the \omterm{def:g2:moon-in-the-sky:moon}{Moon} sailing through ours --- geometry you can
draw with two rays and a ruler, staged on the grandest scale in
nature.''
\end{solution}
