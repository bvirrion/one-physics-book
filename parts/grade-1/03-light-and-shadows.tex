\chapter{Light and Shadows}\label{ch:g1:light-and-shadows}

Switch off the lamp in a room with no window: everything disappears.
Switch it on: the room comes back. Seeing needs \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}. And wherever
there is \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}, there are also \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} --- your faithful dark twin
follows you on every sunny pavement. Let us find out where \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}
comes from, and how \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} are made.

\section{Where light comes from}

\begin{definition}[Light sources]\label{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}
A \emph{light source}\index{light source} is a thing that makes its
own \emph{light}\index{light}: the Sun, a lamp, a candle flame, a
firefly. Everything else --- a book, a ball, the walls, you --- makes
no light of its own: we only see these things when light from a
source falls on them.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Sources or not?]\label{ex:g1:light-and-shadows:sources}
The Sun, a torch, a burning match and a television screen all make
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}: sources. The Moon, a mirror and a white wall can look very
bright, but they make no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} of their own --- they only send back
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} that reaches them. Switch the lamp off, and the brightest
mirror goes dark.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Try it: the dark room]\label{ex:g1:light-and-shadows:darkroom}
In the evening, close the shutters or curtains of a room, switch off
every lamp, and wait while you count slowly to $20$. What do you see?
At first, almost nothing --- with no source, there is no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} to see
by. Then open the door a crack: one thin slice of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} is enough to
bring back tables, chairs and toys.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Never look at the Sun]\label{rem:g1:light-and-shadows:sun}
The Sun is by far the strongest \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light source} we ever meet. Never look
straight at it, not even for a moment, and never through binoculars
or a magnifying glass: its \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} is so strong that it can hurt your
eyes for good. The Sun is to be enjoyed sideways --- by the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} it
throws on everything else.
\end{remark}

\section{How shadows are made}

\begin{definition}[Shadow]\label{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}
A \emph{shadow}\index{shadow} is the dark patch that appears behind a
thing that blocks the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}. \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{Light} cannot go through your body, so
behind you the ground gets no \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} from the Sun: that dark
you-shaped patch is your shadow.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[Making a sharp shadow]\label{met:g1:light-and-shadows:make}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Make the room dark --- \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} hide when \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} comes from
        everywhere;
  \item switch on \emph{one} lamp or torch;
  \item hold a toy between the lamp and the wall;
  \item look at the wall: there is the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}, on the far side of the
        toy from the lamp.
\end{enumerate}
Move the toy closer to the lamp: the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} grows huge. Move it
close to the wall: the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} shrinks to the toy's true size.
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Try it: shadow animals]\label{ex:g1:light-and-shadows:animals}
With the lamp of \cref{met:g1:light-and-shadows:make}, put your hands
in the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} instead of a toy: two fingers up make rabbit ears, both
thumbs crossed make a flying bird. The wall shows the shape your
hands cut out of the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} --- a \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} always keeps the shape of its
blocker, seen from the lamp.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[A shadow is not a thing]\label{rem:g1:light-and-shadows:notathing}
You cannot pick up a \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}, fold it, or keep it in a box. A \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}
is not made of anything: it is a \emph{place where \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} is
missing}. That is why it vanishes the moment the lamp goes out ---
there is nothing there to stay behind.
\end{remark}

\section{Shadows follow the light}

\begin{example}[Which side is the shadow on?]\label{ex:g1:light-and-shadows:opposite}
In the morning, the Sun is on one side of you and your \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} lies on
the \emph{other} side. Face the Sun: your \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} hides behind you.
Turn your back to the Sun: your \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} stretches out in front. The
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} always sits exactly opposite the source.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Try it: chalk around a shadow]\label{ex:g1:light-and-shadows:chalk}
On a sunny morning, stand still in the playground while a friend
draws a chalk line around your \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}. Come back after lunch and
stand on the same spot: your \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} has moved out of its chalk line
and changed its length! The Sun changed its place in the sky --- and
the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} followed. The next chapter follows the Sun through its
whole day.
\end{example}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:1}
Sort into \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light sources} and non-sources: a candle flame; \ the Moon;
\ a firefly; \ a mirror; \ a torch; \ a white wall.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:2}
Why can you see nothing at all in a closed room with no window and no
lamp? What is missing?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:3}
Name the three things needed to make a \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} on the wall, and put
them in the right order along a line.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:4}
The Sun is on your left. On which side is your \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}? You turn
right around --- where is it now?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:5}
Using \cref{met:g1:light-and-shadows:make}, make the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} of the
same toy first huge, then small. What did you move, and which way?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:6}
Can a \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} be picked up and put in a pocket? Explain what a \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow}
really is, using \cref{rem:g1:light-and-shadows:notathing}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:7}
Why must you never look straight at the Sun, even though looking at a
lamp only dazzles you a little?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:8}
At midday the Sun hangs high; in the late afternoon it hangs low.
When is your \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} longer? Draw the two \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadows} of a tree, one for
each moment.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g1:light-and-shadows:9}
Mia says: ``My \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} was in front of me, and after I turned around
it was behind me --- so the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} turned with me.'' Sam says the
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} never moved at all. Think of
\cref{ex:g1:light-and-shadows:opposite}: who is right, and why did it
\emph{look} like the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} turned?
\end{exercise}
