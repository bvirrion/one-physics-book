\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g1:day-and-night} --- Day and Night: The Sun}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:1}
\omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{Sunrise}, midday, afternoon, \omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{sunset}, night.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:2}
At midday the Sun stands at its highest --- and the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:shadow}{shadow} is at its
shortest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:3}
Always on the same side. To check: notice tomorrow morning which
window the first sunlight comes through --- it will be the same
window as today.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:4}
Not true. The Sun shines all night long --- on the other side of the
Earth. Night comes because our side of the spinning Earth has turned
away from the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}, like the back of your head turned away from the
lamp.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:5}
It is night for them, so they are most likely asleep --- their side
of the Earth is turned away from the Sun while ours faces it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:6}
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{Light} on the right cheek and still turning: next the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} reaches
your full face --- \omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{sunrise} (morning) for your nose.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:7}
The ground under our feet. The Earth spins, carrying us around; the
Sun only \emph{seems} to cross our sky because we are turning.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:8}
Seven \omterm{def:g1:day-and-night:daynight}{sunrises} and seven nights --- one of each for every day of the
week.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:day-and-night:9}
She could be right: if our side kept facing the Sun, it would stay
daytime here. But children on the far side would then keep night
forever --- they would not thank Zoe for her idea!
\end{solution}
