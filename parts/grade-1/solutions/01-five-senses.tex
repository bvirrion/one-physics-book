\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g1:five-senses} --- Observing the World: The Five Senses}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:1}
Sight --- the eyes; \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:senses}{hearing} --- the ears; touch --- the skin; smell
--- the nose; taste --- the tongue.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:2}
(a) \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:senses}{Hearing}. (b) Taste. (c) Touch. (d) Sight.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:3}
Smell finds the cake first --- the perfume slips under the door.
Eating the slice uses sight, touch, smell and taste, and even \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:senses}{hearing}
if the crust crunches.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:4}
Answers vary; most children count $4$ or $5$ sounds, and the surprise
is often a quiet one --- the refrigerator humming, or their own
breathing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:5}
For example: ``My shoe is blue, rubbery, and longer than my hand.''
Any three precise words that a friend could check are correct.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:6}
Ana's sentence is the \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:observation}{observation}, made by touch. Bob's sentence is
an opinion: nobody can check ``best'' by looking or touching.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:7}
(a) Smell. (b) Touch --- the skin feels the heat, even from a small
distance. (c) \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:senses}{Hearing}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:8}
Touch alone names all three easily: metal spoon (hard, cold, smooth),
sponge (soft, light), pebble (hard, heavy, round). The spoon is
usually easiest --- cold metal is hard to mistake.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:five-senses:9}
Lay a piece of string along the first line and cut or pinch it to the
same length, then lay it along the second line: the string compares
them without the eyes deciding. The same string can follow a curvy
line too, then be pulled straight --- so the method even compares a
curve with a straight line.
\end{solution}
