\chapter{Observing the World: The Five Senses}\label{ch:g1:five-senses}

A bird sings somewhere above you. The bread smells warm. The pavement
feels cold through your socks. Before doing anything else, physics
looks, listens and touches: this chapter is about observing the world
with the five senses --- and about the tricks they can play on us.

\section{The five senses}

\begin{definition}[The five senses]\label{def:g1:five-senses:senses}
A \emph{sense}\index{sense} is a way the body finds out about the
world. We have five:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{sight}\index{sight} --- the eyes see light, colors and
        shapes;
  \item \emph{hearing}\index{hearing} --- the ears hear sounds;
  \item \emph{touch}\index{touch} --- the skin feels rough or smooth,
        warm or cold;
  \item \emph{smell}\index{smell} --- the nose smells the warm bread;
  \item \emph{taste}\index{taste} --- the tongue tastes sweet, salty,
        sour.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[One apple, five senses]\label{ex:g1:five-senses:apple}
Eating an apple uses every sense: you \emph{see} its red skin, you
\emph{feel} it smooth and round in your hand, you \emph{hear} the
crunch, you \emph{smell} its perfume, you \emph{taste} its sweet
juice. Five senses, one apple.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Try it: the sound walk]\label{ex:g1:five-senses:sounds}
Sit still, close your eyes, and count the sounds around you for a
little while: a car, a voice, the wind, your own breathing. With eyes
closed, the ears notice sounds that the busy eyes made you miss. Most
people find $4$ or $5$ sounds they had not noticed before.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Senses that protect us]\label{rem:g1:five-senses:danger}
The senses are also guards. The nose smells smoke before the eyes see
a fire; the skin pulls the hand back from a hot pan; a bitter taste
warns ``do not swallow this''. Never taste or smell something unknown
on purpose: ask an adult first. The guards work best when we treat
them carefully.
\end{remark}

\section{Observing carefully}

Looking is not yet observing. Everyone has \emph{seen} a ladybug;
who can say how many legs it has?

\begin{definition}[Observation]\label{def:g1:five-senses:observation}
To \emph{observe}\index{observe} is to use the senses carefully, on
purpose, to answer a question --- and then to say or draw exactly
what was noticed, not what was expected. An
\emph{observation}\index{observation} is what careful watching
found out.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[How to observe]\label{met:g1:five-senses:how}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Choose a question: \emph{how many legs does the ladybug
        have?}
  \item Watch (or listen, or touch) slowly, without hurrying;
  \item say what you notice with precise words: not ``it is small'',
        but ``it is smaller than my fingernail'';
  \item draw or tell it, so someone else can check.
\end{enumerate}
A good \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:observation}{observation} can be checked by a friend: if you both look, you
should both find $6$ legs.
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Precise words]\label{ex:g1:five-senses:words}
Compare ``the stone is nice'' with ``the stone is gray, smooth,
round, and colder than my hand''. The second sentence is an
\omterm{def:g1:five-senses:observation}{observation}: another child can pick that stone out of a whole
basket.
\end{example}

\section{When the senses are fooled}

The senses are wonderful, but they can be tricked. That is exactly
why, in the chapters to come, we will learn to \emph{measure}.

\begin{example}[Two lines]\label{ex:g1:five-senses:lines}
In the picture below, which line is longer? Almost everyone answers
``the top one''. Now check with a piece of string: the two lines are
exactly as long as each other. The eyes were fooled by the arrow
tips.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Try it: three bowls]\label{ex:g1:five-senses:bowls}
Ask an adult to prepare three bowls of water: one cool, one lukewarm,
one comfortably warm. Keep one hand in the cool bowl and the other in
the warm bowl while you slowly count to $20$. Then put both hands
together in the middle bowl: the same water feels warm to one hand
and cool to the other! Touch alone cannot be trusted about hot and
cold --- the next chapter takes up exactly this problem.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Helpers for the senses]\label{rem:g1:five-senses:tools}
People build tools that help the senses: glasses and magnifying
glasses help the eyes, a doctor's stethoscope helps the ears. Later
in this book, tools called \emph{instruments} will do even better:
they will turn looking and touching into counting and measuring, and
counting is much harder to fool.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:1}
Name the five senses, and for each one the part of the body that
does the job.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:2}
Which sense tells you: (a) that the radio is on? (b) that the soup
is too salty? (c) that your pullover is scratchy? (d) that the
lights went out?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:3}
A cake is baking in the oven, door closed, in another room. Which
sense finds it first? Which senses will you use when you finally
get a slice?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:4}
Do the sound walk of \cref{ex:g1:five-senses:sounds}: eyes closed,
count the sounds around you. How many did you hear? Which one
surprised you?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:5}
Give an \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:observation}{observation} of your shoe using three precise words, as in
\cref{ex:g1:five-senses:words}. ``Nice'' and ``pretty'' are not
allowed!
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:6}
Ana says: ``The cat is soft.'' Bob says: ``The cat is the best cat.''
Which sentence is an \omterm{def:g1:five-senses:observation}{observation}, and which sense made it? What is
the other sentence?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:7}
Which sense warns you of each danger: (a) smoke somewhere in the
house; (b) a pan too hot to hold; (c) a car coming from behind you?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:8}
A friend closes their eyes. You put a spoon, a sponge and a pebble in
front of them, one at a time. Can they name all three just by touch?
Which was easiest to recognize?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g1:five-senses:9}
Look at the two lines of \cref{ex:g1:five-senses:lines}. Explain to a
friend how to find out for sure which line is longer, without
trusting the eyes. Can your method also compare a line with a curvy
piece of string?
\end{exercise}
