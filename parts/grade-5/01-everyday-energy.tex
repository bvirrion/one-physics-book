\chapter{Energy in Everyday Life}\label{ch:g5:everyday-energy}

A wind-up toy scurries across the floor, slows, and stops --- until
you \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} it again. A flashlight dims as its batteries tire. You
yourself run out of steam before lunch. Everything that moves,
shines, \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sounds} or warms seems to be spending something. Physics has
a name for that something, and it may be the most important word in
this whole book: \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}.

\section{The something that gets things done}

\begin{definition}[Energy]\label{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}
\emph{Energy}\index{energy} is what it takes to make things happen:
to set things moving, to lift them, to \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} them up, to warm them,
to make them \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound}. Nothing goes, glows, or grows without energy
being spent on it --- and whatever is doing the spending must first
have energy to spend.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Energy stores]\label{def:g5:everyday-energy:store}
An \emph{energy store}\index{energy store} is anything holding
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} ready for use. The great everyday stores: \emph{food} (your
body's fuel), \emph{fuels} like wood and petrol, charged
\emph{batteries}, a wound-up \emph{spring}, water held \emph{high}
behind a dam --- and the hot \emph{Sun}, pouring \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} down on
everything, all day, for free.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Spot the store]\label{ex:g5:everyday-energy:spot}
Every going thing has a store behind it. The scurrying toy: its
wound spring. The flashlight: its batteries. The car: the fuel in
its tank. You: your breakfast. The sailboat: the moving \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} of the
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}. When the store runs empty --- spring unwound, batteries flat,
tank dry, lunchtime near --- the going stops.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[No feeding, no going]\label{rem:g5:everyday-energy:nofree}
For centuries, inventors chased machines that would run forever on
nothing --- wheels that turn themselves, clocks that never need
winding. Every single one failed. Their failure is one of physics'
deepest lessons: \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} is never conjured from thin \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}. Whatever
runs is being fed --- find the store, and you understand the
machine.
\end{remark}

\section{Energy changes form}

\begin{example}[Forms of energy]\label{ex:g5:everyday-energy:forms}
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{Energy} shows itself in different forms: \emph{motion} \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} in the
rolling ball and the blowing \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}; \emph{\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}} \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} pouring from
lamp and Sun; \emph{heat} \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} in everything warm;
\emph{electrical} \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} traveling through wires; \emph{stored}
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} waiting quietly in food, fuel, springs and lifted things.
Same something, many forms.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[The energy chain]\label{prop:g5:everyday-energy:chain}
Whenever something happens, \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} is drawn from a store and passed
along, usually changing form on the way. It is never created
from nothing --- and never truly destroyed either: what a machine
``uses up'' has only been passed on, most often ending, sooner or
later, as gentle warmth spread into the surroundings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{method}[Reading an energy chain]\label{met:g5:everyday-energy:read}
To understand any device, trace its chain:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item find the \emph{store} the \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} comes from;
  \item find the \emph{converter} --- the part where the \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}
        changes form;
  \item name the forms coming out --- the useful one, and the
        warmth that leaks alongside.
\end{enumerate}
The flashlight, traced: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} (store) $\to$ \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} (converter)
$\to$ \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} (useful) $+$ a little warmth (the leak --- feel the
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}).
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Chains everywhere]\label{ex:g5:everyday-energy:chains}
Your morning ride, traced: breakfast (store) $\to$ your muscles
(converter) $\to$ motion of the bicycle $+$ warmth (you feel it ---
you glow). The \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} farm: moving \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} $\to$ turning blades $\to$
electrical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} in the wires $\to$ \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} in your lamp tonight.
The dam: high water $\to$ falling water spins the turbines $\to$
electrical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}. Long chains are just short ones linked: form
after form after form.
\end{example}

\section{The Sun at the head of the table}

\begin{example}[Almost every chain starts at the Sun]\label{ex:g5:everyday-energy:sun}
Follow most chains backward and you arrive at the Sun. Breakfast?
Plants grew it by catching sunlight; your cereal is tinned
sunshine. \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{Wind}? The Sun warms the lands unevenly, and the stirred
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} is \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} --- so even sailboats run on sunlight. Wood? Sunlight
stored by trees. The dam's high water? The Sun lifted it: it
evaporated the sea, the vapor rained on the mountains. Solar
panels just skip the middlemen.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[A word we will sharpen]\label{rem:g5:everyday-energy:promise}
For now, \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} is a story well told: stores, forms, chains. Can
it be \emph{measured}, like length in \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} and \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass} in \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:units}{grams}? It
can --- \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} has its own \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:measure}{unit} and its own exact bookkeeping, and
they arrive later in this book, once you have met speed and \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force}
properly. The chains you trace this year are the map; the numbers
will come to live on it.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:1}
What is \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}, in the words of this chapter? Name four things that
cannot happen without it.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:2}
Find the store: a scurrying wind-up mouse; \ a cyclist; \ a
campfire; \ a sailboat; \ a flashlight.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:3}
Name five forms of \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}, with one example of
each from your own day.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:4}
Trace the \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} chain of a toaster, as in
\cref{met:g5:everyday-energy:read}: store, converter, forms out.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:5}
Trace the chain from a dam's lake to your reading lamp tonight.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:6}
A phone ``dies''. What has actually run out? Where did that \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}
go, according to the chain proposition?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:7}
Why does a laptop grow warm underneath while you use it? Which link
of the chain is showing itself?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:8}
Show that a sailboat runs on sunlight, by tracing its chain
backward step by step.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:9}
An inventor announces a lamp that ``needs no \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, no plug, no
fuel --- it simply shines forever''. Which lesson of
\cref{rem:g5:everyday-energy:nofree} does the announcement defy?
What question should you ask the inventor first?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:10}
A rubber ball dropped from shoulder height bounces lower and lower
and finally lies still. ``Its \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} is destroyed,'' says Leo. Use
\cref{prop:g5:everyday-energy:chain} to defend a better ending to
the story --- into what final form did the bouncing \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}
slip?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:everyday-energy:11}
Some houses heat water in black rooftop panels; some cook with
sunlight and curved mirrors; solar cells make electricity from
daylight. Explain the phrase ``solar panels just skip the
middlemen'' from \cref{ex:g5:everyday-energy:sun}: which middlemen
stand between the Sun and a wood fire's warmth?
\end{exercise}
