\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g5:everyday-energy} --- Energy in Everyday Life}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:1}
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{Energy} is what it takes to make things happen. Without it, nothing
can move, be lifted, shine, warm up, or \omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} --- any four.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:2}
The wound spring; the cyclist's food; the burning wood; the moving
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} of the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}; the batteries.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:3}
Motion (the rolling ball), \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} (the lamp), heat (the warm bath),
electrical (the wires to the toaster), stored (breakfast, a
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, a wound spring).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:4}
Electrical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} from the wall (fed, farther back, by a power
station's store) $\to$ the glowing wires inside the toaster
(converter) $\to$ heat toward the bread --- with a little \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light},
the orange glow, alongside.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:5}
High water (store) $\to$ falling water spins the turbine
(converter) $\to$ electrical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} in the wires $\to$ the lamp
(second converter) $\to$ \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}, plus a little warmth.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:6}
Its battery-store has run empty. The \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} was not destroyed: it
was spent along the day's chains --- lighting the screen, making
\omterm{def:g3:sound-around-us:sound}{sound} --- and has ended spread out as gentle warmth in the phone
and the room.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:7}
The leak: every converter passes some \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} into warmth alongside
its useful work. The laptop's chips convert electrical \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}, and
the warmth underneath is the chain's unavoidable side-channel.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:8}
The sails are pushed by \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}; \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} is \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} stirred into motion
because the Sun warms lands and seas unevenly; so the boat's motion
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} arrives, two links back, from sunlight.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:9}
That \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} is never conjured from thin \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} --- whatever runs is
being fed. First question: ``What is the store, and where does the
lamp draw from it?'' If the inventor has no answer, the lamp
belongs with the forever-wheels: in the drawer of famous failures.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:10}
The \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} was passed on, not destroyed: each bounce spent some of
the ball's motion \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} squashing the ball and rubbing the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} and
floor, and all those payments end as a whisper of warmth in ball,
floor and \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}. Too spread out to gather, but not gone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:everyday-energy:11}
Between Sun and wood fire stand the middlemen: trees, which spent
years catching sunlight and storing it as wood, and the fire that
finally unpacks the store. The rooftop panel takes the sunlight's
\omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} directly --- no tree, no years, no fire.
\end{solution}
