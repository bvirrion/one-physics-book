\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g5:air-pressure-weather} --- Air Pressure and Weather}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:1}
The same ball is weighed firm and then deflated: only \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} left it,
and the scale reads less --- the difference is the escaped \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}'s
\omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}. \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{Air} is stuff with \omterm{def:g3:measuring-mass:mass}{mass}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:2}
The push of the squeezed \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} at the bottom of the \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere} on
every surface it touches. It comes from the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} of the deep
ocean of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} above us, and it pushes from every direction --- down,
sideways, and up.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:3}
Because the pushes come balanced: the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} on the other side of your
palm --- and inside your body --- pushes back exactly as hard.
Only an \emph{unbalanced} push, like on the card or the crumpling
bottle, shows its might.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:4}
The water's \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight} pushes the card down; the \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere} pushes the
card up. The \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere} wins --- the card stays and the water is
held in.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:5}
You remove the \emph{\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air}} from inside the straw. The \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere},
pressing down on the juice in the glass, then pushes juice up the
cleared straw: the sky does the lifting.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:6}
Pressing the cup flat squeezed out the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} behind it. With nothing
pushing back from underneath, the \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere}'s push pins the cup to
the tiles.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:7}
It weakens --- less ocean of \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} remains above to press down.
Signs: ears popping on a fast climb, breathlessness in the thin
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} (and tea that boils too cool).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:8}
The push of the \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere} --- \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{air pressure}. A fast fall warns of a
storm on the way: move the picnic indoors.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:9}
Sealed on the peak, the bottle kept thin mountain \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} inside; in
the valley the outside push grew stronger than the trapped push
inside, and the bottle folded. Carried the other way, a
valley-sealed bottle keeps \emph{strong} \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} inside while the
outside push weakens with height: the bottle swells taut --- and a
sealed bag of crisps does the same on every mountain drive.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:10}
\omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{Air} pours steadily out of the heavy high-pressure regions toward
the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} low-pressure ones, and that pouring is the \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind}. So the
old definition completes itself: \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} is \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:air}{air} on the move --- moved
by differences in \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{air pressure}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:air-pressure-weather:11}
With height, the \omterm{def:g5:air-pressure-weather:pressure}{atmosphere}'s push on the water weakens, so
boiling comes easier: the \omterm{def:g4:changes-of-state:boiling-point}{boiling point} slides well below
\qty{100}{\celsius}. And by the plateau law, boiling water can
never climb past its \omterm{def:g4:changes-of-state:boiling-point}{boiling point} --- extra flame only makes
steam. So peak-top water boils cool \emph{and is stuck there}: tea
weak, pasta hard, physics satisfied.
\end{solution}
