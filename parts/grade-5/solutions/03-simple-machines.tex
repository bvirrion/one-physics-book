\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g5:simple-machines} --- Simple Machines: Levers, Pulleys, Inclined Planes}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:1}
The \omterm{def:g3:levers-and-scales:lever}{lever} (crowbar, seesaw, scissors), the \omterm{def:g5:simple-machines:pulley}{pulley} (flagpole, well,
crane), the \omterm{def:g5:simple-machines:ramp}{inclined plane} (wheelchair ramp, mountain road, loading
ramp).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:2}
The fixed \omterm{def:g5:simple-machines:pulley}{pulley} changes only the direction of the pull (haul down,
load goes up). The movable \omterm{def:g5:simple-machines:pulley}{pulley} changes its size: the load hangs
from two strands, so the hand holds only half the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:3}
Pulling down recruits your own \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}: you can lean or even hang on
the rope, letting the Earth's pull help your muscles. Lifting
upward, your arms fight alone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:4}
Half the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}. To raise the load \qty{3}{m}, both strands must
shorten by \qty{3}{m}: your hand hauls $2 \times 3 = \qty{6}{m}$ of
rope.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:5}
The steep ramp stretches it more. The gentle ramp's smaller effort
is paid for in distance: the same height is gained along a much
longer road --- the golden rule's trade.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:6}
Straight up the slope, the pull needed would defeat any engine or
mule team; the zigzags wrap a long, gentle \omterm{def:g5:simple-machines:ramp}{inclined plane} across
the mountainside --- small effort, long road. The whole road is one
stretched-out ramp.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:7}
No machine gives something for nothing: \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} saved is repaid in
distance, exactly. A tenth of the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} costs ten times the road
(or rope).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:8}
Its thread is a long gentle slope wrapped around the rod. The many
easy turns of the screwdriver are the long road; the tiny sinking
of the screw each turn is the small height gained.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:9}
A quarter of the \omterm{def:g4:weight-and-mass:weight}{weight}. Each of the four strands must shorten by
\qty{2}{m}: $4 \times 2 = \qty{8}{m}$ of rope pass through the
worker's hands.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:10}
The new ramp climbs the same \qty{1}{m} in half the road, so it is
twice as steep, and the push roughly doubles. The golden rule
collected instantly: road halved, \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force} doubled --- the crate's
climb costs the same either way, and the movers merely traded many
comfortable coins for a few heavy ones.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:simple-machines:11}
No arrangement can honor it. Raising the load one \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metre} costs a
fixed helping of \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy}; paying with half the \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{force}, the seller
must haul \emph{two} \omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metres} of rope --- \omterm{def:g2:pushes-and-pulls:force}{half-force} along just one
\omterm{def:g2:measuring-length:units}{metre} hands over only half the bill. The advertisement promises a
paid lift at half price: exactly the free lunch that the golden
rule, and the failed forever-machines, forbid.
\end{solution}
