\chapter{Mirrors and Reflection}\label{ch:g5:mirrors-reflection}

\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{Light}, we proved, never bends around corners --- and yet every
morning a copy of you looks out of the bathroom wall, and last year
we promised a trick to see around corners after all. The trick is
not to bend the road but to \emph{fold} it. Everything folds at one
remarkable surface: the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}.

\section{The bounce}

\begin{definition}[Reflection]\label{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}
When \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} strikes a surface and springs back instead of passing
through or dying there, we call it
\emph{reflection}\index{reflection}: the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} is reflected. A
\emph{mirror}\index{mirror} is a surface so smooth that it reflects
\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} in a perfectly orderly way --- polished metal behind glass,
still water, a gleaming spoon.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The bounce rule]\label{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:bounce}
A ray of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} bounces off a \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} the way a perfect ball bounces
off a wall: it leaves exactly as steeply as it arrived, on the
other side. Straight in --- straight back out. Slanting in ---
slanting out at the matching slant. The two angles, arriving and
leaving, are always equal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Aiming a bounce]\label{ex:g5:mirrors-reflection:aiming}
Because the bounce is so orderly, it can be \emph{aimed}: with a
small \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} in the sunshine you can throw a spot of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} onto any
wall you choose --- tilt the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}, the spot obeys. Sailors and
hikers in trouble use exactly this: a flashed \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} aimed at a
far-off ship or helicopter is a signal visible for many
kilometres, powered entirely by the Sun.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Why walls are not mirrors]\label{ex:g5:mirrors-reflection:walls}
A white wall reflects plenty of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} --- the room would be gloomy
otherwise --- so why does it show no \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image}? Up close, its surface
is a landscape of tiny bumps: each arriving ray obeys the bounce
rule on its own little slope, and the orderly army of rays is
scattered to all directions. A \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}'s polish keeps the slopes
flat, the army stays in step, and the picture survives the bounce.
Order, not shininess alone, is what makes an \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image}.
\end{example}

\section{The person behind the glass}

\begin{proposition}[The image rule]\label{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}
A flat \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} shows every object as an \emph{image}\index{image}
standing \emph{behind} the glass, exactly as far behind as the
object stands in front, matching it in size, point for point. Step
back, and your \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} steps back too; the mirror-world is a
faithful, folded copy of ours.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Why the image lives behind]\label{ex:g5:mirrors-reflection:why}
Your eye is a simple soul: it assumes every ray traveled straight.
The bounced ray truly comes from the candle by way of the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}
--- but the eye prolongs it \emph{backward through the glass}, to
where the ray seems to have started. All the bounced rays agree on
that phantom starting point: one candle's worth of rays, one crisp
phantom candle. Nothing stands behind the wall, of course --- the
\omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} is \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}'s most convincing illusion.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The swapped world]\label{ex:g5:mirrors-reflection:swap}
Hold a page of writing to the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}: the letters come back
reversed, left and right exchanged. That is why the word
AMBULANCE is painted mirror-reversed on the front of ambulances:
drivers ahead, glancing into their rear-view \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors}, see it swing
back to plain reading --- and pull aside. Two \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors} can undo the
swap: a double bounce flips the flip.
\end{example}

\section{Folding the road on purpose}

\begin{method}[Building a periscope]\label{met:g5:mirrors-reflection:periscope}
The promised corner-looker, delivered:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item take a tall, narrow box; cut a window near the top of one
        face and another near the bottom of the opposite face;
  \item fix a small \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} inside behind each window, each tilted
        at half a right angle, the two tilts parallel to each
        other;
  \item aim the top window over the wall (or around the corner),
        and look into the bottom window.
\end{enumerate}
The \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}'s road folds twice: down the box between the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors},
then out into your eye. Submarine crews watch the waves through
exactly this --- built long and watertight.
\end{method}

\begin{remark}[Fun-house spoons]\label{rem:g5:mirrors-reflection:curved}
Look into the bowl of a polished spoon: you hang upside down.
Turn it over: you are right side up again, but stretched. Curved
\omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors} still obey the bounce rule at every point --- but their
slopes turn with the curve, so the orderly bounces gather or
spread, and \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{images} shrink, grow, or somersault. Curved \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors}
focus \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} too: a curved dish of sunlight can cook a pot ---
remember the solar cookers of the \omterm{def:g5:everyday-energy:energy}{energy} chapter. Their precise
laws belong to a later year; the spoon is their honest preview.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:1}
State the bounce rule. What does a ray arriving straight-on do?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:2}
A white wall reflects lots of \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}. Why does it show no \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image},
while the polished \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} does?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:3}
You stand \qty{2}{m} in front of a flat \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror}. Where is your
\omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image}, and how far is it from you?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:4}
You walk toward your \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} \omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} at a slow step. What does the
\omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} do, and how fast does the gap between you close?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:5}
Why is AMBULANCE painted backward on the front of ambulances? For
whom does it read correctly, and through what?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:6}
In the periscope, how many times does the \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}'s road fold, and
what does each fold? At what tilt do the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors} stand?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:7}
How can a hiker with a pocket \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} signal a far-away helicopter?
Which two chapter ideas are at work --- name the rule that aims
the flash and the store that powers it.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:8}
Still water mirrors the mountains; \omterm{def:g2:air-around-us:wind}{wind} ruffles the lake and the
picture shatters into glitter. Explain both states with
\cref{ex:g5:mirrors-reflection:walls}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:9}
The eye ``prolongs rays backward''. Use this to explain why your
\omterm{prop:g5:mirrors-reflection:image}{image} stands \emph{behind} the wall the \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} hangs on --- in a
place where certainly nobody stands. What do all the bounced rays
from your nose agree about?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:10}
A shop wants a customer at the door to see around a blind corner
into the aisle. One flat \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirror} must be mounted where the two
corridors meet. How should it be tilted, and why does the same
mounting let the aisle see the door? (\omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{Light}'s roads work both
ways.)
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:mirrors-reflection:11}
\omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{Mirror} writing comes back reversed; a double bounce ``flips the
flip''. Explain why the periscope of
\cref{met:g5:mirrors-reflection:periscope}, with its two \omterm{def:g5:mirrors-reflection:reflection}{mirrors},
shows the scene the right way around and not mirror-reversed.
\end{exercise}
