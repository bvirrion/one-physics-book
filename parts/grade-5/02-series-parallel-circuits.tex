\chapter{Series and Parallel Circuits}\label{ch:g5:series-parallel-circuits}

When one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} died, the whole party string went dark --- the curse
of the single loop. Yet when a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} burns out in your kitchen, the
rest of the house shines on. Somebody wired your house a cleverer
way. Today we learn it: the way of the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches}.

\section{Two ways to wire}

You know the first way: \emph{\omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series}}, all players threaded on
one loop, beads on one necklace. Here is the second.

\begin{definition}[In parallel]\label{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}
Players are \emph{in parallel}\index{parallel circuit} when the
circuit splits into separate \emph{branches}\index{branch} --- one
player per branch --- that fork apart and rejoin. The points where
roads fork or rejoin are called
\emph{junctions}\index{junction}. Each branch is a complete road
from one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{terminal} to the other.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[Building it]\label{met:g5:series-parallel-circuits:build}
With one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, two \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} and wires:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item wire the first \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} to the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, alone, as a plain loop
        --- \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} one;
  \item now wire the second \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} \emph{directly across} the first:
        one wire from each side of \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 1 to the matching side of
        \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 2 --- \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} two;
  \item trace each \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} with your finger: from $+$, every road
        should pass exactly one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} before reaching $-$.
\end{enumerate}
Both \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light} --- and both shine as brightly as a single \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}
would alone.
\end{method}

\section{The rules of the branches}

\begin{proposition}[Rules of the parallel circuit]\label{prop:g5:series-parallel-circuits:rules}
With each player on its own \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{independence}: a gap in one \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} stops only that
        \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} --- unscrew one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, and the other burns on;
  \item \emph{full brightness}: each \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} shines as if it were
        alone --- no sharing of the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s push between
        \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches};
  \item \emph{command posts}: a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} inside a \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} commands
        only that \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}; a switch on the common road, before the
        fork, commands everyone. (The price: the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} feeds
        every \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} at once, and so it drains faster.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[The unscrewing test, replayed]\label{ex:g5:series-parallel-circuits:test}
Do to the parallel pair exactly what once killed the series pair:
unscrew \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 2. \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{Bulb} 1 does not even flicker --- its own \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch},
its own complete road, is untouched. Screw it back, unscrew \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 1
instead: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 2 burns calmly on. The curse of the single loop is
lifted: \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in parallel}, every player minds its own \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Your house is a parallel circuit]\label{ex:g5:series-parallel-circuits:house}
Every lamp, socket and appliance in your home rides its own \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}
of one great \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{parallel circuit}. That is why the kitchen \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} can burn
out while the living room reads on undisturbed; why each room's
\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} commands only its own \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}; and why one main \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} --- in
the fuse box by the meter --- can cut the whole house at once for
repairs. Modern party \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} joined the club: their \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} are wired
so one dead \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} no longer darkens the string.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Series and parallel, side by side]\label{rem:g5:series-parallel-circuits:compare}
One gap: series --- all dark; parallel --- one \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} dark. More
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs}: series --- all dimmer; parallel --- all at full brightness,
but the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} drains faster. One \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} for everything: series
--- anywhere; parallel --- only before the fork. Neither way is
``better'': the flashlight is happily series (one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}, one
\omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}), the house is necessarily parallel. An electrician's first
question about any circuit is simply: \emph{where does the road
fork?}
\end{remark}

\section{Reading circuits}

\begin{method}[Following the roads]\label{met:g5:series-parallel-circuits:follow}
To predict any \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}'s fate in a circuit drawing:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item put your finger on the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s $+$ and walk every
        possible road to $-$, listing the players each road
        passes;
  \item a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} if at least one unbroken road passes through
        it;
  \item players sharing every road are \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series} with each other;
        players on different forks are \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in parallel}.
\end{enumerate}
Walk the roads before you predict --- guessing from the drawing's
shape alone is how circuits fool people.
\end{method}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:1}
What is a \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch}? A \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{junction}? In a \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{parallel circuit} with two \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs},
how many \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches} carry a \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:2}
State the three rules of the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{parallel circuit}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:3}
Two \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in parallel}: \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 1 is unscrewed. What does \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 2 do?
And in the series pair --- what did it do there, and why the
difference?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:4}
Two identical \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs}: pair A is \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series}, pair B \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in parallel}, same
batteries. Which pair shines brighter per \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}? Which drains its
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery} faster?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:5}
In the figure with the two \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switches}, what does each \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} do when:
both \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switches} closed? \ main open? \ main closed, \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}
open?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:6}
Why is a house wired \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in parallel} and not \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series}? Give two
everyday comforts that depend on it.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:7}
Where in a house circuit does a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} command everything at once?
What is that \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch}'s everyday name and use?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:8}
A bedside lamp and its wall socket: are they \omterm{def:g4:series-circuits:series}{in series} or \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in
parallel} with the ceiling \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{light}? How do you know from daily life?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:9}
Draw (with symbols) a circuit for a hallway: one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb},
and this comfort --- the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} must go on and off from a \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} at
\emph{either} end of the hallway\dots\ Try honestly; if your
designs stubbornly fail, explain what is hard. (True two-way
switching needs a cleverer \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{switch} than ours --- the attempt teaches
more than the answer.)
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:10}
A string of modern party \omterm{def:g1:light-and-shadows:source}{lights} keeps shining when one \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} dies
--- but the box warns: ``Do not run the string with more than three
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulbs} removed.'' Using the rules of the \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branches}, explain both the
comfort and, harder, a sensible reason for the warning. (Think of
what each remaining \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{branch} receives from the \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}, and of the
\omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:battery}{battery}'s own effort.)
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:series-parallel-circuits:11}
In the \omterm{ex:g3:first-electric-circuit:switch}{two-switch} figure, an electrician wants to add one more \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb}

series} or \omterm{def:g5:series-parallel-circuits:parallel}{in parallel} with \omterm{def:g3:first-electric-circuit:bulb}{bulb} 2?
\end{exercise}
