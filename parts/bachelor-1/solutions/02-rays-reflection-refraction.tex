\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:4}
Eyes at \qty{1.60}{m}. The ray from the feet reaches the eye after
reflecting at half the eye height, \qty{0.80}{m}; the ray from the top
of the head reflects midway between top and eyes, at \qty{1.65}{m}.
Mirror from \qty{0.80}{m} to \qty{1.65}{m}: height \qty{0.85}{m}, half
the body height --- independent of the distance (the midpoint rule
holds at any distance).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:5}
Small angles: the apparent position is $h' = h/n$ when looking from the
rarer medium, $h' = nh$ from the denser one. Coin: $1.2/1.33 =
\qty{0.90}{m}$. Fisherman: $1.33 \times 1.8 = \qty{2.4}{m}$. Justification:
for a surface point at horizontal distance $x$, $\tan i_2 \approx i_2 =
x/h$ and $\tan i_1 \approx i_1 = x/h'$ with $i_1 = n i_2$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:6}
$\sin\frac{A + D_{\min}}{2} = 1.52\sin 30^\circ = 0.760$, so
$\frac{A + D_{\min}}{2} = 49.5^\circ$, $D_{\min} = 38.9^\circ$.
Emergence needs $r' \leq i_c$; also $\sin r = \sin i/n \leq 1/n$ gives
$r \leq i_c$; so $A = r + r' \leq 2i_c$. Here $i_c = \arcsin(1/1.52) =
41.1^\circ$, $2i_c = 82.3^\circ > 60^\circ$: fine.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:7}
$\mathrm{NA} = \sqrt{1.50^2 - 1.48^2} = \sqrt{0.0596} = 0.244$,
$\theta_a = 14.1^\circ$. $\Delta t/L = (1.50/c)(1.50/1.48 - 1) =

$B_{\max} \approx 1/(2 \times \qty{68}{ns}) = \qty{7.4}{Mbit/s}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:9}
Entry: $\sin r = \sin 45^\circ/1.5 = 0.471$, $r = 28.1^\circ$. The two
faces are parallel, so the incidence on the second face is $r$ and
$\sin i' = n\sin r = \sin i$: $i' = i$, the emerging ray is parallel to
the incident one. Along the inside path of length $e/\cos r$, the
perpendicular offset is $(e/\cos r)\sin(i - r)$:
$d = 2.0 \times \sin 16.9^\circ/\cos 28.1^\circ = \qty{0.66}{cm}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:10}
Two refractions ($2(i - r)$) and two reflections ($2(\pi - 2r)$):
$D = 2\pi + 2i - 6r$. $\dd D/\dd i = 2 - 6\cos i/(n\cos r) = 0$ gives
$3\cos i = n\cos r$, $9\cos^2 i = n^2 - 1 + \cos^2 i$, $\cos^2 i =
(n^2 - 1)/8$. $n = 1.333$: $\cos^2 i = 0.0971$, $i = 71.8^\circ$,
$\sin r = 0.950/1.333$, $r = 45.5^\circ$, $D = 360^\circ + 143.7^\circ -
272.8^\circ = 230.9^\circ$: the bow is at $230.9^\circ - 180^\circ =
51^\circ$ from the anti-solar point, outside the primary ($42^\circ$).
Red ($n = 1.331$) at $50.4^\circ$, violet ($1.343$) at $53.6^\circ$:
the colors are reversed, violet outside.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:11}
$\sin i_c = 1.00026/1.00029 = 0.99997$, $i_c = 89.56^\circ$: rays
within $0.44^\circ = \qty{7.7}{mrad}$ of the road surface are totally
reflected (generally, grazing angle $\approx \sqrt{2\Delta n/n}$). From
\qty{1.2}{m} up, the line of sight grazes at this angle beyond
$1.2/0.0077 \approx \qty{160}{m}$: the road there reflects the sky ---
the ``puddle''.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:12}
$t(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}}{v_1} + \dfrac{\sqrt{b^2 + (d - x)^2}}{v_2}$;
$t'(x) = \dfrac{x}{v_1\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}} - \dfrac{d - x}{v_2\sqrt{b^2 + (d - x)^2}}
= \dfrac{\sin i_1}{v_1} - \dfrac{\sin i_2}{v_2}$, zero exactly when
$\sin i_1/v_1 = \sin i_2/v_2$, i.e.\ $n_1\sin i_1 = n_2\sin i_2$ with
$n = c/v$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:1}
\textbf{1.} $n = c/v$; $v_1 = 3.00\times10^8/1.480 = \qty{2.03e8}{m/s}$.

\textbf{2.} $t = Ln_1/c = 5.0\times10^4 \times 1.48/3.00\times10^8 =
\qty{247}{\micro s}$.

\textbf{3.} $\nu = c/\lambda_0 = \qty{1.94e14}{Hz}$, unchanged in glass;
$\lambda = \lambda_0/n_1 = \qty{1047}{nm}$.

\textbf{4.} A bare \omterm{def:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:fiber}{core} guides only while its surface is perfectly
clean and untouched: dust, water or a grip locally replaces air by a
higher index and light escapes, and scratches scatter it. The \omterm{def:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:fiber}{cladding}
is a sealed, controlled interface with an index step chosen on purpose.

\textbf{5.} $\sin i_c = 1.465/1.480 = 0.9899$, $i_c = 81.8^\circ$.

\textbf{6.} $90^\circ - 81.8^\circ = 8.2^\circ$.

\textbf{7.} $\sin\theta = n_1\sin\theta'$; guided iff $\cos\theta' \geq
n_2/n_1$, i.e.\ $\sin\theta' \leq \sqrt{1 - n_2^2/n_1^2}$, i.e.\
$\sin\theta \leq \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} = \sqrt{2.1904 - 2.1462} = 0.210$:
$\theta_a = 12.1^\circ$.

\textbf{8.} $\mathrm{NA} = 0.210$. A larger step widens the cone, so
more of a diverging source's light (an LED) is captured.

\textbf{9.} The ray is tangent to the circle of radius $R_b$ (inner
edge) and meets the circle of radius $R_b + 2a$ (outer edge); in the
right triangle center--tangent point--impact point, the angle at the
impact point between the ray and the radius (the normal) satisfies
$\sin i = R_b/(R_b + 2a)$. Guided iff $\sin i \geq n_2/n_1$:
$R_b \geq 2a\,\dfrac{n_2/n_1}{1 - n_2/n_1} = \qty{50}{\micro m} \times 97.6
= \qty{4.9}{mm}$. Tighter bends leak.

\textbf{10.} $t_0 = Ln_1/c$; steepest ray path $L/\cos\theta'_{\max} =
Ln_1/n_2$, so $t_1 = Ln_1^2/(n_2 c)$.

\textbf{11.} $\Delta t/L = (n_1/c)(n_1/n_2 - 1) = 4.93\times10^{-9} \times
0.01024 = \qty{50.5}{ns/km}$; over \qty{2}{km}: \qty{101}{ns}.

\textbf{12.} $B_{\max} \approx 1/(2\Delta t) = 1/\qty{202}{ns} \approx
\qty{5}{Mbit/s}$.

\textbf{13.} $B_{\max} L = 1/(2\,\Delta t/L) = 1/(2 \times
\qty{50.5}{ns/km}) \approx \qty{10}{Mbit.s^{-1}.km}$.

\textbf{14.} Rays far from the axis travel longer paths but in lower
index, hence faster; with a well-chosen profile the two effects cancel
and all rays arrive together.

\textbf{15.} $\qty{9}{\micro m} \approx 6\lambda_0$: the \omterm{def:b1:rays-reflection-refraction:fiber}{core} is not
large compared with the wavelength, so diffraction dominates and rays
have no meaning. The wave treatment shows a single guided pattern: no
intermodal spread at all; only chromatic dispersion and attenuation
remain.

\textbf{16.} \qty{100}{km} $\times$ \qty{0.2}{dB/km} $= \qty{20}{dB}$:
$P/P_0 = 10^{-2}$, one percent.

\textbf{17.} $10\log(P_0/P_{\min}) = \qty{30}{dB}$, so $L_{\max} =
30/0.20 = \qty{150}{km}$.

\textbf{18.} \qty{100}{km} costs \qty{20}{dB}: margin \qty{10}{dB}, a
factor $10$ in power.

\textbf{19.} LED: $\Delta t_c = 17 \times 40 \times 100 = \qty{68000}{ps}
= \qty{68}{ns}$, $B \approx 1/\qty{136}{ns} = \qty{7}{Mbit/s}$. Laser:
$\Delta t_c = \qty{170}{ps}$, $B \approx 1/\qty{340}{ps} =
\qty{2.9}{Gbit/s}$.

\textbf{20.} $30/2.0 = \qty{15}{km}$, ten times less. Silica is most
transparent near \qty{1550}{nm} (its attenuation minimum), and optical
amplifiers exist there.

\textbf{21.} $30/30 = \qty{1}{km}$ between amplifiers, against
\qty{150}{km}: the fiber wins by a factor $150$.

\textbf{22.} Intermodal: \qty{101}{ns} ($\to$ \qty{5}{Mbit/s});
chromatic (laser): $17 \times 0.1 \times 2 = \qty{3.4}{ps}$;
attenuation \qty{0.4}{dB}, negligible. The intermodal spread limits:
\qty{5}{Mbit/s}.

\textbf{23.} No intermodal spread; chromatic \qty{170}{ps} $\to$
\qty{2.9}{Gbit/s}; attenuation \qty{20}{dB}, within budget. Chromatic
dispersion limits, at about \qty{3}{Gbit/s}.

\textbf{24.} $80 \times 2.9 \approx \qty{230}{Gbit/s}$.

\textbf{25.} Step index: \qty{10}{Mbit.s^{-1}.km}; single-mode channel:
$2.9 \times 100 = \qty{290}{Gbit.s^{-1}.km}$ --- a factor of about
$3\times10^4$. One fact: a single guided path, hence no geometric
(intermodal) spread of arrival times.
\end{solution}
