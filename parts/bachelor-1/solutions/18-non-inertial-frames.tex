\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:1}
$\tan\alpha = a/g = 0.255$, $\alpha = 14^\circ$ (the bob trails backward);
$T = m\sqrt{g^2 + a^2} = 1.03\,mg$. Braking: same angle and tension, bob

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:2}
$\Omega = 2\pi/4.0 = \qty{1.57}{rad/s}$; $\Omega^2r = \qty{7.4}{m/s^2} = 0.75g$,
directed outward in the rotating frame; the seat (friction and
restraint) supplies the real inward force.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:3}
$\Omega = 2\pi/86164 = \qty{7.29e-5}{rad/s}$; $\Omega^2R = \qty{0.034}{m/s^2}$,
$0.35\%$ of $g$. Floating off needs $\Omega^2R = g$: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{R/g} =
\qty{84}{min}$ --- seventeen times faster than now.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:4}
$a_C = 2\Omega v\sin\lambda = 2 \times 7.29\times10^{-5} \times 27.8 \times 0.707
= \qty{2.9e-3}{m/s^2}$; $F = \qty{1.1}{kN}$. Heading north: pushed east,
onto the right-hand (eastern) rail; heading east: pushed south, again
the right-hand rail.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:5}
$z = \Omega^2r^2/2g$: \qty{2.0}{mm} at \qty{2}{rad/s}, \qty{5.1}{cm} at
\qty{10}{rad/s}. A paraboloid focuses a parallel beam to a point:
spinning mercury is a perfect, cheap, self-polishing parabolic mirror
(that cannot be tilted).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:6}
The centrifugal acceleration $\Omega^2R\cos\lambda$ points away from the
axis; its component perpendicular to the local vertical is $\Omega^2R\cos\lambda
\sin\lambda$, which tilts $\vect g$ by $\delta \approx \Omega^2R\sin\lambda\cos\lambda/g
= 0.034 \times 0.5/9.81 = \qty{1.7e-3}{rad} = 6'$. Equator: $g$ reduced by
$\Omega^2R = \qty{0.034}{m/s^2}$ from rotation (the flattening adds more).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:7}
$\ddot x_E = 2\Omega gt\cos\lambda$: $x_E = \tfrac13\Omega g\cos\lambda\,t^3$; $t =
\sqrt{2h/g} = \qty{4.5}{s}$: $x_E = \tfrac13 \times 7.29\times10^{-5} \times 9.81
\times 0.707 \times 92 = \qty{1.6}{cm}$. Eastward: the top of the shaft
moves east faster (larger radius) than the bottom; the stone keeps that
excess --- or, in the rotating frame, the \omterm{thm:b1:non-inertial-frames:dynamics}{Coriolis force} on a downward
velocity points east.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:8}
$T_F = 23.93/\sin\lambda$: Paris \qty{31.8}{h}; pole \qty{23.9}{h}; equator
infinite (no precession). Paris: $360^\circ/31.8 = 11.3^\circ$ per hour.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:9}
$a_C = 2\Omega v\sin\lambda = \qty{2.1e-3}{m/s^2}$, to the right. Air
converging from all sides is deflected right, so it spirals in
counterclockwise (seen from above): the northern cyclone. A sink drains
in seconds and a tornado forms in minutes: the Coriolis deflection over
such times is negligible against any initial swirl.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:10}
In the car's frame the apparent gravity $\vect g - \vect a$ tilts backward.
The air, heavier, ``falls'' backward; the balloon, lighter than the air it
displaces, is buoyed up the apparent vertical --- it leans \emph{forward},
opposite to a plumb bob.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:11}
$\Omega^2R = g$: $\Omega = \qty{0.31}{rad/s}$, $T = \qty{20}{s}$. $a_C = 2\Omega v
= \qty{0.94}{m/s^2}$, about $0.1g$: walking with the rotation he feels
heavier by $10\%$ (the \omterm{thm:b1:non-inertial-frames:dynamics}{Coriolis force} points outward), against it
lighter. A dropped ball: in the \omterm{thm:b1:newton-dynamics:laws}{inertial frame} it moves straight while
the floor curves up; over the fall time $\sqrt{2h/g} = \qty{0.64}{s}$ the
deviation is of order $\tfrac13\Omega\,g\,t^3 \cdot 2 \approx 2 \times 0.31 \times
9.81 \times 0.26/3 \approx \qty{0.5}{m}$ --- a few decimeters, against the
rotation.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:non-inertial-frames:12}
$GM/(d - r)^2 - GM/d^2 \approx 2GMr/d^3$ (first order in $r/d$). Moon:
$2 \times 6.67\times10^{-11} \times 7.35\times10^{22} \times 6.37\times10^6/
(3.84\times10^8)^3 = \qty{1.1e-6}{m/s^2}$; Sun: $2 \times 1.33\times10^{20}
\times 6.37\times10^6/(1.50\times10^{11})^3 = \qty{5.0e-7}{m/s^2}$; ratio
$0.46$: the Sun's tides are almost half the Moon's, though its
attraction is $180$ times larger --- tides go as $M/d^3$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:non-inertial-frames:1}
\textbf{1.} $T = 2\pi\sqrt{67/9.81} = \qty{16.4}{s}$; $x_0/\ell = 0.045$
($2.6^\circ$): small.

\textbf{2.} $v_{\max} = x_0\omega_0 = 3.0 \times 0.383 = \qty{1.15}{m/s}$; height
$x_0^2/2\ell = \qty{6.7}{cm}$.

\textbf{3.} $T = mg + mv_{\max}^2/\ell = mg(1 + 0.002)$: $1.002\,mg$.

\textbf{4.} $\tfrac12\rho C_xSv_{\max}^2 = 0.5 \times 1.2 \times 0.05 \times 1.3 =
\qty{0.04}{N}$, against $m\omega_0^2x_0 = \qty{12}{N}$: three hundred times
smaller, yet enough to stop the pendulum in a few hours.

\textbf{5.} A long wire gives a long period, hence a slow bob and a
small relative drag, and makes the precession per swing visible; a
heavy bob stores much energy for the drag to eat --- hours of swinging.

\textbf{6.} Weight (vertical) and tension (in the plane of the wire and
the vertical, i.e.\ in the plane of swing): no force has a component
perpendicular to that plane.

\textbf{7.} The plane is fixed among the stars; the ice turns beneath it
eastward at $\Omega$, so the observer sees the plane turn clockwise,
back to its start after one sidereal day, \qty{23.93}{h}.

\textbf{8.} Each half-swing traces a slightly rotated chord: a rosette;
$86164/8.2 \approx 10500$ half-swings, each $0.034^\circ$ further round.

\textbf{9.} $\vect\Omega = \Omega(0, \cos\lambda, \sin\lambda)$.

\textbf{10.} With $\vect v' = (\dot x, \dot y, 0)$:
$\vect\Omega\wedge\vect v' = \Omega(-\sin\lambda\,\dot y,\ \sin\lambda\,\dot x,\ -\cos\lambda\,\dot x)$,
so $-2m\vect\Omega\wedge\vect v'$ has the horizontal components
$2m\Omega\sin\lambda\,(\dot y, -\dot x)$: only $\Omega\sin\lambda$ enters.

\textbf{11.} $\ddot x = -\omega_0^2x + 2\omega_F\dot y$, $\ddot y = -\omega_0^2y -
2\omega_F\dot x$; $\omega_F = 7.29\times10^{-5} \times 0.753 = \qty{5.5e-5}{rad/s}
\ll \omega_0 = \qty{0.38}{rad/s}$.

\textbf{12.} $\ddot u = -\omega_0^2u + 2\omega_F(\dot y - j\dot x) = -\omega_0^2u -
2j\omega_F\dot u$.

\textbf{13.} $-\alpha^2 - 2\omega_F\alpha + \omega_0^2 = 0$: $\alpha = -\omega_F \pm
\sqrt{\omega_F^2 + \omega_0^2} \approx -\omega_F \pm \omega_0$.

\textbf{14.} The bracket is a plane oscillation (a fixed line through the
origin for real $A = B$); the factor $\eu^{-j\omega_Ft}$ rotates the whole
pattern clockwise at $\omega_F$: the plane of swing precesses clockwise at
$\Omega\sin\lambda$.

\textbf{15.} $T_F = 2\pi/\omega_F = \qty{1.14e5}{s} = \qty{31.8}{h}$; $11.3^\circ$
per hour.

\textbf{16.} $\sin\lambda = 0$: the local vertical component of $\vect\Omega$
vanishes; the horizontal \omterm{thm:b1:non-inertial-frames:dynamics}{Coriolis force} is zero for horizontal motion.

\textbf{17.} $2m\omega_Fv_{\max} = 2 \times 28 \times 5.5\times10^{-5} \times 1.15 =
\qty{3.5}{mN}$; weight \qty{275}{N} ($10^{-5}$ of it), restoring force
$m\omega_0^2x_0 = \qty{12}{N}$: three thousand times smaller.

\textbf{18.} $\omega_FT/2 = 5.5\times10^{-5} \times 8.2 = \qty{4.5e-4}{rad}$:
$x_0 \times 4.5\times10^{-4} = \qty{1.4}{mm}$ per half-swing.

\textbf{19.} Pegs a centimeter apart on the ring fall every $\sim 7$
half-swings, about a minute.

\textbf{20.} A hand push gives a sideways velocity: the bob then
describes an ellipse, whose own slow precession (an effect of the
finite \omterm{def:b1:wave-propagation:signal}{amplitude}) masks Foucault's.

\textbf{21.} A radial kick adds energy along the swing without adding
transverse velocity, so the plane is left untouched; a sideways
component would drive the pendulum into an ellipse and fake or hide the
precession.

\textbf{22.} The precession rate measures $\Omega\sin\lambda$, the vertical
component of the Earth's rotation; knowing $\Omega$ from astronomy, the
rate gives the latitude (and vice versa).

\textbf{23.} $\sin\lambda = 23.93/48 = 0.499$: $\lambda = 30^\circ$.

\textbf{24.} Its \omterm{def:b1:angular-momentum:L}{angular momentum} is conserved, so its axis stays fixed
among the stars while the Earth turns under it: the gyrocompass, which
finds true north without a magnet.

\textbf{25.} The \omterm{thm:b1:non-inertial-frames:dynamics}{Coriolis force} $-2m\vect\Omega\wedge\vect v'$; its horizontal
part grows as $\sin\lambda$; one turn in $23.93\,\text{h}/\sin\lambda$,
\qty{31.8}{h} in Paris.
\end{solution}
